## 1. What Is a Computer Network & The Four Network Types?

A **computer network** is two or more computers or devices connected together (by cables or wirelessly) to **share data, files, internet access, software, and hardware peripherals** (like one shared laser printer for a whole school lab!).

### Four Major Network Types
| Network Type | Geographic Coverage | Typical Ownership & Jamaican Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **LAN** *(Local Area Network)* | **Small local area** — one room, home, office, or school campus. | Owned privately by one organisation — e.g. your **school's computer lab** sharing files and one printer via Ethernet & Wi-Fi! |
| **MAN** *(Metropolitan Area Network)* | Spans a **whole city, town, or metropolitan area** (connecting multiple LANs). | Owned by a city government, university, or ISP — e.g. linking **hospital branches or UWI / UTech campuses across Kingston & St Andrew**! |
| **WAN** *(Wide Area Network)* | Spans **countries, continents, or the entire globe** (connecting LANs & MANs via undersea fibre cables & satellites). | Owned collectively by telecoms & global networks — e.g. **the Internet**, or **NCB linking branches in Jamaica, Trinidad, and Barbados**! |
| **Mobile (Cellular) Network** | Wide geographic coverage split into **radio "cells"** served by cell towers (Digicel, Flow). | A **radio-based common carrier** letting smartphones stay connected seamlessly while travelling in cars or buses! |

### Overview of Mobile Network Generations (`2G` → Current `5G`)
*(Syllabus note: You do NOT need to know radio engineering internals — just the practical evolution!)*
- **2G (Second Generation):** Digital voice calls + **SMS text messaging** + very slow basic mobile data.
- **3G (Third Generation):** Introduced real **mobile broadband internet**, email, GPS maps, and basic video calling on early smartphones.
- **4G / LTE (Fourth Generation):** High-speed mobile broadband enabling **smooth HD video streaming (YouTube, Netflix), Zoom classes, online mobile gaming, and cloud apps**!
- **5G (Fifth Generation — Current):** Ultra-fast multi-gigabit speeds, **ultra-low latency** (near-zero lag), and massive capacity for **IoT smart cities, remote robotic surgery, self-driving vehicles, and instant 4K live streaming**!

### Short-Range Wireless Technologies
- **Bluetooth:** Very short-range (~10 m), low-power wireless linking personal peripherals (**wireless earbuds, phone-to-car audio, wireless keyboard/mouse, portable speaker**).
- **Wi-Fi (Wireless Fidelity):** Medium-range (~30–100 m indoors) high-speed wireless LAN connecting laptops, phones, and smart TVs to a router/access point!
- **Hotspot:** A physical location or mobile phone feature sharing a Wi-Fi internet connection with nearby devices (e.g. airport/café public Wi-Fi, or **tethering** your laptop to your phone's 4G data!).

## 2. Levels of Network Privacy: Intranet vs Extranet vs Internet

Paper 1 loves contrasting these three — pay close attention to **WHO is allowed inside!**

| Network Level | Who Can Access It? | Practical Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Intranet** | **PRIVATE — Internal employees/members ONLY** inside one organisation (protected by firewall & staff logins). | A Jamaican bank's internal staff portal holding employee HR forms, policies, and internal memos — blocked from the outside public! |
| **Extranet** | **SEMI-PRIVATE — Controlled extension of an Intranet** granting limited login access to **trusted external partners** (suppliers, distributors, registered customers). | GraceKennedy letting approved supermarkets log in to track wholesale shipments and invoices online — still closed to the general public! |
| **Internet** | **PUBLIC — Worldwide network of networks** open to anyone on Earth with an ISP connection! | Public websites like Schwork, Wikipedia, YouTube, and Jamaican news outlets! |

## 3. Transmission Media (Wired vs Wireless)

Data travels across networks through **transmission media** (channels):

### A. Wired (Guided / Physical) Media
| Wired Medium | Description | Speed & Distance | Security & Interference | Best Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Twisted Pair Cable** *(Ethernet — UTP / STP)* | Pairs of insulated copper wires twisted together to cancel out electrical interference. | Good (100 Mbps – 10 Gbps) over short runs (~100 m). | Cheap & easy to install; susceptible to electromagnetic interference (EMI) & eavesdropping if unshielded. | **Standard school & office LANs** (Ethernet patch cables), telephone lines. |
| **Coaxial Cable** | Thick central copper core surrounded by insulation, braided metal shield, and outer jacket. | Moderate–High over medium distances. | Better shielded against EMI than UTP; stiffer and slightly pricier. | **Cable TV & broadband internet** (e.g. Flow home cable modem line), CCTV cameras. |
| **Fibre-Optic Cable** | Hair-thin strands of pure glass/plastic carrying **pulses of light** instead of electricity! | **Fastest speeds & longest distances** (terabits/sec, km without boosting!). | **Immune to EMI & extremely hard to tap (highest security!)**; **most expensive** & fragile to splice! | **ISP internet backbones, undersea submarine cables linking Jamaica to the world, high-speed business/FTTH fibre!** |

### B. Wireless (Unguided) Media
| Wireless Medium | How It Travels | Strengths & Weaknesses | Real-World Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infrared (IR)** | Short-range invisible light beam requiring **strict direct Line-of-Sight** — **cannot pass through walls!** | Very cheap & interference-free, **BUT** blocked by any solid object or person walking in front! | TV/AC/projector **remote controls**, short-range sensors. |
| **Microwave** | High-frequency radio waves beamed between **Line-of-Sight towers** (up to ~50 km apart, limited by mountains/Earth's curvature!). | High bandwidth without laying cables across rivers/hills; affected by heavy tropical rainstorms! | Linking **mobile cell towers**, inter-parish telecom towers across Jamaican hills! |
| **Satellite** | Microwave signals bounced from an earth station up to an **orbiting space satellite** and back down across huge areas! | Reaches **remote rural villages, ships at sea, planes, and disaster zones** where cables don't exist — **BUT** expensive and has noticeable signal delay (**latency**) & bad-weather fade! | **GPS navigation**, remote rural internet (e.g. Starlink), live international TV broadcasts, hurricane tracking! |

## 4. Basic Network Hardware Components (Know Every Box's Exact Job!)

| Hardware Device | Exact Function in Plain English |
| :--- | :--- |
| **NIC (Network Interface Card / Network Adapter)** | Hardware chip/card built inside every computer or phone that **physically connects it to a network** (via Ethernet port or built-in Wi-Fi radio) and gives it a unique permanent hardware ID (**MAC address**)! |
| **Switch** | Connects multiple devices **inside the SAME LAN** (e.g. 24 computers in your IT lab) and **intelligently forwards data frames ONLY to the exact destination device** (reducing network traffic collisions!). |
| **Router** | Connects **TWO OR MORE DIFFERENT networks together** (e.g. your home LAN to the ISP's WAN / Internet!), reads **IP addresses**, and chooses the **best path** to forward data packets across the globe! |
| **Modem** *(**Mo**dulator–**Dem**odulator)* | Converts **digital signals** from your computer into **analogue/transmission signals** (modulation) to travel over telephone/cable lines, and converts incoming signals back into **digital** (demodulation) for your devices! *(Home ISP boxes often combine Modem + Router + Wi-Fi Switch into one unit!)* |

## 5. Importance of Mobile Communication Technologies (3 Key Fields!)

Mobile networks & smartphones have transformed daily life across Jamaica and the Caribbean:
1. **Education:** Students attend live online classes (Google Classroom, Zoom), read free mobile study sites (like Schwork!) from home or rural areas, collaborate on group SBAs via WhatsApp/Docs, and access e-books anytime — bridging textbook shortages *(challenge: distraction & unequal data costs/digital divide)*.
2. **Commerce (M-Commerce):** Small vendors accept cashless mobile payments (**Lynk**, mobile banking transfers, portable card readers), run Instagram/WhatsApp shops, receive instant bank fraud alerts, and manage delivery tracking on the road!
3. **Journalism:** Reporters file breaking stories, HD photos, and live video broadcasts directly from hurricane scenes or sports meets using 4G/5G phones without heavy satellite vans — plus citizen journalists share real-time updates *(challenge: rapid spread of unverified fake news!)*.

## 6. Worked Example: Designing a School Network & Picking Transmission Media

<WorkedExample
  title="Upgrading a Jamaican High School Campus Network (Paper 2 Style)"
  marks={8}
  problem={
    <>
      A Jamaican high school has <strong>30 desktop computers in Lab A</strong>, a separate <strong>Administration Building 400 metres away</strong> across the campus field, and wants teachers to log into a private grading portal while allowing parents (with assigned passwords) to view ONLY their child’s attendance online.
      <br />
      (a) State the <strong>network device</strong> needed to connect all 30 desktops inside Lab A, and the device needed to connect the school’s network to the Internet.
      <br />
      (b) Recommend the <strong>best wired transmission medium</strong> to link Lab A and the Administration Building (400 m apart) and give <strong>TWO</strong> reasons.
      <br />
      (c) Identify whether the teachers-only grading portal is an <strong>Intranet</strong> or <strong>Extranet</strong>, and classify the parent-accessible attendance portal.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Watch the distance clue: '400 metres away'! Standard Twisted Pair (Ethernet) degrades after ~100 metres — so Fibre-Optic cable is the clear winner for linking buildings across a campus!"
>

### (a) Network Hardware Devices
- Connecting 30 desktops inside Lab A (same LAN): **Switch** *(plus each desktop requires a **NIC**!)*.
- Connecting the school's LAN to the Internet (different network): **Router** *(working with a **Modem** from the ISP)*.

### (b) Best Wired Medium Across 400 Metres: **Fibre-Optic Cable**
1. **Long Distance Without Signal Loss:** Easily spans 400+ metres (far beyond Twisted Pair's ~100 m copper limit!).
2. **Ultra-High Speed & Immune to Interference:** Carries light signals immune to electrical noise/lightning surges and very difficult to intercept!

### (c) Intranet vs Extranet
- **Teachers-only internal grading portal:** **Intranet** *(strictly private to internal school staff)*.
- **Password-protected parent attendance portal:** **Extranet** *(grants controlled access to authorised external stakeholders — parents — while staying closed to the general public!)*.

</WorkedExample>
