## 1. Data vs Information, Sources & Document Types

### Data vs Information
- **Data:** **Raw, unprocessed facts, figures, symbols, or measurements** that have little meaning by themselves (e.g. `82`, `76`, `91`, `K. Campbell`).
- **Information:** **Data that has been processed, organised, calculated, or put into context** so it becomes meaningful and supports human decision-making (e.g. *"K. Campbell scored an average of 83% in IT — Grade I — Honor Roll!"*).
- **Sources of Data & Information:** **People** (interviews, surveys, eyewitness reports), **Places** (libraries, museums, banks, websites, archives), and **Things** (sensors, weather gauges, printed receipts, barcodes, historical artefacts).

### Five Essential Document Types (Syllabus Objective 1.11!)
| Document Type | Clear Definition | Real-World Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Human-Readable Document** | Printed or displayed in normal words/numbers a human can read directly without a machine. | A printed school report card, newspaper, or handwritten order form. |
| **Machine-Readable Document** | Encoded in a format designed to be read automatically by a computer scanner/reader. | A supermarket **barcode**, QR code, magnetic stripe on a debit card, or OMR shaded sheet. |
| **Turnaround Document** | A computer-generated output document sent out to a customer/user that is **returned later as INPUT** back into the same computer system! | A **JPS electricity bill** or **NWC water bill** with a tear-off barcode/OCR payment stub; a pre-printed school attendance OMR sheet! |
| **Hard Copy** | Permanent physical printed output on paper or microfilm. | A printed exam timetable or paper receipt. |
| **Soft Copy** | Temporary electronic digital version stored/viewed on a screen or file. | A `.pdf` invoice attached to an email or a Google Doc on your laptop. |

## 2. Evaluating Reliability of Online Information (4 Criteria!)

Anyone can publish anything on the internet in 30 seconds! Before trusting a website for an SBA or news report, check these **4 official criteria** (**ACRB**):

1. **Authenticity (Authority / Accuracy):** *Who wrote or published it?* Is it from a credible official institution (`.gov.jm`, `.edu`, `.org`, recognised university/journal) with verifiable citations, or an anonymous blog full of spelling errors?
2. **Currency (Up-to-Date):** *When was it written or last updated?* An IT article from 2011 claiming "4 GB hard drives are huge" is outdated — check publication dates and working links!
3. **Relevance:** *Does it actually answer your specific question at the right depth?* A university physics thesis isn't relevant if you need a high-school overview of Jamaican parish populations.
4. **Bias (Objectivity):** *Is the author trying to sell something, push a political agenda, or present only one side?* A smartphone brand's own website claiming "Our phone is #1 on Earth" is biased compared to an independent consumer tech review!

## 3. Data Validation vs Data Verification (**HUGE Exam Topic!**)

Every year on Paper 1 and Paper 2, students lose easy marks by mixing up **Validation** and **Verification**! Memorise this crystal-clear difference:

> - **Data VALIDATION** = Automatic checks performed by **COMPUTER SOFTWARE** at data entry to ensure data is **sensible, reasonable, complete, and follows preset rules**! *(Caution: Validation CANNOT prove a number is factually true — if someone's real age is `16` and you type `18`, a range check `10–20` still accepts `18`!)*
> - **Data VERIFICATION** = Checking that data copied or typed from a **source document matches the original accurately** (catching **human typing mistakes**)!

### All Seven Validation Checks (Memorise Every One!)

| Validation Check | What Rule It Enforces | Concrete Jamaican Exam Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Range Check** | Value must lie between a preset **minimum and maximum limit**. | Exam score must be `0` to `100`; student age `11` to `19`; month `1` to `12`. |
| **2. Data Type Check** | Characters must match the declared **data type** (numbers only vs letters only). | Rejecting `"Twenty"` in a `Price` numeric field, or rejecting `"45"` in a `FirstName` text field! |
| **3. Presence Check** | A mandatory field **cannot be left blank / empty**! | Requiring `TRN`, `LastName`, or `Email` before letting a user submit an online registration form. |
| **4. Length Check** | Entry must contain an **exact, minimum, or maximum number of characters**. | Jamaican **TRN** must be **exactly 9 digits** (`123-456-789`); password must be **at least 8 characters**. |
| **5. Format Check (Picture Check)** | Characters must follow a **strict letter/number pattern**. | Jamaican licence plate (`AB 1234` → 2 letters + 4 digits), Date (`DD/MM/YYYY`), or email containing `@` and `.`! |
| **6. Consistency Check** | Compares **TWO related fields** together to make sure they don't contradict each other! | `DepartureDate` cannot be *earlier* than `ArrivalDate`; a person titled `"Mr."` shouldn't have `Gender = Female`; a `12-year-old` shouldn't have `DriverLicence = Yes`! |
| **7. Reasonableness Check** | Checks whether a value makes **logical practical sense** in real life (even if technically the right data type!). | Flagging a weekly part-time cashier wage of **J\$5,000,000** or an electricity bill of **999,999 kWh** for a tiny studio apartment! |

### Two Data Verification Methods & Two Human Error Types
1. **Double Entry:** Data is typed **TWICE** (by the same clerk or two separate clerks, or like typing your **New Password** and **Confirm Password**!) — the computer compares both entries and flags any mismatch!
2. **Proofreading (Visual Check):** A human reads the entered data on screen/printout **side-by-side against the original paper source document** to spot mistakes!

**Two Classic Human Typing Errors Caught by Verification:**
- **Typographical Error (Typo):** Hitting the wrong key, skipping a character, or adding an extra key (e.g. typing `"Kingtson"` or `"4500"` instead of `"450"`).
- **Transpositional Error:** Swapping the positions of two adjacent characters (e.g. typing **`58`** instead of **`85`**, or **`from`** as **`form`**!).

## 4. File Organisation & Access Methods (Syllabus Objective 1.15!)

How records are stored inside a computer file determines **how fast** the computer can locate any single record:

| File Organisation / Access Method | How Records Are Stored & Found | Best Real-World Application | Pros & Cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Serial** | Records stored **one after another in chronological order of arrival** (unsorted!) — searched from record #1 onward. | **System log files, transaction audit trails, cheap backup archiving** on magnetic tape. | Fast to append new records; **very slow** to search for one specific record! |
| **Sequential** | Records stored in a **strict sorted key order** (e.g. sorted by `EmployeeID` `001, 002, 003…`) — computer reads through records in sequence until finding the match. | **Monthly Payroll Files** *(every single worker gets paid in one batch!)*, utility billing batches, bank statements. | Excellent for **batch processing** where *most/all* records are processed together; **slow** for looking up one random customer instantly! |
| **Direct / Random Access** | Uses a mathematical formula (**hashing algorithm**) or index on the key field to **jump STRAIGHT to the exact storage address** without reading earlier records! | **Real-Time Systems!** — **Bank ATMs**, **Supermarket POS barcode lookups**, **Airline seat reservations**, **Emergency hospital patient lookups**. | **Instant retrieval** regardless of file size (requires disk/SSD — impossible on tape!); slightly more complex file management. |

*(Bonus term you may see on older Paper 1s: **Indexed Sequential** keeps records sorted sequentially PLUS maintains an index table so you can process batches sequentially OR jump straight to one record quickly!)*

## 5. Worked Example: Choosing Validation Checks & Verification on an Online School Portal

<WorkedExample
  title="Applying Validation, Verification & File Access (Paper 2 Scenario)"
  marks={10}
  problem={
    <>
      A Jamaican high school builds an online student registration portal collecting: <code>StudentID</code> (format <code>STU-2026-001</code>), <code>DateOfBirth</code>, <code>EnrollmentDate</code>, <code>Age</code> (11–18), and <code>ParentEmail</code> (mandatory).
      <br />
      (a) Identify the <strong>MOST specific validation check</strong> for each of the following rules:
      <br />
      &nbsp;&nbsp;(i) Ensuring <code>ParentEmail</code> is not left blank.
      <br />
      &nbsp;&nbsp;(ii) Ensuring <code>Age</code> falls between 11 and 18 inclusive.
      <br />
      &nbsp;&nbsp;(iii) Ensuring <code>StudentID</code> follows the exact pattern <code>LLL-DDDD-DDD</code>.
      <br />
      &nbsp;&nbsp;(iv) Ensuring <code>EnrollmentDate</code> is not earlier than <code>DateOfBirth</code>.
      <br />
      (b) A clerk accidentally types a student’s score as <strong><code>79</code></strong> instead of <strong><code>97</code></strong>. Name this type of error AND explain why a Range Check (<code>0–100</code>) will NOT catch it, then state which technique WILL catch it.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Notice part (b) — this exact '79 instead of 97' question is one of the highest-frequency questions in the entire IT syllabus! Always connect: Transpositional Error → still valid within 0–100 (so Validation passes!) → caught by Verification (Double Entry or Proofreading)!"
>

### (a) Specific Validation Checks
- **(i) `ParentEmail` not left blank:** **Presence Check**
- **(ii) `Age` between 11 and 18:** **Range Check**
- **(iii) `StudentID` pattern `LLL-DDDD-DDD`:** **Format Check**
- **(iv) `EnrollmentDate` after `DateOfBirth`:** **Consistency Check** *(compares TWO related fields!)*

### (b) The `79` vs `97` Error
- **Type of Error:** **Transpositional error** *(two adjacent digits swapped)*.
- **Why Validation misses it:** `79` is a valid integer inside the allowed range `0–100`, so the computer's **Range Check** and **Data Type Check** both approve it!
- **How to catch it:** **Data Verification** — specifically **Double Entry** (typing the mark sheet twice so the system flags `79 ≠ 97`) or **Proofreading** against the teacher's original mark book!

</WorkedExample>
