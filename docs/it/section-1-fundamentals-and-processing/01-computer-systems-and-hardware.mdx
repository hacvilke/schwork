## 1. What Is Information Technology (IT)?

**Information Technology (IT)** is the use of **computers, storage devices, networks, software, and electronic communications** to **collect, process, store, protect, retrieve, and share information** accurately and rapidly.

In plain English: whenever a Jamaican supermarket scans your groceries at checkout, a bank sends a WhatsApp/SMS alert for an ATM withdrawal, or a school calculates report cards in Excel, **Information Technology** is turning raw facts into useful information!

---

## 2. Five Major Types of Computer Systems

Paper 1 and Paper 2 frequently ask you to compare computers by **processing speed**, **storage capacity**, **cost**, and **portability**:

| Computer Type | Processing Speed | Storage Capacity | Portability | Real-World Examples & Uses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Supercomputer** | **Fastest on Earth** (quadrillions of calculations per second — *FLOPS*) | Massive specialised parallel storage | Not portable (room-sized) | **Cray** / modern supercomputers: hurricane & weather forecasting (e.g. Meteorological Service models), climate simulations, nuclear & medical research. |
| **2. Mainframe** | **Extremely high** — handles thousands of simultaneous users & transactions reliably | Massive enterprise-grade storage (petabytes) | Not portable (server rooms) | **IBM zEnterprise**: national banks (NCB, Scotiabank), airline booking systems, Tax Administration Jamaica (TAJ), large insurance firms. |
| **3. Desktop System** *(Microcomputer / PC)* | **Moderate to High** (upgradeable CPU, GPU, RAM) | High (500 GB – 4+ TB SSD/HDD) | Low (stationary on a desk) | School computer labs, accounting offices, graphic design studios, home gaming PCs. |
| **4. Mobile Devices** | **Moderate** (optimised for low battery power) | Moderate (64 GB – 1 TB flash) | **Very High** (fits in backpack or pocket) | **Laptops, notebooks, netbooks, smartphones, tablets, handheld game consoles** — studying on the go, mobile banking, field reporting. |
| **5. Embedded Devices** | **Low / Special-Purpose** (dedicated to ONE fixed task) | Very small built-in ROM/flash | Built directly inside another machine | Microcontrollers inside **microwaves, washing machines, car ignition/ABS braking systems, traffic lights, digital watches, answering machines, smart TVs**. |

> **Exam Tip (Supercomputer vs Mainframe!):** Never confuse these two! A **Supercomputer** concentrates *all* its speed on **one giant complex scientific calculation** (like hurricane path modelling). A **Mainframe** excels at **high-volume transaction processing** — serving **thousands of everyday users simultaneously** (like processing 50,000 ATM withdrawals per minute!).

## 3. Major Hardware Components & The IPOS Cycle

**Hardware** means all the physical, touchable electronic and mechanical parts of a computer system. Every computer — from a smartphone to a supercomputer — works through the **IPOS Cycle** (**I**nput → **P**rocessing → **O**utput → **S**torage):

```text
                       ┌──────────────────────────────────┐
                       │   CENTRAL PROCESSING UNIT (CPU)  │
                       │  ┌────────────┐  ┌────────────┐  │
                       │  │Control Unit│  │    ALU     │  │
                       │  │    (CU)    │  │(Math/Logic)│  │
                       │  └────────────┘  └────────────┘  │
                       └───────────▲────────────▲─────────┘
                                   │            │
 ┌──────────────┐      ┌───────────▼────────────▼─────────┐      ┌──────────────┐
 │ INPUT DEVICES├─────►│    PRIMARY MEMORY (RAM & ROM)    ├─────►│OUTPUT DEVICES│
 │(Keyb, Scanner│      │   (Fast working workspace!)      │      │(Monitor,Prnt)│
 └──────────────┘      └────────────────▲─────────────────┘      └──────────────┘
                                        │
                       ┌────────────────▼─────────────────┐
                       │        SECONDARY STORAGE         │
                       │  (SSD, HDD, USB Flash, Optical)  │
                       │    (Permanent long-term save)    │
                       └──────────────────────────────────┘
```

### How Each Component Works
1. **Input Devices:** Capture raw data & user instructions from the outside world and convert them into binary signals (`0`s and `1`s) the computer understands (e.g. keyboard, barcode scanner, microphone).
2. **Central Processing Unit (CPU — the "Brain"):** Fetches, decodes, and executes instructions using three internal parts:
   - **Control Unit (CU):** The "traffic director" — fetches instructions from memory, decodes them, and coordinates signals across all hardware components.
   - **Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU):** Performs all **arithmetic** (`+`, `-`, `*`, `/`) and **logical comparisons** (`>`, `<`, `=`, `AND`, `OR`).
   - **Registers:** Ultra-fast tiny temporary storage slots directly inside the CPU holding the exact data/instruction being worked on right now.
3. **Primary Memory (Main Memory):** Fast electronic memory directly accessed by the CPU — split into **RAM** and **ROM**:
   - **RAM (Random Access Memory):** **Volatile** (*erased the instant power goes off!*), **Read/Write**, holds the Operating System, active programs, and unsaved documents currently being processed.
   - **ROM (Read-Only Memory):** **Non-volatile** (*keeps data even when power is off!*), normally **Read-Only**, holds the permanent startup firmware (**BIOS / UEFI**) that boots the computer when you press the power button!
4. **Secondary Storage (Backing Storage):** **Non-volatile** permanent storage with much larger capacity and lower cost per GB than RAM — stores your OS, software, photos, music, and SBA files long-term!
5. **Output Devices:** Convert processed binary results back into human-understandable form (text/images on screen, printed paper, or sound).

## 4. Secondary Storage Devices & Units of Storage

### Comparing Secondary Storage Media

| Storage Medium | Technology | Access Type | Typical Capacity | Best Use & Key Pros/Cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hard Disk Drive (HDD)** | Spinning magnetic platters + read/write head | Direct / Random | 500 GB – 16+ TB | Cheap per GB for big desktop files; moving parts can break if dropped. |
| **Solid State Drive (SSD) / Flash Drive / Memory Card (SD)** | Solid-state flash chips *(no moving parts!)* | Direct / Random | 16 GB – 8 TB | **Blazing fast**, silent, shock-resistant, portable (USB flash & SD cards for phones/cameras); higher cost per GB than HDD. |
| **Optical Discs (CD / DVD / Blu-Ray)** | Laser reads pits/lands on reflective disc | Direct | CD: ~700 MB; DVD: ~4.7 GB; Blu-Ray: 25–50 GB | Distributing software, movies, music, read-only archives; easily scratched and slower than flash. |
| **Magnetic Tape** | Long coated ribbon wound on reels/cartridges | **Sequential ONLY** *(slow to rewind/fast-forward!)* | Huge (multiple TBs per cartridge) | **Cheap bulk off-site backup & long-term archiving** for banks & government agencies! |

### Units of Storage (Know Every Step & Conversion!)

Computers store everything in **binary digits (bits)**: `0` or `1`.

$$

1\text{ Megabyte (MB)} &= 1{,}024\text{ KB} \quad (\approx 1\text{ million B}) \\
1\text{ Gigabyte (GB)} &= 1{,}024\text{ MB} \quad (\approx 1\text{ billion B}) \\
1\text{ Terabyte (TB)} &= 1{,}024\text{ GB} \quad (\approx 1\text{ trillion B})
\end{aligned}
$$

*(Quick Check on Paper 1 & 2: To convert **smaller → larger** [e.g. MB to GB], **DIVIDE** by 1,024 [or 1,000 if the question states decimal]. To convert **larger → smaller** [e.g. GB to MB], **MULTIPLY** by 1,024!)*

## 5. Cloud Storage vs Local Storage (Syllabus Objective 1.5!)

- **Local Storage:** Saving files directly onto physical hardware in your possession (your laptop's SSD, desktop HDD, USB flash drive, or school server).
- **Cloud Storage:** Saving files on remote internet-connected data-centre servers managed by a provider (e.g. **Google Drive**, **Microsoft OneDrive**, **iCloud**, **Dropbox**).

| Criterion | Local Storage | Cloud Storage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Capacity** | Fixed by the drive size you buy (upgrading requires buying a new drive). | **Highly scalable** — upgrade storage instantly online (though free tiers are capped, e.g. 15 GB). |
| **Cost** | **One-time purchase cost** for the drive; no ongoing monthly subscription. | Often free for small space, then **ongoing monthly/annual subscription fees** for larger plans. |
| **Accessibility** | Works **100% offline** with fast local transfer speeds — **BUT** you only access files if you have that physical device with you! | Accessible **anywhere in the world on any device** (phone, school lab, home PC) + easy real-time collaboration — **REQUIRES active internet**! |
| **Security & Reliability** | Full physical control, **BUT** vulnerable to **fire, theft, laptop drops, or lost USB drives** if you don't back up! | Protected against house fire/lost laptop via automatic provider backups — **BUT** exposed to **account hacking, weak passwords, data breaches, or provider outages**! |

## 6. Worked Example: Storage Capacity Calculation & System Selection

<WorkedExample
  title="Calculating Flash Drive Capacity & Evaluating Storage Choices (Paper 2 Style)"
  marks={8}
  problem={
    <>
      (a) A student has an empty <strong>4 GB</strong> USB flash drive (use <strong>1 GB = 1,024 MB</strong>). How many high-resolution video clips of <strong>256 MB</strong> each can fit on the flash drive?
      <br />
      (b) A Jamaican media student backs up her SBA project on BOTH her laptop’s SSD (local storage) and Google Drive (cloud storage). Give <strong>TWO</strong> advantages of using Cloud Storage and <strong>ONE</strong> security risk she must guard against.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Always show your conversion step FIRST (converting GB into MB so both numbers share the SAME unit!) before dividing — that earns full method marks even if you slip on arithmetic!"
>

### (a) Flash Drive Calculation
1. **Convert Flash Drive Capacity from GB to MB:**
   $$4\text{ GB} \times 1{,}024 = 4{,}096\text{ MB}$$
2. **Divide Total Space by Size per Video Clip:**
   $$\frac{4{,}096\text{ MB}}{256\text{ MB}} = \mathbf{16\text{ video clips}}$$

### (b) Cloud Storage Evaluation
- **Two Advantages of Cloud Storage:**
  1. **Anytime / Anywhere Accessibility:** She can open and edit her SBA from home, the school lab, or her phone without carrying a USB flash drive.
  2. **Protection Against Local Hardware Loss:** Even if her laptop is stolen or crashes, her files remain safely backed up on remote servers.
- **One Security Risk:** Unauthorised access or account hacking (if she uses a weak password or stays logged in on a shared school computer) — mitigated by using a strong password and Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)!

</WorkedExample>
