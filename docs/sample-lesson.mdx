## 1. Key Formulas

<FormulaCard title="The Quadratic Formula" onFormulaSheet={true}>

For any quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ where $a \neq 0$:

$$
x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}
$$

The discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ tells you how many real roots the equation has:
- $\Delta > 0$ — two distinct real roots
- $\Delta = 0$ — one repeated real root (touches the $x$-axis)
- $\Delta < 0$ — no real roots

</FormulaCard>

<FormulaCard title="Axis of Symmetry & Turning Point" onFormulaSheet={false}>

For $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$, the equation of the axis of symmetry is:

$$
x = -\frac{b}{2a}
$$

</FormulaCard>

## 5. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Finding the Roots & Minimum Point of a Quadratic"
  marks={4}
  problem={
    <>
      Given the quadratic function <strong>f(x) = x² − 2x − 3</strong>, determine:
      (a) the roots of <em>f(x) = 0</em>, and (b) the coordinates of its minimum point.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Always check the sign of a first! When a > 0 the parabola opens upward (U-shape), so the turning point is a MINIMUM. When a < 0 it opens downward, giving a MAXIMUM."
>

**(a) Find the roots by factorising:**

$$
x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0
$$

Find two numbers that multiply to $-3$ and add to $-2$: those are $-3$ and $+1$.

$$
(x - 3)(x + 1) = 0
$$

$$
x = 3 \quad \text{or} \quad x = -1
$$

**(b) Find the minimum point:**

Use $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$ with $a = 1$ and $b = -2$:

$$
x = -\frac{-2}{2(1)} = 1
$$

Substitute $x = 1$ back into $f(x)$:

$$
f(1) = (1)^2 - 2(1) - 3 = 1 - 2 - 3 = -4
$$

Therefore, the minimum turning point is **$(1,\,-4)$**.

</WorkedExample>
