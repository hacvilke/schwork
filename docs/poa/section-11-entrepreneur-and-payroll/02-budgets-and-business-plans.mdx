## 1. Cash Flow Projections (Never Run Out of Cash!)

Even a profitable startup can collapse if it runs out of **cash** before customers pay! A **Cash Flow Projection (Cash Budget)** forecasts expected **Cash Inflows** (owner's equity, cash sales, grants, collections from debtors) and **Cash Outflows** (ingredients/purchases, rent, wages, utilities, transport) month by month so an entrepreneur spots any cash deficit in advance.

<FormulaCard
  title="Cash Flow & Production Budget Formulae"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="One month's Closing Cash Balance automatically becomes next month's Opening Cash Balance — and Production Budgets adjust Forecast Sales for desired Closing Stock and existing Opening Stock!"
>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Monthly Closing Cash Balance"
  short="Start with Opening Cash, add all Cash Inflows received that month, and subtract all Cash Outflows paid out that month."
>

$$
\text{Closing Cash} = \text{Opening Cash} + \text{Total Cash Inflows} - \text{Total Cash Outflows}
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Required Production (Units)"
  short="You must make enough units to cover Forecast Sales PLUS keep your target Closing Stock on the shelf, minus whatever Opening Stock you already have!"
>

$$
\text{Production (Units)} = \text{Forecast Sales} + \text{Desired Closing Stock} - \text{Opening Stock}
$$

</FormulaHint>

</FormulaCard>

## 2. Worked Example 1: 3-Month Cash Flow Projection (*Irie Juices Startup*)

<WorkedExample
  title="Irie Juices — 3-Month Cash Flow Projection (Jan–Mar 2026)"
  marks={8}
  problem={
    <>
      <strong>Irie Juices</strong> starts on 1 January 2026 with <strong>J$50,000</strong> owner’s cash.
      <br />
      • Forecast Cash Sales: Jan <strong>J$60,000</strong>; Feb <strong>J$85,000</strong>; Mar <strong>J$110,000</strong>
      <br />
      • Cash Outflows: Ingredients (Jan <strong>35,000</strong>; Feb <strong>45,000</strong>; Mar <strong>50,000</strong>); Rent <strong>J$20,000/month</strong>; Wages &amp; Utilities <strong>J$25,000/month</strong>.
      <br />
      Prepare a 3-Month Cash Flow Projection for January to March 2026.
    </>
  }
  examTip="NEVER include non-cash expenses like Depreciation inside a Cash Flow Projection! Only actual cash coming in or going out belongs in a Cash Flow Projection!"
>

| Item | January (J\$) | February (J\$) | March (J\$) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Opening Cash Balance** *(previous month's closing!)* | **50,000** | **30,000** | **25,000** |
| **Add: Cash Inflows** | | | |
| Cash Sales | 60,000 | 85,000 | 110,000 |
| **Total Cash Available** | **110,000** | **115,000** | **135,000** |
| | | | |
| **Less: Cash Outflows** | | | |
| Ingredients / Raw Materials | 35,000 | 45,000 | 50,000 |
| Shop Rent | 20,000 | 20,000 | 20,000 |
| Wages & Utilities | 25,000 | 25,000 | 25,000 |
| **Total Cash Outflows** | **(80,000)** | **(90,000)** | **(95,000)** |
| **Closing Cash Balance** | **30,000** | **25,000** | **40,000** |

*(Notice cash stays positive every month and begins growing cleanly to **J\$40,000** by March as sales ramp up!)*

</WorkedExample>

## 3. Worked Example 2: 3-Month Sales Budget & Production Budget

<WorkedExample
  title="YardCraft Bags — Sales Budget & Production Budget (Jan–Mar 2026)"
  marks={10}
  problem={
    <>
      <strong>YardCraft Bags</strong> sells handmade tote bags at <strong>J$2,500 per bag</strong>.
      <br />
      • Forecast Sales (units): Jan <strong>100</strong>; Feb <strong>150</strong>; Mar <strong>200</strong>; Apr <strong>250</strong>.
      <br />
      • Stock Policy: Maintain Closing Stock equal to <strong>20% of the following month’s forecast sales</strong>.
      <br />
      • Opening Stock on 1 January 2026: <strong>20 bags</strong>.
      <br />
      Prepare: (a) the <strong>3-Month Sales Budget</strong> and (b) the <strong>3-Month Production Budget</strong> (Jan–Mar 2026).
    </>
  }
  examTip="Notice how February's Opening Stock (30 bags) is simply January's Closing Stock — and in the Total column, ALWAYS use January's Opening Stock (20) and March's Closing Stock (50), NEVER add up all three months' opening stocks!"
>

### (a) Sales Budget (January – March 2026)

| Particulars | January | February | March | Quarter Total |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Forecast Sales *(units)* | 100 | 150 | 200 | **450** |
| × Unit Selling Price *(J\$)* | × 2,500 | × 2,500 | × 2,500 | × 2,500 |
| **Budgeted Sales Revenue (J\$)** | **250,000** | **375,000** | **500,000** | **1,125,000** |

### (b) Production Budget (Units — January – March 2026)

| Particulars | January | February | March | Quarter Total |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Forecast Sales *(units)* | 100 | 150 | 200 | 450 |
| **Add:** Desired Closing Stock *(20% of next month's sales)* | 30 | 40 | 50 | 50 |
| *Total Units Needed* | *130* | *190* | *250* | *500* |
| **Less:** Opening Stock *(units)* | (20) | (30) | (40) | (20) |
| **Required Production (Units)** | **110** | **160** | **210** | **480** |

*(Check: $110 + 160 + 210 = \mathbf{480\text{ bags}}$ ✓!)*

</WorkedExample>

## 4. Essential Sections of a Simple Business Plan

Whether raising chickens, farming cash crops, or launching a craft shop, banks and investors ask for a clear **Business Plan** with **five core sections**:

1. **Executive Summary:** Crisp 1-page snapshot of the entire plan — business name, mission/goals, product/service, funding needed, and key financial highlights *(written last, read first!)*.
2. **Company / Business Background:** Ownership type (Sole Trader, Partnership, Ltd), location, founders' skills, and legal registrations.
3. **Marketing Plan & Market Analysis:** Target customers, competitors, pricing, promotion, and sales targets.
4. **Operations Plan:** How the product/service is made or delivered — equipment, suppliers, staffing, and daily workflow.
5. **Financial Plan:** Startup capital needed, **Cash Flow Projection**, **Sales & Production Budgets**, and projected **Trading & Profit and Loss Account** & **Balance Sheet**!
