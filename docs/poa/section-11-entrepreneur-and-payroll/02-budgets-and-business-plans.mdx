## 1. Cash Flow Projections (The Entrepreneur's Early-Warning Radar!)

More small businesses fail from **running out of cash** than from lack of sales! Even a profitable enterprise can collapse if bills (rent, wages, suppliers) fall due *before* credit customers pay cash.

A **Cash Flow Projection (Cash Budget)** is a month-by-month financial forecast (typically prepared over **3 to 6 months**) estimating expected **Cash Inflows** and **Cash Outflows** to predict the **Closing Cash Balance** at the end of every month:
- **Cash Inflows:** Owner's startup equity/cash, cash sales, cash collected from credit debtors, bank loans, government/agricultural grants, and investment maturities!
- **Cash Outflows:** Cash purchases of raw materials/stock, payments to credit suppliers, rent, wages & salaries, utilities (JPS, NWC, internet), transportation, loan repayments, and owner Drawings!
- **EXCLUDED from Cash Flow Projections:** Non-cash items like **Depreciation**, **Bad Debts**, and **Provisions** NEVER appear because no cash enters or leaves the bank!

<FormulaCard
  title="Cash Flow Projection & Production Budget Formulae"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="One month's Closing Cash automatically opens the next month — and Production Budgets cover Forecast Sales plus target Closing Stock minus Opening Stock!"
>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Monthly Closing Cash Balance"
  short="Start with Opening Cash, add all Cash Inflows received that month, and subtract all Cash Outflows paid out that month."
>

$$
\text{Closing Cash} = \text{Opening Cash} + \text{Total Cash Inflows} - \text{Total Cash Outflows}
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Required Production (Units)"
  short="You must produce enough units to meet Forecast Sales PLUS leave your desired Closing Stock on the shelf, minus any Opening Stock already on hand!"
>

$$
\text{Production (Units)} = \text{Forecast Sales} + \text{Desired Closing Stock} - \text{Opening Stock}
$$

</FormulaHint>

</FormulaCard>

### What Should an Entrepreneur Do If a Cash Deficit Is Forecast? (Paper 2 Theory!)
If Month 2 or Month 3 shows a **negative closing cash balance** (in brackets, e.g. `(J$15,000)`), the entrepreneur can act *before* a crisis hits:
1. **Arrange a short-term Bank Overdraft or small working-capital loan** ahead of time;
2. **Negotiate longer credit terms with suppliers** (e.g. pay in 30–60 days instead of cash on delivery);
3. **Speed up customer cash collections** (encourage cash sales, offer small early-settlement discounts, chase overdue debtors);
4. **Delay non-urgent Fixed Asset purchases** (or lease equipment instead of buying outright for cash); and
5. **Trim non-essential overhead costs and reduce owner Drawings** until cash inflows strengthen!

*(After each month ends, comparing **Projected Cash Flow vs Actual Cash Flow** lets the entrepreneur spot overspending immediately and tighten cost control!)*

## 3. Worked Example 2: 3-Month Sales Budget & Production Budget

*(Directly tested on May/June 2025 Paper 032 Q5 — preparing a 3-month production budget from forecast sales and inventory policy!)*

<WorkedExample
  title="YardCraft Bags — 3-Month Sales Budget & Production Budget (Jan–Mar 2026)"
  marks={10}
  problem={
    <>
      <strong>YardCraft Bags</strong> sells handmade tote bags at <strong>J$2,500 per bag</strong> (direct production cost = <strong>J$1,200 per bag</strong>).
      <br />
      • Forecast Sales (units): Jan <strong>100</strong>; Feb <strong>150</strong>; Mar <strong>200</strong>; Apr <strong>250</strong>.
      <br />
      • Inventory Policy: Maintain Closing Stock equal to <strong>20% of the following month’s forecast sales</strong>.
      <br />
      • Opening Stock on 1 January 2026: <strong>20 bags</strong>.
      <br />
      Prepare: (a) the <strong>3-Month Sales Budget</strong>, (b) the <strong>3-Month Production Budget (Units)</strong>, and (c) Total Budgeted Production Cost for the quarter.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Notice TWO golden rules in the Quarter Total column of a Production Budget: (1) Opening Stock is ONLY January's Opening Stock (20 bags — NEVER add Jan + Feb + Mar!); (2) Closing Stock is ONLY March's Closing Stock (50 bags)!"
>

### (a) Sales Budget (January – March 2026)

| Particulars | January | February | March | Quarter Total |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Forecast Sales *(units)* | 100 | 150 | 200 | **450** |
| × Unit Selling Price *(J\$)* | × 2,500 | × 2,500 | × 2,500 | × 2,500 |
| **Budgeted Sales Revenue (J\$)** | **250,000** | **375,000** | **500,000** | **1,125,000** |

### (b) Production Budget (Units — January – March 2026)

| Particulars | January | February | March | Quarter Total |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Forecast Sales *(units)* | 100 | 150 | 200 | 450 |
| **Add:** Desired Closing Stock *(20% of next month's sales)* | 30 | 40 | 50 | 50 |
| *Total Units Needed* | *130* | *190* | *250* | *500* |
| **Less:** Opening Stock *(previous month's closing!)* | (20) | (30) | (40) | (20) |
| **Required Production (Units)** | **110** | **160** | **210** | **480** |

### (c) Budgeted Production Cost (Quarter)
- $480\text{ bags} \times 1{,}200 =$ **J\$576,000** *(check horizontally: $110 + 160 + 210 = 480$ ✓!)*

</WorkedExample>

## 4. Preparing a Simple Business Plan (Small Enterprise Blueprint!)

Whether starting a **poultry/chicken-rearing farm**, a **cash-crop vegetable farm** (e.g. Scotch bonnet peppers, callaloo), a **craft studio**, or a food stand, banks and grant agencies (such as JBDC or DBJ in Jamaica) require a structured **Business Plan** before lending money.

### Five Essential Sections of a Simple Business Plan

| Section | What Goes Inside (Exam Points!) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Executive Summary** *(Tested May/June 2025 Paper 032 Q1!)* | A crisp 1-page overview written **last** but placed **first**: business name, legal form, mission & core objectives, product/service offered, startup funding requested, and projected sales/profit highlights. |
| **2. Business Background & Management** | Ownership structure (Sole Trader, Partnership, Ltd), physical location, founder's skills & experience, key staff roles, and required licences/registrations (e.g. TRN, Food Handler's Permit, Companies Office registration). |
| **3. Marketing Plan** | Target customers, local market demand, analysis of competitors, pricing strategy (e.g. cost-plus mark-up), promotion/advertising (social media, flyers, samples), and monthly sales targets (**Sales Budget**). |
| **4. Operations / Production Plan** | Step-by-step production or service delivery workflow — land/premises, equipment & tools needed, reliable raw-material suppliers, production targets (**Production Budget**), and quality control. |
| **5. Financial Plan** | Startup capital requirements (owner's equity vs loan/grant), **3–6 Month Cash Flow Projection**, projected **Trading and Profit & Loss Account**, projected **Balance Sheet**, and key ratios (e.g. Return on Investment — ROI!). |

### Three Key Purposes of a Business Plan
1. **Attracts Financing:** Convinces commercial banks, credit unions, and investors that the venture is viable and can repay loans.
2. **Guides Daily Operations:** Gives the entrepreneur clear monthly sales, production, and cash targets to monitor actual performance against.
3. **Identifies Risks Early:** Forces the founder to calculate real costs, competition, and cash shortfalls *on paper* before risking real savings!
