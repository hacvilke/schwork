:::info Essential Understanding
Businesses offer two very different kinds of price reductions — and mixing them up causes instant ledger errors! A **Trade Discount** is a bulk or wholesale markdown subtracted **right on the invoice** and **NEVER entered into any ledger account**. A **Cash Discount** is a reward for paying a credit bill **early**, and it **IS recorded** inside the **Cash Book** and **Income Statement** as either **Discount Allowed** (expense) or **Discount Received** (income).
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | **Trade Discount** (bulk/trade deduction, never in ledger) vs. **Cash Discount** (prompt-payment reward, recorded in Cash Book & Income Statement) |
| **Exam Focus** | Applying Trade Discount **FIRST**, subtracting any returns, and ONLY THEN calculating Cash Discount on the net balance paid |
| **Learning Outcome** | Contrast Trade and Cash Discounts accurately, compute multi-step discounted settlements, and classify Discount Allowed vs. Discount Received |

## 1. Side-by-Side Comparison & Exact Calculation Sequence

| Feature | Trade Discount | Cash Discount |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Why Is It Given?** | Encourage **bulk orders** or allow retailers a profit margin off catalog prices | Encourage credit customers to **pay promptly** (improves seller’s cash flow & cuts bad debts) |
| **When Is It Deducted?** | **Immediately** on the Invoice (and Credit Note if goods are returned) | **Later**, ONLY if the buyer pays within the agreed credit discount window (e.g. `2/10, Net 30`) |
| **Shown in Ledger Accounts?** | **NO — NEVER** recorded in any ledger or Cash Book column | **YES** — entered in the **Discount columns of the 3-Column Cash Book** and transferred to the Income Statement |
| **Effect on Seller’s Profit** | Reduces Net Sales indirectly (because sales are recorded at net price) | Recorded as **Discount Allowed** → **Operating Expense** (reduces Net Profit) |
| **Effect on Buyer’s Profit** | Reduces Cost of Purchases indirectly (purchases entered at net price) | Recorded as **Discount Received** → **Other Income** (increases Net Profit) |

<FormulaCard
  title="Exact Order of Discount Calculations (Trade First, Cash Second!)"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Never add Trade % + Cash % together! Always deduct Trade Discount first, then apply Cash Discount to the remaining balance."
>

$$
\text{Net Invoice Price} = \text{List Price} - (\text{Trade Discount \%} \times \text{List Price})
$$

$$
\text{Balance Owing After Returns} = \text{Net Invoice Price} - \text{Net Credit Notes}
$$

$$
\text{Cash Discount} = \text{Cash Discount \%} \times \text{Balance Owing}
$$

$$
\text{Cash Paid} = \text{Balance Owing} - \text{Cash Discount}
$$

</FormulaCard>

:::warning Never Add the Two Percentages Together!
If List Price = **$1,000**, Trade Discount = **20%**, and Cash Discount = **5%**, the total discount is **NOT** 25% ($250)!
- Step 1: Trade Discount = `20% × $1,000 = $200` → Net Invoice = **$800**.
- Step 2: Cash Discount = `5% × $800 = $40` → Cash Paid = **$760** (total saving = **$240**, NOT $250!).
:::

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Calculating Multi-Step Trade Discount, Returns, Cash Discount & Ledger Entries"
  marks={10}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> On <strong>4 May 2026</strong>, <strong>Pacific Wholesalers</strong>
        sold goods with a catalog list price of <strong>$4,000</strong> on credit to
        <strong> Vista Hardware</strong>, offering a <strong>15% Trade Discount</strong> and
        payment terms of <strong>5/10, Net 30</strong>.
      </p>
      <p>
        On <strong>7 May 2026</strong>, Vista Hardware returned damaged goods with a catalog list
        price of <strong>$400</strong>. On <strong>12 May 2026</strong>, Vista Hardware settled
        its remaining account in full by cheque.
      </p>
      <ol>
        <li>Calculate the <strong>Net Invoice Amount</strong> recorded on 4 May. <em>[2 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Calculate the <strong>Net Credit Note Amount</strong> recorded on 7 May. <em>[2 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Verify whether Vista qualifies for the 5% Cash Discount on 12 May, then calculate the <strong>Cash Discount</strong> and the <strong>Cheque Amount paid</strong>. <em>[4 marks]</em></li>
        <li>State how the Cash Discount is classified in the Income Statement of (a) <strong>Pacific Wholesalers</strong> and (b) <strong>Vista Hardware</strong>. <em>[2 marks]</em></li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

**1. Net Invoice Amount on 4 May (2 marks):**
- Catalog List Price = $4,000
- Less: 15% Trade Discount (`15% × $4,000`) = **$600** *(1 mark)*
- **Net Invoice Amount** = `$4,000 − $600` = **$3,400** *(1 mark)*

**2. Net Credit Note Amount on 7 May (2 marks):**
- List price of returned goods = $400
- Less: 15% Trade Discount (`15% × $400`) = **$60** *(1 mark)*
- **Net Credit Note Amount** = `$400 − $60` = **$340** *(1 mark)*

**3. Qualification, Cash Discount & Cheque Paid on 12 May (4 marks):**
- Days elapsed from 4 May to 12 May = **8 days** (within the 10-day window!) → **YES, Vista qualifies for the 5% Cash Discount** *(1 mark)*.
- Remaining Net Balance Owing = `$3,400 − $340` = **$3,060** *(1 mark)*.
- **Cash Discount** = `5% × $3,060` = **$153** *(1 mark)*.
- **Cheque Amount Paid** = `$3,060 − $153` = **$2,907** *(1 mark)*.

**4. Income Statement Classification of the $153 Cash Discount (2 marks):**
- **(a) Pacific Wholesalers (Seller):** **Discount Allowed ($153)** — shown as an **Operating Expense** *(1 mark)*.
- **(b) Vista Hardware (Buyer):** **Discount Received ($153)** — shown as **Other Income** *(added to Gross Profit)* *(1 mark)*.

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Trade Discount** = Bulk reduction → deducted **FIRST** on Invoice & Credit Note → **NEVER** in ledger.
- **Cash Discount** = Prompt-payment reward → deducted **SECOND** on remaining net balance → **YES** in 3-Column Cash Book (**Discount Allowed** = Dr / Expense; **Discount Received** = Cr / Income).
:::
