:::info Essential Understanding
In real offices — and in structured accounting exams — nobody hands you neat bullet lists saying *"Bought $500 goods on credit from Sam."* Instead, you are handed a stack of **invoices, credit notes, receipts, cheque stubs, and bank slips**! The secret skill of every great bookkeeper is **reading any source document from YOUR business’s perspective** and turning it into one crystal-clear transaction sentence.
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | The **5-Step Document Decoding Method**: spotting **WHO WE ARE** (Issuer vs. Recipient), **Document Type**, **Cash vs. Credit**, **Goods vs. Asset**, and **Net Amount** |
| **Exam Focus** | Converting raw source documents (especially mixed invoices & credit notes) into exact transaction narratives + identifying the right Day Book |
| **Learning Outcome** | Decode any business source document rapidly and translate it into an unambiguous accounting transaction description |

## 1. The Perspective Mirror Rule (Read This Twice!)

One single sheet of paper tells **two opposite stories** depending on whose desk it sits on. Always look first at **whose books you are keeping**!

| Document on Your Desk | If YOUR Business Is the **ISSUER** (Top Letterhead) | If YOUR Business Is the **RECIPIENT** ("To:" / "Received From") |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Invoice** (for resale goods) | *"We **sold** goods on credit to [Customer] for $[Net]."* → **Sales Journal** | *"We **bought** goods on credit from [Supplier] for $[Net]."* → **Purchases Journal** |
| **Invoice** (for a computer/van) | *"We **sold** a non-current asset on credit..."* → **General Journal** | *"We **bought** [Equipment/Van] on credit from [Vendor]..."* → **General Journal** |
| **Credit Note** | *"[Customer] **returned** goods to us worth $[Net] (or overcharge reduced)."* → **Returns Inwards Journal** | *"We **returned** goods worth $[Net] to [Supplier]."* → **Returns Outwards Journal** |
| **Official Receipt** | *"We **received** $[Amount] cash/cheque from [Payer]."* → **Cash Book (Dr)** | *"We **paid** $[Amount] cash/cheque to [Issuer]."* → **Cash Book (Cr)** |
| **Cheque Counterfoil (Stub)** | *(Always kept by the Payer!)* *"We **paid** $[Amount] by cheque to [Payee] for [Purpose]."* → **Cash Book (Cr · Bank)** | *(N/A — stub stays inside the payer’s chequebook)* |
| **Bank Deposit (Pay-In) Slip** | *"We **deposited** $[Amount] cash/cheques into our business bank account."* → **Cash Book (Dr · Bank)** | *(Stamped by Bank for depositor)* |

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Translating Five Real Source Documents for 'Oasis Bakery'"
  marks={10}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> You are the bookkeeper for <strong>Oasis Bakery</strong>.
        Five source documents arrive on your desk during April 2026:
      </p>
      <ol>
        <li><strong>Invoice #772</strong> (April 3): Letterhead <em>Island Flour Mills</em>; To: <em>Oasis Bakery</em>; 30 sacks baking flour @ $25 = $750 less 10% trade discount = <strong>$675</strong>; Terms: Net 30.</li>
        <li><strong>Invoice #109</strong> (April 5): Letterhead <em>Oasis Bakery</em>; To: <em>Seaview Hotel</em>; 100 assorted pastries @ $4 = <strong>$400</strong>; Terms: Net 14.</li>
        <li><strong>Credit Note #CN-15</strong> (April 6): Letterhead <em>Oasis Bakery</em>; To: <em>Seaview Hotel</em>; 10 stale pastries returned = <strong>$40</strong>.</li>
        <li><strong>Cheque Counterfoil #4508</strong> (April 9): Paid to <em>City Power Co.</em>; For: March Electricity Bill; Amount: <strong>$320</strong>.</li>
        <li><strong>Invoice #901</strong> (April 12): Letterhead <em>BakeEquip Ltd.</em>; To: <em>Oasis Bakery</em>; 1 Industrial Commercial Oven = <strong>$4,500</strong>; Terms: 60 days credit.</li>
      </ol>
      <p>
        For each document, write a <strong>clear 1-sentence transaction description</strong> from
        Oasis Bakery’s viewpoint AND state the exact <strong>Book of Original Entry</strong>.
        <em>[2 marks each = 10 marks]</em>
      </p>
    </>
  }
>

1. **Invoice #772 (April 3) — (2 marks):**
   - *Description:* Oasis Bakery **purchased baking flour (goods for resale) on credit** from Island Flour Mills for **$675 net** *(after 10% trade discount)* *(1 mark)*.
   - *Book of Original Entry:* **Purchases Journal** *(1 mark)*.

2. **Invoice #109 (April 5) — (2 marks):**
   - *Description:* Oasis Bakery **sold assorted pastries on credit** to Seaview Hotel for **$400** *(1 mark)*.
   - *Book of Original Entry:* **Sales Journal** *(1 mark)*.

3. **Credit Note #CN-15 (April 6) — (2 marks):**
   - *Description:* Seaview Hotel **returned stale pastries** worth **$40** to Oasis Bakery *(1 mark)*.
   - *Book of Original Entry:* **Returns Inwards Journal** *(1 mark)*.

4. **Cheque Counterfoil #4508 (April 9) — (2 marks):**
   - *Description:* Oasis Bakery **paid $320 by cheque** to City Power Co. for the March electricity bill *(1 mark)*.
   - *Book of Original Entry:* **Cash Book** *(Credit side — Bank column)* *(1 mark)*.

5. **Invoice #901 (April 12) — (2 marks):**
   - *Description:* Oasis Bakery **purchased an industrial commercial oven (Non-Current Asset) on credit** from BakeEquip Ltd. for **$4,500** *(1 mark)*.
   - *Book of Original Entry:* **General Journal** *(1 mark — because an oven is long-term equipment, NOT resale flour/pastries!)*.

</WorkedExample>

## 5. True or False Check

<TrueFalseQuiz
  title="True or False: Translating Source Documents"
  items={[
    {
      statement: 'If your business’s name appears at the top letterhead of a Credit Note, it means your business returned goods to a supplier.',
      answer: false,
      explanation: 'Credit Notes are issued by the SELLER — so if your name is on the letterhead, a CUSTOMER returned goods to YOU (Returns Inwards).',
    },
    {
      statement: 'A Cheque Counterfoil (stub) in your business chequebook represents a bank payment recorded on the Credit side of the Cash Book.',
      answer: true,
      explanation: 'Whenever you write a cheque to pay a bill or supplier, the counterfoil is your source document for a Credit entry in the Bank column of the Cash Book.',
    },
    {
      statement: 'An invoice for a new delivery truck bought on credit by a clothing store is translated as a credit purchase of a Non-Current Asset.',
      answer: true,
      explanation: 'A clothing store trades in clothes, not trucks — so the delivery truck is a Non-Current Asset entered in the General Journal.',
    },
    {
      statement: 'When translating an invoice showing "Terms: 2/10, Net 30", you immediately deduct the 2% cash discount before writing the invoice amount.',
      answer: false,
      explanation: 'Never deduct a cash discount on the invoice date! Cash discount is earned ONLY later if the customer actually pays within the 10-day window.',
    },
    {
      statement: 'An Official Receipt issued BY your supplier TO your business proves that your business paid cash or cheque to that supplier.',
      answer: true,
      explanation: 'Receiving an Official Receipt means you paid money out, recorded on the Credit side of your Cash Book.',
    },
  ]}

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- Always ask **3 rapid questions** for every source document:
  1. **Whose name is on the letterhead?** *(Issuer = Seller/Payee; "To:" = Buyer/Payer)*
  2. **Cash or Credit — and Stock or Fixed Asset?**
  3. **Did I take the Net Total AFTER Trade Discount (and NOT subtract an unearned Cash Discount)?**
:::
