## 1. The Strict Rules for the Four Credit Day Books

Before writing a single line into a Day Book, memorise the **two strict bouncer rules** that decide what is allowed inside:

1. **ONLY CREDIT transactions are allowed!** If a customer pays cash, cheque, or debit card on the spot, it goes straight to the **Cash Book** — NEVER into the Sales Journal or Purchases Journal!
2. **ONLY GOODS FOR RESALE (Stock / Inventory) are allowed!** If a clothing store buys shirts on credit to sell → **Purchases Journal**. If that same clothing store buys a delivery van, cash register, or office desk on credit → **General Journal** (because those are Fixed Assets, NOT stock for resale)!

### The Shared Five-Column Layout
Good news — all four Credit Day Books (**Sales Journal**, **Purchases Journal**, **Returns Inwards Journal**, and **Returns Outwards Journal**) use the **exact same clean five-column layout**:

> **Date** | **Details *(Customer or Supplier Name)*** | **Invoice / Note No.** | **Folio** | **Amount (J\$)**

*(What do the **Folio** codes mean? They act like page bookmarks telling you where the entry was posted: **`SL 1`** = Sales Ledger page 1, **`PL 2`** = Purchases Ledger page 2, **`GL 4`** = General Ledger page 4!)*

### Remember: List Price vs Net Invoice Amount!
When a transaction mentions a **Trade Discount** (e.g. *"Sold goods to D. Grant, list price J\$20,000 less 10% trade discount"*), always subtract the Trade Discount first and enter **ONLY the net figure (J\$18,000)** in the final Amount column (you may show the list price and trade discount in an inner details column if your exam paper has two amount columns).

## 2. How Day Books Complete Double Entry (Two-Step Posting!)

A Day Book is **NOT** a Ledger account — it is a chronological list. So how does Double Entry (`Dr = Cr`) get completed? Through a super-neat **two-step posting process**:

- **Step 1 (Daily / Throughout the Month):** Each individual customer or supplier listed in the Day Book is posted to their own **Personal Account** in the Sales Ledger or Purchases Ledger (so we always know *today* how much each person owes!).
- **Step 2 (At Month-End):** Add up the entire Day Book column and post that **ONE single grand total** to the **opposite side** of the matching **Nominal Account** in the General Ledger!

| Day Book | Step 1: Individual Names Posted Daily | Step 2: Month-End Total Posted to General Ledger |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sales Journal** | **DEBIT** each customer's account in the **Sales Ledger** *(they owe us more — Asset ↑)* | **CREDIT** the **Sales Account** *(Revenue ↑)* |
| **Purchases Journal** | **CREDIT** each supplier's account in the **Purchases Ledger** *(we owe them more — Liability ↑)* | **DEBIT** the **Purchases Account** *(Cost/Expense ↑)* |
| **Returns Inwards Journal** | **CREDIT** each customer's account in the **Sales Ledger** *(reduces what they owe us — Asset ↓)* | **DEBIT** the **Returns Inwards Account** *(reduces Sales)* |
| **Returns Outwards Journal** | **DEBIT** each supplier's account in the **Purchases Ledger** *(reduces what we owe them — Liability ↓)* | **CREDIT** the **Returns Outwards Account** *(reduces Purchases)* |

:::tip Quick Check on Double Entry!
Notice how every single row above balances automatically! For example, in the Sales Journal: if three customers are debited J\$24,000 + J\$18,500 + J\$12,500 in the Sales Ledger, and the Sales Account is credited with the total **J\$55,000** in the General Ledger, then **Total Debits (J\$55,000) = Total Credits (J\$55,000)**!
:::

## 4. The General Journal (Journal Proper)

Think of the **General Journal** *(also called **Journal Proper**)* as the "official instruction logbook" for every transaction that does **not** belong in Cash, Petty Cash, or the four Credit Stock Day Books.

### The Five Classic Uses of the General Journal
1. **Buying or selling Fixed Assets on credit** (e.g. buying office computers, machinery, or a delivery van on credit — *tested on May/June 2025 Paper 032 Q1!*).
2. **Opening Entries:** Recording all starting Assets, Liabilities, and opening Capital when opening a brand-new set of accounting books (where $\text{Opening Capital} = \text{Total Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities}$).
3. **Year-End Adjusting Entries:** Depreciation, Bad Debts written off, Provision for Doubtful Debts, Accruals, and Prepayments.
4. **Closing Entries:** Transferring Sales, Purchases, Expenses, and Drawings at year-end.
5. **Correction of Accounting Errors & Non-Cash Special Entries:** Fixing Suspense Account errors, owner taking **goods (stock)** for personal use (`Dr Drawings, Cr Purchases`), or writing off a contra between a debtor and creditor.

### Exact Presentation Format (Never Forget the Narration!)
Every General Journal entry has **three strict layout rules**:
1. Write the **Debit account(s) first**, flush left, with the amount in the **Dr** column.
2. Write the **Credit account(s) underneath**, slightly **indented to the right**, with the amount in the **Cr** column.
3. Directly below the credit line, write a brief **Narration** in brackets *(starting with "Being…")* explaining the reason and source document for the entry — **examiners award dedicated marks for clear narrations!**

### Two Practical General Journal Examples

**Example 1 — Bought Office Equipment on credit from TechJa Ltd for J\$95,000 on May 25, AND owner took J\$6,000 of stock home for personal use on May 29:**

| Date | Details | Folio | Dr (J\$) | Cr (J\$) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| May 25 | Office Equipment | GL 8 | 95,000 | |
| | &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;TechJa Ltd | GL 15 | | 95,000 |
| | *(Being purchase of office equipment on credit from TechJa Ltd, Inv. #904)* | | | |
| May 29 | Drawings | GL 19 | 6,000 | |
| | &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Purchases | GL 2 | | 6,000 |
| | *(Being stock withdrawn by owner at cost for personal use)* | | | |

**Example 2 — Opening Journal Entry on 1 June 2026:** *Assets: Delivery Van J\$180,000, Stock J\$45,000, Bank J\$25,000; Liability: Creditor J. Gayle J\$30,000. Find Opening Capital!*

| Date | Details | Folio | Dr (J\$) | Cr (J\$) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| June 1 | Delivery Van | GL 5 | 180,000 | |
| | Stock | GL 6 | 45,000 | |
