## 1. The Three-Column Cash Book (Why It Is Special!)

The **Cash Book** is unique: it is **both a Book of Original Entry AND a Ledger account** (combining the Cash Account and the Bank Account side by side so you don't need separate Cash and Bank T-accounts!).

- **Left Side — Debit (Dr) = Money COMING IN (Receipts):**
  - `Discount Allowed` *(memorandum column — never balanced off!)*
  - `Cash (J\$)` *(notes & coins received)*
  - `Bank (J\$)` *(cheques banked, card receipts, online transfers received)*
- **Right Side — Credit (Cr) = Money GOING OUT (Payments):**
  - `Discount Received` *(memorandum column — never balanced off!)*
  - `Cash (J\$)` *(notes & coins paid out)*
  - `Bank (J\$)` *(cheques written, standing orders, bank transfers paid)*

---

## 2. Contra Entries (`C`) — Moving Your Own Money!

A **Contra Entry** happens when **both the Debit AND the Credit** of a transaction occur **inside the Cash Book itself** — you are simply shifting the business's own money between the **Cash Till** and the **Bank Account**. Mark **`C`** in the Folio column on both sides!

| Contra Transaction | Debit Side (Money In) | Credit Side (Money Out) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Paid cash from till into the bank** *(banking cash)* | **Dr Bank** — write `"Cash"` in Details, **`C`** in Folio | **Cr Cash** — write `"Bank"` in Details, **`C`** in Folio |
| **Withdrew cash from bank for office use** | **Dr Cash** — write `"Bank"` in Details, **`C`** in Folio | **Cr Bank** — write `"Cash"` in Details, **`C`** in Folio |

:::warning Classic Exam Pitfall!
If the owner withdraws cash from the bank for **personal use**, that is **NOT** a contra entry — it is **Drawings** (`Cr Bank`, `Dr Drawings` in the General Ledger)!
Also remember: if a business buys **Office Equipment (J\$36,000)** and pays **Office Equipment Repairs (J\$4,000)** on the same day, record them as **two separate lines** — do NOT lump capital expenditure and revenue repairs together!
:::

## 4. The Petty Cash Book & The Imprest System

Instead of writing cheques or cluttering the main Cash Book for J\$500 taxi fares or J\$300 envelopes, a junior clerk keeps a small locked cash box controlled by the **Imprest System**:

1. **Fixed Float (Imprest Amount):** At the start of every month, the petty cashier starts with the **exact same fixed sum** (say, **J\$6,000**).
2. **Petty Cash Vouchers:** Every tiny payment is recorded in the **Total Paid** column AND sorted into an **Analysis Column** (e.g. *Stationery*, *Travelling*, *Cleaning*).
3. **Reimbursement (Restoring the Imprest):** At month-end, the main cashier refunds the **exact amount spent**, bringing the float right back to **J\$6,000**!
