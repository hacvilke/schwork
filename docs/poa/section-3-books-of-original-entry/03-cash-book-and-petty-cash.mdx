## 1. The Cash Book's Unique Dual Role

Every other Day Book (Sales Journal, Purchases Journal, etc.) is *only* a Book of Original Entry. The **Cash Book** is special: it is **BOTH a Book of Original Entry AND a Ledger Account** at the same time!

Because Cash and Bank transactions happen every single day, accountants put the **Cash Account** and the **Bank Account** side by side inside one book so they never have to draw separate Cash and Bank T-accounts in the General Ledger.

### Evolution of the Cash Book (Know All Three Types!)
- **Two-Column Cash Book:** Has **Cash** and **Bank** columns on each side.
- **Three-Column Cash Book** *(most commonly tested on exams!)*: Adds **Discount Allowed** on the Debit side and **Discount Received** on the Credit side right beside Cash and Bank!

### Left Side vs Right Side Rules
- **Left Side — Debit (Dr) = Money COMING IN (Receipts):**
  - `Discount Allowed` *(memorandum column — cash discounts we granted to debtors who paid early)*
  - `Cash (J\$)` *(physical notes & coins entering the till)*
  - `Bank (J\$)` *(cheques banked, debit/credit card receipts, online transfers entering our bank account)*
- **Right Side — Credit (Cr) = Money GOING OUT (Payments):**
  - `Discount Received` *(memorandum column — cash discounts suppliers granted to us for paying early)*
  - `Cash (J\$)` *(physical notes & coins paid out of the till)*
  - `Bank (J\$)` *(cheques written, bank transfers paid, standing orders, direct debits)*

:::tip Golden Rule for the Discount Columns!
The **Discount Allowed** and **Discount Received** columns are **NEVER balanced off** against each other — they are two completely different accounts! At month-end:
- Simply **total** the Debit Discount column → post **Dr Discount Allowed Account** (Expense) in the General Ledger.
- Simply **total** the Credit Discount column → post **Cr Discount Received Account** (Other Income) in the General Ledger.
*(The individual customer/supplier accounts were already credited/debited with the discount when each line was posted!)*
:::

## 2. Contra Entries (`C`) — Moving Your Own Money!

A **Contra Entry** happens when **both halves (Debit and Credit)** of a transaction occur **inside the Cash Book itself** — you are simply transferring the business's own money between the **Cash Till** and the **Bank Account**. Always write **`C`** in the Folio column on both sides so nobody posts it again to the General Ledger!

| Contra Transaction | Debit Side (Money In) | Credit Side (Money Out) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Paid cash from till into the bank** *(banking cash)* | **Dr Bank** — write `"Cash"` in Details, **`C`** in Folio | **Cr Cash** — write `"Bank"` in Details, **`C`** in Folio |
| **Withdrew cash from bank for office use** | **Dr Cash** — write `"Bank"` in Details, **`C`** in Folio | **Cr Bank** — write `"Cash"` in Details, **`C`** in Folio |

### Watch Out for Three Real Exam Traps (From the May/June 2025 Subject Report!)
1. **Owner's Personal Withdrawal is NOT a Contra Entry:** If the owner withdraws cash from the bank for *personal/private use*, record `Cr Bank` (Details: `Drawings`) and `Dr Drawings` in the General Ledger — **never** `Dr Cash`!
2. **"Keep a Cash Float and Bank the Rest" Narrative Contra** *(tested directly on May/June 2025 Paper 02 Q1!)*: Suppose on 30 June the question says: *"Deposited all remaining cash into the bank except for a J\$5,000 cash float."*
   - **Step 1:** Total all Debit Cash receipts so far, and subtract all Credit Cash payments so far to see how much physical cash is sitting in the till (say, J\$23,000).
   - **Step 2:** Subtract the float kept back ($23{,}000 - 5{,}000 =$ **J\$18,000**).
   - **Step 3:** Record a **Contra Entry (`C`)** for **J\$18,000**: `Cr Cash (Details: Bank)` and `Dr Bank (Details: Cash)` — leaving exactly **J\$5,000** as `Balance c/d` in Cash!
3. **Separate Capital vs Revenue Expenditure on the Same Cheque** *(also highlighted on May/June 2025 Q1!)*: If a business pays J\$36,000 for **new Office Equipment** and J\$4,000 for **Equipment Repairs**, write them on **TWO separate lines** (`Office Equipment 36,000` and `Equipment Repairs 4,000`) — NEVER lump them into one J\$40,000 line!

## 3. Interpreting Closing Balances (`Cash` vs `Bank`)

After balancing the **Cash** and **Bank** columns separately using the `Balance c/d` → `Balance b/d` method:

| Closing `Balance b/d` | What It Means | Where It Goes on the Balance Sheet |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cash — Debit `Balance b/d`** | Physical cash remaining in the till/safe at month-end. *(Cash can **NEVER** have a Credit balance — you cannot hand out more physical notes than you hold!)* | **Current Asset** (*Cash in Hand*) |
| **Bank — Debit `Balance b/d`** | Positive money sitting safely in the business's bank account (deposits > cheques written). | **Current Asset** (*Cash at Bank*) |
| **Bank — Credit `Balance b/d`** | **Bank Overdraft!** Cheques & payments out exceeded deposits in — the business owes the bank that exact amount! | **Current Liability** (*Bank Overdraft*) |

## 5. The Analytical Petty Cash Book & The Imprest System

Writing a business cheque or cluttering the main Cash Book every time someone buys J\$400 of envelopes or pays J\$600 for a messenger's taxi fare would waste huge amounts of time. Instead, businesses delegate small daily payments to a petty cashier using the **Analytical Petty Cash Book** under the **Imprest System**:

### How the Imprest System Works (3 Simple Steps)
1. **Set a Fixed Float (Imprest Amount):** Management agrees on a fixed starting amount for every period (e.g. **J\$6,000** per month).
2. **Record & Categorise Every Payment:** Every payment requires a signed **Petty Cash Voucher** (with receipt attached). The clerk enters the amount twice — once in **Total Paid**, and once in the matching **Analysis Column** (e.g. *Stationery*, *Travelling*, *Cleaning*, or *Ledger Accounts* if paying a small creditor).
3. **Reimburse Exact Spending:** At period-end, the main cashier inspects all vouchers and refunds the **EXACT sum spent** — bringing the petty cash box right back to **J\$6,000** on the 1st of the next month!

### Why Businesses Love the Imprest System (Exam Advantages!)
- **Strong Fraud Control:** At any moment, `Cash left in the tin + Total signed vouchers` MUST equal the fixed Imprest Float (J\$6,000) — any missing J\$100 shows up immediately!
- **Limits Exposure:** Because only a small fixed float is kept in the office tin, maximum risk of theft is strictly capped.
- **Keeps the Main Cash Book & General Ledger Tidy:** Instead of 40 tiny expense entries in the General Ledger, only **one monthly total per analysis column** is debited to *Stationery*, *Travelling*, *Cleaning*, etc.!
