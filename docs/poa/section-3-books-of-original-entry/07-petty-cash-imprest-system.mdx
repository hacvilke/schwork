:::info Essential Understanding
Writing a bank cheque — or cluttering the main Cash Book — every time someone buys $4 of postage stamps, $7 of bus fare, or $12 of office cleaning spray would waste hours! Instead, businesses keep a locked **Petty Cash Box** managed by a junior cashier using the **Imprest System**: start each period with a **fixed cash float** (say $200), pay small expenses against signed vouchers, and at period-end top up **the exact amount spent** so the float returns right back to $200!
:::

## 1. How the Imprest System Works

Under the **Imprest System**, the Chief Cashier gives the Petty Cashier a **fixed starting float** (called the **Imprest Amount**, e.g. **$200**).

<FormulaCard
  title="The Imprest System Check & Restoration Formulas"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="At any moment, cash left in the tin plus signed vouchers equals the fixed float — and reimbursement always equals exact total spent."
>

$$
\text{Imprest Float} = \text{Cash Remaining in Box (Balance c/d)} + \text{Total Vouchers Spent}
$$

$$
\text{Reimbursement (Top-Up)} = \text{Total Payments Made During Period} = \text{Imprest Float} - \text{Balance c/d}
$$

</FormulaCard>

### Four Strong Internal Control Advantages of the Imprest System
1. **Instant Theft/Error Check:** A supervisor can open the petty cash box on any random afternoon — `Cash on hand + Signed Vouchers` MUST equal the exact Imprest Float!
2. **Caps Maximum Cash Risk:** Because the box only ever holds a modest fixed float (e.g., $150–$300), maximum possible cash loss is strictly limited.
3. **Keeps the Main Cash Book Clean:** Hundreds of tiny receipts stay inside the Petty Cash Book; only **one reimbursement entry** per month appears in the main Cash Book.
4. **Fast General Ledger Posting:** Analysis columns sort expenses automatically (Postage, Travel, Stationery, etc.) so **one monthly total per column** posts straight to the Debit of each expense account!

:::note What Goes in the "Ledger" Analysis Column?
Sometimes a tiny payment settles a **credit supplier’s personal account** (e.g. paying $18 cash to clear a small stationery creditor, *J. Marks*) rather than a regular cash expense. That payment goes in the rightmost **Ledger Accounts** column and is posted directly to the Debit of *J. Marks* in the Purchases Ledger!
:::

| | | **Total Expenses** | | **130** | **25** | **44** | **26** | **35** |

**Verification & General Ledger Postings:**
1. **Cross-Cast Check:** `$25 (Postage) + $44 (Stationery) + $26 (Travelling) + $35 (Cleaning) = $130` = Total Payments (**$130**) ✓!
2. **Imprest Restoration Check:** Cash left (`$20`) + Reimbursement (`$130`) = **$150 Fixed Float** ✓!
3. **General Ledger Postings (Mar 7):** Debit Postage A/c **$25**, Debit Stationery A/c **$44**, Debit Travelling A/c **$26**, Debit Cleaning A/c **$35** *(total Debits = $130, matching the $130 Credit in the Petty Cash Book!)*.

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Reimbursement = Total Amount SPENT** (NEVER equal to Balance c/d!).
- Always **cross-cast** (`Sum of Analysis Columns = Total Payments`) before balancing!
- Post **column totals** to the **DEBIT** of each expense account in the General Ledger, and **individual items** in the **Ledger** column to the Debit of each supplier in the Purchases Ledger.
:::
