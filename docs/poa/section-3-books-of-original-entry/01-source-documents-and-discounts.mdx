## 1. Why Do Businesses Use Books of Original Entry?

Imagine running a busy supermarket in Kingston that serves 500 customers and receives 20 supplier deliveries every day. If an accounts clerk tried to post every single receipt and invoice directly into the main Ledger, the Ledger would quickly become enormous, cluttered, and almost impossible to check!

Instead, businesses first record transactions straight from **Source Documents** into specialised **Books of Original Entry** *(also called **Day Books**, **Journals**, or **Subsidiary Books**)*. Think of them as labelled daily folders:

- **Keeps the General Ledger clean:** Instead of posting 300 separate credit sales into the Sales Account, you enter them daily in the Sales Journal and post **one single monthly total** to the Sales Account!
- **Allows division of labour:** One clerk can handle the Sales Journal, another the Purchases Journal, and another the Cash Book at the exact same time.
- **Creates a clear audit trail:** Every entry includes the date, customer/supplier name, invoice number, and folio reference — making errors and fraud much easier to spot.

### The Seven Books of Original Entry at a Glance

| Book of Original Entry | Transactions Recorded Inside | Source Document Used |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Sales Journal** *(Sales Day Book)* | **Credit sales** of goods held for resale (stock) | **Duplicate Sales Invoice** *(copy kept by seller)* |
| **2. Purchases Journal** *(Purchases Day Book)* | **Credit purchases** of goods for resale (stock) | **Original Purchases Invoice** *(received from supplier)* |
| **3. Returns Inwards Journal** *(Sales Returns Book)* | Credit goods **returned TO us by customers** | **Duplicate Credit Note** *(issued by us)* |
| **4. Returns Outwards Journal** *(Purchases Returns Book)* | Credit goods **returned BY us to suppliers** | **Original Credit Note received** *(or Debit Note sent)* |
| **5. Cash Book** | **ALL cash and bank** receipts & payments (notes, coins, cheques, cards, online transfers) | **Cash Receipts**, till tapes, **cheque counterfoils (stubs)**, bank deposit slips, e-banking alerts |
| **6. Petty Cash Book** | Small, everyday cash expenses (bus/taxi fare, tea, stamps, pens, cleaning items) | **Petty Cash Vouchers** |
| **7. General Journal** *(Journal Proper)* | All **non-routine / non-cash** items: buying/selling Fixed Assets on credit, opening entries, year-end adjustments, and error corrections | **Journal Vouchers**, contracts, memos, fixed-asset invoices |

## 2. Cash vs Credit Transactions & Forms of Payment

Every business purchase or sale falls into one of two categories based on **WHEN payment takes place**:

### A. Cash Transactions (Paid Immediately → Cash Book!)
Payment is settled **at the time of purchase or sale**. Notice that in accounting, "cash transaction" does **NOT** mean only paper notes and coins — it includes **every immediate payment method**:
1. **Physical Cash:** Notes and coins (recorded in the **Cash** column).
2. **Cheques:** Written bank instructions payable on presentation (recorded in the **Bank** column).
3. **Debit Cards:** Deduct funds immediately from the buyer's bank account via POS terminal (recorded in the **Bank** column).
4. **Electronic / Digital Payment Methods** *(tested in recent Jamaican exams!)*: Online bank transfers (e.g. NCB, Scotiabank, Sagicor online banking / RTGS / ACH), mobile wallets (e.g. **Lynk**, PayPal, Apple Pay), and standing orders/direct debits (recorded in the **Bank** column).

### B. Credit Transactions (Paid Later → Credit Day Books!)
Goods or services change hands **today**, while payment is agreed for a **future date** (e.g. *"Net 30 days"*).
- **Credit Sales** create a **Debtor (Account Receivable)** and go into the **Sales Journal**.
- **Credit Purchases** create a **Creditor (Account Payable)** and go into the **Purchases Journal**.
- **Credit Cards:** For the *buyer*, paying with a credit card creates a liability owed to the credit card company (paid later when the monthly card bill arrives). For the *seller*, credit card sales are deposited swiftly into the seller's bank account (minus a small processing fee).

## 3. Essential Source Documents (How to Read & Translate Them!)

Examiners frequently print a mock **Invoice**, **Credit Note**, or **Statement of Account** and ask you to either fill in missing numbers or write the exact transaction description (Syllabus Objectives 3.4 & 3.5 — tested on the May/June 2025 Paper 02 Q1!).

### A. Sales Invoice vs Purchases Invoice
It is actually the **EXACT SAME document** seen from two different viewpoints!
- **Seller's viewpoint:** Keeps the **duplicate copy** → it is a **Sales Invoice** (goes in the **Sales Journal**).
- **Buyer's viewpoint:** Receives the **original copy** → it is a **Purchases Invoice** (goes in the **Purchases Journal** — *unless* it is for a Fixed Asset, which goes in the **General Journal**!).
- **Key details shown on an Invoice:** Seller's name & address, Buyer's name & address, Invoice Number, Date, Description & Quantity of goods, Unit Price, Extended Total, **Trade Discount** (deducted right on the invoice!), **Net Invoice Total**, and **Payment / Cash Discount Terms** (e.g. *"2.5% 14 days, Net 30 days"*).

### B. Credit Note vs Debit Note
| Document | Who Sends It & Why? | Effect on Customer's Balance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Credit Note** | Sent by the **Seller** to the **Buyer** when goods are returned damaged/unwanted, or when the seller **overcharged** on an earlier invoice. *(Often printed in red!)* | **REDUCES** what the customer owes the seller. |
| **Debit Note** | Sent by the **Seller** to the **Buyer** when the seller **undercharged** on an invoice (or missed an item). *(Note: Buyers also sometimes send a Debit Note to notify a supplier that faulty goods are being sent back and request a Credit Note.)* | **INCREASES** what the customer owes the seller *(when issued by seller for undercharge)*. |

### C. Statement of Account *(Tested on May/June 2025 Paper 02!)*
A **Statement of Account** is a **monthly summary** sent by a seller to a credit customer showing:
- Opening balance owed at the start of the month (`Balance b/f`),
- Every **Invoice** issued during the month (**increases** the balance — Debit column),
- Every **Credit Note** issued and **Payment / Cheque / Discount** received during the month (**decreases** the balance — Credit column), and
- The final **Amount Due** at month-end!

*(Important: A Statement of Account is a **reminder and checking tool** — it is **NOT** a Book of Original Entry source document for new entries, because each individual invoice, credit note, and cheque was already recorded when it occurred!)*

### D. Receipts, Cheque Counterfoils & Petty Cash Vouchers
- **Receipt:** Official proof that cash or cheque payment was received.
- **Cheque Counterfoil (Stub):** The left-hand slip left in the chequebook after tearing out a cheque — records date, payee, reason, and J\$ amount paid.
- **Petty Cash Voucher:** A numbered slip signed by the employee receiving small cash and authorised by a supervisor, with the store receipt stapled to it.

## 4. Trade Discount vs Cash Discount (Big Exam Favourite!)

Mixing up **Trade Discount** and **Cash Discount** is one of the fastest ways to lose easy marks. Compare every detail below:

| Feature | **Trade Discount** | **Cash Discount** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Purpose** | Reward for **buying in bulk** or being a fellow trade buyer/retailer. | Reward for **paying a credit invoice promptly** within a set credit window (e.g. *"5% within 14 days"*). |
| **When calculated?** | Deducted **immediately on the invoice** from the catalogue/list price. | Deducted **later**, ONLY if the buyer actually settles the bill before the discount deadline! |
| **Entered in the Ledger?** | **NEVER!** Only the **net invoice total** (after trade discount) is recorded in the Day Book and Ledger. | **YES!** Recorded in the Discount columns of the **Three-Column Cash Book** and posted to the Ledger. |
| **Income Statement treatment** | Not shown separately (Sales and Purchases are already recorded net of trade discount). | **Discount Allowed** (given to our debtors) = **Operating Expense** in P&L.<br/>**Discount Received** (earned from our suppliers) = **Other Revenue** in P&L! |

:::warning Never Add Discount Percentages Together!
If an invoice carries a **10% Trade Discount** and a **5% Cash Discount**, the total discount is **NOT 15%**!
1. Always deduct **Trade Discount first** from the List Price to get the **Net Invoice Price**.
2. Then calculate **Cash Discount ONLY on the Net Invoice Price**!
:::

## 5. Past-Paper-Style Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Calculating Trade Discount, Cash Discount & Completing a Statement of Account"
  marks={6}
  problem={
    <>
      <strong>Part (a)</strong> Jamaica Office Depot sells goods with a catalogue list price of <strong>J$40,000</strong> on credit to{' '}
      <strong>R. Nelson</strong> on 4 May. A <strong>10% Trade Discount</strong> is granted on the invoice, plus terms of{' '}
      <strong>5% Cash Discount</strong> if paid within 14 days.
      <br />
      &nbsp;&nbsp;(i) What amount is entered in Jamaica Office Depot’s <strong>Sales Journal</strong>?
      <br />
      &nbsp;&nbsp;(ii) If R. Nelson returns goods with a net value of <strong>J$4,000</strong> on 8 May and pays the remaining balance on 12 May by cheque, calculate the <strong>Cash Discount</strong> and the <strong>exact cheque amount</strong>.
    </>
  }
  examTip="ALWAYS remember to subtract any Returns Inwards (Credit Note) BEFORE calculating a Cash Discount on settlement! A customer only earns Cash Discount on the net amount they actually still owe."
>

**(i) Amount entered in the Sales Journal (4 May):** *(2 marks)*
- Trade Discount = $10\% \times 40{,}000 =$ **J\$4,000** *(deducted on invoice only — NOT entered in any ledger account!)*
- Net Invoice Price = $40{,}000 - 4{,}000 =$ **J\$36,000** → entered in the **Sales Journal**.

**(ii) Cash Discount & Cheque Paid (12 May — within 14-day window!):** *(4 marks)*
- Amount owed after 8 May return = $36{,}000 - 4{,}000 =$ **J\$32,000**
- Cash Discount Allowed = $5\% \times 32{,}000 =$ **J\$1,600** *(entered in Cash Book Discount Allowed column)*
- Cheque Amount Paid = $32{,}000 - 1{,}600 =$ **J\$30,400** *(entered in Cash Book Bank column)*

*(Notice how cleanly all three steps flow: List Price J\$40,000 → less 10% Trade Discount = J\$36,000 → less Return J\$4,000 = J\$32,000 owed → less 5% Cash Discount J\$1,600 = **J\$30,400 cheque**!)*

</WorkedExample>
