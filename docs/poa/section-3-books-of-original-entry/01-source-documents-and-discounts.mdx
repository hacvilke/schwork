## 1. What Are Books of Original Entry? (Kid-Friendly Explanation)

Imagine running a busy supermarket in Kingston with 400 sales a day. If you tried to write every single receipt straight into the main Ledger, the Ledger would quickly become a giant, messy pile of paper!

Instead, businesses first sort similar transactions into **Books of Original Entry** *(also called **Day Books** or **Subsidiary Books**)* — like colour-coded folders where transactions are first written down straight from **Source Documents** (receipts, invoices, cheques, vouchers) before neat totals are posted to the Ledger.

| The 7 Books of Original Entry | What Goes Inside | Main Source Document |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Sales Journal (Sales Day Book)** | **Credit sales** of goods held for resale | **Sales Invoice** *(duplicate/copy kept by seller)* |
| **2. Purchases Journal (Purchases Day Book)** | **Credit purchases** of goods for resale | **Purchases Invoice** *(original invoice received from supplier)* |
| **3. Returns Inwards Journal** *(Sales Returns)* | Goods returned **by our credit customers** | **Credit Note issued** *(copy)* |
| **4. Returns Outwards Journal** *(Purchases Returns)* | Goods we returned **to our credit suppliers** | **Credit Note received** *(or Debit Note issued)* |
| **5. Cash Book** | **ALL cash and bank** receipts & payments (including cheques, cards, online transfers) | **Receipts**, cash register slips, **cheque counterfoils (stubs)**, bank deposit slips, electronic transfer alerts |
| **6. Petty Cash Book** | Small everyday cash expenses (bus/taxi fare, tea, stamps, pens, cleaning) | **Petty Cash Vouchers** |
| **7. General Journal (Journal Proper)** | Everything else — non-cash, unusual, or year-end items (buying/selling a Fixed Asset on credit, opening entries, depreciation, bad debts, correcting errors) | **Journal Vouchers**, memos, contracts, fixed-asset invoices |

## 2. Cash vs Credit Transactions & Modern Payment Methods

- **Cash Transaction:** Payment happens **immediately** when the goods or services change hands — whether paid in physical **cash**, by **cheque**, by **debit card**, or via instant **bank transfer / mobile wallet** (e.g. Lynk, NCB/Scotiabank online transfer, PayPal). Recorded straight in the **Cash Book**!
- **Credit Transaction:** Goods or services are supplied **now**, and payment is promised **later** (e.g. "Net 30 days"). Recorded first in the **Sales Journal** or **Purchases Journal** (or General Journal for a Fixed Asset on credit).
- **Credit Card note:** For the *buyer*, buying on a credit card creates a debt owed to the credit card issuer (paid later). For the *seller*, credit card sales are normally settled quickly into the seller's bank account (minus a small merchant fee).

:::tip Source Document Quick Tip: Credit Note vs Debit Note
- **Credit Note:** Sent by the **seller** to a customer to **reduce** the amount the customer owes (because goods were damaged, faulty, returned, or overcharged).
- **Debit Note:** Sent to **increase** an amount owed (for example, when an invoice undercharged a customer) — or sent by a buyer requesting a Credit Note when returning faulty goods.
:::

## 3. Trade Discount vs Cash Discount (Big Exam Favourite!)

Students constantly lose marks by mixing these two up — keep them crystal clear:

| Feature | **Trade Discount** | **Cash Discount** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Why is it given?** | Reward for **buying in bulk** or being a fellow trader/retailer. | Reward for **paying a credit invoice promptly** (e.g. "5% within 14 days"). |
| **When is it deducted?** | Deducted **immediately on the invoice** before calculating the invoice total. | Deducted **later**, ONLY when the bill is actually paid on time. |
| **Recorded in the Ledger?** | **NEVER!** Only the **net invoice price** is recorded in the books. | **YES!** Entered in the Discount columns of the **Three-Column Cash Book** and posted to the Ledger. |
| **Two types** | One type (just lowers the invoice price). | **Discount Allowed** (we give it to our debtors → *Expense*) and **Discount Received** (suppliers give it to us → *Other Income*). |

<WorkedExample
  title="Calculating Trade Discount vs Cash Discount on an Invoice"
  marks={4}
  problem={
    <>
      Jamaica Office Depot sells goods with a catalogue list price of <strong>J$40,000</strong> on credit to{' '}
      <strong>R. Nelson</strong>. A <strong>10% Trade Discount</strong> is granted on the invoice, plus terms of{' '}
      <strong>5% Cash Discount</strong> if paid within 14 days.
      <br />
      (a) What amount is entered in the <strong>Sales Journal</strong>?
      <br />
      (b) If R. Nelson pays within 10 days by cheque, how much is the <strong>Cash Discount</strong> and how much is written on the cheque?
    </>
  }
  examTip="ALWAYS calculate Trade Discount first on the list price, then calculate Cash Discount ONLY on the reduced net invoice figure — NEVER add 10% + 5% = 15%!"
>

**(a) Net Invoice Price entered in the Sales Journal:** *(2 marks)*
- Trade Discount = $10\% \times 40{,}000 =$ **J\$4,000** *(not recorded in the ledger)*
- Net Invoice Price = $40{,}000 - 4{,}000 =$ **J\$36,000** → entered in the Sales Journal.

**(b) Cash Discount & Cheque Paid (within 14 days):** *(2 marks)*
- Cash Discount Allowed = $5\% \times 36{,}000 =$ **J\$1,800** *(recorded in Cash Book Discount Allowed column)*
- Cheque Amount Paid = $36{,}000 - 1{,}800 =$ **J\$34,200** *(recorded in Cash Book Bank column)*

</WorkedExample>

    {

    {
      prompt: 'Cheque counterfoil (stub) showing J$25,000 paid to JPS for electricity',

    {
