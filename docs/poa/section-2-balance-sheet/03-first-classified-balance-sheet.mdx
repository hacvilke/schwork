## 1. What Is a Balance Sheet & What Does Every Subtotal Tell You?

A **Balance Sheet** (also called the **Statement of Financial Position**) is a formal financial snapshot taken on the **final day** of an accounting period (for example, 31 December 2025) showing what the business owns (**Assets**), what it owes (**Liabilities**), and the owner's stake (**Capital**).

Notice the key difference from the Trading and Profit & Loss Account:
- The Profit & Loss Account is like a **movie** covering the whole year (*"for the year ended 31 December 2025"*).
- The Balance Sheet is like a **photograph** frozen at midnight on closing day (*"as at 31 December 2025"*).

$$

</FormulaHint>

### Why Working Capital Is Watched So Closely!
- **Healthy Positive Working Capital** (Current Assets > Current Liabilities): The business can pay its creditors, utility bills, and staff wages on time and even take advantage of cash discounts.
- **Negative Working Capital** (Current Liabilities > Current Assets — called a *Working Capital Deficit*): Even if the firm owns a J\$20 million building, it may run out of cash to pay immediate bills, triggering overdraft fees, supplier cut-offs, or insolvency!

## 2. Anatomy of a High-Scoring Balance Sheet (Presentation Rules)

Examiners award dedicated marks for clean presentation (highlighted in the May/June 2025 Subject Report!). Follow these **five layout rules** every single time:

1. **The Proper Heading:** Always write **Business / Owner Name** on line 1, and **"Balance Sheet as at [Exact Date]"** (or *Statement of Financial Position as at [Date]*) on line 2. Never write *"for the year ended"*!
2. **Clear Section Subheadings:** Clearly label **FIXED ASSETS**, **CURRENT ASSETS**, **LESS: CURRENT LIABILITIES**, **FINANCED BY / CAPITAL**, and **LONG-TERM LIABILITIES**.
3. **Consistent Column Alignment:** Put currency signs (`J$` or `$`) cleanly at the top of each amount column. Use the inner column(s) for item lists and subtotals, and the outermost right column for major section totals.
4. **Brackets `( )` for Subtraction:** Always enclose deducted totals — such as **Current Liabilities** and **Drawings** (or **Net Loss**) — inside parentheses like `(48,000)`.
5. **Double Underline Final Matching Totals:** Put a single line above a total and a neat **double line (`===`)** underneath the final matching balance on both the top half and bottom half.

### Step-by-Step Method to Build a Balance Sheet Without Mistakes
1. **Tag every balance on the question paper first** using quick pencil codes: `FA` (Fixed Asset), `CA` (Current Asset), `CL` (Current Liability), `LTL` (Long-Term Liability), `CAP` (Opening Capital, Net Profit, Drawings).
2. **Write the 3-part heading** and set up your columns.
3. **Build the Top Half:** List all `FA` in Order of Permanence → total them. List all `CA` in Order of Permanence → subtotal them → subtract total `CL` to get **Working Capital** → add `FA + Working Capital` = **Capital Employed**.
4. **Build the Bottom Half (`FINANCED BY`):** Start with Opening Capital → **Add** Net Profit (or **Less** Net Loss) → **Less** Drawings = **Closing Capital** → add all `LTL` → grand total **Capital Employed**.
5. **Tick off every figure** from the question list to make sure nothing was skipped or used twice — **Top Half MUST equal Bottom Half!**

## 3. Vertical Format vs Horizontal (T-Format) Balance Sheet

Both formats come straight from $A = C + L$ and produce identical numbers:
- **Vertical Format** *(modern British/Caribbean exam standard)*: Lists Assets and Current Liabilities on top to spotlight **Working Capital**, then shows **Financed By (Capital + Long-Term Liabilities)** underneath.
- **Horizontal (T-Format)** *(seen in some older past papers)*: Places **Assets** on the left side and **Capital + Liabilities** on the right side like a giant T-account — both sides total to **Total Assets**.
