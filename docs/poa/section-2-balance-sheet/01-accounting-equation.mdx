## 1. The Three Building Blocks of the Balance Sheet

Every business — no matter how big or small — owns resources, and every single dollar of those resources had to come from somewhere: either from the **owner** or from **outsiders** who lent money or sold goods on credit.

| Element | Clear Definition | Common Examples (British / Caribbean Terms) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Assets ($A$)** | Resources **owned or controlled** by the business that have monetary value and bring future economic benefit. | Premises (Land & Buildings), Motor Vehicles, Fixtures & Fittings, Stock (Inventory), Debtors (Accounts Receivable), Cash at Bank, Cash in Hand. |
| **Capital ($C$)** *(Owner's Equity)* | The **owner's financial claim** on the business — the money and resources the owner put into the firm (plus profits kept in, minus drawings taken out). | Opening Capital, Additional Capital introduced, Net Profit (increases Capital), Drawings (decreases Capital). |
| **Liabilities ($L$)** | **Debts and obligations owed** by the business to outsiders (creditors, banks, lenders) that must be paid in the future. | Creditors (Accounts Payable), Bank Overdraft, Accrued Expenses, Bank Loan, Mortgage. |

---

## 2. The Accounting Equation (Hover or Tap Any Formula!)

Because every asset owned by the business is funded either by the owner's **Capital** or by outside **Liabilities**, the two sides **ALWAYS** equal each other:

<FormulaCard
  title="The Accounting Equation & Its Rearrangements"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Everything the business owns (Assets) equals what the owner put in (Capital) plus what the business owes outsiders (Liabilities). Knowing any two gives you the third!"
>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Standard Accounting Equation"
  short="Resources owned by the business (Assets) always equal the owner's stake (Capital) plus outside debts (Liabilities)."
>

$$
\text{Assets} = \text{Capital} + \text{Liabilities} \qquad (A = C + L)
$$

</FormulaHint>

You can rearrange the same equation to find **Capital** or **Liabilities** whenever one is missing on an exam table:

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Finding Capital (Net Assets)"
  short="Subtract everything the business owes outsiders (Liabilities) from everything it owns (Assets) — what's left belongs to the owner (Capital)."
>

$$
\text{Capital} = \text{Assets} - \text{Liabilities} \qquad (C = A - L)
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Finding Liabilities"
  short="Subtract the owner's Capital from total Assets to find how much the business owes to creditors and lenders (Liabilities)."
>

$$
\text{Liabilities} = \text{Assets} - \text{Capital} \qquad (L = A - C)
$$

</FormulaHint>

</FormulaCard>

## 4. How Business Transactions Affect $A = C + L$

Under the **Dual Aspect** concept, every single transaction changes **at least two items** so that $A = C + L$ stays perfectly balanced after every event.

Suppose **K. Morgan Traders** starts on 1 January with:
- **Assets** = J\$200,000 | **Capital** = J\$160,000 | **Liabilities** = J\$40,000 *(Check: $200{,}000 = 160{,}000 + 40{,}000$)*

Watch what happens after five typical Jamaican business transactions:

| # | Transaction | Effect on Assets ($A$) | Effect on Capital ($C$) | Effect on Liabilities ($L$) | New Balance ($A = C + L$) |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Bought a delivery van **on credit** from AutoWorld for **J\$85,000** | **+85,000** *(Motor Vehicles ↑)* | No effect | **+85,000** *(Creditors ↑)* | $285{,}000 = 160{,}000 + 125{,}000$ |
| **2** | Paid a creditor **J\$15,000 by cheque** | **−15,000** *(Bank ↓)* | No effect | **−15,000** *(Creditors ↓)* | $270{,}000 = 160{,}000 + 110{,}000$ |
| **3** | Owner introduced **J\$30,000** extra cash into the business bank | **+30,000** *(Bank ↑)* | **+30,000** *(Capital ↑)* | No effect | $300{,}000 = 190{,}000 + 110{,}000$ |
| **4** | Bought office equipment for **J\$20,000 cash** | **+20,000** *(Equipment ↑)* & **−20,000** *(Cash ↓)* → **Net 0** | No effect | No effect | $300{,}000 = 190{,}000 + 110{,}000$ |
| **5** | Owner took **J\$10,000 cash** for personal use (**Drawings**) | **−10,000** *(Cash ↓)* | **−10,000** *(Capital ↓)* | No effect | $290{,}000 = 180{,}000 + 110{,}000$ |

:::tip Notice Transaction #4!
Exchanging **one asset for another asset** (buying equipment for cash, or receiving a cheque from a debtor) changes **total Assets by J\$0** — one asset goes up by the exact amount another asset goes down!
:::

## 5. Past-Paper-Style Worked Example & Practice Drills

<WorkedExample
  title="Finding Capital from a List of Balances (Classic Past-Paper Style)"
  marks={5}
  problem={
    <>
      On 31 March, a sole trader’s books show the following balances in Jamaican dollars:
      <br />
      Premises <strong>J$300,000</strong>; Plant &amp; Machinery <strong>J$100,000</strong>;
      Debtors <strong>J$70,000</strong>; Prepayments <strong>J$1,000</strong>;
      Creditors <strong>J$50,000</strong>; Bank Overdraft <strong>J$80,000</strong>.
      <br />
      Calculate the sole trader’s <strong>Capital</strong> using the Accounting Equation.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Never guess which column an item belongs to! Group ALL Assets first, group ALL Liabilities second (watch out for Bank Overdraft — it is a LIABILITY, not an asset!), then calculate Capital = Assets − Liabilities."
>

**Step 1 — Total all Assets ($A$):** *(2 marks)*
- Premises = J\$300,000
- Plant & Machinery = J\$100,000
- Debtors = J\$70,000
- Prepayments = J\$1,000
- **Total Assets** = $300{,}000 + 100{,}000 + 70{,}000 + 1{,}000 =$ **J\$471,000**

**Step 2 — Total all Liabilities ($L$):** *(2 marks)*
- Creditors = J\$50,000
- Bank Overdraft = J\$80,000 *(money owed to the bank)*
- **Total Liabilities** = $50{,}000 + 80{,}000 =$ **J\$130,000**

**Step 3 — Apply $C = A - L$:** *(1 mark)*

$$
\text{Capital} = 471{,}000 - 130{,}000 = \mathbf{341{,}000} \quad (\text{JMD})
$$

Therefore, the sole trader's **Capital = J\$341,000**.

</WorkedExample>

  title="True or False: Testing the Accounting Equation"

      statement: 'Buying stock (inventory) for J$25,000 cash increases Total Assets by J$25,000.',

      explanation:
        'Cash at Bank (Asset) decreases by J$40,000 and Bank Loan (Liability) decreases by J$40,000, keeping A = C + L balanced.',
    },
    {
      statement: 'If Total Assets are J$600,000 and Capital is J$420,000, then Total Liabilities must be J$180,000.',
      answer: true,
      explanation:
        'Using L = A − C: J$600,000 − J$420,000 = J$180,000.',
    },
    {
      statement: 'When a debtor pays J$12,000 owed to the business by cheque, Capital increases by J$12,000.',
      answer: false,
      explanation:
        'Collecting money from a debtor only swaps one asset for another: Bank increases by J$12,000 and Debtors decreases by J$12,000. Capital does NOT change.',
    },
  ]}
