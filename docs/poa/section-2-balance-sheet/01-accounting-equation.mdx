## 1. The Big Idea Behind the Accounting Equation

Think about starting a small business from scratch. Suppose you want to open a juice bar in Half Way Tree, and the business needs **J\$500,000** worth of resources (blenders, fridge, fruit stock, and cash in the register). Where can that J\$500,000 come from?

There are only **TWO possible sources of funding** in the entire world:
1. **Money put in by the Owner** → called **Capital** (say, J\$350,000 of your own savings); and
2. **Money borrowed or owed to Outsiders** → called **Liabilities** (say, a J\$150,000 bank loan or unpaid supplier invoices).

Notice immediately: **Resources Owned (J\$500,000) = Owner's Funding (J\$350,000) + Outsiders' Funding (J\$150,000)**! Because every single resource in the business had to be supplied by *either* the owner *or* an outsider, this balance is an **unbreakable law** of accounting.

---

## 2. The Three Core Elements Defined

| Element | Full Definition | Plain-English Test | Common British / Caribbean Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Assets ($A$)** | Economic resources **owned or controlled** by the business as a result of past transactions that have measurable monetary value and bring future benefit. | *"Does the business OWN this resource, or does someone owe THIS money TO the business?"* | Land & Buildings (Premises), Machinery, Furniture & Fittings, Motor Vehicles, Stock (Inventory), Debtors (Accounts Receivable), Prepaid Expenses, Cash at Bank, Cash in Hand. |
| **Capital ($C$)** *(Owner's Equity)* | The **owner's net financial stake (claim)** in the business — what would remain for the owner if all Assets were sold at book value and all Liabilities paid off. | *"How much of the business's resources belong to the OWNER?"* | Opening Capital, Additional Capital introduced, **Net Profit** *(increases Capital)*, **Drawings** *(decreases Capital)*, **Net Loss** *(decreases Capital)*. |
| **Liabilities ($L$)** | Present **debts and financial obligations owed** by the business to outsiders (creditors, banks, tax authorities) that must be settled in the future. | *"Does the business OWE this money to someone OUTSIDE the business?"* | Creditors (Accounts Payable), Bank Overdraft, Accrued Expenses (unpaid bills), Bank Loans, Mortgages, Debentures. |

## 3. The Accounting Equation & Its Three Forms

Hover or tap any formula below to see a plain-English explanation:

<FormulaCard
  title="The Accounting Equation & Rearrangements"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Assets always equal Capital plus Liabilities. Whenever you know any two figures, simple addition or subtraction gives the missing third figure!"
>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Standard Form (Find Assets)"
  short="Total resources owned by the business (Assets) equal the owner's stake (Capital) plus all outside debts (Liabilities)."
>

$$
\text{Assets} = \text{Capital} + \text{Liabilities} \qquad (A = C + L)
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Net Assets Form (Find Capital)"
  short="Take everything the business owns (Assets) and subtract everything it owes outsiders (Liabilities) — what remains is Net Assets, which equals the owner's Capital!"
>

$$
\text{Capital} = \text{Assets} - \text{Liabilities} \qquad (C = A - L)
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Liabilities Form (Find Liabilities)"
  short="Subtract the owner's Capital from Total Assets to reveal how much of the business was financed by outside debts (Liabilities)."
>

$$
\text{Liabilities} = \text{Assets} - \text{Capital} \qquad (L = A - C)
$$

</FormulaHint>

</FormulaCard>

### The Expanded Accounting Equation (How Profit & Drawings Fit In!)
Why does Capital change from 1 January to 31 December? Because **Revenues (Sales)** earn profit for the owner (increasing Capital), **Expenses** cost the business money (decreasing Capital), and **Drawings** take value out for personal use (decreasing Capital):

$$
\text{Closing Capital} = \text{Opening Capital} + \text{Additional Capital} + \text{Revenue} - \text{Expenses} - \text{Drawings}
$$

*(Since $\text{Revenue} - \text{Expenses} = \text{Net Profit}$, that simplifies to $\text{Closing Capital} = \text{Opening Capital} + \text{Net Profit} - \text{Drawings}$ — a formula you will use constantly on Balance Sheets!)*

## 5. How Every Business Transaction Keeps $A = C + L$ Balanced

Under the **Dual Aspect** principle, **every single transaction produces TWO effects** — never just one! There are **four basic patterns** a transaction can follow:

1. **Asset ↑ and Asset ↓ (Asset Swap):** Total Assets stay **unchanged**! *(e.g. Buying a van for cash; collecting a cheque from a debtor; depositing cash into the bank.)*
2. **Asset ↑ and Liability ↑:** Both sides of the equation grow by the exact same amount. *(e.g. Buying stock or equipment on credit; taking a bank loan.)*
3. **Asset ↓ and Liability ↓:** Both sides shrink by the exact same amount. *(e.g. Paying a creditor by cheque; repaying part of a bank loan.)*
4. **Asset ↑/↓ and Capital ↑/↓:** Happens whenever the owner invests cash (**+A, +C**), takes Drawings (**−A, −C**), earns profit/revenue (**+A, +C**), or pays an expense (**−A, −C**).

### Step-by-Step Walkthrough Table
Suppose **K. Morgan Traders** begins on 1 January with:
- **Assets** = J\$200,000 | **Capital** = J\$160,000 | **Liabilities** = J\$40,000 *(Check: $200{,}000 = 160{,}000 + 40{,}000$)*

Watch how each transaction updates the equation step by step:

| # | Transaction | Effect on Assets ($A$) | Effect on Capital ($C$) | Effect on Liabilities ($L$) | Updated Equation ($A = C + L$) |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Bought a delivery van **on credit** from AutoWorld for **J\$85,000** | **+85,000** *(Motor Vehicles ↑)* | No effect | **+85,000** *(Creditors ↑)* | $285{,}000 = 160{,}000 + 125{,}000$ |
| **2** | Paid a creditor **J\$15,000 by cheque** | **−15,000** *(Bank ↓)* | No effect | **−15,000** *(Creditors ↓)* | $270{,}000 = 160{,}000 + 110{,}000$ |
| **3** | Owner introduced **J\$30,000** extra cash into the business bank | **+30,000** *(Bank ↑)* | **+30,000** *(Capital ↑)* | No effect | $300{,}000 = 190{,}000 + 110{,}000$ |
| **4** | Bought office equipment for **J\$20,000 cash** | **+20,000** *(Equip ↑)* & **−20,000** *(Cash ↓)* → **Net 0** | No effect | No effect | $300{,}000 = 190{,}000 + 110{,}000$ |
| **5** | Sold stock costing **J\$18,000** for **J\$25,000 cash** *(Profit = J\$7,000!)* | **+25,000** *(Cash ↑)* & **−18,000** *(Stock ↓)* → **+7,000** | **+7,000** *(Profit ↑)* | No effect | $307{,}000 = 197{,}000 + 110{,}000$ |
| **6** | Paid monthly shop rent of **J\$12,000** by cheque *(Expense)* | **−12,000** *(Bank ↓)* | **−12,000** *(Expense reduces profit/Capital)* | No effect | $295{,}000 = 185{,}000 + 110{,}000$ |
| **7** | Owner took **J\$10,000 cash** for personal use (**Drawings**) | **−10,000** *(Cash ↓)* | **−10,000** *(Capital ↓)* | No effect | $285{,}000 = 175{,}000 + 110{,}000$ |

:::warning Watch Out for Transaction #5 (Selling Stock at a Profit)!
Students often miss this classic Paper 1 trick: if you sell stock that cost **J\$18,000** for **J\$25,000**, Cash (or Debtors) rises by **J\$25,000**, Stock falls by **J\$18,000** (Net Assets = **+J\$7,000**), and **Capital rises by the J\$7,000 profit** — NOT by J\$25,000!
:::

## 6. Past-Paper-Style Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Finding Capital from a Mixed List of Account Balances"
  marks={6}
  problem={
    <>
      On 31 March, a sole trader’s books show the following balances in Jamaican dollars:
      <br />
      Premises <strong>J$300,000</strong>; Plant &amp; Machinery <strong>J$100,000</strong>;
      Debtors <strong>J$70,000</strong>; Prepayments <strong>J$1,000</strong>;
      Creditors <strong>J$50,000</strong>; Bank Overdraft <strong>J$80,000</strong>;
      Loan from K. Lewis (3 years) <strong>J$40,000</strong>.
      <br />
      Calculate the sole trader’s <strong>Capital</strong> using the Accounting Equation.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Never guess which side an item belongs to! First mark every single item with (A) for Asset or (L) for Liability — watch out for Bank Overdraft (always L!) and Loan FROM someone (always L!) — then compute Capital = Total Assets − Total Liabilities."
>

**Step 1 — Group and Total all Assets ($A$):** *(2 marks)*
- Premises = J\$300,000
- Plant & Machinery = J\$100,000
- Debtors = J\$70,000
- Prepayments (Prepaid Expenses) = J\$1,000
- **Total Assets** = $300{,}000 + 100{,}000 + 70{,}000 + 1{,}000 =$ **J\$471,000**

**Step 2 — Group and Total all Liabilities ($L$):** *(2 marks)*
- Creditors = J\$50,000
- Bank Overdraft = J\$80,000 *(short-term debt owed to the bank)*
- Loan from K. Lewis = J\$40,000 *(long-term debt owed to K. Lewis)*
- **Total Liabilities** = $50{,}000 + 80{,}000 + 40{,}000 =$ **J\$170,000**

**Step 3 — Apply $C = A - L$:** *(2 marks)*

$$
\text{Capital} = 471{,}000 - 170{,}000 = \mathbf{301{,}000} \quad (\text{JMD})
$$

Therefore, the sole trader's **Capital = J\$301,000**. *(Quick check: $301{,}000 + 170{,}000 = 471{,}000$ ✓)*

</WorkedExample>
