## 1. Why Do We Classify Balance Sheet Items?

Imagine handing a bank manager a messy 25-line list mixing together your delivery truck, cash register coins, 15-year mortgage, unpaid JPS bill, and unsold stock. The banker couldn't easily tell whether you can pay next week's bills!

A **Classified Balance Sheet** groups similar items under clear headings so readers can immediately judge two crucial things:
1. **Long-term strength** (Fixed Assets vs Long-Term Liabilities & Capital); and
2. **Short-term liquidity** (Current Assets vs Current Liabilities — can the firm pay its bills over the next 12 months?).

---

## 2. Classifying Assets: Fixed (Non-Current) vs Current

Assets are split into two main classes based on **TWO tests**: *(1) WHY was the item acquired?* and *(2) HOW LONG will the business keep it?*

### A. Fixed Assets (Non-Current Assets)
**Definition:** Long-term resources acquired **for ongoing use in operating the business** — **NOT bought for resale** — and expected to last **more than one accounting year (12 months)**.

Fixed Assets are further divided into:
- **Tangible Fixed Assets** *(physical items you can touch)*:
  1. **Land and Buildings (Premises)**
  2. **Plant and Machinery**
  3. **Furniture, Fixtures and Fittings** *(shelves, counters, display cabinets)*
  4. **Office & Computer Equipment** *(computers, printers, POS terminals)*
  5. **Motor Vehicles** *(delivery vans, company trucks)*
- **Intangible Fixed Assets** *(valuable long-term rights without physical shape — seen later in Partnerships/Companies)*: **Goodwill**, **Patents**, **Trademarks**, **Copyrights**.

### B. Current Assets
**Definition:** Short-term resources that are **already cash**, or are expected to be **sold, consumed, or converted into cash within one accounting year (12 months)** during normal trading.

The **five standard Current Assets** you will meet on almost every Jamaican exam paper:
1. **Stock (Inventory):** Unsold goods bought specifically for resale to customers.
2. **Debtors (Accounts Receivable):** Credit customers who owe the business money for goods/services already supplied.
3. **Prepaid Expenses (Prepayments):** Operating bills already paid in advance for *next* year (e.g. Prepaid Insurance or Prepaid Rent — the business is owed that future service!).
4. **Accrued Income (Income Receivable):** Revenue already earned this year (e.g. rent receivable from a sub-tenant) that has not yet been received in cash.
5. **Cash at Bank** *(positive bank balance)* and **Cash in Hand** *(notes/coins in the safe, register, or petty cash tin)*.

:::tip The "Purpose Test" — The Same Item Can Change Category!
Never classify an item purely by its name — always ask **what kind of business owns it!**
- At a **bakery**, a delivery van is a **Fixed Asset** (used to deliver bread for 6 years).
- At a **car dealership** (e.g. Stewart's Automotive), cars on the showroom floor are bought **for resale** — so those cars are **Stock (Current Asset)**!
- Similarly, computers at a law office are **Fixed Assets**, whereas computers on the shelf at a computer store are **Stock (Current Asset)**!
:::

## 3. Order of Permanence vs Order of Liquidity

The syllabus requires you to list Balance Sheet items in **either** of two recognised orders (the May/June 2025 Subject Report specifically reminded students to master Order of Permanence and know both formats!).

### A. Order of Permanence *(Most Common in Jamaican & British Exams!)*
Starts with the assets that stay in the business **longest** (hardest to convert into cash) and works down to **Cash in Hand** last:
- **Fixed Assets:** Premises → Plant & Machinery → Fixtures & Fittings → Office Equipment → Motor Vehicles
- **Current Assets:** **Stock** → **Debtors** → **Prepayments** → **Cash at Bank** → **Cash in Hand**
- **Capital & Liabilities (if ordered by permanence):** Capital → Long-Term Liabilities → Current Liabilities.

### B. Order of Liquidity *(Exact Reverse!)*
**Liquidity** means how quickly and easily an asset can be turned into spendable cash without losing value. **Order of Liquidity** starts with the **most liquid** item first and finishes with the least liquid last:
- **Current Assets:** **Cash in Hand** → **Cash at Bank** → **Prepayments** → **Debtors** → **Stock**
- **Fixed Assets:** Motor Vehicles → Office Equipment → Fixtures & Fittings → Plant & Machinery → Premises
- **Liabilities:** Current Liabilities first (due soonest!) → Long-Term Liabilities → Capital last.

| Memory Trick | Order of Permanence | Order of Liquidity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Starts with…** | **Premises** *(most permanent)* / **Stock** *(top of Current Assets)* | **Cash in Hand** *(most liquid — already cash!)* |
| **Ends with…** | **Cash in Hand** *(bottom of Current Assets)* | **Premises** *(hardest to sell quickly for cash)* |

## 4. Classifying Liabilities & Capital

### A. Current Liabilities
**Definition:** Short-term debts and obligations owed by the business that must be settled **within one accounting year (12 months)**.
- **Creditors (Accounts Payable):** Suppliers owed money for goods bought on credit (usually due within 30–60 days).
- **Bank Overdraft:** Withdrawing more money from the business bank current account than was deposited — the bank can demand repayment at short notice, so it is **ALWAYS a Current Liability**!
- **Accrued Expenses (Expenses Owing):** Bills for services already used up this year that remain unpaid at year-end (e.g. unpaid JPS electricity, NWC water, or unpaid wages).
- **Prepaid Income (Unearned Revenue):** Cash received in advance from a customer/tenant for goods or rent not yet provided (we owe them the service or a refund!).
- **Short-Term Loan:** Any loan (or portion of a loan) repayable within **12 months or less**.

### B. Long-Term Liabilities (Non-Current Liabilities)
**Definition:** Debts owed to outsiders that are repayable **over a period longer than one accounting year (more than 12 months)**.
- **Bank Loan** *(e.g. a 5-year expansion loan from NCB or Scotiabank)*
- **Mortgage** *(long-term loan secured against Land & Buildings, often 10–25 years)*
- **Loan from a Finance Company or Individual** *(repayable in > 1 year)*
- **Debentures** *(long-term fixed-interest loan certificates issued by Limited Companies)*

### C. Capital (Owner's Equity)
**Definition:** The owner's net claim on the business's assets. Unlike Liabilities (owed to outsiders), Capital is owed to the **owner** and changes every year according to profit and personal withdrawals:

## 5. Six Classic Exam Classification Traps (Memorise These!)

Examiners put these exact items into Paper 1 & Paper 2 to catch students who skim too fast:

| Tricky Item | Correct Classification | Why Students Lose Marks! |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bank Overdraft** *(or Bank shown in the Credit column of a Trial Balance!)* | **Current Liability** | Regular **Cash at Bank (Dr)** is a Current Asset, but a **Bank Overdraft (Cr)** means you owe the bank money! *(Highlighted in the May/June 2025 Subject Report!)* |
| **Loan FROM J. Brown (5 yrs)** | **Long-Term Liability** | "Loan **from**" means the business borrowed it and **owes** J. Brown. |
| **Loan TO M. Grant** | **Asset** *(Current if ≤ 1 yr; Non-Current if > 1 yr)* | "Loan **to**" means M. Grant owes **the business** money — it is money coming back to us! |
| **Prepaid Expense (e.g. Prepaid Insurance)** | **Current Asset** | Sounds like an expense, yet because we paid *in advance* for next year, the insurer owes us future cover — an **Asset**! |
| **Accrued Expense (e.g. Wages Owing)** | **Current Liability** | We already used the labour/electricity and haven't paid yet — a short-term debt (**Liability**)! |
| **Drawings** | **Deduction from Capital** *(NOT an Expense or Asset!)* | Personal withdrawals by the owner never enter the Profit & Loss Account; they are subtracted from Capital on the Balance Sheet. |

## 6. Past-Paper-Style Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Classifying a Mixed Trial Balance & Ordering Current Assets"
  marks={8}
  problem={
    <>
      Classify each of the following items for <strong>Hilltop Electronics</strong> (a shop that sells televisions and speakers) as a <strong>Fixed Asset</strong>, <strong>Current Asset</strong>, <strong>Current Liability</strong>, or <strong>Long-Term Liability</strong>:
      <br />
      (a) Unsold televisions on the display floor &nbsp;|&nbsp;
      (b) Delivery van used for customer drop-offs &nbsp;|&nbsp;
      (c) Cash at Bank (Credit balance in Trial Balance) &nbsp;|&nbsp;
      (d) Loan to Sales Supervisor (repayable in 8 months) &nbsp;|&nbsp;
      (e) 10-Year Mortgage on the shop building &nbsp;|&nbsp;
      (f) Prepaid Shop Insurance.
      <br />
      Then arrange these four Current Assets in <strong>Order of Permanence</strong>: Cash in Hand, Debtors, Stock, Cash at Bank.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Always spot the word 'Credit balance' next to Bank — in a Trial Balance, a Credit Bank balance is ALWAYS a Bank Overdraft (Current Liability)!"
>

**Part 1 — Classifications** *(1 mark each = 6 marks)*
- **(a) Unsold televisions on the display floor:** **Current Asset (Stock)** — bought specifically for resale.
- **(b) Delivery van:** **Fixed Asset (Non-Current Asset)** — used in business operations for multiple years, not for resale.
- **(c) Cash at Bank (Credit balance):** **Current Liability (Bank Overdraft)** — a Credit balance in the Bank account means the shop owes the bank money!
- **(d) Loan TO Sales Supervisor (8 months):** **Current Asset** — the employee owes the business money repayable within 12 months.
- **(e) 10-Year Mortgage:** **Long-Term Liability** — debt repayable over more than one year.
- **(f) Prepaid Shop Insurance:** **Current Asset** — service already paid for in advance for the upcoming period.

**Part 2 — Current Assets in Order of Permanence (least liquid → most liquid)** *(2 marks)*
1. **Stock** → 2. **Debtors** → 3. **Cash at Bank** → 4. **Cash in Hand**

</WorkedExample>
