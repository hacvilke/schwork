## 1. Classifying Assets: Fixed vs Current

On a Classified Balance Sheet, **Assets** are split into two clear groups based on **how long** the business intends to keep them and **why** they were bought:

### A. Fixed Assets (Non-Current Assets)
**Definition:** Long-term resources acquired **for use in running the business** — **NOT for resale** — expected to last **more than one accounting year**.

Common British/Caribbean Fixed Assets (listed in **Order of Permanence** — hardest to convert to cash first):
1. **Land and Buildings (Premises)**
2. **Plant and Machinery**
3. **Furniture, Fixtures and Fittings**
4. **Office / Computer Equipment**
5. **Motor Vehicles**

### B. Current Assets
**Definition:** Short-term resources that are already cash, or are expected to be **sold, used up, or converted into cash within one accounting year** (12 months) during normal trading.

Common British/Caribbean Current Assets (listed in **Order of Permanence** — least liquid first, most liquid last):
1. **Stock (Inventory)** — unsold goods held for resale
2. **Debtors (Accounts Receivable)** — customers who owe the business money from credit sales
3. **Prepaid Expenses (Prepayments)** — expenses paid in advance (e.g. next year's insurance)
4. **Cash at Bank** — positive money held in the business bank account
5. **Cash in Hand** — physical notes and coins kept at the business (e.g. petty cash / cash register)

:::tip Order of Permanence vs Order of Liquidity
- **Order of Permanence** *(most common in Jamaican / British Balance Sheets)*: starts with the most permanent asset (**Premises**) down to **Cash in Hand**.
- **Order of Liquidity** *(exact reverse)*: starts with **Cash in Hand** → **Cash at Bank** → **Debtors** → **Stock** → up to **Premises** last.
:::

## 2. Classifying Liabilities & Capital

### A. Current Liabilities
**Definition:** Short-term debts owed by the business that must be settled **within one accounting year (12 months)**.
- **Creditors (Accounts Payable)** — suppliers owed money for credit purchases
- **Bank Overdraft** — withdrawing more from the business current account than is deposited (short-term debt owed to the bank)
- **Accrued Expenses (Expenses Owing)** — bills used up this year but not yet paid (e.g. unpaid JPS electricity or wages owing)
- **Short-Term Loan** — any loan repayable in **1 year or less**

### B. Long-Term Liabilities (Non-Current Liabilities)
**Definition:** Long-term debts owed to outsiders that are repayable **over more than one accounting year** (longer than 12 months).
- **Bank Loan** (e.g. a 5-year business expansion loan)
- **Mortgage** (a long-term loan secured against land/buildings)
- **Loan from a Finance Company or Individual** (repayable over several years)
- **Debentures** *(for limited companies)*

### C. Capital (Owner's Equity)
**Definition:** The owner's net investment in the business. Over an accounting year, Capital grows with **Net Profit** (and extra capital introduced) and shrinks when the owner takes **Drawings** (or suffers a **Net Loss**).

## 3. Classic Exam Classification Traps (Watch Out!)

| Tricky Item | Correct Classification | Why Students Get Caught! |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bank Overdraft** | **Current Liability** | Regular **Cash at Bank** is a Current Asset, but a **Bank Overdraft** means you owe the bank money! |
| **Loan FROM J. Brown (5 yrs)** | **Long-Term Liability** | "Loan **from**" means the business borrowed it and **owes** J. Brown. |
| **Loan TO M. Grant** | **Asset** *(Current if ≤ 1 yr, Non-Current if > 1 yr)* | "Loan **to**" means someone owes **us** money — it is a resource coming back to the business! |
| **Motor Vans at a Car Dealership** | **Current Asset (Stock)** | At a bakery, a van is a **Fixed Asset**. At a car dealership, cars are bought **for resale**, making them **Stock (Current Asset)**! |
