:::info Essential Understanding
When a bakery buys a **$40,000 delivery van** that will run for **5 years**, charging all $40,000 as an expense in Year 1 would crush Year 1 profit and make Years 2–5 look artificially free! **Depreciation** applies the **Matching Concept** by systematically spreading the cost of a Non-Current Asset across the years it helps earn revenue. Crucially, **depreciation is a NON-CASH expense** — it does NOT set aside a cash pile to buy a new van!
:::

## 1. What Is Depreciation & Why Does It Happen?

**Depreciation** is the **systematic allocation of the depreciable cost of a tangible Non-Current Asset over its estimated useful economic life** *(also described as the loss in value of a fixed asset through use or time)*.

### Four Main Causes of Depreciation
1. **Physical Wear and Tear:** Daily use, friction, weather, and rust gradually wear down machinery, vehicles, and furniture.
2. **Obsolescence (Economic Factor):** New technology or changing customer tastes make older computers, phones, or software outdated before they physically break!
3. **Passage of Time:** Leases, patents, and copyrights expire automatically after a fixed number of years even if barely used.
4. **Depletion:** Natural resource assets (quarries, mines, oil wells, timberlands) lose value as raw materials are extracted.

*(Note: **Freehold Land** normally does **NOT** depreciate because it has an unlimited useful life!)*

## 2. The Two Core Depreciation Formulas

<FormulaCard
  title="Straight-Line vs. Reducing-Balance Depreciation Formulas"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Straight-Line charges an EQUAL dollar amount every year; Reducing-Balance applies a fixed % to the shrinking Net Book Value."
>

### Method 1: Straight-Line (Fixed Instalment) Method

$$
\text{Annual Depreciation} = \frac{\text{Original Cost} - \text{Estimated Scrap (Residual) Value}}{\text{Estimated Useful Life (Years)}}
$$

*Or when given a fixed percentage rate on Cost:*

$$
\text{Annual Depreciation} = \text{Rate \%} \times \text{Original Cost}
$$

### Method 2: Reducing-Balance (Diminishing Balance) Method

$$
\text{Annual Depreciation} = \text{Rate \%} \times \text{Net Book Value (at start of year)}
$$

where:

$$
\text{Net Book Value (NBV)} = \text{Original Cost} - \text{Accumulated Depreciation to Date}
$$

</FormulaCard>

### Pro-Rata Rule (Mid-Year Purchases!)
Watch the policy statement in exam questions!
- **"Pro-rata (month-by-month) basis":** Multiply annual depreciation by `Months Owned ÷ 12`. *(Example: Asset bought **1 July** for a **31 Dec** year-end = `6/12` of a full year!)*
- **"Full year’s depreciation in year of purchase, none in year of sale":** Charge a full `12/12` year even if bought in October!

## 5. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="3-Year Depreciation Schedules (Straight-Line, Reducing-Balance & Mid-Year Pro-Rata)"
  marks={12}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> On <strong>1 January 2024</strong>, <strong>Island Express Delivery</strong>
        purchased two Non-Current Assets (financial year ends 31 December):
      </p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Office Equipment:</strong> Cost $16,000; Estimated Scrap Value $1,000; Estimated Useful Life 5 years (<strong>Straight-Line Method</strong>).</li>
        <li><strong>Delivery Van:</strong> Cost $40,000; depreciated at <strong>20% per annum (Reducing-Balance Method)</strong>.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>
        Additionally, on <strong>1 July 2026</strong>, the business bought a new <strong>Computer System for $12,000</strong>
        (depreciated at <strong>25% straight-line on a pro-rata monthly basis</strong>).
      </p>
      <ol>
        <li>Prepare a 3-year schedule (2024–2026) showing Annual Depreciation, Accumulated Depreciation, and Closing NBV for the <strong>Office Equipment</strong>. <em>[4 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Prepare a 3-year schedule (2024–2026) for the <strong>Delivery Van</strong>. <em>[6 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Calculate the 2026 depreciation charge for the <strong>Computer System</strong> bought on 1 July 2026. <em>[2 marks]</em></li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

**1. Office Equipment — Straight-Line Method (4 marks):**
- Annual Depreciation = `($16,000 − $1,000) ÷ 5 years` = **$3,000 per year** *(1 mark)*

| Year | Original Cost ($) | Annual Depr. ($) | Accum. Depr. ($) | Closing NBV ($) |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **2024** | 16,000 | 3,000 | 3,000 | **13,000** |
| **2025** | 16,000 | 3,000 | 6,000 | **10,000** |
| **2026** | 16,000 | 3,000 | 9,000 | **7,000** |

**2. Delivery Van — Reducing-Balance at 20% (6 marks):**

| Year | Opening NBV ($) | Calculation (`20% × NBV`) | Annual Depr. ($) | Accum. Depr. ($) | Closing NBV ($) |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **2024** | 40,000 | `20% × $40,000` | **8,000** | 8,000 | **32,000** |
| **2025** | 32,000 | `20% × $32,000` | **6,400** | 14,400 | **25,600** |
| **2026** | 25,600 | `20% × $25,600` | **5,120** | 19,520 | **20,480** |

**3. Mid-Year Computer System — Pro-Rata Straight-Line (2 marks):**
- Owned from **1 July 2026 to 31 December 2026** = **6 months** (`6/12` of a year).
- 2026 Depreciation = `$12,000 × 25% × (6 ÷ 12)` = `$3,000 × 0.5` = **$1,500** *(2 marks)*.
- *(Total 2026 Depreciation Expense across all 3 assets = `$3,000 + $5,120 + $1,500` = **$9,620**!)*

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Straight-Line** = `(Cost − Scrap) ÷ Useful Life` (or `% × Original Cost`) → **equal every year**.
- **Reducing-Balance** = `% × Net Book Value` → **shrinks every year**.
- Always check for **mid-year purchase dates** (`months ÷ 12`) and never depreciate **Freehold Land**!
:::
