:::info Essential Understanding
At year-end, a business’s Cash Book rarely matches the true revenue earned or true expenses incurred during the year! Some December bills have not been paid yet (**accruals**), while some insurance or rent paid in November actually covers next year (**prepayments**). The **Accrual and Matching Concepts** ensure every dollar of revenue and expense is assigned to the **exact accounting period it belongs to** — regardless of when cash changes hands!
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | **Accrual Basis vs. Cash Basis**, the **Matching Principle**, **Cut-Off Date**, and why year-end adjustments are required for a **True and Fair** profit figure |
| **Exam Focus** | Explaining the difference between Cash Basis and Accrual Basis, identifying how ignoring accruals/prepayments distorts profit, and calculating true annual expense/revenue |
| **Learning Outcome** | Apply the Accrual and Matching concepts accurately to determine true revenue and expenses for any accounting period |

## 1. Accrual Concept vs. Matching Concept

Although they work as a team, know the sharp focus of each principle:

| Accounting Concept | Core Rule | Plain-English Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Accrual Concept** | Record **revenues when earned** and **expenses when incurred** — **NOT** simply when cash is received or paid. | *"Did the service happen THIS year? Then it belongs in THIS year’s books — cash timing comes second!"* |
| **Matching Concept** | Match all **expenses incurred** in generating revenue **against the revenue of the exact same accounting period**. | *"Don’t compare 12 months of Sales against only 10 months of Rent — match apples to apples!"* |

### Cash Basis vs. Accrual Basis at a Glance

| Feature | Cash Basis *(Unacceptable for True Accounts)* | Accrual &amp; Matching Basis *(Required Standard)* |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **When is Revenue recorded?** | Only when cash/cheque is actually received | When goods are delivered or service is performed (**earned**) |
| **When is Expense recorded?** | Only when cash/cheque is actually paid | When the benefit/service is used up (**incurred**) |
| **Unpaid year-end bills** | Ignored until paid next year | **Added** to this year’s expense &amp; shown as a **Current Liability** |
| **Advance payments for next year** | Dumped entirely into this year’s expense | **Subtracted** from this year’s expense &amp; shown as a **Current Asset** |
| **Profit Accuracy** | Misleading &amp; easily manipulated by delaying payments | **True &amp; fair view** of real financial performance |

### What Happens If You Forget an Adjustment? (Paper 1 Favorite!)

| Forgotten Year-End Adjustment | Effect on Expenses / Revenue | Effect on Net Profit | Effect on Balance Sheet |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Forgot Accrued Expense** *(bill owing)* | Expense **Understated** (too low) | Profit **Overstated** (too high!) | Current Liabilities **Understated** |
| **Forgot Prepaid Expense** *(paid ahead)* | Expense **Overstated** (too high) | Profit **Understated** (too low!) | Current Assets **Understated** |
| **Forgot Accrued Revenue** *(income owed)* | Revenue **Understated** (too low) | Profit **Understated** (too low!) | Current Assets **Understated** |
| **Forgot Unearned Revenue** *(cash in advance)* | Revenue **Overstated** (too high) | Profit **Overstated** (too high!) | Current Liabilities **Understated** |

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Comparing Cash Basis Profit vs. True Accrual & Matching Profit"
  marks={12}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> <strong>HarborView Printing</strong> closes its books on
        <strong> 31 December 2026</strong>. Its cash records and year-end notes show:
      </p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Cash Sales collected during 2026:</strong> $82,000.</li>
        <li><strong>Credit printing jobs completed in Dec 2026</strong> (customers will pay in Jan 2027): $14,000.</li>
        <li><strong>Advance cash deposit received on 22 Dec 2026</strong> for a February 2027 conference brochure: $3,500.</li>
        <li><strong>Wages paid in cash during 2026:</strong> $26,500; staff wages for the final week of Dec 2026 unpaid at year-end: $2,500.</li>
        <li><strong>Insurance paid on 1 July 2026</strong> covering the 12 months to 30 June 2027: $4,800.</li>
        <li><strong>Shop Rent paid in cash during 2026:</strong> $15,000. <em>(Note: This includes $1,000 paid in Jan 2026 for unpaid Dec 2025 rent, and December 2026 rent of $1,200 is still unpaid at 31 Dec 2026.)</em></li>
      </ul>
      <ol>
        <li>Calculate HarborView Printing’s profit under the <strong>Cash Basis</strong>. <em>[3 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Calculate the true <strong>2026 Revenue, 2026 Expenses, and True Net Profit</strong> under the <strong>Accrual &amp; Matching Basis</strong>. <em>[7 marks]</em></li>
        <li>List the <strong>Current Assets</strong> and <strong>Current Liabilities</strong> created by these year-end adjustments. <em>[2 marks]</em></li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

**1. Cash Basis Calculation (3 marks — Wrong for Financial Statements!):**
- Cash Receipts = `$82,000 Cash Sales + $3,500 Advance Deposit` = **$85,500** *(1 mark)*
- Cash Payments = `$26,500 Wages + $4,800 Insurance + $15,000 Rent` = **$46,300** *(1 mark)*
- Cash Basis Surplus = `$85,500 − $46,300` = **$39,200** *(1 mark)*

**2. True Accrual & Matching Basis for 2026 (7 marks):**
- **True 2026 Revenue:** `$82,000 Cash Sales + $14,000 Credit Sales earned in Dec` = **$96,000** *(Exclude the $3,500 deposit for Feb 2027!)* *(2 marks)*
- **True 2026 Expenses:**
  - `Wages` = `$26,500 paid + $2,500 Dec owing` = **$29,000** *(1 mark)*
  - `Insurance` = `$4,800 × (6 months: Jul–Dec ÷ 12 months)` = **$2,400** *(6 months = $2,400 prepaid for 2027!)* *(1.5 marks)*
  - `Shop Rent` = `$15,000 paid − $1,000 (belonged to 2025!) + $1,200 (Dec 2026 owing)` = **$15,200** *(1.5 marks)*
  - *Total True 2026 Expenses* = `$29,000 + $2,400 + $15,200` = **$46,600**
- **True 2026 Net Profit** = `$96,000 − $46,600` = **$49,400** *(1 mark)*
*(Notice how Cash Basis understated true profit by **$10,200**!)*

**3. Balance Sheet Items Created at 31 December 2026 (2 marks):**
- **Current Assets:** Trade Receivables / Accrued Revenue **$14,000**; Prepaid Insurance **$2,400** *(1 mark)*.
- **Current Liabilities:** Unearned Revenue (Customer Deposit) **$3,500**; Accrued Wages **$2,500**; Accrued Rent **$1,200** *(1 mark)*.

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Accrual Concept:** Recognize when **earned / incurred**, NOT when cash moves.
- **Matching Concept:** Pair this period’s expenses against this period’s revenues.
- **Golden Shortcut:** **Accrued (Owing)** → **ADD**; **Prepaid / Advance** → **SUBTRACT**!
:::
