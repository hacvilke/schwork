## 1. Capital Expenditure vs Revenue Expenditure

The May/June 2025 Subject Report noted that **many candidates still confuse Capital Expenditure and Revenue Expenditure** (both in sole trader Cash Books and in club accounts!). Whenever a business spends money, run it through this simple **Three-Question Test**:

1. *Does this buy a brand-new Fixed Asset lasting more than 1 year?* → **Capital Expenditure!**
2. *Is this an initial cost necessary to bring a new Fixed Asset into working condition (e.g. shipping, import duty, legal fees for land, machine installation)?* → **Capital Expenditure!**
3. *Does this permanently **upgrade** an existing Fixed Asset — increasing its earning capacity, size, or useful life beyond its original state?* → **Capital Expenditure!**
- If the answer to all three is **NO** (it merely maintains, repairs, fuels, or runs daily business operations) → **Revenue Expenditure!**

| Feature | **Capital Expenditure** | **Revenue Expenditure** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Purpose** | Acquiring, installing, or permanently improving **Fixed Assets** for long-term use (> 1 year). | Day-to-day running costs, routine servicing, repairs, and buying stock for resale (benefits current year only). |
| **Real Examples** | Buying premises, machinery, or a delivery van; **freight & installation** of a new oven; **legal stamp duty** on buying land; building a warehouse extension; **installing air conditioning** into an older van; painting a **brand-new** building for the first time. | Petrol/diesel; **oil changes, brake pads & tyre replacements**; repairing a broken printer; staff wages; JPS electricity; shop rent; insurance; **repainting** an existing shop every two years; buying stock for resale. |
| **Financial Statement** | **Balance Sheet** *(increases Fixed Assets — capitalised and depreciated over useful life)* | **Trading & Profit & Loss Account** *(deducted immediately as an Expense / Cost of Sales)* |
| **What If You Mix Them Up?** | **Capital treated as Revenue:** Expenses are **overstated** → **Net Profit is understated** AND **Fixed Assets are understated**! | **Revenue treated as Capital:** Expenses are **understated** → **Net Profit is overstated** AND **Fixed Assets are overstated**! |

*(Quick bonus — **Capital Receipts vs Revenue Receipts:** Cash received from selling an old Fixed Asset, taking a bank loan, or issuing shares is a **Capital Receipt** [Balance Sheet]; cash received from normal Sales, Rent Received, or Commission Received is a **Revenue Receipt** [Income Statement]!)*

## 2. Why Year-End Adjustments Are Required (Accrual & Matching)

Cash rarely lines up neatly with the calendar! Suppose your accounting year ends on **31 December 2025**:
- You used electricity throughout December, yet JPS won't send the bill until **10 January 2026**.
- You paid your annual shop insurance on **1 November 2025**, covering 12 months all the way to **31 October 2026** (10 months belong to *next* year!).

Under the **Accrual and Matching Concept**, your 2025 Profit & Loss Account MUST include **ONLY the exact 12 months of revenue earned in 2025** and **ONLY the exact 12 months of expenses incurred in 2025** — no more, no less!

### Master Cheat-Sheet: All Four Accrual & Prepayment Adjustments

| Adjustment Type | Plain-English Definition | Effect on **Profit & Loss Account** | Where on the **Balance Sheet** | Opening `b/d` Side on 1 Jan Next Year |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **1. Accrued Expense** *(Expense Owing / Due)* | Service already used this year, **not yet paid** at year-end. | **ADD** to expense paid *(belongs to this year!)* | **Current Liability** | **Credit `b/d`** |
| **2. Prepaid Expense** *(Expense Paid in Advance)* | Cash already paid for **next year's** benefit. | **SUBTRACT** from expense paid *(remove next year's slice!)* | **Current Asset** | **Debit `b/d`** |
| **3. Accrued Revenue** *(Income Receivable / Owing)* | Income already earned this year, **not yet received** (e.g. tenant owes Dec rent). | **ADD** to revenue received *(earned this year!)* | **Current Asset** | **Debit `b/d`** |
| **4. Prepaid Revenue** *(Income Received in Advance)* | Cash received early for **next year's** service (e.g. tenant paid Jan–Mar rent early). | **SUBTRACT** from revenue received *(not earned yet!)* | **Current Liability** | **Credit `b/d`** |

## 3. Handling Opening AND Closing Balances in the Same Year!

On Paper 2, businesses often have **BOTH** an opening accrual/prepayment from last year (`1 Jan`) **AND** a closing accrual/prepayment at year-end (`31 Dec`). Don't panic — follow this clean logic for **any Expense Account**:

$$\text{Expense for This Year (to P\&L)} = \text{Cash Paid This Year} - \text{Opening Accrual} + \text{Closing Accrual} + \text{Opening Prepayment} - \text{Closing Prepayment}$$

**Why does that make 100% logical sense?**
- **Subtract Opening Accrual** because part of the cash paid this year merely settled *last* year's unpaid bill!
- **Add Closing Accrual** because you still owe *this* year's final unpaid bill!
- **Add Opening Prepayment** because cash paid *last* year actually bought coverage used *this* year!
- **Subtract Closing Prepayment** because part of the cash paid this year buys coverage for *next* year!

*(And inside a T-account, you don't even have to memorise the formula — simply put Opening `b/d` on its true Asset/Liability side, enter Cash Paid on Debit, insert Closing `c/d` so Closing `b/d` lands on its true Asset/Liability side, and the exact **balancing figure on the Credit side** is automatically your **Profit & Loss transfer**!)*

## 4. Worked Example: Journal Entries & T-Accounts (Expenses AND Revenues!)

<WorkedExample
  title="Adjusting Accrued Electricity, Prepaid Insurance & Accrued Rent Receivable (31 Dec 2025)"
  marks={12}
  problem={
    <>
      During the year ended <strong>31 December 2025</strong>, Kingston Traders had the following balances and bank entries:
      <br />
      <strong>(a) Electricity:</strong> On 1 Jan 2025, Electricity Accrued b/d was <strong>J$4,000</strong>. During 2025, cheques paid to JPS totalled <strong>J$59,000</strong>. On 31 Dec 2025, Electricity owing (accrued) was <strong>J$5,000</strong>.
      <br />
      <strong>(b) Insurance:</strong> Paid <strong>J$28,000</strong> by cheque during 2025, which includes <strong>J$4,000</strong> prepaid for Jan–Feb 2026.
      <br />
      <strong>(c) Rent Received (Revenue):</strong> Sub-let a storeroom at <strong>J$10,000 per month</strong> (J$120,000/yr). Tenant paid <strong>J$100,000</strong> by cheque during 2025; two months’ rent (<strong>J$20,000</strong>) was still owing at 31 Dec 2025.
      <br />
      Show the Ledger T-Accounts and exact Profit &amp; Loss and Balance Sheet treatment for all three items.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Always check the side of your closing Balance b/d on 1 January next year! Current Asset (Prepaid Expense or Accrued Revenue) = DEBIT b/d; Current Liability (Accrued Expense or Prepaid Revenue) = CREDIT b/d!"
>

### (a) Electricity Account *(Opening AND Closing Accrual!)*
- Check: $\text{Paid } 59{,}000 - \text{Opening Accrued } 4{,}000 + \text{Closing Accrued } 5{,}000 = \mathbf{60{,}000}$ to P&L!

<TAccount
  title="Electricity Account (Expense — Opening & Closing Accruals)"
  debit={[
    { date: '2025', details: 'Bank (cheques paid)', amount: '59,000' },
    { date: 'Dec 31', details: 'Accrued c/d', amount: '5,000', isBalance: true },
  ]}
  credit={[
    { date: '2025 Jan 1', details: 'Accrued b/d (last year)', amount: '4,000', isBalance: true },
    { date: 'Dec 31', details: 'Profit & Loss (Expense)', amount: '60,000' },
  ]}
  total="64,000"
  creditBroughtDown={{ date: '2026 Jan 1', details: 'Accrued b/d', amount: '5,000', isBalance: true }}
  note="P&L Expense = J$60,000 | Balance Sheet Current Liability = J$5,000 (Credit b/d)."
/>

### (b) Insurance Account *(Closing Prepayment)*
- **Year-End Journal:** `Dr Prepaid Insurance J$4,000` / `Cr Insurance J$4,000`

<TAccount
  title="Insurance Account (Expense — with Year-End Prepayment)"
  debit={[
    { date: '2025', details: 'Bank (paid)', amount: '28,000' },
  ]}
  credit={[
    { date: 'Dec 31', details: 'Profit & Loss (Expense)', amount: '24,000' },
    { date: 'Dec 31', details: 'Prepaid c/d', amount: '4,000', isBalance: true },
  ]}
  total="28,000"
  debitBroughtDown={{ date: '2026 Jan 1', details: 'Prepaid b/d', amount: '4,000', isBalance: true }}
  note="P&L Expense = J$24,000 | Balance Sheet Current Asset = J$4,000 (Debit b/d)."
/>

### (c) Rent Received Account *(Revenue Account with Accrued Income!)*
- Remember: **Revenue** increases on the **CREDIT** side (`Cr Rent Received`, `Dr Bank` when tenant pays)!
- **Year-End Journal:** `Dr Accrued Rent Receivable J$20,000` / `Cr Rent Received J$20,000`

<TAccount
  title="Rent Received Account (Other Revenue — with Accrued Income)"
  debit={[
    { date: 'Dec 31', details: 'Profit & Loss (Other Revenue)', amount: '120,000' },
  ]}
  credit={[
    { date: '2025', details: 'Bank (received from tenant)', amount: '100,000' },
    { date: 'Dec 31', details: 'Accrued c/d (still owing)', amount: '20,000', isBalance: true },
  ]}
  total="120,000"
  debitBroughtDown={{ date: '2026 Jan 1', details: 'Accrued b/d (Receivable)', amount: '20,000', isBalance: true }}
  note="P&L Other Revenue (Add to GP) = J$120,000 (full 12 months earned!) | Balance Sheet Current Asset = J$20,000 (Debit b/d)."
/>

</WorkedExample>
