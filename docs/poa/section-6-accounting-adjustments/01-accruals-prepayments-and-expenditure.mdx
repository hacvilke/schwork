## 1. Capital Expenditure vs Revenue Expenditure (Kid-Friendly Rule!)

Whenever a business spends money, accountants ask one simple question: **"Does this build or upgrade a long-term Fixed Asset, or does it just keep the business running day to day?"**

| Feature | **Capital Expenditure** | **Revenue Expenditure** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Plain-English Meaning** | Spending to **buy, install, deliver, or permanently upgrade** a **Fixed Asset** that lasts **more than 1 year**. | Everyday spending to **run, maintain, or repair** the business and its assets during the **current year**. |
| **Examples** | Buying a delivery van; **delivery/installation costs** of a new machine; building a shop extension; **installing air conditioning** in a truck; legal fees to buy land. | Buying petrol/fuel; **oil changes & tyre repairs**; repairing a broken photocopier; staff wages; rent; repainting office walls; electricity bills. |
| **Where It Goes** | **Balance Sheet** *(increases Fixed Assets)* | **Profit & Loss Account** *(deducted as an Expense)* |
| **What If You Mix Them Up?** | Recording Capital Expenditure as Revenue Expenditure **overstates expenses** and **understates Net Profit & Fixed Assets**! | Recording Revenue Expenditure as Capital Expenditure **understates expenses** and **overstates Net Profit & Fixed Assets**! |

## 2. Why Year-End Adjustments Matter (Accrual / Matching Concept)

Under the **Accrual (Matching) Concept**, this year's **Trading and Profit & Loss Account** must match **every dollar of revenue earned this year** against **every dollar of expense actually used up this year** — whether cash has changed hands yet or not!

### The Four Adjustment Cheat-Sheet (Memorise This!)

| Adjustment Type | Plain-English Meaning | Effect on **Profit & Loss Account** | Where on the **Balance Sheet** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Accrued Expense** *(Expense Owing)* | Used the service this year, **haven't paid yet** (e.g. unpaid December JPS bill). | **ADD** to that expense *(we owe it for this year!)* | **Current Liability** *(credit balance)* |
| **2. Prepaid Expense** *(Paid in Advance)* | Already paid cash for **next year's** benefit (e.g. insurance covering next year). | **SUBTRACT** from that expense *(belongs to next year!)* | **Current Asset** *(debit balance)* |
| **3. Accrued Revenue** *(Income Owing)* | Earned the income this year, **haven't received cash yet** (e.g. tenant owes rent). | **ADD** to that revenue *(earned this year!)* | **Current Asset** *(debit balance)* |
| **4. Prepaid Revenue** *(Received in Advance)* | Customer paid us early for **next year's** service (e.g. rent received for next year). | **SUBTRACT** from that revenue *(not earned yet!)* | **Current Liability** *(credit balance)* |

## 3. Worked Example: Journal Entries & T-Accounts for Accruals & Prepayments

<WorkedExample
  title="Adjusting Electricity (Accrued) & Insurance (Prepaid) at 31 Dec 2025"
  marks={8}
  problem={
    <>
      During the year ended <strong>31 December 2025</strong>, a Jamaican shop paid:
      <br />
      (a) <strong>J$55,000</strong> by cheque for <strong>Electricity</strong>. On 31 December 2025, December’s electricity bill of <strong>J$5,000</strong> was still unpaid (accrued).
      <br />
      (b) <strong>J$28,000</strong> by cheque for <strong>Insurance</strong>, which includes <strong>J$4,000</strong> prepaid for January–February 2026.
      <br />
      Show the <strong>Electricity Account</strong>, the <strong>Insurance Account</strong>, and their treatment in the Final Accounts.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Check the side of Balance b/d on 1 January! Accrued Expense = Current Liability → Credit Balance b/d. Prepaid Expense = Current Asset → Debit Balance b/d!"
>

### (a) Electricity Account *(Accrued Expense)*
- **Journal Entry (Year-End):** `Dr Electricity J$5,000` / `Cr Accrued Electricity J$5,000`
- **Transfer to Profit & Loss Account:** `55,000 + 5,000 =` **J\$60,000** *(Expense in P&L)*
- **Balance Sheet (Current Liability):** Accrued Electricity = **J\$5,000**

<TAccount
  title="Electricity Account (Nominal — with Year-End Accrual)"
  debit={[
    { date: '2025', details: 'Bank (paid)', amount: '55,000' },
    { date: 'Dec 31', details: 'Accrued c/d', amount: '5,000', isBalance: true },
  ]}
  credit={[
    { date: 'Dec 31', details: 'Profit & Loss', amount: '60,000' },
  ]}
  total="60,000"
  creditBroughtDown={{ date: '2026 Jan 1', details: 'Accrued b/d', amount: '5,000', isBalance: true }}
  note="Credit Balance b/d of J$5,000 = Current Liability (Accrued Electricity) on the Balance Sheet!"
/>

### (b) Insurance Account *(Prepaid Expense)*
- **Journal Entry (Year-End):** `Dr Prepaid Insurance J$4,000` / `Cr Insurance J$4,000`
- **Transfer to Profit & Loss Account:** `28,000 − 4,000 =` **J\$24,000** *(Expense in P&L)*
- **Balance Sheet (Current Asset):** Prepaid Insurance = **J\$4,000**

<TAccount
  title="Insurance Account (Nominal — with Year-End Prepayment)"
  debit={[
    { date: '2025', details: 'Bank (paid)', amount: '28,000' },
  ]}
  credit={[
    { date: 'Dec 31', details: 'Profit & Loss', amount: '24,000' },
    { date: 'Dec 31', details: 'Prepaid c/d', amount: '4,000', isBalance: true },
  ]}
  total="28,000"
  debitBroughtDown={{ date: '2026 Jan 1', details: 'Prepaid b/d', amount: '4,000', isBalance: true }}
  note="Debit Balance b/d of J$4,000 = Current Asset (Prepaid Insurance) on the Balance Sheet!"
/>

</WorkedExample>
