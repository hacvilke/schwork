:::info Essential Understanding
Imagine a business spends **$18,000** building a brand-new warehouse extension — and a junior clerk accidentally records it under *"Repairs & Maintenance"* in the Income Statement! Instantly, **Net Profit is crushed by $18,000** AND **Premises on the Balance Sheet are understated by $18,000**! Distinguishing **Capital Expenditure** from **Revenue Expenditure** (and Capital vs. Revenue Receipts) is one of the fastest ways to earn guaranteed Paper 1 &amp; Paper 2 marks.
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | **Capital Expenditure** (`Balance Sheet — NCA`), **Revenue Expenditure** (`Income Statement — Expense`), **Capital Receipts**, and **Revenue Receipts** |
| **Exam Focus** | Classifying tricky real-world bills (e.g. **installation costs** &amp; **legal fees on purchase** = Capital; **repainting** &amp; **tires** = Revenue) and calculating the **exact effect of misclassification on Profit and Assets** |
| **Learning Outcome** | Classify expenditures and receipts accurately and calculate corrected Net Profit and Non-Current Assets when misclassifications occur |

## 1. Capital vs. Revenue Expenditure — Side-by-Side Comparison

| Feature | Capital Expenditure *(CapEx)* | Revenue Expenditure *(RevEx)* |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Purpose** | Acquiring, delivering, installing, or **permanently improving/extending** a Non-Current Asset | **Day-to-day running costs**, buying resale stock, or **normal maintenance/repairs** that merely keep an asset working |
| **Length of Benefit** | **Long-term** *(more than 1 accounting year)* | **Short-term** *(consumed within the current year)* |
| **Frequency** | **Non-recurring** *(occasional big investments)* | **Recurring** *(regular weekly/monthly/annual bills)* |
| **Financial Statement** | **Balance Sheet → Non-Current Assets** *(then depreciated gradually over useful life)* | **Income Statement → Cost of Sales or Operating Expenses** *(100% deducted this year)* |
| **Classic Tricky Examples** | • Legal fees &amp; stamp duty buying Premises<br/>• Delivery &amp; installation of a **new** machine<br/>• Painting a **brand-new** building for the first time<br/>• Installing a new AC unit or extending a warehouse | • Petrol, oil changes, servicing &amp; replacement tires<br/>• Annual vehicle insurance &amp; road license<br/>• **Re-painting** existing walls or replacing broken glass<br/>• Staff wages, electricity, rent &amp; Purchases for resale |

### Capital Receipts vs. Revenue Receipts
- **Capital Receipts** *(Balance Sheet — Non-recurring financing or asset disposal)*: Owner injecting Capital, taking a Long-Term Bank Loan, issuing Shares/Debentures, or **proceeds from selling an old Non-Current Asset**.
- **Revenue Receipts** *(Income Statement — Recurring trading/operating income)*: Cash &amp; Credit **Sales**, **Rent Received**, **Commission Received**, **Discount Received**, and **Interest Received**.

### Profit & Balance Sheet Distortion Rules (Exam Gold!)

| Classification Mistake | Effect on Operating Expenses | Effect on Net Profit | Effect on Non-Current Assets |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Capital Exp. wrongly put as Revenue Exp.** *(e.g. new van put in Repairs)* | **Overstated** *(too high)* | **Understated** *(too low — ADD back to fix!)* | **Understated** *(too low — ADD back to fix!)* |
| **Revenue Exp. wrongly put as Capital Exp.** *(e.g. petrol/repairs added to Van)* | **Understated** *(too low)* | **Overstated** *(too high — SUBTRACT to fix!)* | **Overstated** *(too high — SUBTRACT to fix!)* |

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Classifying Transactions & Correcting Draft Net Profit and Non-Current Assets"
  marks={12}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> During 2026, <strong>BlueBay Supermart</strong> completed the
        following six transactions:
      </p>
      <ol>
        <li>Purchased a new refrigerated display unit ($8,500) plus $500 electrical installation cost ($9,000 total).</li>
        <li>Paid $1,400 for annual delivery van insurance and road license.</li>
        <li>Built a new concrete storage room extension at the back of the shop ($22,000).</li>
        <li>Paid $1,800 to repaint existing shop walls and repair leaking roof tiles.</li>
        <li>Received $6,000 from selling an old, surplus delivery van.</li>
        <li>Received $1,200 monthly rental income from subletting unused parking spaces.</li>
      </ol>
      <p>
        <strong>Part A:</strong> Classify each item (1–6) as <em>Capital Expenditure</em>,
        <em>Revenue Expenditure</em>, <em>Capital Receipt</em>, or <em>Revenue Receipt</em>. <em>[6 marks]</em><br/>
        <strong>Part B:</strong> BlueBay’s draft accounts showed <strong>Net Profit = $34,000</strong> and
        <strong> Non-Current Assets = $95,000</strong> before discovering that Item 1 ($9,000) was
        wrongly debited to <em>Shop Repairs Expense</em> and Item 4 ($1,800) was wrongly debited to
        <em>Premises</em>. Calculate the <strong>Corrected Net Profit</strong> and
        <strong> Corrected Non-Current Assets</strong> (ignoring depreciation). <em>[6 marks]</em>
      </p>
    </>
  }
>

### Part A: Classification Table *(6 marks — 1 mark each)*

| # | Transaction | Classification | Reason |
| :-: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **1** | New refrigerated display unit ($8,500) + installation ($500) | **Capital Expenditure** | Acquires &amp; installs a new Non-Current Asset (`> 1 year`) |
| **2** | Annual van insurance &amp; road license ($1,400) | **Revenue Expenditure** | Recurring annual running cost |
| **3** | New concrete storage room extension ($22,000) | **Capital Expenditure** | Permanent structural addition increasing asset value |
| **4** | Repainting existing walls &amp; fixing roof leaks ($1,800) | **Revenue Expenditure** | Routine repair/maintenance restoring normal condition |
| **5** | Cash received from selling old van ($6,000) | **Capital Receipt** | Non-recurring proceeds from disposing of a fixed asset |
| **6** | Rental income from parking spaces ($1,200) | **Revenue Receipt** | Recurring operating income earned in the Income Statement |

### Part B: Corrected Net Profit & Corrected Non-Current Assets *(6 marks)*

| Adjustment Step | Effect on Net Profit ($) | Effect on Non-Current Assets ($) |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| **Draft Figure Reported** | **34,000** | **95,000** |
| **Fix Item 1:** Remove $9,000 Capital Exp. wrongly charged to *Shop Repairs* *(cuts Expenses → raises Profit &amp; adds Equipment!)* | `+9,000` | `+9,000` |
| **Fix Item 4:** Remove $1,800 Revenue Exp. wrongly capitalized in *Premises* *(adds Expense → lowers Profit &amp; trims Premises!)* | `(1,800)` | `(1,800)` |
| **Corrected Final Figure** | **$41,200** | **$102,200** |

*(Notice how both Net Profit and Non-Current Assets shift by the exact same net `+$7,200` — proving the accounting equation stays perfectly balanced!)*

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Capital Expenditure** → Buy/install/upgrade long-term asset → **Balance Sheet (NCA)**.
- **Revenue Expenditure** → Day-to-day running/repairs → **Income Statement (Expense)**.
- **CapEx put as RevEx** = Profit **understated**; **RevEx put as CapEx** = Profit **overstated**!
:::
