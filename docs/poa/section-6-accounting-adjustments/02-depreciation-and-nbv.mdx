## 1. What Is Depreciation? (Kid-Friendly Definition!)

Imagine buying a brand-new delivery van today for **J\$500,000**. After driving it across Jamaica for 3 years, is it still worth J\$500,000? **No!** It has lost value through daily use, age, and newer models coming out.

**Depreciation** is simply **the gradual loss in value of a Fixed Asset over its useful working life** — or, in accounting terms, **spreading the cost of a Fixed Asset fairly as an expense across the years it helps the business earn income** *(applying the Matching & Prudence concepts!)*.

*(Note: **Land** is the one Fixed Asset that normally does **NOT** depreciate because land does not wear out!)*

### Five Classic Causes of Depreciation (Exam Theory!)
1. **Wear and Tear (Physical Deterioration):** Daily use, rust, friction, and weather slowly wearing down vans, machinery, and furniture.
2. **Obsolescence:** Newer, faster technology makes old equipment outdated (e.g. an older office computer or point-of-sale machine).
3. **Passage of Time (Effluxion of Time):** Assets with fixed time limits (like a 10-year lease or patent) lose value every year even if untouched.
4. **Depletion:** Natural resources (bauxite mines, quarries, oil wells) lose value as minerals are extracted.
5. **Inadequacy:** The business grows so big that a small machine can no longer keep up with demand.

## 2. Two Depreciation Methods & Net Book Value (Hover or Tap Any Formula!)

<FormulaCard
  title="Depreciation & Net Book Value (NBV) Formulae"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Straight-Line charges the exact SAME dollar amount every year; Reducing Balance charges a percentage of the shrinking Net Book Value so depreciation is bigger in early years and smaller later!"
>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="1. Straight-Line Method (Equal Instalment)"
  short="Take the asset's original Cost, subtract what you expect to sell it for at the end (Disposal/Scrap Value), and divide evenly by the Number of Years it will last."
>

$$
\text{Annual Depreciation} = \frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Disposal (Scrap) Value}}{\text{Number of Years}}
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="2. Reducing Balance Method (Diminishing Balance)"
  short="Multiply the given percentage by the current Net Book Value (what's left after previous years' depreciation) — so each year's charge gets smaller!"
>

$$
\text{Annual Depreciation} = \text{Rate (\%)} \times \text{Net Book Value at Start of Year}
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="3. Net Book Value (NBV)"
  short="What the Fixed Asset is worth on the Balance Sheet today: Original Cost minus ALL depreciation charged so far (Provision for Depreciation)."
>

$$
\text{Net Book Value (NBV)} = \text{Cost} - \text{Provision for Depreciation (Accumulated)}
$$

</FormulaHint>

</FormulaCard>

:::tip Why Do Businesses Pick One Method Over the Other?
- **Straight-Line** suits assets that wear evenly every year (buildings, furniture, fixtures).
- **Reducing Balance** suits assets that lose value quickly when new and need more repairs as they age (motor vehicles, computers, machinery) — high depreciation + low repairs early on balances low depreciation + higher repairs later!
- Remember **Consistency**: once a business picks a method, it should stick with it year after year!
:::

## 4. Worked Example 2: Reducing Balance Method (Step-by-Step!)

<WorkedExample
  title="Reducing Balance Method — 20% per Annum for 3 Years"
  marks={6}
  problem={
    <>
      On <strong>1 January 2024</strong>, a bakery buys an industrial oven (Machinery) for <strong>J$200,000</strong>.
      It depreciates machinery at <strong>20% per annum using the Reducing Balance Method</strong>.
      <br />
      Calculate the annual depreciation charge, accumulated provision, and Net Book Value for <strong>2024, 2025, and 2026</strong>.
    </>
  }
  examTip="With Reducing Balance, ALWAYS take the percentage of the NEW reduced Net Book Value — NEVER go back to original Cost in Year 2 or Year 3!"
>

- **Year 1 (2024):**
  - Depreciation = $20\% \times 200{,}000 =$ **J\$40,000**
  - New NBV = $200{,}000 - 40{,}000 =$ **J\$160,000**
- **Year 2 (2025):** *(Take 20% of the NEW reduced value J\$160,000!)*
  - Depreciation = $20\% \times 160{,}000 =$ **J\$32,000**
  - Accumulated Provision = $40{,}000 + 32{,}000 =$ **J\$72,000**
  - New NBV = $160{,}000 - 32{,}000 =$ **J\$128,000**
- **Year 3 (2026):** *(Take 20% of J\$128,000!)*
  - Depreciation = $20\% \times 128{,}000 =$ **J\$25,600**
  - Accumulated Provision = $72{,}000 + 25{,}600 =$ **J\$97,600**
  - New NBV = $128{,}000 - 25{,}600 =$ **J\$102,400** *(Check: $200{,}000 - 97{,}600 = 102{,}400$ ✓)*

| Year | Cost (J\$) | Annual Depreciation to P&L (J\$) | Accumulated Provision (J\$) | Balance Sheet NBV (J\$) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **2024** | 200,000 | 40,000 | 40,000 | 160,000 |
| **2025** | 200,000 | 32,000 | 72,000 | 128,000 |
| **2026** | 200,000 | 25,600 | 97,600 | **102,400** |

</WorkedExample>

## 5. Practice Checks

<QuickCheck
  paper="Paper 1"
  marks={1}
  question="Office equipment cost J$155,000, has a useful life of 5 years, and a disposal value of J$20,000. Using the Straight-Line Method, what is the Net Book Value at the END of Year 3?"
  options={[
    'J$27,000',
    'J$74,000',
    'J$81,000',
    'J$101,000',
  ]}
  correctIndex={1}
  explanation="Annual depreciation = (155,000 − 20,000) ÷ 5 = J$27,000/yr. After 3 years, accumulated depreciation = 3 × 27,000 = J$81,000. Net Book Value = 155,000 − 81,000 = J$74,000!"
/>

<QuickCheck
  paper="Paper 1"
  marks={1}
  question="A motor vehicle costing J$100,000 is depreciated at 10% per annum using the Reducing Balance Method. What is its Net Book Value at the end of Year 2?"

    'J$81,000',
    'J$89,000',

  ]}

  explanation="Year 1 dep = 10% × 100,000 = 10,000 → NBV = 90,000. Year 2 dep = 10% × 90,000 = 9,000 → NBV = 90,000 − 9,000 = J$81,000!"
