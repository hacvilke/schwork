:::info Essential Understanding
Every business — from a single-person juice stall to a multinational airline — tracks Assets, Liabilities, Revenue, and Expenses. What **really changes** in accounting across a **Sole Trader**, a **Partnership**, and a **Corporation (Limited Liability Company)** is the **Ownership / Equity section**, **how profits are shared**, **who is legally liable for debts**, and **which statutory statements are required**.
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | Comparing **Sole Traders**, **Partnerships**, and **Corporations (LLCs)** from an **accounting** perspective |
| **Exam Focus** | Contrasting Capital/Equity accounts, Profit Distribution (Appropriation / Dividends), Liability, and Audit/Disclosure rules |
| **Learning Outcome** | Identify the legal type of a business simply by inspecting its Equity section and Financial Statements |

## 1. Master Accounting Comparison Table

| Accounting Feature | Sole Trader | Partnership | Corporation (Limited Liability Company) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ownership** | **1 owner** | **2 to 20 partners** (governed by a Partnership Deed or Partnership Act) | **Shareholders** (Private Ltd or Public PLC; can have thousands of owners) |
| **Separate Legal Entity?** | **No** (legally the owner and business are one person — though still separated in the books!) | **No** (ordinary partnerships are not separate legal persons) | **Yes** — the company can own property, sign contracts, and be sued in its own name |
| **Liability for Debts** | **Unlimited** — personal assets (house, car) can be seized to pay business debts | **Unlimited** (for general partners; *limited partners* exist in special cases) | **Limited** — shareholders only risk losing the money invested in their shares |
| **Equity / Capital Accounts** | One **Capital A/c** and one **Drawings A/c** | Separate **Capital A/c**, **Current A/c**, and **Drawings A/c** for EACH partner | **Share Capital** (Ordinary & Preference shares) + **Reserves** (Retained Earnings, General Reserve) |
| **How Net Profit Is Shared** | **100%** added directly to the owner’s Capital (less Drawings) | Divided via a **Profit & Loss Appropriation Account** (partner salaries, interest on capital, profit-sharing ratio) | Corporation tax deducted first; remaining profit paid as **Dividends** or kept as **Retained Earnings** |
| **Financial Statements & Audit** | Income Statement & Balance Sheet (private; no mandatory statutory audit) | Income Statement, **Appropriation Account**, & Balance Sheet (private) | Full statutory Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Statement of Changes in Equity & Cash Flows; **public companies must publish audited accounts** |

:::note Two Bonus Structures Covered Later in Section 9
Later in **Section 9**, you will also meet two special organizations:
- **Co-operatives:** Owned democratically by members ("one member, one vote"); surpluses are shared as *patronage refunds* and reserves.
- **Non-Profit Organizations (Clubs & Societies):** Exist to serve members/causes rather than earn profit; they prepare a **Receipts & Payments Account**, an **Income & Expenditure Account** (showing *Surplus/Deficit*), and track **Accumulated Fund** instead of Capital!
:::

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Identifying Business Types & Comparing Accounting Features"
  marks={9}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> You are shown short extracts from the year-end Equity sections
        of three different enterprises:
      </p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Firm X:</strong> Issued Ordinary Share Capital $200,000; General Reserve $45,000; Retained Earnings $32,000; Proposed Dividends $15,000.</li>
        <li><strong>Firm Y:</strong> Opening Capital $28,000; Add Net Profit $14,500; Less Drawings $6,000; Closing Capital $36,500.</li>
        <li><strong>Firm Z:</strong> Capital Accounts — Maya $40,000, Devon $40,000; Current Accounts — Maya $6,200, Devon $4,800; Appropriation A/c profit split 1:1.</li>
      </ul>
      <ol>
        <li>Identify the <strong>type of business organization</strong> for Firm X, Firm Y, and Firm Z. <em>[3 marks]</em></li>
        <li>State <strong>two accounting clues</strong> from Firm X’s extract that prove your answer. <em>[2 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Compare <strong>Firm Y</strong> and <strong>Firm X</strong> on: (a) liability for business debts and (b) legal requirement to publish audited financial statements. <em>[4 marks]</em></li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

**1. Business Organization Types (3 marks — 1 mark each):**
- **Firm X:** **Corporation / Limited Liability Company**
- **Firm Y:** **Sole Trader**
- **Firm Z:** **Partnership**

**2. Two Accounting Clues Proving Firm X Is a Limited Company (2 marks — any 2):**
- It raises equity through **Issued Ordinary Share Capital** *(1 mark)*.
- It distributes profits through **Dividends** and retains profit in **General Reserve / Retained Earnings** rather than using Drawings *(1 mark)*.

**3. Comparison Between Firm Y (Sole Trader) and Firm X (Limited Company) (4 marks):**
- **(a) Liability for Debts (2 marks):** Firm Y’s owner faces **unlimited liability** — personal assets can be claimed to settle unpaid business debts *(1 mark)*, whereas Firm X’s shareholders enjoy **limited liability**, risking only the capital invested in their shares *(1 mark)*.
- **(b) Publication & Audit Rules (2 marks):** Firm Y’s statements are **private** with no statutory audit requirement *(1 mark)*, whereas Firm X must file statutory financial statements and (if publicly traded) **publish audited annual reports** for shareholders and regulators *(1 mark)*.

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Sole Trader** → 1 Capital A/c, Drawings, unlimited liability, private reports.
- **Partnership** → Capital + Current A/cs per partner, **Appropriation Account**, unlimited liability (ordinary partners).
- **Corporation (LLC)** → **Share Capital**, Reserves, **Dividends**, limited liability, separate legal entity, statutory filings/audits.
:::
