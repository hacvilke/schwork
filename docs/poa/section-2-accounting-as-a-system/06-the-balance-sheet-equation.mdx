:::info Essential Understanding
Every single Balance Sheet in the world rests on one unbreakable mathematical law: **Assets = Capital + Liabilities**. Why? Because **every resource a business owns (Assets)** had to be paid for by **someone** — either put in by the **owner (Capital)** or borrowed from **outsiders (Liabilities)**. No matter how many transactions occur, both sides of this scale MUST stay perfectly equal!
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | Defining **Assets, Liabilities, and Capital**, rearranging `A = C + L`, and proving how every transaction keeps the equation balanced |
| **Exam Focus** | Solving missing-figure tables (`A = C + L`, `C = A − L`, `L = A − C`) and tracing how multi-step transactions alter all three elements |
| **Learning Outcome** | Classify items accurately as Assets, Liabilities, or Capital and compute updated totals after any series of business transactions |

## 1. The Core Elements & Formula Sheet

| Element | Simple Definition | Common Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Assets (A)** | Economic resources **owned or controlled** by the business that bring future value | Premises, Machinery, Motor Vehicles, Inventory (Stock), Trade Receivables (Debtors), Bank, Cash |
| **Capital / Equity (C)** | Resources **supplied by the owner** (the owner’s residual claim on net assets) | Owner’s starting cash/equipment + Net Profit − Drawings |
| **Liabilities (L)** | Debts or obligations **owed to outsiders** (creditors and lenders) | Bank Loans, Mortgages, Trade Payables (Creditors), Bank Overdraft, Accrued Expenses |

<FormulaCard
  title="The Accounting (Balance Sheet) Equation"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Resources owned by the business always equal owner’s investment plus outside debts."
>

$$
\text{Assets} = \text{Capital} + \text{Liabilities}
$$

**Rearrangements (Net Worth & Debt):**

$$
\text{Capital} = \text{Assets} - \text{Liabilities} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \text{Liabilities} = \text{Assets} - \text{Capital}
$$

**Expanded Equation (Showing Profit & Drawings):**

$$
\text{Assets} = \text{Opening Capital} + \text{Revenue} - \text{Expenses} - \text{Drawings} + \text{Liabilities}
$$

</FormulaCard>

      </p>

        <li>Bought styling equipment worth $3,500 on credit from SalonPro.</li>

**Part A — Finding Missing Figures (4 marks — 1 mark each):**
1. **Alpha Traders:** Assets = Capital + Liabilities = $48,500 + $21,500 = **$70,000**
2. **Beta Boutique:** Capital = Assets − Liabilities = $94,000 − $31,200 = **$62,800**
3. **Gamma Repairs:** Liabilities = Assets − Capital = $62,400 − $45,000 = **$17,400**
4. **Delta Designs:** Capital = Assets − Liabilities = $115,000 − $42,500 = **$72,500**

**Part B — Step-by-Step Transaction Effect Table (6 marks — 2 marks per transaction):**

| Step | Transaction | Assets ($) | Capital ($) | Liabilities ($) | Check (`A = C + L`) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Start** | Opening Balances | **40,000** | **30,000** | **10,000** | 40,000 = 40,000 ✓ |
| **1.** | Bought equipment $3,500 on credit | **+3,500** (Equipment ↑) | No Change | **+3,500** (Payables ↑) | 43,500 = 43,500 ✓ |
| **2.** | Paid $1,500 cash to trade payable | **−1,500** (Cash ↓) | No Change | **−1,500** (Payables ↓) | 42,000 = 42,000 ✓ |
| **3.** | Received $2,000 cash from debtor | **+2,000 Cash / −2,000 Receivables = $0 net** | No Change | No Change | 42,000 = 42,000 ✓ |
| **End** | **Final Totals** | **$42,000** | **$30,000** | **$12,000** | **$42,000 = $30,000 + $12,000 ✓** |

*(Look at Transaction 3 — examiners LOVE testing receiving cash from a credit customer! Because Cash goes up $2,000 and Trade Receivables goes down $2,000, Total Assets stay exactly $42,000!)*

## 5. True or False Check

<TrueFalseQuiz
  title="True or False: The Balance Sheet Equation"
  items={[
    {
      statement: 'If a business has Total Assets of $85,000 and Total Liabilities of $25,000, then Capital must equal $110,000.',
      answer: false,
      explanation: 'Capital = Assets − Liabilities = $85,000 − $25,000 = $60,000 (not $85,000 + $25,000!).',
    },
    {
      statement: 'Collecting $1,000 cash from a credit customer (Trade Receivable) leaves Total Assets unchanged.',
      answer: true,
      explanation: 'Cash increases by $1,000 while Trade Receivables decrease by $1,000 — an equal swap within Assets that leaves Total Assets unchanged.',
    },
    {
      statement: 'Buying $5,000 of inventory on credit increases both Total Assets and Total Liabilities by $5,000.',
      answer: true,
      explanation: 'Inventory (Asset) rises by $5,000 and Trade Payables (Liability) rises by $5,000, keeping A = C + L balanced.',
    },
    {
      statement: 'A business bank overdraft is classified as a Current Asset because it involves a bank account.',
      answer: false,
      explanation: 'A bank overdraft means you owe money TO the bank, making it a Current Liability (not an Asset!).',
    },
    {
      statement: 'When a business earns a Net Profit, Owner’s Capital increases.',
      answer: true,
      explanation: 'Net Profit belongs to the owner and increases Capital (while Net Losses and Drawings decrease Capital).',
    },
  ]}
/>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- Memorize all 3 rearrangements: **`A = C + L`**, **`C = A − L`**, **`L = A − C`**.
- Watch out for the **two classic exam traps**:
  1. **Asset Swaps** (buying equipment for cash, collecting cash from debtors) → **Total Assets = No Change!**
  2. **Bank Overdraft** → **Liability**, NOT an Asset!
:::
