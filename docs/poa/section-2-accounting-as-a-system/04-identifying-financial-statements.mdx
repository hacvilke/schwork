:::info Essential Understanding
At the end of every accounting period, hundreds of daily ledger entries are distilled into a concise dashboard of **Financial Statements**. Think of the three main statements like a health checkup: the **Income Statement** is a *movie* showing profit earned over the year, the **Balance Sheet** is a *photograph* showing wealth and debts on one exact date, and the **Cash Flow Statement** tracks the actual *cash pulse* moving in and out.
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | Purpose, heading format, key components, and interconnections of the **Income Statement**, **Balance Sheet**, and **Statement of Cash Flows** |
| **Exam Focus** | Knowing WHICH financial statement answers a specific user question + exact wording of period vs. date headings |
| **Learning Outcome** | Identify all primary financial statements, explain what each measures, and trace how they link together |

## 1. The Three Core Financial Statements at a Glance

| Financial Statement | Formal / Modern Name | Time Heading Rule | Core Question Answered | Main Building Blocks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Income Statement** | Statement of Profit or Loss (formerly *Trading & Profit & Loss A/c*) | **"For the year ended 31 December 20XX"** *(a time period)* | *Did the business earn a profit or make a loss during the period?* | Sales Revenue, Cost of Goods Sold, Gross Profit, Other Income, Operating Expenses, **Net Profit / (Loss)** |
| **2. Balance Sheet** | Statement of Financial Position | **"As at 31 December 20XX"** *(one single snapshot date)* | *What does the business own, owe, and what is the owner’s net worth right now?* | Non-Current Assets, Current Assets, Current Liabilities, Non-Current Liabilities, **Owner’s Equity (Capital)** |
| **3. Cash Flow Statement** | Statement of Cash Flows | **"For the year ended 31 December 20XX"** *(a time period)* | *Where did actual cash come from, where did it go, and how much cash is left?* | Cash Flows from **Operating**, **Investing**, and **Financing** Activities → **Closing Cash Balance** |

*(Larger companies also publish a **Statement of Changes in Equity** explaining movements in share capital, reserves, and dividends across the year.)*

### Crucial Insight: Profit ≠ Cash!
Students often ask: *"If we already have an Income Statement showing Net Profit, why do we need a Cash Flow Statement?"*
- Imagine you sell $50,000 of goods **on 60-day credit** in December. Your **Income Statement** records $50,000 revenue right away (Accrual concept), making profit look great!
- Yet until customers actually pay, your **Cash Flow Statement** shows **$0 cash collected** — so you could still struggle to pay next Friday’s wages!
- That is why smart managers and banks read **all three statements together**.

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Routing Financial Questions & Items to the Right Statement"
  marks={10}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Part A (6 marks):</strong> For each account balance below, state whether it
        belongs on the <strong>Income Statement</strong> or the <strong>Balance Sheet</strong>:
      </p>
      <ol>
        <li>Motor Vehicles ($24,000)</li>
        <li>Sales Revenue ($95,000)</li>
        <li>Trade Payables ($8,200)</li>
        <li>Staff Wages Expense ($18,500)</li>
        <li>Electricity Expense ($2,100)</li>
        <li>Bank Loan repayable in 5 years ($15,000)</li>
      </ol>
      <p>
        <strong>Part B (4 marks):</strong> A bank manager reviewing Summit Hardware’s accounts
        asks two questions: (i) <em>"Can the store generate enough cash this year to pay monthly loan
        installments?"</em> and (ii) <em>"What exact title wording should appear on the top of Summit’s
        Balance Sheet dated 31 December 2026?"</em> Answer both questions clearly.
      </p>
    </>
  }
>

**Part A — Classifying Items by Financial Statement (6 marks — 1 mark each):**
1. **Motor Vehicles ($24,000):** **Balance Sheet** *(Non-Current Asset)*
2. **Sales Revenue ($95,000):** **Income Statement** *(Revenue)*
3. **Trade Payables ($8,200):** **Balance Sheet** *(Current Liability)*
4. **Staff Wages Expense ($18,500):** **Income Statement** *(Operating Expense)*
5. **Electricity Expense ($2,100):** **Income Statement** *(Operating Expense)*
6. **Bank Loan repayable in 5 years ($15,000):** **Balance Sheet** *(Non-Current Liability)*

**Part B — Answering the Bank Manager (4 marks):**
- **(i) Which statement answers cash repayment ability? (2 marks):** The **Statement of Cash Flows** *(1 mark)* — because it tracks actual cash generated from Operating Activities (after paying suppliers and wages) rather than accrual-based paper profit *(1 mark)*.
- **(ii) Exact Balance Sheet heading wording (2 marks):**
  > **Summit Hardware**
  > **Balance Sheet as at 31 December 2026**
  *(1 mark for business name + statement title; 1 mark for using **"as at 31 December 2026"** rather than "for the year ended")*.

</WorkedExample>

---

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Income Statement** = *Movie of performance* → **"For the year ended ..."** → Revenue, COGS, Expenses → **Net Profit**.
- **Balance Sheet** = *Snapshot of position* → **"As at ..."** → Assets, Liabilities, Capital.
- **Cash Flow Statement** = *Actual cash movement* → **"For the year ended ..."** → Operating, Investing, Financing → **Closing Cash**.
:::
