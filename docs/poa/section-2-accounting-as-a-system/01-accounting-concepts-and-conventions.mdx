:::info Essential Understanding
If every accountant invented their own rules for when to count sales or how to value buildings, financial statements would be impossible to compare or trust. **Accounting concepts and conventions** are the universal ground rules (part of Generally Accepted Accounting Principles, or **GAAP**) that keep every set of accounts consistent, realistic, and comparable.
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | The core rules of accounting: **Business Entity, Going Concern, Money Measurement, Historical Cost, Dual Aspect, Accrual/Matching, Prudence, Consistency, Materiality** |
| **Exam Focus** | Matching a real business transaction to the exact accounting concept that governs it — and explaining WHY |
| **Learning Outcome** | Define each major concept/convention and apply it correctly to everyday business transactions |

## 1. The Nine Core Accounting Concepts & Conventions

Study this master reference table carefully — these nine rules reappear in **every single section** of Principles of Accounts:

| Concept / Convention | Plain-English Rule | Everyday Business Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Business Entity** | The business is treated as completely separate from its owner(s) for accounting purposes | When the owner uses business cash to buy personal groceries, it is recorded as **Drawings**, NOT a business expense |
| **2. Going Concern** | Assume the business will continue operating for the foreseeable future (not shutting down tomorrow) | Machinery is valued at cost less depreciation over its useful life rather than quick-sale scrap value |
| **3. Money Measurement** | Only transactions and events that can be objectively measured in money are recorded in the books | A delivery van ($28,000) is recorded; staff loyalty or the owner’s good reputation is NOT entered in the ledger |
| **4. Historical Cost** | Assets are recorded initially at their actual original purchase price shown on the invoice | Land bought 6 years ago for $50,000 stays recorded at $50,000 even if a friend guesses it might fetch $80,000 today |
| **5. Dual Aspect** | Every transaction has **two equal and opposite effects** (Debit = Credit) | Buying a $3,000 laptop with cash increases Equipment (+$3,000) and decreases Cash (−$3,000) |
| **6. Accrual (Matching)** | Record revenues when **earned** and expenses when **incurred**, matching them to the same period regardless of when cash moves | December’s unpaid electricity bill ($420) is included in December’s expenses even though cash is paid in January |
| **7. Prudence (Conservatism)** | Never overstate profits or assets, and never understate losses or liabilities; anticipate expected losses early, never unrealized gains | Creating a **Provision for Doubtful Debts** and valuing inventory at the **lower** of cost or net realizable value |
| **8. Consistency** | Use the same accounting methods from year to year so financial statements can be compared fairly | Keeping the same 10% straight-line depreciation policy each year instead of switching randomly to boost profit |
| **9. Materiality** | Apply strict capitalization rules to significant amounts, while tiny inexpensive items can be written off immediately as expenses | A $4 stapler lasting 5 years is expensed immediately as Stationery rather than depreciated over 5 years |

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Matching Business Transactions to Accounting Concepts"
  marks={10}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Question:</strong> State the specific <strong>accounting concept or convention</strong>
        illustrated by each of the five independent situations below, and give a <strong>brief
        1-sentence reason</strong> for each choice. <em>[2 marks each = 10 marks]</em>
      </p>
      <ol>
        <li>A supermarket buys a $9 calculator that will last four years, but immediately writes the full $9 off as Stationery Expense.</li>
        <li>A store owner pays her home electricity bill out of the business bank account and instructs the clerk to debit Drawings.</li>
        <li>A furniture retailer creates a 5% Provision for Doubtful Debts at year-end because some credit customers may not pay.</li>
        <li>A bakery’s head chef wins a national culinary award, but no dollar amount for "chef talent" is entered into the Balance Sheet.</li>
        <li>A hardware store includes $850 of December warehouse rent in this year’s Income Statement even though the landlord will be paid on January 5.</li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

1. **$9 calculator written off immediately (2 marks):**
   - *Concept:* **Materiality** *(1 mark)*.
   - *Reason:* Because $9 is too small to affect users’ decisions, expensing it immediately saves unnecessary depreciation tracking *(1 mark)*.

2. **Home electricity bill debited to Drawings (2 marks):**
   - *Concept:* **Business Entity** *(1 mark)*.
   - *Reason:* The owner’s personal household expenses must be kept strictly separate from business operating expenses *(1 mark)*.

3. **Creating a 5% Provision for Doubtful Debts (2 marks):**
   - *Concept:* **Prudence (Conservatism)** *(1 mark)*.
   - *Reason:* Foreseeable credit losses are recognized immediately so Net Profit and Trade Receivables are not overstated *(1 mark)*.

4. **Award-winning chef’s skill omitted from the Balance Sheet (2 marks):**
   - *Concept:* **Money Measurement** *(1 mark)*.
   - *Reason:* Accounting records only items that can be objectively quantified in monetary terms, not qualitative traits like skill or morale *(1 mark)*.

5. **Including unpaid December rent in this year’s expenses (2 marks):**
   - *Concept:* **Accrual (Matching)** *(1 mark)*.
   - *Reason:* Expenses must be recorded in the period the benefit was used (December), regardless of when cash is actually paid *(1 mark)*.

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Accrual** = *right time period* (earned/incurred, not just cash paid).
- **Prudence** = *stay cautious* (record expected losses early, never count unmade gains).
- **Consistency** = *same method across years* (for fair comparisons).
- **Materiality** = *don't over-complicate tiny dollar amounts* (like a $5 stapler).
:::
