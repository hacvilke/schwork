:::info Essential Understanding
Accounting is not a random pile of paperwork done once a year — it is an **ordered, repeating loop** called the **Accounting Cycle**. Every single business transaction follows the same exact pathway from a raw **source document** (like a sales invoice) through journals, ledgers, a trial balance, and year-end adjustments, all the way to the **financial statements** — and then loops back to start the next financial year!
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | The **6-stage repeating sequence** of the accounting cycle from source documents to final financial statements |
| **Exam Focus** | Placing stages in the **exact chronological order** and knowing the purpose of each step |
| **Learning Outcome** | Trace any business transaction cleanly through every stage of the accounting cycle |

## 1. The Six Core Stages of the Accounting Cycle

Notice how the accounting cycle is literally the **blueprint of the entire Principles of Accounts course**:

| Stage | What Happens Here? | Key Records Created | Where You Study It Next |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Source Documents** | Business transactions occur and generate verifiable written or digital proof | Sales invoices, purchase invoices, receipts, credit notes, cheque counterfoils | Section 3 (Topic 3.2) |
| **2. Books of Original Entry** | Transactions are first recorded chronologically by type in specialized day books/journals | Sales Journal, Purchases Journal, Returns Books, Cash Book, Petty Cash Book, General Journal | Section 3 (Topics 3.1–3.8) |
| **3. Posting to Ledgers** | Entries from day books are classified and posted into individual double-entry T-accounts | General Ledger, Sales Ledger (receivables), Purchases Ledger (payables) | Section 4 (Topics 4.1–4.6) |
| **4. Trial Balance** | At period-end, all ledger balances are listed in Debit and Credit columns to test arithmetical accuracy | Unadjusted Trial Balance (Total Debits = Total Credits) | Section 4 (Topics 4.7–4.8) |
| **5. Year-End Adjustments** | Accounts are updated for accruals, prepayments, bad/doubtful debts, and depreciation | Adjusting Journal Entries & Adjusted Trial Balance | Section 6 (Topics 6.1–6.7) |
| **6. Financial Statements & Closing** | Final reports are prepared, temporary income/expense accounts are closed to profit, and permanent balances carry forward (`b/d`) to Year 2 | Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Post-Closing Balances | Section 5 (Topics 5.1–5.7) |

:::note Why Is It Called a "Cycle"?
At the end of Stage 6, **temporary accounts** (Sales, Purchases, Rent, Wages) are closed to zero to calculate profit for the year, while **permanent accounts** (Cash, Buildings, Equipment, Payables, Capital) carry their closing balances (`c/d`) forward as the **opening balances (`b/d`)** of the brand-new period — restarting the loop smoothly!
:::

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Ordering & Explaining Jumbled Stages of the Accounting Cycle"
  marks={8}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Question:</strong> A new accounting intern wrote down the steps of the accounting
        cycle on sticky notes, but dropped them on the floor in the wrong order:
      </p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>A.</strong> Prepare the Income Statement and Balance Sheet</li>
        <li><strong>B.</strong> Post transactions to the General and Subsidiary Ledgers</li>
        <li><strong>C.</strong> Collect and verify Sales Invoices and Receipts</li>
        <li><strong>D.</strong> Extract a Trial Balance to test arithmetical accuracy</li>
        <li><strong>E.</strong> Record daily entries into Books of Original Entry</li>
        <li><strong>F.</strong> Make year-end adjustments for accruals and depreciation</li>
      </ul>
      <ol>
        <li>Arrange letters <strong>A to F</strong> in the <strong>exact chronological order</strong> of the accounting cycle. <em>[3 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Explain why <strong>Step E</strong> must happen before <strong>Step B</strong>. <em>[2 marks]</em></li>
        <li>State <strong>one reason</strong> why <strong>Step D</strong> (Trial Balance) is prepared before <strong>Step A</strong> (Financial Statements). <em>[3 marks]</em></li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

**1. Exact Chronological Order (3 marks — ½ mark per step in position):**
- **C → E → B → D → F → A**
  1. **C** — Collect and verify Source Documents
  2. **E** — Record daily entries into Books of Original Entry
  3. **B** — Post transactions to the Ledgers
  4. **D** — Extract a Trial Balance
  5. **F** — Make year-end adjustments
  6. **A** — Prepare the Income Statement and Balance Sheet

**2. Why Books of Original Entry (E) come before Ledgers (B) (2 marks):**
- Books of Original Entry provide a **chronological first record** grouped by transaction type (credit sales, credit purchases, cash) *(1 mark)*. Summarizing totals in day books first prevents cluttering the General Ledger with hundreds of individual daily invoices *(1 mark)*.

**3. Why the Trial Balance (D) is prepared before Financial Statements (A) (3 marks):**
- The Trial Balance verifies that **total Debit balances equal total Credit balances**, catching arithmetical or one-sided posting errors early *(1 mark)*.
- It also gathers **every account balance onto one single summary sheet** *(1 mark)*, making it much faster and cleaner to draft accurate Income Statements and Balance Sheets without flipping through every ledger page *(1 mark)*.

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- Memorize the 6-letter chain **S-B-L-T-A-F**: **S**ource documents → **B**ooks of original entry → **L**edgers → **T**rial balance → **A**djustments → **F**inal statements.
- Always remember the loop: **Closing balances (`c/d`)** of Assets, Liabilities, and Capital become next year’s **Opening balances (`b/d`)**!
:::
