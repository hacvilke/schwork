## 1. Co-operative Societies (Member-Owned & Democratic!)

A **Co-operative Society** is a voluntary, self-help enterprise owned, democratically controlled, and patronised by its own **members** for their mutual benefit — rather than maximising dividends for outside shareholders!

### Common Types of Co-operatives in Jamaica & the Caribbean
- **Financial Co-operatives (Credit Unions):** e.g. *COK Sodality*, *JTA Co-op Credit Union*, *EduCom* — members save deposits and borrow at fair interest rates.
- **Agricultural / Marketing Co-operatives:** Farmers pool harvests (e.g. coffee, bananas, cocoa) to buy bulk fertilisers cheaply and negotiate stronger export prices.
- **Consumer Co-operatives:** Member-owned grocery/retail stores selling essentials at fair prices.
- **Producer / Workers' Co-operatives:** Craft makers, fishermen, or workers jointly own boats, workshops, or machinery.

### Five Classic Co-operative Principles (High-Yield Exam Theory!)
1. **Voluntary and Open Membership:** Anyone who fits the common bond (e.g. teachers, parish residents, farmers) may join or leave freely without discrimination.
2. **Democratic Member Control ("One Member, One Vote"):** At the Annual General Meeting, every member gets **exactly ONE vote** — whether they own 5 shares or 500 shares!
3. **Members Are Both Owners AND Customers:** Capital comes mainly from **members' shares, savings deposits, and retained reserves**, plus bank/league loans.
4. **Limited Return on Capital & Fair Surplus Distribution:** Instead of paying huge shareholder profits, annual **Surplus** is distributed legally and fairly:
   - **Statutory Reserve Fund** *(mandatory % required by Co-operative law to protect members!)*
   - **Education Fund** *(training members, youth, and committee officers)*
   - **Honorarium** *(modest thank-you allowance to voluntary committee officers)*
   - **Interest / Dividend on Members' Shares** *(capped % on share capital)*
   - **Patronage Refund (Rebate / Bonus)** *(returned to members in proportion to how much business/purchases they did with the co-operative!)*
5. **Limited Liability:** Registered co-operatives are separate legal entities protecting members' personal assets.

## 3. Non-Profit Organisations (Clubs, Charities & Societies)

Sports clubs, youth clubs, churches, and charities exist to **serve members or the community**, NOT to generate commercial profit. They raise funds through **Members' Subscriptions**, **Donations**, **Entrance Fees**, **Fundraising Events**, and sometimes a small **Refreshment / Bar Trading** section.

### Sole Trader Terms → Non-Profit Terms Translation Table

| Sole Trader Term | **Non-Profit (Club) Equivalent** | Key Accounting Difference |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cash Book** | **Receipts and Payments Account** | Cash-basis summary of **ALL cash & bank inflows (Dr)** and **ALL cash & bank outflows (Cr)** — includes **BOTH Capital AND Revenue cash items**! |
| **Profit & Loss Account** | **Income and Expenditure Account** | Accrual-basis summary of **REVENUE income** and **REVENUE expenses** for **THIS year ONLY** — **excludes Capital Expenditure** (like buying new equipment) and includes non-cash items like Depreciation! |
| **Net Profit / Net Loss** | **Surplus / Deficit** | **Surplus** = Income > Expenditure; **Deficit** = Expenditure > Income. |
| **Owner's Capital** | **Accumulated Fund** | The club's net worth: $\text{Accumulated Fund} = \text{Club Assets} - \text{Club Liabilities}$! |

### Receipts & Payments Account vs Income & Expenditure Account (Exam Favourite!)

| Feature | **Receipts & Payments Account** | **Income & Expenditure Account** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Basis** | **Cash Basis** (actual cash/cheques received & paid) | **Accrual Basis** (matched to the current year only!) |
| **Opening & Closing Balance** | Starts with Opening Cash/Bank `b/d` and ends with Closing Cash/Bank `c/d` (`Dr` = positive cash, `Cr` = Bank Overdraft!) | Has NO opening cash balance — ends with **Surplus** or **Deficit**! |
| **Capital Items (e.g. Buying Equipment)** | **INCLUDED** (because cash left the bank!) | **EXCLUDED** (goes to Fixed Assets on Balance Sheet; only *Depreciation* enters Income & Expenditure!) |
| **Accruals, Prepayments & Depreciation** | **EXCLUDED** (no cash moved!) | **INCLUDED**! |

*(Syllabus Note: On the Revised Syllabus, Paper 2 focuses directly on preparing a neat **Receipts and Payments Account** — including spotting an opening/closing Bank Overdraft and distinguishing Capital vs Revenue expenditure, as tested directly on May/June 2025 Paper 02 Q5!)*

### (b) Capital vs Revenue Expenditure
- **Capital Expenditure:** **New Sports Equipment (J\$28,000)** — acquires a long-term Fixed Asset used across multiple years. It appears in Receipts & Payments (cash was paid), but is **EXCLUDED** from an Income & Expenditure Account and recorded instead under Fixed Assets on the Balance Sheet!
- **Revenue Expenditure:** Bar Purchases (J\$24,000), Clubhouse Rent (J\$30,000), Secretary's Expenses (J\$12,000), and Competition Prizes (J\$8,000) — recurring annual running expenses.
