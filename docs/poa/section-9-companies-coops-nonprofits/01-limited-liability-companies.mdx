## 1. Raising Capital: Shares (Equity) vs Debentures (Debt)

Unlike a sole trader or partnership, a **Limited Liability Company** is a **separate legal entity** owned by **shareholders** with **limited liability** and **perpetual succession** (continuous life even if owners change).

Companies raise long-term money in two main ways — **Equity** (selling ownership shares) and **Debt** (borrowing via debentures or bank loans):

| Feature | **Ordinary Shares** *(Equity)* | **Preference Shares** *(Equity)* | **Debentures** *(Long-Term Debt!)* |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Who are they?** | True **owners** (voting shareholders) | Owners with **priority** dividend rights | **Long-term lenders / creditors** (NOT owners!) |
| **Voting Rights?** | **Yes** (vote at AGM) | Usually **No** | **No** |
| **Return Paid** | **Variable Dividend** (only if profit allows, paid *after* preference shares) | **Fixed % Dividend** (e.g. *8% Preference Shares*, paid *before* ordinary shares) | **Fixed % Interest** (e.g. *10% Debentures* — **MUST be paid even if the company makes a loss!**) |
| **Where Return Goes** | **Appropriation Account** *(deducted from profit available)* | **Appropriation Account** *(deducted from profit available)* | **Profit & Loss Account** *(Operating/Finance **Expense** — NEVER in Appropriation Account!)* |
| **Where on Balance Sheet** | **Shareholders' Equity** | **Shareholders' Equity** | **Long-Term Liabilities** |

*(Note: **Cumulative Preference Shares** accumulate any unpaid dividend into future years if profits are too low in one year! Also distinguish **Authorised Share Capital** — the maximum shares the company is legally allowed to sell — from **Issued Share Capital**, the shares actually sold and paid for!)*

## 2. Journal Entries to Issue Shares & Debentures

When a company issues shares and debentures for cash/cheque, simply **Debit Bank (or Cash)** for the total money received and **Credit** each capital/debenture account!

**Example:** Island Fresh Foods Ltd issues `200,000` Ordinary Shares @ J\$2 (`J\$400,000`), `50,000` 8% Preference Shares @ J\$2 (`J\$100,000`), and `J\$100,000` of 10% Debentures for cash:

| Date | Details | Dr (J\$) | Cr (J\$) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Jan 1 | Bank / Cash | 600,000 | |
| | &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Ordinary Share Capital | | 400,000 |
| | &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;8% Preference Share Capital | | 100,000 |
| | &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;10% Debentures | | 100,000 |
| | *(Being issue of ordinary shares, 8% preference shares, and 10% debentures for cash)* | | |

## 3. Examiner Alerts on Company Appropriation Accounts!

Recent exam reports highlight **four costly mistakes** — avoid ALL four and earn full marks:
1. **Debenture Interest** is an **EXPENSE in the Profit & Loss Account** (and any unpaid debenture interest at year-end is a **Current Liability** on the Balance Sheet!) — **NEVER** put Debenture Interest in the Appropriation Account!
2. **Dividends** (both **interim/paid** AND **proposed**) belong in the **Appropriation Account** — **NEVER** put dividends as an expense in the Income Statement!
3. **Transfer to General Reserve:** Put ONLY **THIS YEAR'S transfer** (e.g. `J\$30,000`) in the Appropriation Account — then **ADD** it to the old General Reserve (`50,000 + 30,000 = 80,000`) on the Balance Sheet! Do NOT put the old reserve in the Appropriation Account!
4. **Proposed Dividends** not yet paid at year-end appear under **Current Liabilities** on the Balance Sheet!
