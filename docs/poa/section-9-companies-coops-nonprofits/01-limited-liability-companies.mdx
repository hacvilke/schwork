## 1. Features, Advantages & Disadvantages of Limited Liability Companies

A **Limited Liability Company (Corporation)** is an incorporated business that exists as a **separate legal entity** completely distinct from its owners (**shareholders**), managed by a **Board of Directors** elected at the Annual General Meeting (AGM).
- **Private Limited Company (`Ltd`):** Shares are held privately (e.g. family or close associates) and **cannot** be sold publicly on a stock exchange.
- **Public Limited Company (`PLC` / Listed):** Invites the general public to buy shares on a stock exchange (e.g. **Jamaica Stock Exchange — JSE**) and must publish audited financial statements annually.

| Key Advantages of a Limited Company | Key Disadvantages of a Limited Company |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Limited Liability:** Shareholders can lose *only* the money invested in shares — personal homes and savings are legally protected. | **Higher Formation Costs & Legal Red Tape:** Must register legal documents (Articles of Incorporation) and file annual statutory returns. |
| **Huge Capital Raising:** Can sell thousands/millions of shares and issue debentures to fund large projects. | **Loss of Privacy (`PLC`):** Must publish audited Income Statements and Balance Sheets openly (rivals can inspect them!). |
| **Perpetual Succession (Continuity):** The company survives indefinitely even if shareholders die or sell their shares. | **Separation of Ownership & Control:** Ordinary shareholders own the company, yet hired Directors run it daily (and profits are taxed as Corporation Tax before dividends). |

## 2. Equity vs Debt: Ordinary Shares, Preference Shares & Debentures

Companies raise long-term funds in **two main ways**: **Equity Capital** (selling ownership shares) and **Debt Capital** (borrowing via debentures or bank loans). Memorise the rights and accounting rules for all three:

| Feature | **Ordinary Shares** *(Equity)* | **Preference Shares** *(Equity)* | **Debentures** *(Long-Term Debt!)* |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Status of Holder** | True **owners** (risk-takers) | Owners with **priority** dividend & repayment rights | **Long-term creditors / lenders** (NOT owners!) |
| **Voting Rights at AGM** | **Yes** *(1 share = 1 vote)* | Usually **No** | **No** |
| **Return Paid** | **Variable Dividend** — depends on profit; paid **LAST** (after preference shares) | **Fixed % Dividend** (e.g. *8% Preference Shares*) — paid **BEFORE** ordinary shares | **Fixed % Interest** (e.g. *10% Debentures*) — **MUST be paid even if the company makes a LOSS!** |
| **Income Statement vs Appropriation** | **Appropriation Account** *(deducted from profit available)* | **Appropriation Account** *(deducted from profit available)* | **Profit & Loss Account** *(Finance **Expense** — NEVER in Appropriation Account!)* |
| **Balance Sheet Section** | **Shareholders' Equity** | **Shareholders' Equity** | **Long-Term Liabilities** |

### Crucial Share Capital Vocabulary (Paper 1 & 2!)
- **Authorised (Registered) Share Capital:** The **maximum** value of shares the company is legally permitted to issue *(shown as a memo note only — NOT added into Balance Sheet totals!)*.
- **Issued & Paid-Up Share Capital:** The shares **actually sold and paid for** by shareholders *(this is the figure added into Shareholders' Equity!)*.
- **Cumulative vs Non-Cumulative Preference Shares:** If a company earns zero profit in Year 1 and skips dividends, **Cumulative** preference shareholders are owed Year 1's missed dividend in arrears in Year 2 *before* ordinary shareholders get a cent! **Non-cumulative** holders lose that skipped year permanently.
- **Reserves (Part of Shareholders' Equity!):** Undistributed profits kept inside the company to fund future growth or protect against hard times — specifically **Retained Earnings** (*Unappropriated Profits*) and **General Reserve** (*Revenue Reserves*).

## 3. Journal Entries to Issue Shares & Debentures + Dividend Maths

When a company issues shares and debentures for cash/cheque, **Debit Bank** with total cash raised and **Credit** each share/debenture account!

**Example:** Island Fresh Foods Ltd issues `200,000` Ordinary Shares @ J\$2 (`J\$400,000`), `50,000` 8% Preference Shares @ J\$2 (`J\$100,000`), and `J\$100,000` of 10% Debentures:

| Date | Details | Dr (J\$) | Cr (J\$) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Jan 1 | Bank | 600,000 | |
| | &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Ordinary Share Capital | | 400,000 |
| | &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;8% Preference Share Capital | | 100,000 |
| | &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;10% Debentures | | 100,000 |
| | *(Being issue of ordinary shares, 8% preference shares, and 10% debentures for cash)* | | |

### Two Ways Exams State Dividends (Never Get Caught!)
1. **As a Percentage (`%`) of Issued Share Capital Value:**
   - e.g. *8% Preference Dividend* on `50,000 shares @ J$2 (= J$100,000)` → $8\% \times 100{,}000 =$ **J\$8,000**!
2. **As Cents / Dollars PER SHARE:**
   - e.g. *Ordinary Dividend of J\$0.20 per share* on `200,000 Ordinary Shares` → $200{,}000 \times 0.20 =$ **J\$40,000**!

## 4. Five Critical Examiner Warnings (From the May/June 2025 Subject Report!)

Company final accounts were **Question 2 & Question 3 on May/June 2025 Paper 02** — and candidates lost huge marks on these exact five points:

1. **Debenture Interest Belongs in P&L — AND Check for Accrued Debenture Interest!**
   - Suppose a company has **J\$40,000 of 10% Debentures** (annual interest = **J\$4,000**), yet the Trial Balance Debit column only shows `Debenture Interest Paid = J$200`!
   - You MUST charge the **FULL J\$4,000** as an Expense in the **Profit & Loss Account**, AND put the unpaid **J\$3,800 (`4,000 − 200`)** under **Current Liabilities** (`Accrued Debenture Interest`) on the Balance Sheet!
2. **NEVER Put Dividends as Expenses in the Income Statement!** Dividends (both *Interim/Paid* and *Proposed*) belong **strictly in the Appropriation Account**!
3. **Include BOTH Paid AND Proposed Dividends in the Appropriation Account!** Don't leave out proposed dividends! And remember: **ONLY Proposed (unpaid) Dividends** appear under **Current Liabilities** on the Balance Sheet (already-paid interim dividends left Bank during the year!).
4. **Transfer to General Reserve:** Put ONLY **THIS YEAR'S transfer** (e.g. `J\$30,000`) inside the Appropriation Account (as a **deduction** from profits available) — NEVER put the old General Reserve balance (`J\$50,000`) inside the Appropriation Account! On the Balance Sheet, show the new combined General Reserve (`50,000 + 30,000 = J$80,000`).
5. **NEVER Add Retained Earnings `b/f` Twice** and **NEVER put Revenue or Expenses into the Appropriation Account!**
