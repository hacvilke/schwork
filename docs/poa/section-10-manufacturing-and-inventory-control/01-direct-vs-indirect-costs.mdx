:::info Essential Understanding
Up to now, every sole trader, partnership, and company we studied was a **merchandising business** — buying finished goods and reselling them. A **manufacturing business** (a bakery, furniture workshop, garment factory, or bottling plant) *creates* its own products from raw materials! Before preparing a Manufacturing Account, you must sort every business cost cleanly into **three exact buckets**: **1. Direct Costs *(Prime Cost)***, **2. Indirect Factory Costs *(Factory Overheads)***, and **3. Non-Manufacturing Expenses *(Office, Selling & Finance — Income Statement ONLY!)***.
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | **Direct Costs** (`Direct Materials + Direct Labor + Direct Expenses = Prime Cost`), **Indirect Factory Overheads**, **Non-Manufacturing Costs**, and **Shared Cost Apportionment** |
| **Exam Focus** | Instantly distinguishing **Direct vs. Indirect Labor**, separating **Carriage Inwards on Raw Materials** *(Direct!)* from **Carriage Outwards** *(Selling Expense!)*, and splitting shared bills *(e.g. `75%` Factory / `25%` Office)* |
| **Learning Outcome** | Classify manufacturing and non-manufacturing costs accurately and apportion shared expenses between the factory and the office |

## 1. The Three Cost Buckets of Every Manufacturing Business

### Bucket 1: Direct Costs (`= PRIME COST`)
A **Direct Cost** can be **physically and economically traced directly to each individual unit** produced!
1. **Direct Materials:** The main raw ingredients becoming part of the finished product *(e.g., timber in chairs, flour in bread, fabric in shirts)* — **PLUS Carriage Inwards on Raw Materials**!
2. **Direct Labor *(Manufacturing / Productive Wages)*:** Wages of workers who **hands-on build, assemble, bake, or sew** the product *(e.g., carpenters, machine operators, bakers)*.
3. **Direct Expenses:** Any special cost incurred directly for a specific product or unit *(e.g., **Royalties** paid per unit patented/manufactured, or hiring a special cutting mold for one custom job)*.

$$\text{Prime Cost} = \text{Direct Materials Consumed} + \text{Direct Labor} + \text{Direct Expenses}$$

### Bucket 2: Indirect Factory Costs (`= FACTORY OVERHEADS`)
A **Factory Overhead** is a genuine **production cost inside the factory**, yet it **cannot be traced cleanly to one single unit**:
- **Indirect Materials:** Machine lubricating oil, glue, nails, sandpaper, factory cleaning cloths.
- **Indirect Labor:** Factory supervisor/foreman salary, factory cleaner, maintenance engineer, factory night security guard.
- **Indirect Factory Expenses:** Factory rent &amp; rates, factory power/electricity, repairs to plant, **Depreciation of Factory Machinery &amp; Factory Buildings**.

### Bucket 3: Non-Manufacturing Expenses *(NEVER in Manufacturing Account!)*
Costs incurred **OUTSIDE the factory floor** go strictly to the **Income Statement (Profit &amp; Loss section)**:
- **Administration:** Office salaries, office rent, stationery, audit fees, **Depreciation of Office Equipment**.
- **Selling &amp; Distribution:** Advertising, sales commissions, **Carriage Outwards**, **Depreciation of Delivery Vans**.
- **Finance:** Bank loan interest, debenture interest, discounts allowed, bad debts.

### 2. Indirect Factory Costs (`= Factory Overheads`) *(4 marks)*
| Cost Item | Sub-Class | $ |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Factory Supervisor’s Salary | Indirect Labor | 14,000 |

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Prime Cost** = `Direct Materials (+ Carriage Inwards) + Direct Labor + Direct Expenses (Royalties)`.
- **Factory Overheads** = Indirect factory costs (`Supervisor, Lubricants, Plant Depr., Factory Rent/Power`).
- **Non-Factory Costs** (`Office, Selling, Carriage Outwards, Van Depr., Finance`) → **Income Statement ONLY**!
:::
