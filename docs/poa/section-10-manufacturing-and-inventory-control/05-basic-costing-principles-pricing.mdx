:::info Essential Understanding
When a customer walks into a print shop, custom furniture workshop, or bakery and asks for a **price quote BEFORE the job is even built**, how does the owner avoid quoting too low and losing money? By combining **Absorption Costing** *(computing an **Overhead Absorption Rate — OAR** so every custom job carries its fair slice of factory rent, electricity, and machinery depreciation!)* with **Cost-Plus Pricing** *(adding a target **Profit Mark-Up** or **Margin** onto Full Cost to set the Quoted Selling Price)*!
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | **Absorption Costing** (`OAR = Budgeted Factory Overheads ÷ Budgeted Labor/Machine Hours`), **Job / Batch Costing**, and **Cost-Plus Pricing** (`Mark-Up % on Cost` vs. `Margin % on Selling Price`) |
| **Exam Focus** | Computing **OAR** accurately, absorbing overheads into a specific custom job, never confusing **Mark-Up** with **Margin**, and evaluating **pros &amp; cons of Cost-Plus Pricing** |
| **Learning Outcome** | Apply absorption costing and cost-plus pricing principles to calculate full job costs and quote profitable selling prices |

## 1. Absorption Costing & Cost-Plus Formulas

<FormulaCard
  title="OAR, Mark-Up & Margin Formulas (Exam Essentials!)"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Check carefully whether the profit % is quoted on COST (Mark-Up) or on SELLING PRICE (Margin)!"
>

$$
\textbf{Overhead Absorption Rate (OAR)} = \frac{\text{Budgeted Factory Overheads}}{\text{Budgeted Direct Labor Hours (or Machine Hours)}}
$$

$$
\textbf{Cost-Plus Selling Price (Mark-Up on Cost)} = \text{Total Cost} \times \left(1 + \text{Mark-Up \%}\right)
$$

$$
\textbf{Selling Price From Margin (\% of Sales)} = \frac{\text{Total Cost}}{1 - \text{Margin \%}}
$$

</FormulaCard>

### Mark-Up vs. Margin Quick Conversion Check!
Suppose **Total Job Cost = `$4,000`**:
- **With `25%` MARK-UP *(25% of Cost)*:** Profit = `25% × $4,000 = $1,000` → **Selling Price = `$5,000`**! *(Notice: `$1,000 ÷ $5,000 = 20%` Margin!)*
- **With `25%` MARGIN *(25% of Selling Price)*:** Cost (`$4,000`) equals **`75%`** of Selling Price → **Selling Price = `$4,000 ÷ 0.75 = $5,333.33`** *(Profit = `$1,333.33`!)*
- **Key Takeaway:** For the exact same percentage (`25%`), a **Margin ALWAYS produces a HIGHER selling price** than a **Mark-Up**!

### Advantages &amp; Limitations of Cost-Plus Pricing
- **Advantages:** Simple and fast to calculate; guarantees that ALL direct costs AND fixed overheads are recovered when budgeted volume is met; feels transparent and fair to clients on custom jobs.
- **Limitations:** **Ignores market demand &amp; competitor prices** *(if your quote is `$5,000` and a rival quotes `$4,400`, you lose the order!)*; gives **weak incentive to cut factory waste** *(higher cost simply gets marked up into a higher price!)*.

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Job Costing, Overhead Absorption Rate & Mark-Up vs. Margin Quote (Kingston Custom Cabinetry)"
  marks={12}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> <strong>Kingston Custom Cabinetry</strong> budgets annual
        <strong> Factory Overheads of $60,000</strong> and <strong>4,000 Direct Labor Hours</strong>
        for 2026. A corporate client requests a quotation for <strong>Job #408 (10 custom mahogany
        conference desks)</strong> requiring:
      </p>
      <ul>
        <li>Direct Materials (mahogany &amp; hardware): <strong>$1,600</strong></li>
        <li>Direct Labor: <strong>80 Direct Labor Hours at $12.50 per hour</strong></li>
        <li>Direct Expense (custom brass logo engraving): <strong>$200</strong></li>
      </ul>
      <ol>
        <li>Calculate the workshop’s <strong>Overhead Absorption Rate (OAR)</strong> per Direct Labor Hour. <em>[2 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Prepare the <strong>Job Cost Sheet for Job #408</strong> showing Prime Cost, Absorbed Factory Overheads, Total Full Cost, and Full Cost per desk. <em>[5 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Calculate the Quoted Selling Price (total and per desk) under: (a) a <strong>25% Mark-Up on Cost</strong>; and (b) a <strong>25% Profit Margin on Selling Price</strong>! <em>[5 marks]</em></li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

### 1. Overhead Absorption Rate (OAR) *(2 marks)*
$$\text{OAR} = \frac{\text{Budgeted Factory Overheads}}{\text{Budgeted Direct Labor Hours}} = \frac{\$60,000}{4,000\text{ hrs}} = \mathbf{\$15.00\text{ per Direct Labor Hour}}$$

### 2. Job Cost Sheet — Job #408 *(10 Custom Conference Desks — 5 marks)*
| Cost Element | Working | $ |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Direct Materials | Given | 1,600 |
| Direct Labor | `80 hrs × $12.50/hr` | 1,000 |
| Direct Expense *(Logo Engraving)* | Given | 200 |
| **PRIME COST** | | **2,800** |
| **Add: Absorbed Factory Overheads** | `80 hrs × $15.00 OAR` | **1,200** |
| **TOTAL FULL PRODUCTION COST** | | **$4,000** |
| **Full Production Cost per Desk** | `$4,000 ÷ 10 desks` | **$400.00** |

### 3. Quoted Selling Price: Mark-Up vs. Margin *(5 marks)*
- **(a) Using `25%` Mark-Up on Cost *(2.5 marks)*:**
  - Profit = `25% × $4,000` = **`$1,000`**
  - **Total Quote** = `$4,000 + $1,000` = **`$5,000.00`** (**`$500.00` per desk**!)
- **(b) Using `25%` Profit Margin on Selling Price *(2.5 marks)*:**
  - Since Profit = `25%` of Selling Price, Total Cost (`$4,000`) = **`75%`** of Selling Price!
  - **Total Quote** = `$4,000 ÷ 0.75` = **`$5,333.33`** (**`$533.33` per desk**; Profit = `$1,333.33` → Check: `$1,333.33 ÷ $5,333.33 = 25%` ✓!).

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **OAR** = `Budgeted Factory Overheads ÷ Budgeted Labor (or Machine) Hours`.
- **Full Job Cost** = `Direct Materials + Direct Labor + Direct Expenses + (Job Hours × OAR)`.
- **Mark-Up** = `% × Cost`; **Margin** = `Cost ÷ (1 − Margin %)`.
:::
