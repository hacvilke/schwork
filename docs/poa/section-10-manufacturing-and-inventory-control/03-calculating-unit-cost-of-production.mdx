:::info Essential Understanding
Knowing your factory spent **`$180,000`** this year is only half the story — you CANNOT price a pair of shoes or value unsold Closing Finished Goods until you divide by total good units completed to find the **Unit Cost of Production (`$180,000 ÷ 15,000 = $12.00/pair`)**! Even better, splitting Unit Cost into **Variable Direct Cost per unit** *(stays flat!)* and **Fixed Overhead per unit** *(shrinks as volume grows!)* reveals the hidden power of **economies of scale**!
:::

## 1. The Unit Cost Formula & Three Real-World Uses

<FormulaCard
  title="Unit Cost of Production & Closing Stock Formula"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Always divide by GOOD UNITS PRODUCED (never units sold!)"
>

$$
\text{Unit Cost of Production} = \frac{\text{Total Cost of Production}}{\text{Total Number of Good Units Produced}}
$$

$$
\text{Closing Finished Goods Value} = \text{Unsold Units on Hand} \times \text{Unit Cost of Production}
$$

</FormulaCard>

### Why Divide by Units PRODUCED — Not Units SOLD?
- Suppose a factory **produced `15,000` pairs of shoes** for `$180,000`, and **sold `13,000` pairs** *(leaving `2,000` pairs in Closing Finished Goods)*.
- That `$180,000` built **ALL `15,000` pairs** (both the `13,000` sold AND the `2,000` sitting on shelves!) → True Unit Cost = `$180,000 ÷ 15,000` = **`$12.00/pair`**!
- Dividing by `13,000` units sold (`$13.85/pair`) would falsely pretend the `2,000` unsold pairs cost `$0` to make!

### Fixed vs. Variable Cost Behavior When Output Changes
- **Variable Direct Costs** *(Direct Materials + piece-rate Direct Labor)* rise in total as you make more units, so their **cost per unit stays CONSTANT** *(e.g. `$9.00/pair` at any volume)*.
- **Fixed Factory Overheads** *(Factory Rent, Supervisor Salary, Straight-Line Machinery Depreciation)* stay constant in total — so as production rises, **Fixed Overhead per unit FALLS** *(spreading the same `$45,000` rent over more pairs → **Economies of Scale**!)*!

### 1. 2026 Unit Cost Breakdown (`15,000` Good Pairs Produced) *(4 marks)*
| Cost Component | Total Cost ($) | ÷ 15,000 Pairs | Unit Cost ($/pair) |
| :--- | ---: | :---: | ---: |
| Raw Materials Consumed | 75,000 | ÷ 15,000 | $5.00 |
| Direct Labor | 60,000 | ÷ 15,000 | $4.00 |
| **Prime Cost per Pair** | **135,000** | ÷ 15,000 | **$9.00** |
| Factory Overheads *(Fixed)* | 45,000 | ÷ 15,000 | $3.00 |
| **Total Unit Cost of Production** | **$180,000** | ÷ 15,000 | **$12.00** |

### 2. Value of Closing Finished Goods Inventory *(2 marks)*
- Unsold completed pairs on hand: `2,000 pairs`
- Closing Finished Goods Valuation: `2,000 × $12.00` = **`$24,000`** *(note: COGS for 13,000 sold pairs = `$156,000`; `$156k + $24k = $180k` ✓!)*.

### 3. 2027 Expansion to `25,000` Pairs & Supplier Comparison *(6 marks)*
- **Variable Direct Costs (`25,000` pairs × `$9.00/pair`):** **`$225,000`** *(1.5 marks)*
- **Fixed Factory Overheads *(unchanged)*:** **`$45,000`** *(1 mark)*
- **Projected 2027 Total Cost of Production:** `$225,000 + $45,000` = **`$270,000`** *(1 mark)*
- **New 2027 Unit Cost of Production:** `$270,000 ÷ 25,000` = **`$10.80` per pair** *(Variable `$9.00` + Fixed `$1.80` — a **`$1.20/pair` drop** via economies of scale!)* *(1.5 marks)*
- **Comparison vs. `$11.50` Supplier Quote:** At `25,000` pairs, manufacturing in-house (`$10.80`) is **`$0.70/pair` cheaper** than buying externally (`$11.50`) — saving **`$17,500`** (`25,000 × $0.70`) while maintaining full quality control! *(1 mark)*

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Unit Cost** = `Total Cost of Production ÷ Good Units PRODUCED` *(NEVER divide by units sold!)*.
- **Closing Finished Goods** = `Unsold Completed Units × Unit Cost of Production`.
- **Higher Output** → Variable cost/unit stays **flat**; Fixed Overhead/unit **drops**!
:::
