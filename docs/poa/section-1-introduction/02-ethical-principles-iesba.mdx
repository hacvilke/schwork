## 1. What Is Ethics in Accounting & Why Does It Matter?

Accounting numbers hold enormous power. A single profit figure can determine whether a bank approves a J\$10 million loan, whether employees keep their jobs, how much tax goes toward building roads and hospitals, and whether investors lose their life savings.

**Ethics in accounting** refers to the moral rules, values, and professional standards that guide accountants and bookkeepers to act honestly, fairly, competently, and responsibly when recording, reporting, and advising on financial matters.

Even if an action is technically difficult to catch, ethics demands doing what is **right and truthful** — not just what pleases a boss or makes profit look good on paper.

### Who Sets the Global Code of Ethics? (IESBA)
Professional accounting bodies around the world — including the **Institute of Chartered Accountants of Jamaica (ICAJ)** and the **Institute of Chartered Accountants of the Caribbean (ICAC)** — adopt the global *International Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants* published by **IESBA**:

- **IESBA** stands for the **International Ethics Standards Board for Accountants**.
- Its Code rests on **five fundamental ethical principles** that apply to *every* accountant, whether working in a large audit firm, a small local shop, or a government ministry.

## 2. The Five IESBA Fundamental Ethical Principles (Detailed Guide)

Use the memory hook **I-O-P-C-P** so you can recall all five exact syllabus names cleanly under exam pressure:

### 1. Integrity
- **Formal Meaning:** To be straightforward and honest in all professional and business relationships.
- **In Plain English:** Always tell the truth in the books. Never fake invoices, hide cash sales, alter dates, or knowingly put your name on a misleading report — even if a senior manager pressures you.
- **What Good Practice Looks Like:** Recording every cash sale honestly on the day it occurs, even during a slow month when the owner wishes profit looked higher.
- **What a Breach Looks Like:** Creating fictitious "credit sales" in December to artificially inflate profit before applying for a bank loan (**window dressing / creative accounting**).

### 2. Objectivity
- **Formal Meaning:** Not to compromise professional or business judgement because of **bias, conflict of interest, or undue influence** of others.
- **In Plain English:** Keep your financial decisions fair, neutral, and fact-based. Never let personal friendships, family ties, bribes, gifts, or threats from a boss bend your judgement.
- **What Good Practice Looks Like:** Declaring a **conflict of interest** in writing and stepping off a purchasing committee because your sister's company submitted one of the bids.
- **What a Breach Looks Like:** Accepting a free hotel weekend or J\$50,000 "gift" from a supplier right before approving their overpriced supply contract.

### 3. Professional Competence and Due Care
- **Formal Meaning:** To attain and maintain professional knowledge and skill at the level required to ensure clients or employers receive competent service, and to act diligently in accordance with current technical and legal standards.
- **In Plain English:** Know your craft properly, keep learning when tax laws or accounting rules change, double-check your arithmetic, and **never accept work you are not qualified to perform** without proper supervision or specialist help.
- **What Good Practice Looks Like:** Attending annual ICAJ workshops on updated Jamaican statutory payroll rates (NIS, NHT, Education Tax, PAYE) and reconciling ledgers carefully before publishing final statements.
- **What a Breach Looks Like:** Filing a company's tax return using outdated tax rates from five years ago because you couldn't be bothered to look up the new rules, or rushing payroll calculations without checking overtime figures.

### 4. Confidentiality
- **Formal Meaning:** To respect the confidentiality of information acquired through professional and business relationships, and not disclose such information to third parties without proper authority — nor use it for personal advantage.
- **In Plain English:** Keep company and client financial secrets locked away. Never gossip about salaries, profits, debts, or upcoming business deals to friends, family, or competitors — and never use private info to make personal money (such as **insider trading**).
- **What Good Practice Looks Like:** Password-protecting payroll spreadsheets, locking physical ledger cabinets, and refusing to discuss a client's bank balance outside authorised meetings.
- **The Lawful Exceptions (Classic Exam Trap!):** An accountant **may or must** disclose confidential information ONLY when:
  1. The client or employer gives **explicit permission**; OR
  2. There is a **legal duty or court order** requiring disclosure (e.g. a court subpoena, a lawful Tax Administration Jamaica audit demand, or mandatory reporting of confirmed money laundering under anti-money-laundering law).

### 5. Professional Behaviour
- **Formal Meaning:** To comply with relevant laws and regulations and avoid any conduct that discredits the accounting profession.
- **In Plain English:** Obey the law, meet statutory deadlines, market your services truthfully, treat colleagues and clients respectfully, and never do anything that makes the public lose trust in accountants.
- **What Good Practice Looks Like:** Filing returns honestly on schedule, advertising qualifications truthfully, and maintaining polite, lawful standards both at work and in public.
- **What a Breach Looks Like:** Making false boasts to steal another accountant's clients, ignoring statutory filing laws, or engaging in personal tax fraud that brings the entire profession into disrepute.

## 3. Common Unethical Accounting Practices Tested on Exams

Examiners often describe a short real-life workplace problem and ask you to name the malpractice and the ethical principle(s) breached:

| Unethical Practice | What It Means in Plain English | Ethical Principles Breached |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tax Evasion** *(Illegal!)* | Deliberately hiding income, inventing fake expenses, or falsifying records to illegally pay less tax. *(Contrast with **Tax Avoidance / Tax Planning**, which uses lawful deductions allowed by tax law.)* | **Integrity** & **Professional Behaviour** |
| **Embezzlement / Misappropriation of Funds** | Stealing business cash or stock entrusted to your care and covering it up with false journal entries (e.g. "teeming and lading"). | **Integrity** & **Professional Behaviour** |
| **Window Dressing (Creative Accounting)** | Manipulating the timing of sales, expenses, or asset values so the Balance Sheet or Profit & Loss Account looks healthier than reality just before a loan application. | **Integrity** & **Objectivity** |
| **Conflict of Interest & Bribery** | Letting a personal relationship, financial stake, or secret kickback influence which supplier or contractor gets business. | **Objectivity** & **Integrity** |
| **Insider Trading / Data Leaks** | Tipping off a friend or relative to buy or sell company shares based on confidential profit figures not yet released to the public. | **Confidentiality**, **Integrity** & **Professional Behaviour** |
| **Negligence / Careless Reporting** | Producing sloppy, unchecked financial statements or applying rules you do not understand, misleading decision-makers. | **Professional Competence & Due Care** |

## 4. Consequences of Inappropriate Ethical Behaviour

When ethical standards are broken, the damage ripples across **three levels** — memorize specific points from each level for Paper 2 essay/structured questions!

### A. Consequences for the Accountant / Accounts Clerk
- **Loss of employment:** Immediate dismissal for gross misconduct and damaged career references.
- **Loss of professional status:** Heavy fines, suspension, or permanent expulsion from professional accounting bodies (losing the legal right to practise as a Chartered Accountant).
- **Legal punishment:** Civil lawsuits for negligence/damages, heavy court fines, and **imprisonment** for criminal fraud, embezzlement, or tax evasion.
- **Loss of personal reputation & integrity:** Once caught falsifying accounts, employers and banks will no longer trust the individual with financial responsibility.

### B. Consequences for the Business / Organisation
- **Financial penalties & back-taxes:** TAJ assesses steep interest, fines, and tax audits when records are falsified.
- **Flawed business decisions:** Overstated profit tricks owners into over-expanding or taking excessive drawings; understated profit may cause investors to pull out.
- **Loss of credit & investors:** Banks freeze loans or call in overdrafts; suppliers stop offering credit terms once financial dishonesty becomes public.
- **Reputational collapse & bankruptcy:** Customers boycott dishonest companies, lawsuits drain cash reserves, and the business may be forced to close.

### C. Consequences for Society & the Economy
- **Innocent workers lose jobs** when fraudulent companies collapse.
- **Reduced public services:** Tax evasion deprives the country of revenue needed for public schools, hospitals, roads, and policing.
- **Public distrust in business:** Major accounting scandals shake investor confidence across the entire stock market and banking system.

:::tip How to Handle Ethical Pressure on the Job (Exam Scenario Tip!)
If an exam story says *"Your manager orders you to leave out J\$150,000 of unpaid bills so the business qualifies for a loan — what should you do?"*, remember the proper ethical sequence:
1. **Refuse politely and explain the accounting & legal rule** (Integrity & Objectivity).
2. **Document your concerns** in writing.
3. **Escalate internally** to a higher authority (senior partner, audit committee, or board of directors) if the pressure continues.
4. **Seek confidential advice** from a professional body or legal adviser — and if the firm insists on fraud, **resign** rather than sign false accounts!
:::

## 5. Past-Paper-Style Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Analysing an Ethical Breach Scenario"
  marks={6}
  problem={
    <>
      Marsha is the accounts clerk at <strong>Suncoast Supplies</strong>. Her brother owns a delivery van
      company that submits a bid 30% higher than rival couriers. Marsha hides the cheaper bids from her
      manager so her brother wins the contract, and she also texts him Suncoast’s confidential budget cap.
      (a) Identify <strong>two</strong> IESBA ethical principles Marsha breached. (b) Explain{' '}
      <strong>one consequence</strong> of her actions for Suncoast Supplies and <strong>one</strong> for Marsha.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Name the EXACT IESBA principle first (Integrity, Objectivity, Confidentiality, Professional Competence & Due Care, or Professional Behaviour), then quote the specific action from the passage to prove why it was breached."
>

**(a) Two IESBA Ethical Principles Breached** *(2 marks each = 4 marks)*
1. **Objectivity** *(also breaches **Integrity**)*: Marsha allowed a family **conflict of interest** to bias her judgement and dishonestly concealed cheaper rival bids so her brother would win the contract.
2. **Confidentiality**: Marsha disclosed Suncoast Supplies' private internal budget cap to an outside bidder without authorisation for personal/family gain.

**(b) Consequences** *(1 mark each = 2 marks)*
- **For Suncoast Supplies:** The company overpays by 30% on courier services, unnecessarily inflating expenses and lowering Net Profit and cash flow.
- **For Marsha:** She faces summary dismissal for gross misconduct, loss of future accounting references, and potential legal action for breach of trust and fraud.

</WorkedExample>
