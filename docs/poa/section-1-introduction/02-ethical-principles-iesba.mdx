## 1. What Is Ethics in Accounting & Who Is IESBA?

**Ethics in accounting** refers to the moral rules and professional standards that guide accountants to act honestly, fairly, and responsibly when recording and reporting financial information.

Because investors, banks, workers, and governments rely heavily on financial statements, accountants worldwide follow the **IESBA Code of Ethics**:

- **IESBA** stands for the **International Ethics Standards Board for Accountants**.
- It publishes the global *International Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants* built around **five fundamental ethical principles** (adopted by accounting bodies worldwide, including in Jamaica and the wider Caribbean).

---

## 2. The 5 IESBA Fundamental Ethical Principles

Use the acronym **I-O-P-C-P** to remember all five principles cleanly in Paper 1 and Paper 2:

| Principle | Formal Definition | Plain-English Meaning | Workplace Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Integrity** | To be straightforward and honest in all professional and business relationships. | Tell the truth in the numbers — never lie, fake receipts, or hide transactions. | Refusing a manager's request to leave J$120,000 of cash sales out of the books to pay less tax. |
| **2. Objectivity** | Not to compromise professional or business judgement because of bias, conflict of interest, or undue influence of others. | Stay fair and unbiased — don't let gifts, family ties, or pressure bend your decisions. | Declaring a conflict of interest and stepping aside when your cousin's company bids for a supply contract. |
| **3. Professional Competence & Due Care** | To attain and maintain professional knowledge and skill at the level required, and act diligently in line with technical standards. | Know your work properly, double-check your figures, and stay updated on current laws and accounting rules. | Attending annual workshops on updated Jamaican tax rates and checking payroll calculations before issuing salaries. |
| **4. Confidentiality** | To respect the confidentiality of information acquired through professional and business relationships. | Keep client and company financial records private — never gossip about them or use them for personal gain, unless the law requires disclosure. | Locking payroll files and refusing to tell friends how much the CEO earns or how much debt a client owes. |
| **5. Professional Behaviour** | To comply with relevant laws and regulations and avoid any conduct that discredits the accounting profession. | Follow the law and act respectfully so people continue to trust accountants. | Filing company tax returns truthfully on time and never making false claims to attack rival accounting firms. |

:::note One Important Exception to Confidentiality
An accountant **must** break confidentiality **only when legally required or permitted by law** — for example, under a court subpoena, to report confirmed money laundering to financial authorities, or with the client's explicit written permission.
:::

## 3. Appropriate vs Inappropriate Application of Ethical Principles

Examiners frequently give short workplace stories and ask whether an ethical principle was applied **appropriately** or **inappropriately**, and **what happens next**.

| Ethical Principle | Appropriate Application | Inappropriate Application (Breach) | Direct Consequence of Breach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Integrity** | Recording all cash and credit sales honestly even during a slow quarter. | Creating fake "sales" invoices in December so the store looks more profitable for a bank loan. | Misled bank approves an unsafe loan; fraud charges, fines, and loss of business credibility. |
| **Objectivity** | Auditing a company with zero personal financial stake in it. | Accepting a free resort weekend from a supplier right before approving their overpriced contract. | Biased purchasing drains company cash; disciplinary dismissal and loss of investor trust. |
| **Professional Competence & Due Care** | Consulting a senior tax specialist before filing an unfamiliar corporate tax return. | Guessing GCT and PAYE rates instead of checking current rules, causing major underpayments. | Heavy TAJ penalties and interest charges for the client; negligence lawsuits against the accountant. |
| **Confidentiality** | Using strong passwords and keeping client profit figures strictly private. | Telling a relative to buy shares in a client company before a confidential merger is announced (**insider trading**). | Criminal prosecution for insider trading, loss of accounting licence, and broken client trust. |
| **Professional Behaviour** | Advertising services honestly and complying with statutory deadlines. | Falsifying personal tax returns or boasting online that you can "make any company's taxes disappear". | Brings the entire profession into disrepute; suspension or expulsion from professional accounting bodies. |

### Overall Results of Inappropriate Ethical Behaviour
When ethical principles are ignored, the fallout spreads far beyond one error:
- **For the Accountant:** Firing, heavy fines, revocation of professional membership/licence, civil lawsuits, or imprisonment for fraud.
- **For the Business:** Misstated financial statements, bad management decisions, TAJ penalties, loss of bank financing, customer boycott, or bankruptcy.
- **For Society & Economy:** Job losses for innocent employees, lost tax revenue for schools/hospitals, and public distrust in businesses.

## 5. Past-Paper-Style Worked Example & Quick Checks

<WorkedExample
  title="Analysing an Ethical Breach Scenario"
  marks={6}
  problem={
    <>
      Marsha is the accounts clerk at <strong>Suncoast Supplies</strong>. Her brother owns a delivery van
      company that submits a bid 30% higher than rival couriers. Marsha hides the cheaper bids from her
      manager so her brother wins the contract, and she also texts him Suncoast’s confidential budget cap.
      (a) Identify <strong>two</strong> IESBA ethical principles Marsha breached. (b) Explain{' '}
      <strong>one consequence</strong> of her actions for Suncoast Supplies and <strong>one</strong> for Marsha.
    </>
  }
  examTip="When a Paper 2 question asks you to 'identify and explain' ethical breaches, name the EXACT IESBA term first (Integrity, Objectivity, Confidentiality, Professional Competence & Due Care, or Professional Behaviour), then quote the specific action from the story."
>

**(a) Two IESBA Ethical Principles Breached** *(2 marks each = 4 marks)*
1. **Objectivity** *(and Integrity)*: Marsha allowed a family **conflict of interest** to bias her judgement and dishonestly hid rival bids to favour her brother.
2. **Confidentiality**: Marsha leaked Suncoast Supplies' private internal budget cap to an outside bidder for personal/family advantage without authorisation.

**(b) Consequences** *(1 mark each = 2 marks)*
- **For Suncoast Supplies:** The business overpays by 30% on delivery costs, directly reducing its Net Profit and cash flow.
- **For Marsha:** She faces summary dismissal for gross misconduct, loss of future accounting employment references, and possible legal action for breach of trust.

</WorkedExample>
