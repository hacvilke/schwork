## 1. Limited Liability vs Unlimited Liability

Before comparing business types, make sure you understand **liability** — how much the owners can personally lose if the business fails and cannot pay its debts:

| Type of Liability | Plain-English Meaning | Who Has It? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Unlimited Liability** | Owners are **personally responsible** for all business debts. If the business goes bankrupt and its assets aren't enough to pay creditors, creditors can legally claim the owners' personal property (house, car, personal savings). | **Sole Traders** and **General Partners** in an ordinary partnership. |
| **Limited Liability** | Owners' (shareholders') financial risk is **capped at the amount they invested** in shares. Their personal homes and savings are legally protected because the company is a separate legal person. | **Limited Liability Companies (`Ltd` & `PLC`)**, **Co-operatives**, and **Limited Partners**. |

:::tip Exam Connection: Separate Legal Entity vs Separate Accounting Entity
Remember from Lesson 1.3: **every** business (even a Sole Trader) is a *separate accounting entity* in its books. However, only incorporated organisations (like **Limited Companies** and **Co-operatives**) are **separate legal entities** in court — which is what gives their owners **Limited Liability**!
:::

## 2. Types of Business Organisations (British & Caribbean Terms)

| Business Type | Ownership & Control | Liability | How Capital Is Raised & Profit Shared | Jamaican Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sole Trader (Sole Proprietor)** | Owned and controlled by **one** person. Easy and inexpensive to start. | **Unlimited liability** | Owner's personal savings & small loans. Owner keeps **all profits** (and bears all losses). | Local barber shop, neighbourhood grocery, freelance electrician. |
| **Partnership** | Owned by **2 to 20** partners (or more for certain professions) governed by a **Partnership Deed / Agreement**. | **Unlimited liability** (for ordinary/general partners; *limited partners* have limited liability) | Partners contribute capital together. Profits/losses are shared according to the Partnership Deed (or equally if none exists). | Law firms, medical clinics, family accounting practices. |
| **Private Limited Company (`Ltd`)** | Owned by **shareholders** (often family or close associates); shares are **NOT** sold to the general public on the stock exchange. | **Limited liability** | Selling private shares + bank loans / debentures. Shareholders receive **dividends** out of profits. | Family-owned Jamaican distributors, local manufacturing `Ltd` firms. |
| **Public Limited Company (`PLC` / Corporation)** | Owned by the **general public** through shares traded freely on a stock exchange (e.g. Jamaica Stock Exchange — JSE). Run by a **Board of Directors**. | **Limited liability** | Issuing shares to the public + debentures/bonds. Must publish audited financial statements. | GraceKennedy, NCB Financial Group, Wisynco, Carreras. |
| **Co-operative Society** | Owned and democratically controlled by its **members** who use its services (**one member = one vote**, regardless of how many shares they hold). | **Limited liability** | Members buy share capital / deposits. Surpluses are returned to members as **patronage refunds / dividends**. | Credit unions (e.g. COK Sodality, JTA Co-op Credit Union), farmers' co-operatives. |
| **Non-Profit Organisation (Clubs, Charities & Societies)** | Formed to serve a social, sporting, religious, or charitable purpose — **NOT** to make profit for owners. Run by a management committee. | Usually limited if incorporated as a non-profit company; unincorporated clubs rely on committee members. | Membership **subscriptions**, donations, grants, and fundraising events. Excess income is a **Surplus** (not profit), reinvested into the club. | Sports clubs, youth charities, churches, Parent-Teacher Associations. |
| **Public Sector / Statutory Corporation** | Owned and controlled by the **Government** on behalf of citizens to provide essential public services. | Backed by the Government / state funds. | Government subventions (taxes), loans, and service fees. Focus is public service rather than private profit. | National Water Commission (NWC), Jamaica Urban Transit Company (JUTC). |
| **Franchise** | A local owner (**franchisee**) pays fees & royalties to operate under an established brand's name, recipes, and system (**franchisor**). | Depends on legal structure chosen by the franchisee (often a `Ltd` company). | Franchisee pays initial fee + ongoing royalties; keeps profits after fees. | KFC Jamaica, Burger King Jamaica, Domino's Pizza outlets. |

## 3. Main Financial Statements Overview

Accountants summarise a business's records into **three core financial statements** (British / Caribbean terms shown first alongside modern international names):

| Financial Statement | Alternative Name | Time Frame | What It Shows |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Trading and Profit & Loss Account** | **Income Statement** *(or Income & Expenditure Account for Non-Profits)* | **For the year ended…** *(a period of time)* | **Performance:** Turnover (Sales), Cost of Goods Sold, **Gross Profit**, Expenses, and **Net Profit** (or Net Loss). |
| **2. Balance Sheet** | **Statement of Financial Position** | **As at…** *(a single snapshot date, e.g. 31 December)* | **Position:** **Fixed Assets**, **Current Assets**, **Current Liabilities**, **Long-Term Liabilities**, and **Capital**. |
| **3. Cash Flow Statement** | **Statement of Cash Flows** | **For the year ended…** *(a period of time)* | **Liquidity:** Exact **cash inflows and outflows** broken into Operating, Investing, and Financing activities — explains why a profitable firm can still run low on cash! |

*(Partnerships and Limited Companies also prepare an **Appropriation Account** / **Statement of Changes in Equity** to show how Net Profit is divided into partner salaries/interest/profit shares or company dividends and retained earnings.)*

## 5. Past-Paper-Style Worked Example & Quick Checks

<WorkedExample
  title="Choosing the Right Financial Statement & Liability Form"
  marks={5}
  problem={
    <>
      Devon runs a small bakery as a <strong>Sole Trader</strong>. His cousin suggests converting the
      bakery into a <strong>Private Limited Company (Ltd)</strong> before taking a large bank loan to buy
      commercial ovens.
      (a) State <strong>two advantages</strong> Devon gains by forming a Private Limited Company instead
      of staying a Sole Trader.
      (b) Name the financial statement Devon should show the bank to display his bakery’s{' '}
      <strong>Assets, Liabilities, and Capital on 31 December</strong>.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Notice wording clues in exam questions: 'for the year ended' always points to the Trading & Profit and Loss Account (Income Statement) or Cash Flow Statement, while 'as at [date]' ALWAYS points to the Balance Sheet!"
>

**(a) Two Advantages of Converting to a Private Limited Company (`Ltd`)** *(4 marks)*
1. **Limited Liability:** If the expanded bakery cannot repay the oven loan, Devon's personal home and savings are legally protected; he can only lose the capital he invested in shares. *[2 marks]*
2. **Easier Capital Raising & Continuity:** The company can invite family or private investors to buy shares to fund expansion, and the business continues to exist even if an owner retires or passes away. *[2 marks]*

**(b) Financial Statement Required** *(1 mark)*
- The **Balance Sheet** *(Statement of Financial Position)* as at 31 December.

</WorkedExample>
