## 1. Liability & Legal Entity (Two Rules Students Often Confuse!)

Before studying the different types of business organisations, make sure you understand **liability** — the legal answer to: *"If this business goes broke and cannot pay its bills, can creditors take the owners' personal houses, cars, and bank savings?"*

| Feature | Unlimited Liability | Limited Liability |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **What it means** | The owners are **personally responsible for 100% of business debts**. If business assets cannot cover creditors, courts can seize the owners' personal property. | The owners' financial risk is **strictly capped at the amount they invested** (their shares). Their personal homes, vehicles, and private savings are legally shielded. |
| **Legal Status** | **Unincorporated** — the business and owner are the same legal person in court. | **Incorporated (Separate Legal Entity)** — the company exists as its own legal "person" that can own property, sign contracts, sue, and be sued in its own name! |
| **Who has it?** | **Sole Traders** and **General (Ordinary) Partners**. | **Limited Liability Companies (`Ltd` & `PLC`)**, **Co-operatives**, and **Limited Partners**. |

:::tip Crucial Exam Distinction: Separate Accounting Entity vs Separate Legal Entity
- **Every** business — including a tiny Sole Trader — is a **Separate Accounting Entity** (so personal spending goes to Drawings, never Business Expenses).
- However, **ONLY incorporated organisations** (such as **Limited Companies** and **Co-operatives**) are **Separate Legal Entities** in court — which is what gives their owners **Limited Liability** and **perpetual succession** (the company survives even if a shareholder dies!).
:::

## 2. Detailed Guide to Types of Business Organisations

Every organisation type has **unique accounting features** that you will later prepare full statements for in Sections 5, 8, and 9. Learn their structure, pros/cons, and special accounts right here:

### 1. Sole Trader (Sole Proprietor)
- **Ownership & Control:** Owned and managed by **one individual** (though they may employ staff). Simplest and cheapest business to form.
- **Capital & Profit:** Financed by the owner's personal savings, retained profits, and personal/bank loans. Owner keeps **100% of Net Profit** — and bears **100% of any loss**.
- **Liability:** **Unlimited liability.** No legal continuity (if the owner dies or retires, the business legally ends unless sold).
- **Special Accounting Features:** Single **Capital Account** and **Drawings Account**; prepares a *Trading and Profit & Loss Account* and *Balance Sheet* (no legal duty to publish accounts to the public).
- **Advantages:** Fast decision-making, minimal legal paperwork, total privacy of accounts, owner keeps all profits.
- **Disadvantages:** Limited capital for expansion, heavy personal workload, unlimited personal financial risk, limited continuity.

### 2. Partnership
- **Ownership & Control:** Owned by **2 to 20 partners** (sometimes higher for licensed professionals like accountants or lawyers) pooling capital and skills. Governed by a written **Partnership Agreement (Deed)** — or the **Partnership Act 1890** if no deed exists.
- **Liability:** **Unlimited liability** for General/Active partners (each partner is bound by contracts signed by other partners — *mutual agency*). A *Limited Partnership* may include Limited Partners whose liability is capped, provided at least one General Partner carries unlimited liability.
- **Special Accounting Features:** Prepares a Trading & P&L Account **PLUS a Partnership Appropriation Account** (dividing Net Profit into Interest on Capital, Partner Salaries, Interest on Drawings, and Share of Residual Profit), plus individual **Capital Accounts** and **Current Accounts** for each partner.
- **Advantages:** More capital and diverse expertise than a sole trader, shared workload and losses, still relatively private and inexpensive to set up.
- **Disadvantages:** Unlimited liability, risk of disagreements between partners, profits must be shared, and legal dissolution whenever a partner leaves or dies.

### 3. Limited Liability Companies (Corporations: Private `Ltd` vs Public `PLC`)
A company is formed by registering formal legal documents (e.g. Articles of Incorporation) with the Companies Office of Jamaica, creating a **separate legal entity** owned by **shareholders** and managed by an elected **Board of Directors**.

| Feature | Private Limited Company (`Ltd`) | Public Limited Company (`PLC` / Listed Corp.) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Who can buy shares?** | Private individuals (often family or close associates); shares **CANNOT** be advertised or sold freely to the general public. | The **general public** can freely buy and sell shares on a stock exchange (e.g. **Jamaica Stock Exchange — JSE**). |
| **Capital Raising** | Moderate — private share issues + bank loans. | Huge — public share offers, preference shares, and debentures/bonds. |
| **Financial Privacy** | More private; files simpler returns with the Registrar. | Must publish **audited annual financial statements** publicly every year. |
| **Jamaican Examples** | Family-owned Jamaican distributors, local hardware/manufacturing `Ltd` firms. | GraceKennedy, NCB Financial Group, Wisynco, Carreras, Sagicor Group Jamaica. |

- **Special Accounting Features (Both `Ltd` & `PLC`):** Capital is split into **Ordinary Shares** and **Preference Shares** (never "Owner's Capital" or "Drawings"!); profits distributed to shareholders are called **Dividends**; undistributed profits are kept in **Retained Earnings** and **General Reserves**; long-term debt certificates are called **Debentures**.

### 4. Co-operative Society
- **Ownership & Control:** A member-owned business formed to serve the shared economic needs of its members (e.g. affordable loans at **Credit Unions** like COK Sodality or JTA Co-op Credit Union, or bulk supplies for **Farmers' Co-operatives**).
- **Democratic Rule:** Operates strictly on **"one member = one vote"** at the Annual General Meeting — regardless of whether a member owns 10 shares or 1,000 shares!
- **Liability & Surpluses:** **Limited liability**; members buy shares/deposits, and annual surpluses are partly placed into a **Statutory Reserve** (required by law!) and partly returned to members as **dividends / patronage refunds** based on how much they used the co-operative.

### 5. Non-Profit Organisations (Clubs, Charities, Churches & Societies)
- **Purpose:** Formed for social, sporting, educational, religious, or charitable goals — **NOT** to generate private profit for owners (e.g. local football clubs, Parent-Teacher Associations, youth charities). Run by an elected **Management Committee** (President, Secretary, Treasurer).
- **Funding:** Financed by member **Subscriptions**, donations, grants, entrance fees, and fundraising events (or a small refreshment bar).
- **Special Accounting Features (Exam Favourite!):**
  - Instead of a Cash Book summary, they prepare a **Receipts and Payments Account**.
  - Instead of a Profit & Loss Account, they prepare an **Income and Expenditure Account**.
  - They NEVER report "Net Profit" or "Net Loss" — they report a **Surplus** (Income > Expenditure) or **Deficit** (Expenditure > Income)!
  - They NEVER have "Owner's Capital" — their net worth is called the **Accumulated Fund** (`Total Assets − Total Liabilities`)!

### 6. Public Sector Enterprises & Franchises (Quick Awareness)
- **Public Sector / Statutory Corporations:** Owned and funded by the **Government** to deliver essential public utilities and services rather than maximise profit (e.g. National Water Commission — NWC, JUTC).
- **Franchise:** A local entrepreneur (**franchisee**) pays an upfront fee and ongoing royalties to operate under a proven global brand's trademark, recipes, and systems (**franchisor** — e.g. KFC Jamaica, Domino's). The franchisee still chooses their own legal form (usually a `Ltd` company).

## 3. The Main Financial Statements (Exact Names, Dates & Roles)

Every organisation summarises its accounting cycle into formal financial statements. Notice the **British/Caribbean titles**, their **modern international equivalents**, and their **exact heading date wording** (marks are deducted on Paper 2 if you get the date phrase wrong!):

| Financial Statement | Modern / Alternative Name | Exact Date Phrase in Heading | What It Shows & Who Prepares It |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Trading and Profit & Loss Account** | **Income Statement** | **For the year ended [Date]** *(covers a full period)* | Measures **financial performance** of profit-making firms: Sales (Turnover), Cost of Goods Sold, **Gross Profit**, Operating Expenses, and **Net Profit (or Loss)**. |
| **2. Income and Expenditure Account** | *(Used by Non-Profits)* | **For the year ended [Date]** *(covers a full period)* | Measures **performance of Non-Profit Organisations** (clubs/charities) on an accrual basis: compares Subscriptions & Income against Expenses to find a **Surplus** or **Deficit**. |
| **3. Balance Sheet** | **Statement of Financial Position (SOFP)** | **As at [Date]** *(a single snapshot day)* | Shows **financial position** on closing day: **Fixed Assets**, **Current Assets**, **Current Liabilities**, **Working Capital**, **Capital / Equity (or Accumulated Fund)**, and **Long-Term Liabilities**. Prepared by **ALL** organisations! |
| **4. Cash Flow Statement** | **Statement of Cash Flows** | **For the year ended [Date]** *(covers a full period)* | Tracks **actual cash inflows and cash outflows** split into Operating, Investing, and Financing activities. Crucial because a profitable business on paper can still go bankrupt if customers fail to pay cash on time! |
| **5. Appropriation Account** *(Supplemental)* | **Statement of Changes in Equity** | **For the year ended [Date]** | Prepared by **Partnerships, Limited Companies & Co-operatives** right below the P&L Account to show how Net Profit / Surplus is divided (partner salaries & profit shares; company dividends, transfers to reserves, and retained earnings). |

## 4. Past-Paper-Style Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Comparing Business Structures & Matching Financial Statements"
  marks={7}
  problem={
    <>
      Devon runs a growing bakery in Ocho Rios as a <strong>Sole Trader</strong>. He wants to expand by buying commercial ovens, and his cousin suggests forming a <strong>Private Limited Company (Ltd)</strong>.
      <br />
      <strong>(a)</strong> State <strong>two advantages</strong> and <strong>one disadvantage</strong> Devon should consider before converting to a Private Limited Company.
      <br />
      <strong>(b)</strong> Name the exact financial statement Devon must give his bank to show:
      <br />
      &nbsp;&nbsp;(i) the bakery’s profit earned during 2025; and
      <br />
      &nbsp;&nbsp;(ii) the bakery’s Assets, Liabilities, and Capital on 31 December 2025.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Always pair the exact accounting terminology with the right business type: Sole Traders have Capital & Drawings; Companies have Share Capital & Dividends; Non-Profits have Accumulated Fund & Surplus/Deficit!"
>

**(a) Evaluating Conversion to a Private Limited Company (`Ltd`)** *(5 marks)*
- **Two Advantages** *(2 marks each = 4 marks)*:
  1. **Limited Liability:** As a separate legal entity, the company shields Devon's personal home, car, and savings if the business ever fails to repay the oven loan — he can only lose his share investment.
  2. **Greater Capital & Continuity:** The company can issue private shares to family or trusted investors to fund the ovens without taking on crushing debt, and the company continues legally even if a shareholder retires or passes away.
- **One Disadvantage** *(1 mark)*:
  - **Higher formation costs and stricter legal regulation:** Incorporating requires legal registration fees, formal statutory filings, and sharing ownership/control (and dividends) with other shareholders.

**(b) Financial Statements Required** *(1 mark each = 2 marks)*
- **(i)** **Trading and Profit & Loss Account** *(Income Statement)* **for the year ended 31 December 2025**.
- **(ii)** **Balance Sheet** *(Statement of Financial Position)* **as at 31 December 2025**.

</WorkedExample>
