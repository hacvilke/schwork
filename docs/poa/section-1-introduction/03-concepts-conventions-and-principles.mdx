## 1. What Are Accounting Concepts, Conventions & Principles?

If every accountant invented their own rules for measuring profit or valuing equipment, nobody could compare two businesses or trust their reports. Accountants therefore follow shared rules known as **GAAP** (*Generally Accepted Accounting Principles*):

- **Accounting Concepts:** The fundamental building-block assumptions on which all financial statements rest (e.g. *Separate Entity*, *Accrual*, *Going Concern*).
- **Accounting Conventions:** Widely agreed customs and practical habits followed when applying concepts so statements stay fair and comparable (e.g. *Prudence*, *Consistency*, *Materiality*).
- **Accounting Principles:** The combined rules and guidelines (concepts + conventions) that govern how transactions are recorded and reported.

## 2. The Core Principles You Must Master (Step by Step)

### 1. Separate Entity (Business Entity) Concept
- **Rule:** The business is treated as a **completely separate financial unit** from its owner(s). Only transactions belonging to the business are recorded in the business's books.
- **Step-by-step check:**
  1. Ask: *Did this transaction happen for the business or for the owner's personal life?*
  2. If the owner puts personal money **into** the business → record as **Capital**.
  3. If the owner takes business cash or goods for **personal use** → record as **Drawings** (reducing Capital), **NOT** as a business expense!
  4. If the owner pays their personal rent from their personal bank account → **do NOT record it** in the business books at all.

### 2. Accrual and Matching Concept
- **Rule:** Revenue is recorded in the period it is **earned** (not necessarily when cash is received), and expenses are recorded in the period they are **incurred** (not necessarily when cash is paid). Expenses must be **matched** against the revenue they helped earn in the same accounting period.
- **Hover or tap the formula below** to see it in plain English:

  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Count all income earned this year (even if the customer hasn't paid cash yet) minus all expenses used up this year (even if the bill is still unpaid)."
>

<FormulaHint

>

$$
\text{Net Profit} = \text{Revenue Earned in Period} - \text{Expenses Incurred in Period}
$$

</FormulaHint>

- **Step-by-step check:**
  1. **Accrued Expense (owing at year-end):** Business used December electricity of J\$18,000 but pays in January → **ADD** J\$18,000 to this year's Electricity Expense and show J\$18,000 as a **Current Liability**.
  2. **Prepaid Expense (paid in advance):** Business paid J\$60,000 rent covering 2 months of *next* year → **SUBTRACT** next year's portion from this year's Rent Expense and show it as a **Current Asset**.

### 3. Prudence (Conservatism) Concept
- **Rule:** Accountants must exercise caution under uncertainty: **never overstate assets or profits, and never understate liabilities or losses.**
- **Step-by-step check:**
  1. **Anticipated losses:** Record them **immediately** as soon as they are reasonably expected (e.g. creating a *Provision for Doubtful Debts* for customers who may not pay, or writing off *Bad Debts*).
  2. **Anticipated profits:** Do **NOT** record them until actually realised (e.g. if your building's market value rises, don't count it as sales profit until sold).
  3. **Closing Stock (Inventory) rule:** Always value stock at the **lower of Cost or Net Realisable Value (NRV)**.

### 4. Consistency Concept
- **Rule:** Once a business chooses an accounting policy or method (such as *Straight-Line* depreciation or *FIFO* stock valuation), it must apply that **same method year after year**.
- **Why it matters:** Switching methods back and forth distorts profit trends and makes it impossible to tell whether the business truly improved or just changed the math.
- **Can a method ever change?** Only if there is a genuine, justified business reason — and the change (plus its J$ effect) must be **fully disclosed** in the notes to the financial statements.

## 3. Supporting Principles Often Tested on Past Papers

| Principle | Plain-English Rule | Quick Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Going Concern** | Assume the business will keep operating for the foreseeable future (not shutting down tomorrow). | Machinery is valued at cost minus depreciation over its useful life, not at panic liquidation prices. |
| **Historical Cost** | Record assets at their original purchase price shown on the invoice/receipt. | A delivery van bought for J\$2,400,000 stays recorded at J\$2,400,000 cost (less depreciation), even if a dealer guesses it's worth J\$2,800,000. |
| **Money Measurement** | Only record items that can be expressed reliably in money (J\$). | Goods worth J\$50,000 are recorded; "high staff morale" or "great location" is NOT recorded in the ledger. |
| **Dual Aspect** | Every transaction has two equal and opposite sides: **Debit = Credit**, keeping $\text{Assets} = \text{Capital} + \text{Liabilities}$. | Buying equipment for J\$90,000 cash increases Equipment (+J\$90,000) and decreases Cash (−J\$90,000). |
| **Materiality** | Treat tiny, low-cost items practically without wasting time depreciating them over years. | A J\$1,200 stapler is expensed immediately as Stationery rather than depreciated over 5 years as a Non-Current Asset. |

## 4. Appropriate vs Inappropriate Application & Results

This comparison table is one of the highest-yield exam tables in POA Section 1 — know both the **mistake** and the **exact financial distortion** it causes!

| Principle | Appropriate Application | Inappropriate Application | Results / Consequences of Inappropriate Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Separate Entity** | Charging owner's J\$45,000 family groceries taken from the shop to **Drawings**. | Recording the owner's personal groceries as a business **Expense**. | Business expenses are **overstated** → **Net Profit is understated** → business pays unfairly low tax and looks less profitable than it is. |
| **Accrual & Matching** | Including J\$22,000 of unpaid December water bills in this year's Expenses and Current Liabilities. | Leaving unpaid December bills out until paid next year (cash basis). | Expenses & Liabilities are **understated** → **Net Profit is overstated** → owners/banks are misled into thinking the business is healthier than it really is. |
| **Prudence** | Creating a 5% Provision for Doubtful Debts when debtors are overdue, and valuing damaged stock at lower NRV. | Counting doubtful debtors (accounts receivable) as 100% collectible and valuing damaged goods above selling price. | **Assets and Net Profit are overstated** → owners may withdraw cash that doesn't really exist, risking insolvency. |
| **Consistency** | Using Straight-Line depreciation at 10% every year on shop fittings. | Switching from Straight-Line to Reducing Balance whenever management wants higher profit. | Profit figures are **manipulated and incomparable** across years → investors and lenders lose confidence in the reports. |
| **Historical Cost** | Recording land at its J\$6,000,000 purchase contract price. | Randomly raising land to J\$9,500,000 based on an owner's personal guess. | **Assets and Capital are artificially inflated** without objective proof, violating reliability. |

## 6. Past-Paper-Style Worked Example & Quick Checks

<WorkedExample
  title="Quantifying the Result of Misapplying Accounting Principles"
  marks={6}
  problem={
    <>
      <strong>Portmore Auto Parts</strong> reported a draft Net Profit of <strong>J$620,000</strong> for
      the year ended 31 December. Before finalising the accounts, the accountant discovered two errors:
      <ol>
        <li>The owner’s personal home internet bill of <strong>J$48,000</strong> was included in Office Expenses.</li>
        <li><strong>J$35,000</strong> of December warehouse rent had been used but not yet paid or recorded.</li>
      </ol>
      (a) Name the accounting concept breached in each case. (b) Calculate the corrected Net Profit.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Always work step-by-step on profit-correction questions: removing a wrongly included expense INCREASES Net Profit (+); adding a missing expense DECREASES Net Profit (−)."
>

**(a) Concepts Breached** *(2 marks)*
1. Including the owner's personal home internet bill as a business expense breaches the **Separate Entity concept** (it should be debited to **Drawings**).
2. Omitting unpaid December warehouse rent breaches the **Accrual and Matching concept** (the expense belongs to the current year and is also a Current Liability).

**(b) Corrected Net Profit Calculation** *(4 marks)*

| Step | Adjustment | J\$ |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| Draft Net Profit | Starting figure | 620,000 |
| Add back personal internet bill | Was wrongly deducted as an expense | +48,000 |
| Deduct accrued December rent | Incurred this year but omitted | −35,000 |
| **Corrected Net Profit** | **True profit for the year** | **633,000** |

The draft Net Profit was understated by J\$13,000 overall; the true Net Profit is **J\$633,000**.

</WorkedExample>
