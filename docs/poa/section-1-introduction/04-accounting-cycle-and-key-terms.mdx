## 2. Plain-English Guide to the Key Terms in the Cycle

If all the new accounting vocabulary feels like a lot at once, keep this clean step-by-step cheat sheet right beside you as you study Sections 2–6:

### Stage 1: Source Documents (Receipts, Invoices & More)
A **source document** is the original paper or digital proof that a transaction took place.
- **Receipt:** Proof that **cash or cheque payment** was received (e.g. a customer pays J$15,000 cash for paint).
- **Invoice:** Proof of a **credit sale or credit purchase** (goods delivered now, paid later).
- **Credit Note / Debit Note:** Proof of goods returned or an overcharge/undercharge correction.
- **Cheque Counterfoil / Bank Slip:** Proof of payments made from or deposits made into the business bank account.

### Stage 2: Journalise (Books of Original Entry)
To **journalise** means to make the **very first chronological record** of a transaction from its source document into a Book of Original Entry (also called a **Journal** or **Day Book**):
- Cash & bank transactions → **Cash Book** (small cash expenses → **Petty Cash Book**)
- Credit sales & purchases → **Sales Journal** & **Purchases Journal**
- Returns → **Returns Inwards Journal** & **Returns Outwards Journal**
- Everything else (buying equipment on credit, corrections, adjusting & closing entries) → **General Journal**

### Stage 3: Debit (Dr) & Credit (Cr) — Posting to Ledgers
Every account in the Ledger is drawn like a large **"T"** (a **T-account**) with two sides:
- **Debit (Dr)** is ALWAYS the **LEFT** side.
- **Credit (Cr)** is ALWAYS the **RIGHT** side.

Under the **Dual Aspect** principle (**Double-Entry Bookkeeping**), every single transaction is recorded with an equal Debit and Credit:

$$

</FormulaHint>

:::tip Quick Preview of Debit & Credit (You'll Master This in Section 4!)
Remember **DEAD CLIC** — **D**ebit increases **E**xpenses, **A**ssets, and **D**rawings; **C**redit increases **L**iabilities, **I**ncome (Revenue), and **C**apital.
:::

### Stage 4 & Stage 6: Trial Balance (Unadjusted vs Adjusted)
A **Trial Balance** is a two-column list (Debit column and Credit column) of every ledger account balance on a given date.
- **Unadjusted Trial Balance (Stage 4):** Prepared *before* year-end adjustments to check that `Total Dr = Total Cr`.
- **Adjusted Trial Balance (Stage 6):** Prepared *after* posting adjusting entries so final statements are built from accurate, up-to-date numbers.

### Stage 5: Adjusting Entries
**Adjusting entries** are journal entries made right at the **end of the accounting period** to obey the **Accrual, Matching, and Prudence** concepts before drafting financial statements:
- Recording unpaid bills (**accrued expenses**) or unpaid income (**accrued revenue**)
- Removing payments that belong to next year (**prepayments**)
- Recording wear-and-tear on equipment (**depreciation**)
- Recording **bad debts** and **provision for doubtful debts**

### Stage 7: Financial Statements (Income Statement & Balance Sheet)
- **Income Statement (Trading & Profit and Loss Account):** Summarises **Revenue** and **Expenses** over the accounting period to show **Gross Profit** and **Net Profit (or Loss)**.
- **Balance Sheet (Statement of Financial Position):** A snapshot on the **final day** of the period listing **Assets**, **Liabilities**, and **Capital** (showing $\text{Assets} = \text{Capital} + \text{Liabilities}$).

### Stage 8: Closing Entries & Post-Closing Trial Balance
**Closing entries** are special end-of-year journal entries that **empty (zero out) all temporary accounts** so the new year starts fresh at J$0:

| Account Type | Examples | What Happens at Year-End? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Temporary (Nominal) Accounts** | Sales, Purchases, Rent Expense, Wages, Drawings | **Closed** to the Income Statement / Capital → balance resets to **J$0** for next year. |
| **Permanent (Real & Personal) Accounts** | Cash, Bank, Equipment, Stock (Inventory), Debtors (Accounts Receivable), Creditors (Accounts Payable), Bank Loan, Capital | **Balanced carried down (c/d)** and carried into next year on the **Post-Closing Trial Balance**. |

## 3. Adjusting Entries vs Closing Entries (Classic Exam Trap!)

Students often mix up **Stage 5 (Adjusting Entries)** and **Stage 8 (Closing Entries)**. Keep the difference razor-sharp:

| Feature | Adjusting Entries (Stage 5) | Closing Entries (Stage 8) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **When prepared?** | **Before** preparing the Financial Statements. | **After** preparing the Financial Statements. |
| **Main purpose** | Update revenue, expense, asset, and liability accounts to apply Accrual, Matching & Prudence. | Reset all temporary revenue, expense, and drawings accounts to J$0 and transfer Net Profit/Loss to Capital. |
| **Example** | Recording J$18,500 of unpaid December JPS electricity. | Transferring total annual Sales and Electricity Expense into the Income Statement (Profit & Loss Account). |

## 5. Past-Paper-Style Worked Example & Quick Checks

<WorkedExample
  title="Putting Accounting Cycle Stages in Correct Chronological Order"
  marks={5}
  problem={
    <>
      The following five stages of the accounting cycle are listed out of order:
      <br />
      <strong>(A)</strong> Prepare Financial Statements &nbsp;|&nbsp;
      <strong>(B)</strong> Collect Source Documents &nbsp;|&nbsp;
      <strong>(C)</strong> Record Adjusting Entries &nbsp;|&nbsp;
      <strong>(D)</strong> Post to Ledger Accounts &nbsp;|&nbsp;
      <strong>(E)</strong> Journalise in Books of Original Entry
      <br />
      Arrange these five stages in the correct order and briefly state the purpose of stage <strong>(C)</strong>.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Paper 1 and Paper 2 love asking you to sequence the Accounting Cycle. Always anchor your order with: Documents → Journal → Ledger → Trial Balance → Adjustments → Statements → Closing!"
>

**Correct Chronological Order** *(3 marks)*
1. **(B) Collect Source Documents** (receipts, invoices, cheques)
2. **(E) Journalise in Books of Original Entry** (chronological prime entry)
3. **(D) Post to Ledger Accounts** (double-entry Debits and Credits)
4. **(C) Record Adjusting Entries** (after the unadjusted trial balance)
5. **(A) Prepare Financial Statements** (Income Statement and Balance Sheet, followed by Closing Entries)

**Purpose of Stage (C) — Adjusting Entries** *(2 marks)*
- Adjusting entries update revenue, expense, asset, and liability accounts at the end of the period so the financial statements obey the **Accrual, Matching, and Prudence** concepts (for example, adding unpaid bills, removing prepayments, and recording depreciation).

</WorkedExample>
