:::info Essential Understanding
A business can own a $500,000 building and still collapse next Tuesday if it lacks cash to pay staff wages and electricity bills! **Working Capital** (also called *Net Current Assets* or *Circulating Capital*) is the **short-term financial cushion** left over after subtracting Current Liabilities from Current Assets — measuring how smoothly the business can fund its everyday trading cycle.
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | Calculating **Working Capital (`Current Assets − Current Liabilities`)**, displaying it inside the Vertical Balance Sheet, and tracing how transactions change it |
| **Exam Focus** | Knowing WHICH transactions **increase**, **decrease**, or leave Working Capital **unchanged** (classic Paper 1 & Paper 2 favorite!) |
| **Learning Outcome** | Compute Working Capital accurately, interpret positive vs. negative Working Capital, and analyze transaction effects on Working Capital |

## 1. The Working Capital Formula & Transaction Effect Rules

<FormulaCard
  title="Working Capital (Net Current Assets)"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Short-term resources available to run day-to-day operations after covering short-term debts."
>

$$
\text{Working Capital} = \text{Current Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities}
$$

- If **Current Assets > Current Liabilities** → **Positive Working Capital** (healthy short-term cushion).
- If **Current Liabilities > Current Assets** → **Working Capital Deficit / Negative Working Capital** (severe liquidity warning!).

</FormulaCard>

### The Golden Shortcut: How Transactions Change Working Capital!
Watch this effortless trick! A transaction changes Working Capital **ONLY if it touches ONE Current item (`CA` or `CL`) and ONE Non-Current/Capital item**! If it touches **TWO Current items** by the same amount, **Working Capital stays UNCHANGED!**

| Business Transaction | Effect on CA | Effect on CL | **Net Effect on Working Capital (`CA − CL`)** |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **1. Owner invests $10,000 cash** | Cash +$10,000 | — | **INCREASES by +$10,000** |
| **2. Take out a 5-year Bank Loan ($15,000)** | Bank +$15,000 | — *(Non-Current!)* | **INCREASES by +$15,000** |
| **3. Sell old delivery van for $6,000 cash** | Bank +$6,000 | — *(NCA ↓)* | **INCREASES by +$6,000** |
| **4. Sell stock costing $800 for $1,200 cash** | Cash +$1,200 / Stock −$800 | — | **INCREASES by +$400** *(the Gross Profit!)* |
| **5. Buy new $12,000 van paying by cheque** | Bank −$12,000 | — *(NCA ↑)* | **DECREASES by −$12,000** |
| **6. Owner withdraws $2,000 cash (Drawings)** | Bank −$2,000 | — *(Capital ↓)* | **DECREASES by −$2,000** |
| **7. Pay $1,500 cheque to Trade Payable** | Bank −$1,500 | Payables −$1,500 | **NO CHANGE ($0)!** *(`−1,500 − (−1,500) = 0`)* |
| **8. Collect $900 cash from Trade Receivable** | Cash +$900 / Rec. −$900 | — | **NO CHANGE ($0)!** *(Swap inside CA!)* |
| **9. Buy $3,000 stock on 30-day credit** | Stock +$3,000 | Payables +$3,000 | **NO CHANGE ($0)!** *(`+3,000 − (+3,000) = 0`)* |

**Part B — Independent Effects on Working Capital (5 marks — 1 mark each):**
1. **Owner invests $3,000 cash:** **Increases by +$3,000** *(Cash CA +$3,000; Capital ↑)* → `$7,000`

## 5. True or False Check

<TrueFalseQuiz
  title="True or False: Working Capital"
  items={[
    {
      statement: 'Paying $1,500 by cheque to settle a Trade Payable reduces Working Capital by $1,500.',
      answer: false,
      explanation: 'Paying a Trade Payable reduces Current Assets (Bank −$1,500) AND reduces Current Liabilities (Payables −$1,500) equally — so Working Capital stays UNCHANGED!',
    },
    {
      statement: 'Buying a $10,000 delivery van using business cash immediately decreases Working Capital by $10,000.',
      answer: true,
      explanation: 'Cash (a Current Asset) drops by $10,000 while the delivery van is a Non-Current Asset, so Working Capital falls by $10,000.',
    },
    {
      statement: 'Converting a $5,000 Bank Overdraft into a 5-Year Bank Loan increases Working Capital by $5,000.',
      answer: true,
      explanation: 'Bank Overdraft (Current Liability) drops by $5,000 while the 5-Year Loan is a Non-Current Liability — immediately boosting Working Capital by +$5,000!',
    },
    {
      statement: 'A business with Current Assets of $18,000 and Current Liabilities of $22,000 has a Working Capital deficit of $4,000.',
      answer: true,
      explanation: 'Working Capital = $18,000 − $22,000 = −$4,000 (a deficit), warning of short-term cash trouble.',
    },
    {
      statement: 'A 10-year Mortgage on business premises should be subtracted when calculating Working Capital.',
      answer: false,
      explanation: 'Only CURRENT Liabilities (due within 12 months) are subtracted to compute Working Capital — a 10-year Mortgage is a Non-Current Liability!',
    },
  ]}
/>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **`Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities`** (also called *Net Current Assets*).
- **Quick Effect Test:** Both halves Current (e.g., cash from debtor, pay creditor, credit stock purchase) = **NO CHANGE**! One Current + one Non-Current/Capital = **CHANGES Working Capital**!
:::
