:::info Essential Understanding
Raw profit dollars alone can fool you! Suppose Shop A earns **$20,000 Net Profit** on **$100,000 Sales** while Shop B earns **$25,000 Net Profit** on **$500,000 Sales** — Shop A actually keeps **20 cents per dollar** (20% margin) whereas Shop B only keeps **5 cents per dollar** (5% margin)! **Profitability and efficiency ratios** turn raw Income Statement figures into sharp, comparable yardsticks across years and competitors.
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | **Gross Profit Margin (`GP %`)**, **Mark-Up**, **Net Profit Margin (`NP %`)**, and **Rate of Stock (Inventory) Turnover** |
| **Exam Focus** | Choosing the exact right denominator (`Net Sales` vs. `COGS` vs. `Average Stock`), using the right **unit** (`%` vs. `times`), and interpreting changes across years |
| **Learning Outcome** | Compute all four profitability & inventory-efficiency ratios accurately and explain what each figure reveals about business performance |

## 1. The Four Profitability & Stock Efficiency Formulas

<FormulaCard
  title="Profitability & Stock Turnover Formulas (Memorize All 4!)"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Margin divides by Net Sales; Mark-Up divides by Cost of Goods Sold; Stock Turnover divides COGS by Average Inventory."
>

$$
\text{Gross Profit Margin (GP \%)} = \frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Net Sales}} \times 100
$$

$$
\text{Mark-Up (\%)} = \frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Cost of Goods Sold}} \times 100
$$

$$
\text{Net Profit Margin (NP \%)} = \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Net Sales}} \times 100
$$

$$
\text{Rate of Stock Turnover} = \frac{\text{Cost of Goods Sold}}{\text{Average Inventory}} \quad \text{(times per year)}
$$

where:

$$
\text{Average Inventory} = \frac{\text{Opening Inventory} + \text{Closing Inventory}}{2}
$$

</FormulaCard>

### Unit & Formula Trap Checklist

| Ratio | Correct Unit | #1 Classic Mistake to Avoid |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Gross Profit Margin** | **Percentage (`%`)** | Dividing by Gross Sales instead of **Net Sales** *(always subtract Returns Inwards first!)* |
| **Mark-Up** | **Percentage (`%`)** | Confusing Margin (÷ Sales) with Mark-Up (÷ Cost of Goods Sold)! |
| **Net Profit Margin** | **Percentage (`%`)** | Using Gross Profit instead of **Net Profit** in the numerator |
| **Rate of Stock Turnover** | **Times** *(e.g. `6.4 times`)* | Dividing **Sales** instead of **Cost of Goods Sold** by Average Inventory — Stock is valued at COST, so the numerator MUST be **COGS**! |

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Calculating & Comparing Profitability and Stock Turnover Across Two Years"
  marks={12}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> The summarized Income Statement figures for
        <strong> SunCoast Electronics</strong> across two years are shown below:
      </p>
      <table>
        <thead>
          <tr><th>Item</th><th>2025 ($)</th><th>2026 ($)</th></tr>
        </thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Net Sales</td><td>160,000</td><td>200,000</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Opening Inventory</td><td>18,000</td><td>22,000</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Net Purchases</td><td>100,000</td><td>132,000</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Closing Inventory</td><td>22,000</td><td>26,000</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Operating Expenses</td><td>32,000</td><td>46,000</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
      <ol>
        <li>Calculate <strong>Cost of Goods Sold</strong>, <strong>Gross Profit</strong>, and <strong>Net Profit</strong> for both 2025 and 2026. <em>[4 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Calculate to 1 decimal place for BOTH years: (a) <strong>Gross Profit Margin (%)</strong>, (b) <strong>Mark-Up (%)</strong>, (c) <strong>Net Profit Margin (%)</strong>, and (d) <strong>Rate of Stock Turnover (times)</strong>. <em>[6 marks]</em></li>
        <li>State <strong>two sharp observations</strong> comparing 2026 performance against 2025. <em>[2 marks]</em></li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

**1. Core Profit Figures (4 marks):**
- **2025:**
  - `COGS` = `$18,000 + $100,000 − $22,000` = **$96,000**
  - `Gross Profit` = `$160,000 − $96,000` = **$64,000**
  - `Net Profit` = `$64,000 − $32,000` = **$32,000**
- **2026:**
  - `COGS` = `$22,000 + $132,000 − $26,000` = **$128,000**
  - `Gross Profit` = `$200,000 − $128,000` = **$72,000**
  - `Net Profit` = `$72,000 − $46,000` = **$26,000**

**2. Ratios Table (6 marks):**
- *Average Inventory:* 2025 = `($18,000 + $22,000) ÷ 2 = $20,000`; 2026 = `($22,000 + $26,000) ÷ 2 = $24,000`.

| Ratio | 2025 Calculation &amp; Result | 2026 Calculation &amp; Result | Trend |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **(a) Gross Profit Margin** | `(64,000 ÷ 160,000) × 100` = **40.0%** | `(72,000 ÷ 200,000) × 100` = **36.0%** | **↓ Fell 4.0%** |
| **(b) Mark-Up** | `(64,000 ÷ 96,000) × 100` = **66.7%** | `(72,000 ÷ 128,000) × 100` = **56.3%** | **↓ Fell 10.4%** |
| **(c) Net Profit Margin** | `(32,000 ÷ 160,000) × 100` = **20.0%** | `(26,000 ÷ 200,000) × 100` = **13.0%** | **↓ Fell 7.0%** |
| **(d) Stock Turnover** | `96,000 ÷ 20,000` = **4.8 times** | `128,000 ÷ 24,000` = **5.3 times** | **↑ Improved** |

**3. Two Sharp Observations (2 marks):**
1. **Higher sales masked weaker profit efficiency:** Even though Net Sales jumped by $40,000 (+25%) and Stock Turnover sped up from `4.8` to `5.3 times` (likely due to lowering prices), **Gross Profit Margin fell from 40% to 36%** *(1 mark)*.
2. **Operating expenses surged faster than sales:** Net Profit Margin dropped sharply from **20.0% down to 13.0%** — Expenses ate 23% of sales in 2026 (`36% − 13%`) compared to 20% in 2025 (`40% − 20%`), reducing final Net Profit dollars by $6,000 *(1 mark)*.

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Margin** = `GP ÷ Net Sales × 100` (`%`); **Mark-Up** = `GP ÷ COGS × 100` (`%`).
- **Net Profit Margin** = `NP ÷ Net Sales × 100` (`%`).
- **Stock Turnover** = `COGS ÷ Average Inventory` (**`times`** — NEVER `%`!).
:::
