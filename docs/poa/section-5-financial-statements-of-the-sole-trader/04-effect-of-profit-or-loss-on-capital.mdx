:::info Essential Understanding
Why does a sole trader bother computing **Net Profit** in the Income Statement? Because in a one-owner business, **100% of Net Profit belongs to the owner — directly increasing Owner’s Capital**! Conversely, a **Net Loss** shrinks Owner’s Capital — and **Drawings** shrink it further. Mastering the **Owner’s Capital Equation** also unlocks a classic exam skill: **finding Net Profit backwards when only Opening and Closing Balance Sheets are known!**
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | The **Owner’s Capital Bridge** (`Closing Capital = Opening Capital + Additional Capital + Net Profit [or − Net Loss] − Drawings`) and **Statement of Affairs** profit recovery |
| **Exam Focus** | Showing Net Profit vs. Net Loss accurately in the Balance Sheet Equity section, closing the Capital T-account, and solving for **missing Net Profit, Drawings, or Opening Capital** |
| **Learning Outcome** | Demonstrate exactly how Net Profit, Net Loss, Additional Capital, and Drawings alter Owner’s Capital both in the Balance Sheet and in the Capital T-Account |

## 1. The Owner’s Capital Equation & T-Account Transfers

<FormulaCard
  title="The Owner’s Capital Bridge Equations"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Profit and new investments grow Capital; Losses and Drawings shrink Capital."
>

**Forward Formula (Finding Closing Capital):**

$$
\text{Closing Capital} = \text{Opening Capital} + \text{Additional Capital} + \text{Net Profit} - \text{Drawings}
$$

*(If the business makes a **Net Loss**, **SUBTRACT** Net Loss instead of adding Net Profit!)*

**Reverse Formula (Finding Missing Net Profit or Net Loss):**

$$
\text{Net Profit / (Loss)} = \text{Closing Capital} - \text{Opening Capital} + \text{Drawings} - \text{Additional Capital}
$$

</FormulaCard>

### Double-Entry Closing Transfers Into the Capital Account

| Year-End Item | Effect on Owner’s Capital | Closing Journal Entry | Side of Capital T-A/c |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: |
| **Net Profit** | **Increases (+)** | Dr Income Statement · **Cr Capital** | **Credit (`Cr`)** |
| **Net Loss** | **Decreases (−)** | **Dr Capital** · Cr Income Statement | **Debit (`Dr`)** |
| **Additional Capital** | **Increases (+)** | Dr Bank/Cash/Asset · **Cr Capital** | **Credit (`Cr`)** |
| **Drawings** *(cash or goods)* | **Decreases (−)** | **Dr Capital** · Cr Drawings | **Debit (`Dr`)** |

:::warning Watch the Drawings Trap When Solving Backwards for Profit!
Students often ask: *"Why do we **ADD** Drawings when calculating Net Profit backwards?"*
Think logically! Suppose you started with **$50,000** and ended with **$55,000** (up +$5,000) — **EVEN THOUGH** you pulled **$8,000** out for personal living expenses! For your Capital to STILL grow by $5,000 after taking $8,000 out, your business had to earn **`$5,000 + $8,000 = $13,000`** of Net Profit!
:::

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Capital T-Account Presentation & Solving Missing Net Profit Backwards"
  marks={10}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Part A (5 marks):</strong> <strong>Maya’s Floral Studio</strong> began 2026 with
        <strong> Opening Capital of $42,000</strong>. On 1 July 2026, Maya invested an additional
        <strong> $8,000</strong> from her personal savings into the business bank account. During
        the year, Maya withdrew <strong>$5,500</strong> in cash and took <strong>$500</strong> of
        store goods (at cost) for family gifts. Because of road construction outside her shop, the
        business suffered a <strong>Net Loss of $3,400</strong> for the year ended 31 December 2026.
        Prepare Maya’s <strong>Capital T-Account</strong> balanced off on 31 December 2026.
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>Part B (5 marks):</strong> Another sole trader, <strong>Devon Repairs</strong>,
        had Total Assets of $68,000 and Total Liabilities of $22,000 on 1 January 2026. On
        31 December 2026, Total Assets were $84,000 and Total Liabilities were $26,000. During the
        year, Devon introduced $4,000 additional capital and withdrew $9,500 in Drawings. Calculate
        Devon’s <strong>Net Profit or Net Loss</strong> for 2026.
      </p>
    </>
  }
>

**Part A — Maya’s Capital Account (5 marks):**
*(Total Drawings = `$5,500 cash + $500 goods = $6,000`!)*

**Dr &emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp; Capital Account (Maya’s Floral Studio) &emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp; Cr**

| Date | Details | $ | Date | Details | $ |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Dec 31 | Income Statement *(Net Loss)* | 3,400 | Jan 1 | Balance b/d | 42,000 |
| Dec 31 | Drawings *($5,500 + $500)* | 6,000 | Jul 1 | Bank *(Additional Capital)* | 8,000 |
| **Dec 31** | **Balance c/d** | **40,600** | | | |
| | **Total** | **50,000** | | **Total** | **50,000** |
| | | | **Jan 1** | **Balance b/d** | **40,600** |

*(Check: `$42,000 + $8,000 − $3,400 − $6,000 = $40,600` Closing Capital ✓!)*

---

**Part B — Finding Devon Repairs’ Net Profit Backwards (5 marks):**
1. **Opening Capital (1 Jan):** `Assets − Liabilities` = `$68,000 − $22,000` = **$46,000** *(1 mark)*
2. **Closing Capital (31 Dec):** `Assets − Liabilities` = `$84,000 − $26,000` = **$58,000** *(1 mark)*
3. **Apply Reverse Capital Formula:** *(3 marks)*
   - `Net Profit = Closing Capital − Opening Capital + Drawings − Additional Capital`
   - `Net Profit = $58,000 − $46,000 + $9,500 − $4,000`
   - **Net Profit = `$17,500`**!

*(Forward double-check: `$46,000 + $4,000 + $17,500 − $9,500 = $58,000` ✓!)*

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Forward:** `Closing Capital = Opening Capital + Additional Capital + Net Profit (or − Net Loss) − Drawings`.
- **Reverse (Missing Profit):** `Net Profit = Closing Capital − Opening Capital + Drawings − Additional Capital`.
- **Capital T-Account:** **Credit** increases (Opening `b/d`, Added Capital, **Net Profit**); **Debit** decreases (**Net Loss**, **Drawings**)!
:::
