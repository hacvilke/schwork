:::info Essential Understanding
Profitability ratios read the **Income Statement**, but they don’t tell you whether a business can pay its bills next week — or whether the owner’s investment is earning more than a plain bank savings account! **Financial Position Ratios** inspect the **Balance Sheet** to test **Liquidity** (**Current Ratio** and **Acid-Test / Quick Ratio**) and **Investment Return** (**Return on Investment / Return on Capital Employed**).
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | **Current Ratio (`X : 1`)**, **Acid-Test / Quick Ratio (`X : 1`)**, and **Return on Investment / Capital Employed (`%`)** |
| **Exam Focus** | Subtracting **Closing Inventory** (and Prepayments) for the Acid-Test Ratio, expressing liquidity ratios strictly as **`X.XX : 1`**, and evaluating results against standard benchmarks |
| **Learning Outcome** | Calculate and interpret Current Ratio, Acid-Test Ratio, and Return on Investment (ROI / ROCE) cleanly from any Balance Sheet |

## 1. The Three Financial Position & Return Formulas

<FormulaCard
  title="Liquidity & Return Ratios (Memorize Exact Units!)"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Liquidity ratios compare short-term assets to short-term debts (X : 1); ROI/ROCE measures profit generated per $100 invested (%)."
>

$$
\text{Current Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}} \quad \text{(expressed as } X : 1 \text{)}
$$

$$
\text{Acid-Test (Quick) Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets} - \text{Closing Inventory} - \text{Prepayments}}{\text{Current Liabilities}} \quad \text{(expressed as } X : 1 \text{)}
$$

*(Note: When there are no prepayments, the numerator is simply `Trade Receivables + Bank + Cash` = `Current Assets − Closing Inventory`!)*

$$
\text{Return on Investment (ROI)} = \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Capital Invested}} \times 100 \quad (\%)
$$

*(Note: For **Return on Capital Employed (ROCE)**, divide Net Profit by **Capital Employed** = `Capital + Non-Current Liabilities` = `Total Net Assets`, or Opening/Average Capital as directed by the question!)*

</FormulaCard>

### Standard Benchmarks You Must Know!

| Ratio | Typical Healthy Benchmark | Too Low Warning | Too High Warning |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Current Ratio** | **`1.5 : 1` to `2 : 1`** | **Below `1 : 1`** — cannot cover short-term bills; Working Capital deficit! | **Above `3.5 : 1`** — excess idle cash, slow-moving stock, or uncollected debtors |
| **Acid-Test (Quick) Ratio** | **`1 : 1`** *(roughly `0.9:1` to `1.2:1`)* | **Below `0.8 : 1`** — urgent cash crunch; relies too heavily on selling slow stock | **Well above `2 : 1`** — too much idle cash earning zero return |
| **Return on Investment (ROI)** | **Higher than bank interest / rival firms** *(e.g. `12%–25%+`)* | **Below safe bank deposit rate (e.g. `< 5%`)** — owner takes business risk for less return than a safe bank account! | *(Higher is generally better — check debt risk!)* |

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Computing Current Ratio, Acid-Test Ratio & Return on Investment From a Balance Sheet"
  marks={12}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> You are given the year-end financial summary of
        <strong> CoralTech Supplies</strong> as at <strong>31 December 2026</strong>:
      </p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Non-Current Assets (NBV):</strong> $70,000</li>
        <li><strong>Current Assets:</strong> Closing Inventory $21,000; Trade Receivables $11,000; Bank $3,000; Cash $1,000</li>
        <li><strong>Current Liabilities:</strong> Trade Payables $14,000; Accrued Expenses $2,000; Short-Term Bank Overdraft $4,000</li>
        <li><strong>Opening Capital (1 Jan 2026):</strong> $66,000 &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Net Profit for 2026:</strong> $16,500 &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Drawings:</strong> $6,500 &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>5-Year Bank Loan:</strong> $10,000</li>
      </ul>
      <ol>
        <li>Calculate Total Current Assets, Total Current Liabilities, and Working Capital. <em>[3 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Calculate to 2 decimal places: (a) <strong>Current Ratio</strong>, (b) <strong>Acid-Test Ratio</strong>, (c) <strong>Return on Opening Capital (ROI)</strong>, and (d) <strong>Return on Capital Employed (ROCE)</strong>. <em>[6 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Evaluate CoralTech’s short-term liquidity and investment return clearly. <em>[3 marks]</em></li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

**1. Current Totals & Working Capital (3 marks):**
- `Total Current Assets` = `$21,000 + $11,000 + $3,000 + $1,000` = **$36,000** *(1 mark)*
- `Total Current Liabilities` = `$14,000 + $2,000 + $4,000` = **$20,000** *(1 mark)*
- `Working Capital` = `$36,000 − $20,000` = **$16,000** *(1 mark)*

**2. Financial Position & Return Ratios (6 marks):**
- **(a) Current Ratio:** `$36,000 ÷ $20,000` = **`1.80 : 1`** *(1.5 marks)*
- **(b) Acid-Test (Quick) Ratio:** `($36,000 − $21,000) ÷ $20,000` = `$15,000 ÷ $20,000` = **`0.75 : 1`** *(1.5 marks)*
- **(c) Return on Opening Capital (ROI):** `($16,500 ÷ $66,000) × 100` = **`25.00%`** *(1.5 marks)*
- **(d) Return on Capital Employed (ROCE):**
  - *Closing Capital* = `$66,000 + $16,500 − $6,500 = $76,000`
  - *Capital Employed* = `$76,000 + $10,000 Loan = $86,000` *(= NCA $70,000 + WC $16,000 ✓)*
  - `ROCE` = `($16,500 ÷ $86,000) × 100` = **`19.19%`** *(1.5 marks)*

**3. Evaluation of Liquidity & Return (3 marks):**
- **Liquidity:** On the surface, the **Current Ratio of `1.80 : 1`** looks comfortably inside the ideal `1.5:1–2:1` range *(1 mark)*. However, the **Acid-Test Ratio of `0.75 : 1`** sits **below the `1 : 1` safety mark** because **$21,000 (58% of all Current Assets!) is tied up in Closing Inventory** — CoralTech needs to sell stock faster or chase receivables to clear its $4,000 overdraft *(1 mark)*.
- **Return on Investment:** Both **ROI (`25.0%`)** and **ROCE (`19.19%`)** are **very strong**, comfortably beating normal bank savings interest rates and rewarding the owner well for capital invested *(1 mark)*.

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Current Ratio** = `CA ÷ CL` → write **`X.XX : 1`** (benchmark `1.5:1 – 2:1`).
- **Acid-Test Ratio** = `(CA − Stock [− Prepayments]) ÷ CL` → write **`X.XX : 1`** (benchmark `1 : 1`).
- **ROI / ROCE** = `(Net Profit ÷ Capital [or Capital Employed]) × 100` → write **`%`**!
:::
