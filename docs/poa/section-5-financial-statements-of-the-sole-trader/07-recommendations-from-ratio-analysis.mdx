:::info Essential Understanding
Calculating ratios is only **Step 1** — managers don’t get paid simply to write `"Acid-Test = 0.65 : 1"` on a page! The true value of accounting is **turning ratios into concrete, realistic business recommendations** — while also respecting the **limitations of ratio analysis** (like inflation, seasonal timing, and non-financial factors) so your advice stays sharp and grounded.
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | Linking every **weak ratio symptom** directly to its **root cause** and **2–3 specific management actions** + knowing the **limitations of ratio analysis** |
| **Exam Focus** | Answering Paper 2 advisory questions: *"Based on the ratios calculated above, recommend three specific actions the owner should take to improve profitability / liquidity"* |
| **Learning Outcome** | Diagnose business strengths and weaknesses from a full ratio scorecard and write tailored, practical management recommendations |

## 1. The Complete Ratio-to-Recommendation Playbook

Never write generic phrases like *"try to increase sales"* or *"improve the ratio"*! Examiners reward **specific operational levers** linked directly to the problem ratio:

| Weak Ratio Symptom | Specific Concrete Recommendations (Use These on Exam Day!) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Low or Falling Gross Profit Margin (`GP %`)** | 1. **Shop around / negotiate** bulk trade discounts with cheaper reliable suppliers.<br/>2. **Review selling prices** slightly upward on high-demand items.<br/>3. **Tighten stock security & storage** to eliminate shoplifting, wastage, and spoilage.<br/>4. Shift sales mix toward **higher-margin products**. |
| **Low Net Profit Margin (`NP %`)** *(especially when GP % is healthy!)* | 1. **Audit and trim operating overheads** (energy waste, unnecessary overtime, high shop rent, inefficient deliveries).<br/>2. **Improve credit vetting** to cut Bad Debts expense.<br/>3. Renegotiate high-interest overdrafts into lower-rate loans to slash finance costs. |
| **Slow Rate of Stock Turnover** | 1. Run **clearance discounts / bundle promotions** to turn dusty slow-moving stock into cash.<br/>2. **Buy smaller, more frequent inventory batches** (avoid over-ordering slow lines).<br/>3. Upgrade marketing & display of slow items; discontinue dead stock lines. |
| **Weak Liquidity (Current `< 1.5:1` or Acid-Test `< 1:1`)** | 1. **Chase overdue Trade Receivables faster** (send prompt reminders; offer `2%` early cash discounts).<br/>2. **Reduce excess Inventory** via promotions so stock converts back into cash.<br/>3. **Cut owner’s personal Drawings** or inject fresh Owner’s Capital.<br/>4. **Refinance short-term Bank Overdraft** into a Long-Term Loan (or sell unused fixed assets for cash). |
| **Low Return on Investment (`ROI / ROCE`)** | 1. Apply the GP % and NP % fixes above to **boost Net Profit**.<br/>2. **Sell idle, underused Non-Current Assets** (e.g., unused machinery/vans) so capital works harder.<br/>3. Reinvest profits only in high-return product lines. |

### Four Important Limitations of Ratio Analysis (Paper 2 Favorite!)
Even top analysts remember that ratios do NOT tell the whole story:
1. **Historical (Past) Data:** Ratios use last year’s figures — market conditions, competitors, or supplier prices may already have shifted today.
2. **Ignores Inflation:** Comparing 2020 sales to 2026 sales without adjusting for inflation makes dollar growth look artificially better.
3. **Different Accounting Policies:** Comparing two shops is tricky if Shop A uses straight-line depreciation and Shop B uses reducing-balance depreciation.
4. **Ignores Qualitative (Non-Financial) Factors:** Ratios cannot measure staff morale, customer loyalty, brand reputation, or a highway opening right outside the store next month!

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Writing a Complete Advisory Report From a Two-Year Ratio Scorecard"
  marks={12}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> You are reviewing the 2025 vs. 2026 Ratio Scorecard of
        <strong> BayView Furniture Mart</strong> (where safe local bank deposits pay <strong>6%</strong>):
      </p>
      <table>
        <thead>
          <tr><th>Ratio</th><th>2025</th><th>2026</th><th>Industry Average</th></tr>
        </thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Gross Profit Margin</td><td>44.0%</td><td>43.5%</td><td>42.0%</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Net Profit Margin</td><td>18.0%</td><td>9.5%</td><td>16.0%</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Rate of Stock Turnover</td><td>5.2 times</td><td>2.8 times</td><td>5.0 times</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Current Ratio</td><td>2.0 : 1</td><td>2.2 : 1</td><td>1.8 : 1</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Acid-Test (Quick) Ratio</td><td>1.1 : 1</td><td>0.58 : 1</td><td>1.0 : 1</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Return on Investment (ROI)</td><td>17.5%</td><td>8.0%</td><td>15.0%</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
      <ol>
        <li>Identify <strong>one clear strength</strong> and <strong>three specific weaknesses</strong> revealed by the scorecard. <em>[4 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Recommend <strong>four concrete, tailored actions</strong> the owner of BayView Furniture Mart should take in 2027 to turn the business around. <em>[4 marks]</em></li>
        <li>State <strong>two limitations</strong> the owner should bear in mind when interpreting these ratios. <em>[4 marks]</em></li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

**1. One Strength & Three Specific Weaknesses (4 marks — 1 mark each):**
- **Strength:** **Gross Profit Margin (`43.5%`)** stayed steady and beats the `42.0%` industry average — showing BayView’s pricing and supplier sourcing remain strong.
- **Weakness 1 (Runaway Operating Expenses):** Even with strong GP %, **Net Profit Margin nearly halved** from `18.0%` to `9.5%` (well below the `16.0%` industry average), showing expenses jumped from `26%` to `34%` of sales!
- **Weakness 2 (Severe Over-Stocking & Weak Liquidity):** **Stock Turnover crashed from `5.2` to `2.8 times`** while the **Acid-Test Ratio sank to `0.58 : 1`** (despite a `2.2 : 1` Current Ratio!) — proving cash is dangerously trapped in slow-moving furniture stock.
- **Weakness 3 (Falling Capital Efficiency):** **ROI plunged from `17.5%` to `8.0%`**, barely beating a risk-free `6%` bank deposit and trailing the `15%` industry norm.

**2. Four Concrete, Tailored Recommendations for 2027 (4 marks — 1 mark each):**
1. **Audit & Cut Overheads:** Review showroom rent, electricity, overtime wages, delivery van costs, and bad debts immediately to pull Operating Expenses back toward `26%` of sales.
2. **Clear Slow-Moving Stock:** Run promotional floor-model clearance sales on aging furniture to speed up Stock Turnover back toward `5 times/year` and turn dusty stock into cash.
3. **Adopt Leaner Reordering & Faster Credit Collection:** Order smaller, demand-driven stock shipments and offer `2%` early cash discounts to credit customers to lift the Acid-Test Ratio back above `1.0 : 1`.
4. **Pause High Drawings & Trim Idle Assets:** Limit owner Drawings temporarily and dispose of any unused delivery vehicles/equipment to boost cash liquidity and restore ROI above `15%`.

**3. Two Limitations of Ratio Analysis (4 marks — 2 marks each):**
1. **Historical & Snapshot Bias:** Ratios reflect past 2026 results and a single Dec 31 Balance Sheet date — if BayView just stocked up heavily on Dec 28 for a big January New Year’s sale, Stock Turnover and Acid-Test could look artificially gloomy *(2 marks)*.
2. **Ignores Qualitative Factors & Inflation:** Ratios omit vital non-financial clues (like furniture quality, showroom location, staff skill, or inflation distorting sales comparisons) and may differ across firms using different depreciation methods *(2 marks)*.

</WorkedExample>
