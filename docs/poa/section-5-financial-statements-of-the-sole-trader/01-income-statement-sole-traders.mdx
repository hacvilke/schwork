:::info Essential Understanding
The **Income Statement** (formerly called the *Trading and Profit & Loss Account*) is the financial scoreboard of a trading sole trader. Presented in clean **vertical 3-column format**, it answers two vital questions in exact sequence: first, *"How much **Gross Profit** did we earn strictly from buying and reselling goods?"* and second, *"After adding other income and paying all operating expenses, what is our bottom-line **Net Profit** (or Net Loss)?"*
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | Two-part vertical Income Statement (**Trading Section** → Gross Profit; **Profit & Loss Section** → Net Profit), exact **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)** math, and **Carriage Inwards vs. Carriage Outwards** placement |
| **Exam Focus** | Constructing a spotless vertical Sole-Trader Income Statement from a Trial Balance + Closing Inventory note without accidentally mixing in Balance Sheet items! |
| **Learning Outcome** | Draft a properly titled, arithmetically exact vertical Income Statement for any trading sole trader |

## 1. Exact Vertical Income Statement Formulas & Tricky Placements

<FormulaCard
  title="Master Income Statement Equations"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Compute Net Sales and Cost of Goods Sold first to find Gross Profit, then add Other Income and subtract Operating Expenses."
>

$$
\text{Net Sales (Turnover)} = \text{Sales} - \text{Returns Inwards}
$$

$$
\text{Net Purchases} = \text{Purchases} - \text{Returns Outwards} + \text{Carriage Inwards}
$$

$$
\text{Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)} = \text{Opening Inventory} + \text{Net Purchases} - \text{Closing Inventory}
$$

$$
\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Net Sales} - \text{Cost of Goods Sold}
$$

$$
\text{Net Profit} = \text{Gross Profit} + \text{Other Income} - \text{Operating Expenses}
$$

</FormulaCard>

### The Four Classic Exam Traps Inside an Income Statement

| Tricky Item | Exact Income Statement Slot | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Carriage Inwards** | **Trading Section** — added directly to Purchases inside COGS! | Transport paid to bring stock IN is part of the direct cost of acquiring goods for sale |
| **Carriage Outwards** | **Profit & Loss Section** — listed under **Operating Expenses**! | Shipping goods OUT to customers is a selling/distribution overhead, NOT a buying cost |
| **Discount Received** | **Add: Other Income** *(directly below Gross Profit)* | Prompt-payment reward earned from suppliers increases overall profit |
| **Discount Allowed** | **Less: Operating Expenses** | Prompt-payment allowance given to customers is a selling/finance cost |

:::warning Never Put Drawings or Trade Receivables in an Income Statement!
When drafting an Income Statement from a full Trial Balance, **leave out** every Balance Sheet account (Premises, Equipment, Debtors, Creditors, Bank, Cash, Loans, Capital, and Drawings)! Note one rare special adjustment: if the owner **took goods for personal use**, you deduct those **Goods for Own Use (Drawings)** from **Purchases** inside the Trading Section (and add them to Drawings on the Balance Sheet)!
:::

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Heading:** Always write **`Income Statement for the year ended [Date]`**!
- **Carriage Inwards** → add to Purchases (Trading Section); **Carriage Outwards** → Operating Expenses!
- **Discount Received** → Add below Gross Profit; **Discount Allowed** → Operating Expenses!
- Never mix **Drawings, Assets, or Liabilities** into an Income Statement!
:::
