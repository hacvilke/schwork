Imagine a busy wholesaler with **600 credit customers** in its Sales Ledger and **250 credit suppliers** in its Purchases Ledger! ## 1. What Are the Two Control Accounts & Why Do Businesses Use Them?

A **Control Account** is a summary account kept inside the **General Ledger** whose balance checks the total of all individual balances in a subsidiary personal ledger:
1. **Sales Ledger Control Account (SLCA)** *(Total Trade Receivables / Total Debtors Account)* — checks the **Sales Ledger**.
2. **Purchases Ledger Control Account (PLCA)** *(Total Trade Payables / Total Creditors Account)* — checks the **Purchases Ledger**.

### Four Core Purposes & Advantages (Paper 1 &amp; Paper 2 Favorite!)
1. **Fast Error Detection &amp; Localization:** If the SLCA balance (`$42,400`) does not match the sum of individual customer balances (`$41,650`), management immediately knows the `$750` error lives inside the **Sales Ledger** — instead of hunting blindly through the entire Trial Balance!
2. **Fraud Prevention (Segregation of Duties):** A **senior accountant** prepares the Control Account independently from Day Book totals, while **ledger clerks** post individual customer/supplier accounts — making it far harder for a clerk to steal cash and hide it by writing off a customer’s balance!
3. **Instant Totals for Final Accounts &amp; Trial Balance:** Instead of waiting to balance 600 individual customer accounts, management can pull Total Trade Receivables and Total Trade Payables straight off the Control Accounts in minutes.
4. **Sharper Credit Management:** Monitoring total receivables and payables monthly helps managers spot overdue debt spikes, control cash flow, and manage supplier payment terms proactively.

## 2. Exact Source Book for Every Control Account Item (Memorize!)

Remember the **#1 Golden Rule**: Control Account figures come from **Books of Original Entry** — NEVER copied from the individual customer/supplier ledgers (otherwise you’d copy the same mistake twice and ruin the independent check!):

| Control Account Entry | Exact Book of Original Entry Source |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Credit Sales** *(SLCA Dr)* | **Sales Journal** *(Sales Day Book)* |
| **Credit Purchases** *(PLCA Cr)* | **Purchases Journal** *(Purchases Day Book)* |
| **Returns Inwards** *(SLCA Cr)* | **Returns Inwards Journal** |
| **Returns Outwards** *(PLCA Dr)* | **Returns Outwards Journal** |
| **Cash / Cheques received from Debtors &amp; Discount Allowed** *(SLCA Cr)* | **Cash Book** *(Debit side)* |
| **Cash / Cheques paid to Suppliers &amp; Discount Received** *(PLCA Dr)* | **Cash Book** *(Credit side)* |
| **Dishonored Cheques from Customers** *(SLCA Dr)* | **Cash Book** *(Credit side)* or **General Journal** |
| **Cash Refunds to Customers / from Suppliers** | **Cash Book** |
| **Bad Debts Written Off** *(SLCA Cr)* | **General Journal** |
| **Interest Charged on Overdue Accounts** *(SLCA Dr / PLCA Cr)* | **General Journal** |
| **Set-Offs / Contras** *(between SLCA &amp; PLCA)* | **General Journal** |

## 5. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Mapping Source Books & Diagnosing a Control Account Discrepancy"
  marks={12}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> <strong>Caribbean Wholesale Co.</strong> maintains Sales and
        Purchases Ledger Control Accounts in its General Ledger.
      </p>
      <ol>
        <li>State the exact <strong>Book of Original Entry</strong> from which each of these six Control Account items is obtained: (a) Total Credit Sales, (b) Total Returns Outwards, (c) Cash Received from Debtors, (d) Discount Received from Suppliers, (e) Bad Debts Written Off, (f) Set-Off (Contra) between Sales &amp; Purchases Ledgers. <em>[6 marks]</em></li>
        <li>At 31 December 2026, the <strong>Sales Ledger Control Account</strong> showed a closing Debit balance of <strong>$42,400</strong>, while the sum of all individual customer balances in the Sales Ledger totaled only <strong>$41,650</strong> (a difference of <strong>$750</strong>). Suggest <strong>three specific errors</strong> that could explain this $750 gap. <em>[3 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Name <strong>three types of errors</strong> that Control Accounts CANNOT detect, even when the Control Account matches the individual ledger schedule perfectly. <em>[3 marks]</em></li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

**1. Exact Books of Original Entry (6 marks — 1 mark each):**
- **(a) Total Credit Sales:** **Sales Journal** *(Sales Day Book)*
- **(b) Total Returns Outwards:** **Returns Outwards Journal**
- **(c) Cash Received from Debtors:** **Cash Book** *(Debit side)*
- **(d) Discount Received from Suppliers:** **Cash Book** *(Credit side)*
- **(e) Bad Debts Written Off:** **General Journal**
- **(f) Set-Off (Contra):** **General Journal**

**2. Three Possible Causes of the `$750` Gap (`SLCA $42,400` vs. `Schedule $41,650`, 3 marks):**
1. A `$750` credit sale invoice was included in the Sales Journal total (entering SLCA), but **omitted** from the individual customer’s ledger account *(1 mark)*.
2. The **Sales Journal total was overcast by `$750`** when posted to the SLCA (making SLCA `$750` too high) *(1 mark)*.
3. An individual customer’s Debit balance of `$750` was **omitted when totaling the List of Sales Ledger Balances** *(or a `$750` credit receipt was posted twice in an individual customer’s account)* *(1 mark)*.

**3. Three Errors Control Accounts CANNOT Detect (3 marks — 1 mark each):**
1. **Error of Complete Omission** (invoice completely left out of Sales Journal and individual account).
2. **Error of Commission** (credit sale posted to the wrong customer’s account inside the Sales Ledger).
3. **Error of Original Entry** (wrong dollar amount written in the Sales Journal and posted equally to the customer’s account).

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **4 Uses:** Locate errors fast · Deter fraud (segregation of duties) · Quick Totals for Final Accounts · Better credit control.
- **Independent Source:** ALWAYS **Books of Original Entry** — NEVER individual personal ledgers!
- **Cannot Detect:** **Commission**, **Complete Omission**, and **Original Entry**!
:::
