:::info Essential Understanding
You finish your Trial Balance and celebrate because **Total Debits = Total Credits**! Wait — does a balanced Trial Balance guarantee there are **zero mistakes** in the books? **Absolutely NOT!** Six sneaky families of errors leave Debits equal to Credits — while another family breaks Trial Balance agreement immediately and requires a **Suspense Account**. Spotting which family an error belongs to is Step 1 of every Error Correction question!
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | The **6 Errors NOT Affecting Trial Balance (`COPCOR`)** vs. **5 Errors AFFECTING Trial Balance** (requiring Suspense) |
| **Exam Focus** | Naming the exact error from a 1-sentence business scenario (especially **Commission vs. Principle**!) and stating whether it affects Trial Balance agreement |
| **Learning Outcome** | Classify any bookkeeping mistake accurately by name and predict whether it will cause the Trial Balance to disagree |

## 1. Group A: The 6 Errors That Do NOT Affect Trial Balance Agreement (`COPCOR`)

Because equal Debit and Credit totals were still posted (even though the accounts, classes, sides, or amounts were wrong), the **Trial Balance STILL balances** — and you **NEVER use a Suspense Account** to fix them!

| Mnemonic | Error Name | Clear Definition | Classic Exam Example |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **C** | **Error of Commission** | Posted to the **wrong personal account** of the **SAME class** (e.g., wrong customer or wrong supplier) | Credit sale of `$500` to **A. Brown** posted to **B. Brown’s** account *(both are Debtors!)* |
| **O** | **Error of Complete Omission** | A transaction is **completely left out** of the books (neither Debit nor Credit entered) | Cash purchase of goods `$900` was completely forgotten and never recorded anywhere |
| **P** | **Error of Principle** | Posted to the **wrong CLASS of account**, breaking fundamental accounting rules (usually Asset vs. Expense!) | Buying a new Delivery Van `$15,000` debited to **Motor Repairs Expense** instead of **Motor Vehicles (Asset)** |
| **C** | **Compensating Errors** | Two (or more) **unrelated errors** accidentally cancel each other out by the exact same dollar amount | Sales Book overcast by `$100` (Cr `+$100`) AND Rent Expense overcast by `$100` (Dr `+$100`) |
| **O** | **Error of Original Entry** | The **wrong dollar figure** is written on the source book and posted identically to BOTH Debit and Credit | Invoice for `$470` entered as `$740` in Purchases Journal and posted as `$740` to BOTH Purchases and Supplier |
| **R** | **Complete Reversal of Entries** | Right accounts and right amount, but **Debit and Credit sides are swapped upside down** | Paid `$300` cash to Creditor K. Ali: wrongly **Debited Cash** and **Credited K. Ali**! |

---

## 2. Group B: Errors That DO Affect Trial Balance Agreement (Suspense Required!)

Whenever Debits and Credits become unequal, the **Trial Balance fails to agree** and you **MUST use a Suspense Account**:
1. **Single-Sided Entry (Partial Omission):** Recording the Debit half without the Credit half (or vice versa) — e.g. entering `$500` cash received in the Cash Book, but forgetting to credit the customer’s account.
2. **Casting (Addition) Error:** Adding up a Day Book (Sales Book, Purchases Book, Returns Book) or ledger column incorrectly (undercast = too low; overcast = too high).
3. **One-Sided Transposition / Wrong Amount:** Debiting Cash `$720` but crediting the Debtor `$270` (off by `$450`!).
4. **Posting Both Entries to the Same Side:** Debiting Bank `$400` AND Debiting Customer `$400` instead of crediting Customer (leaves Credits short by **`2 × $400 = $800`**!).
5. **Omitting a Ledger Balance (or listing it on the wrong side)** when copying balances into the Trial Balance.

        <li>A new office laser printer costing $1,600 (paid by cheque) was debited to <em>Stationery Expense</em>.</li>
        <li>The Sales Book total was overcast by $300, and the Electricity Expense account was also overcast by $300.</li>
        <li>A credit purchase of $670 from Apex Ltd was recorded as $760 in the Purchases Journal and posted as $760 to both Purchases and Apex Ltd.</li>
        <li>Cash of $400 received from credit customer M. Chin was debited to M. Chin’s account and credited to the Cash Account.</li>
        <li>The Sales Journal total was undercast (under-added) by $500 when posted to the Sales Account.</li>
        <li>A cheque for $920 paid for Shop Rent was credited as $920 in the Cash Book, but debited as $290 in the Rent Account.</li>
      </ol>
    </>

| # | Exact Error Name *(1 mark)* | Affects Trial Balance? *(1 mark)* | Quick Why |
| :-: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **1** | **Error of Commission** | **No** *(No Suspense)* | Wrong customer, same class (both Debtors); Dr still = Cr (`$850`) |
| **2** | **Error of Complete Omission** | **No** *(No Suspense)* | `$0` Dr and `$0` Cr entered → Trial Balance still agrees |
| **3** | **Error of Principle** | **No** *(No Suspense)* | Non-Current Asset (`Office Equipment`) wrongly put in Expense (`Stationery`) |
| **4** | **Compensating Errors** | **No** *(No Suspense)* | `+$300` Credit error accidentally cancels `+$300` Debit error |
| **5** | **Error of Original Entry** | **No** *(No Suspense)* | Wrong `$760` posted equally to BOTH Debit and Credit |
| **6** | **Complete Reversal of Entries** | **No** *(No Suspense)* | Right accounts &amp; `$400` amount, but Debit and Credit flipped |
| **7** | **Casting Error (Undercast)** | **YES** *(Uses Suspense!)* | Sales Credit is `$500` too low → Debits exceed Credits by `$500` |
| **8** | **Single-Sided Transposition Error** | **YES** *(Uses Suspense!)* | Rent Debit (`$290`) vs Bank Credit (`$920`) → Debits short by `$630` |

</WorkedExample>
