:::info Essential Understanding
At the end of every month, your business’s **Cash Book (Bank Column)** says you have **`$3,420`** in the bank — yet the monthly **Bank Statement** sent by your bank says **`$3,790`**! Did someone steal money? Almost never! Because businesses and banks record transactions at **different moments** (and banks process automatic fees and transfers before telling you!), the two balances rarely agree on Day 1. Learning the **exact 2 families of differences** makes Bank Reconciliation effortless!
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | **The Bank Statement Mirror Rule**, **Items on Bank Statement NOT Yet in Cash Book** (Standing Orders, Direct Debits, Credit Transfers, Bank Charges, Dishonored Cheques), and **Timing Differences** (Unpresented Cheques &amp; Late Lodgements) |
| **Exam Focus** | Sorting any discrepancy instantly into **Stage 1 (Update the Cash Book!)** vs. **Stage 2 (Put ONLY on the Bank Reconciliation Statement!)** and distinguishing **Standing Order** vs. **Direct Debit** |
| **Learning Outcome** | Explain why Cash Book and Bank Statement balances disagree and sort every item into its exact reconciliation stage |

## 1. First — Master the "Bank Statement Mirror Rule"!

Students get confused when a Bank Statement says **`Credit Balance $3,790`** while the Cash Book says **`Debit Balance $3,420`**. Why are they opposite?
- In **YOUR** books, cash in the bank is an **ASSET** → Increases are **Debits (`Dr`)**, Withdrawals are **Credits (`Cr`)**.
- In the **BANK’S** books, YOUR deposit is money the bank **OWES BACK TO YOU (a Liability!)** → Deposits are **Credits (`Cr`)**, Withdrawals are **Debits (`Dr`)**!

| Account Status | In YOUR Cash Book (Bank Col.) | On the BANK STATEMENT |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Favorable (Positive) Cash Balance** | **DEBIT (`Dr`)** *(Asset)* | **CREDIT (`Cr`)** *(Bank owes you!)* |
| **Bank Overdraft (Negative Balance)** | **CREDIT (`Cr`)** *(Liability)* | **DEBIT (`Dr`)** *(You owe the bank!)* |

## 2. The Two Families of Differences (Know Every Term!)

### Family 1: Items on Bank Statement NOT Yet in Cash Book → **UPDATE CASH BOOK!**
*(The bank already processed these — our Cash Book is simply behind!)*

| Item | Plain-English Meaning | Cash Book Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Credit Transfer / Direct Deposit** | A customer transfers money straight into our bank account electronically | **DEBIT** *(+Cash)* |
| **Dividends / Bank Interest Received** | Investments or savings pay cash directly into our bank account | **DEBIT** *(+Cash)* |
| **Bank Charges / Overdraft Interest** | Monthly service fees, cheque-book charges, or overdraft interest deducted by bank | **CREDIT** *(−Cash)* |
| **Standing Order (SO)** | Instruction by **US** for the bank to pay a **FIXED dollar amount** on a fixed date every month (e.g., `$600` rent or loan installment) | **CREDIT** *(−Cash)* |
| **Direct Debit (DD)** | Permission for a **SUPPLIER** to pull a **VARIABLE amount** from our account as bills change (e.g., electricity or phone bills) | **CREDIT** *(−Cash)* |
| **Dishonored (NSF / Bounced) Cheque** | A customer’s cheque we deposited bounced due to *"Insufficient Funds"* or signature error | **CREDIT** *(−Cash)* |
| **Cash Book Errors** | E.g., We wrote a `$430` cheque as `$340` in our Cash Book (or undercast Cash Book) | **Dr or Cr** *(fix error)* |

### Family 2: Timing Differences (In Cash Book, NOT Yet on Bank Statement) → **BANK RECONCILIATION STATEMENT ONLY!**
*(Our Cash Book is ALREADY right — the bank just hasn’t processed these yet! NEVER put these in the Adjusted Cash Book!)*

| Timing Difference | Plain-English Meaning | Effect on Bank Reconciliation |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Unpresented Cheques** *(Outstanding Cheques)* | Cheques WE wrote and mailed to suppliers (credited in our Cash Book), which the suppliers have **not yet cashed** at the bank | **ADD** to Adjusted Cash Book Balance |
| **Late Lodgements** *(Uncredited Deposits / Deposits in Transit)* | Cash/cheques WE deposited near month-end (debited in our Cash Book) that have **not yet cleared** onto the Bank Statement | **SUBTRACT** from Adjusted Cash Book Balance |
| **Bank Errors** *(Rare)* | E.g., Bank accidentally charged another company’s cheque to our account | Adjust on **Bank Reconciliation Statement** *(notify bank!)* |

## 5. Worked Example

<WorkedExample

      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> On <strong>31 October 2026</strong>, <strong>BreezeTech Services</strong>’
        Cash Book (Bank Column) showed a <strong>Debit balance of $3,420</strong>, while its

      </p>
      <ol>
        <li>Credit transfer from customer R. Clarke appearing on Bank Statement only: <strong>$650</strong>.</li>
        <li>Dividend received directly into the bank account: <strong>$180</strong>.</li>
        <li>Bank charges shown on the Bank Statement only: <strong>$70</strong>.</li>
        <li>Standing order for shop insurance paid by the bank: <strong>$320</strong>.</li>
        <li>Dishonored cheque from customer T. Mills shown on Bank Statement: <strong>$250</strong>.</li>
        <li>Cheques written to suppliers not yet presented to bank: #104 ($540) and #107 ($260) = <strong>$800</strong>.</li>
        <li>Cash &amp; cheques deposited late on 31 October not yet credited by bank: <strong>$620</strong>.</li>
      </ol>
      <p>
        <strong>Required:</strong> (a) Classify all 7 items into <strong>Stage 1 (Adjusted Cash Book: Dr or Cr)</strong>
        vs. <strong>Stage 2 (Bank Reconciliation Statement: Add or Less)</strong> <em>[7 marks]</em>;
        (b) Prove mathematically that these 7 items explain the entire gap between <strong>$3,420</strong> and <strong>$3,790</strong> <em>[5 marks]</em>.
      </p>
    </>
  }
>

### (a) Classification Table *(7 marks — 1 mark each)*

| # | Item | Amount ($) | Stage | Exact Treatment |
| :-: | :--- | ---: | :---: | :--- |
| **1** | Credit Transfer (R. Clarke) | 650 | **Stage 1** | **DEBIT** Adjusted Cash Book *(+Cash)* |
| **2** | Dividend Received | 180 | **Stage 1** | **DEBIT** Adjusted Cash Book *(+Cash)* |
| **3** | Bank Charges | 70 | **Stage 1** | **CREDIT** Adjusted Cash Book *(−Cash)* |
| **4** | Standing Order (Insurance) | 320 | **Stage 1** | **CREDIT** Adjusted Cash Book *(−Cash)* |
| **5** | Dishonored Cheque (T. Mills) | 250 | **Stage 1** | **CREDIT** Adjusted Cash Book *(−Cash)* |
| **6** | Unpresented Cheques (#104, #107) | 800 | **Stage 2** | **ADD** on Bank Reconciliation Statement |
| **7** | Late Lodgement (31 Oct) | 620 | **Stage 2** | **SUBTRACT** on Bank Reconciliation Statement |

### (b) Mathematical Reconciliation Proof *(5 marks)*
- **Stage 1 — True Adjusted Cash Book Balance:**
  `$3,420 + ($650 + $180) − ($70 + $320 + $250)` = `$3,420 + $830 − $640` = **`$3,610` (Debit)** *(3 marks)*
- **Stage 2 — Bridge to Bank Statement Balance:**
  `$3,610 + $800 (Unpresented) − $620 (Late Lodgement)` = **`$3,790` (Credit)** = **Exact Bank Statement Balance ✓!** *(2 marks)*

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Mirror Rule:** Positive = **Dr** in Cash Book, **Cr** on Bank Statement; Overdraft = **Cr** in Cash Book, **Dr** on Bank Statement.
- **Stage 1 (Adj. CB):** Credit Transfers, Dividends/Interest (`Dr`) · Bank Charges, Standing Orders, Direct Debits, Dishonored Cheques, CB Errors (`Cr/Dr`).
- **Stage 2 (BRS ONLY):** **+ Unpresented Cheques**, **− Late Lodgements**!
:::
