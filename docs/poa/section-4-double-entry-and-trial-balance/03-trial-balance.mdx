## 1. What Is a Trial Balance & Why Do We Prepare It?

A **Trial Balance** is a **two-column schedule** (Debit column and Credit column) listing the **`Balance b/d`** of every open ledger account — including Cash and Bank from the Cash Book — on a specific date (for example, *"Trial Balance as at 31 May 2026"*).

*(Important exam fact: A Trial Balance is **NOT** a ledger account and **NOT** a formal published Financial Statement — it is an internal **working check** prepared right between the Ledgers and the Final Accounts!)*

### Three Main Uses of a Trial Balance
1. **Checks Arithmetical Accuracy of Double Entry:** Since every transaction was posted with an equal Debit and Credit, the grand sum of all Debit balances **MUST equal** the grand sum of all Credit balances. If they disagree, you know immediately that an error occurred!
2. **Blueprint for Final Accounts:** It gathers every single account balance from the General Ledger, Sales Ledger, Purchases Ledger, and Cash Book onto **one clean page**, making it quick and orderly to draft the **Trading and Profit & Loss Account** and **Balance Sheet**.
3. **Helps Locate One-Sided Posting Errors Early:** Finding an arithmetic mistake at month-end is far easier than hunting through 12 months of entries at year-end!

---

## 2. Exact Column Guide: Debit Column vs Credit Column

Every single account lands in the exact same column as its normal **`Balance b/d`** (straight from **`DEAD CLIC`**!). Memorise the tricky accounts marked with `★`:

| Always in the **DEBIT (Dr)** Column | Always in the **CREDIT (Cr)** Column |
| :--- | :--- |
| All **Assets** (Premises, Machinery, Vans, Equipment, Debtors, Cash, Positive Cash at Bank) | **Capital** *(Owner's Equity)* |
| **Opening Stock (1 Jan)** `★` | **Sales** *(Revenue)* |
| **Purchases** & **Carriage Inwards** `★` | **Returns Outwards** *(Purchases Returns)* `★` |
| **Returns Inwards** *(Sales Returns)* `★` | All **Liabilities** (Creditors, **Bank Overdraft** `★`, Bank Loan, Mortgage, Debentures) |
| All **Expenses** (Rent, Wages, Electricity, Insurance, Carriage Outwards, Bad Debts, Depreciation) | **Discount Received** & Other Incomes (Rent Received, Commission Received) `★` |
| **Discount Allowed** `★` | **Provision for Depreciation** `★` *(contra-asset!)* |
| **Drawings** `★` | **Provision for Doubtful Debts** `★` *(contra-asset!)* |

:::warning Classic Exam Trap: Opening Stock vs Closing Stock!
- **Opening Stock** (stock left over from last year) sits **INSIDE** the Trial Balance in the **DEBIT column**!
- **Closing Stock** (counted on the last day of *this* year) is normally **NOT inside** the Trial Balance — it appears as a **note underneath** the Trial Balance! *(Why? Because Closing Stock has not yet been journalised into the Ledger before the Trading Account is prepared!)*
:::

## 4. What If a Trial Balance Does NOT Balance? (Fast Detective Tricks!)

If Total Debits ≠ Total Credits, a **one-sided error** happened (such as posting only the Debit and forgetting the Credit, entering J\$500 on Dr and J\$5,000 on Cr, putting a balance in the wrong Trial Balance column, or mis-adding a column). Try these **three fast checks** in order:
1. **Re-add both Trial Balance columns** cleanly from bottom to top.
2. **Divide the difference by 2:** If your Trial Balance is out by **J\$8,000**, look for a balance of **J\$4,000** placed in the wrong column (putting a J\$4,000 Debit item into Credit swings the gap by $2 \times 4{,}000 = 8{,}000$!).
3. **Check if the difference divides evenly by 9:** If the gap is divisible by 9 (e.g. J\$270 or J\$1,800), look for a **transposition error** (swapping two adjacent digits, such as writing `740` instead of `470` → difference `270`!).
*(If an imbalance cannot be found before publishing statements, the temporary difference is placed in a **Suspense Account** — mastered in Section 7.3!)*

## 5. Limitations of the Trial Balance (6 Errors It CANNOT Catch!)

A balancing Trial Balance is **NOT absolute proof** that the books are 100% accurate — it only proves that `Total Debits = Total Credits`. Six classic **two-sided errors** leave the Trial Balance agreeing perfectly! (You will fix these with General Journal entries in Section 7.3 — learn their exact names now!)

| Error Type | Clear Definition | Concrete Exam Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Error of Omission** | A transaction is **completely left out** of the books — neither the Debit nor the Credit was entered. | Forgot entirely to record a J\$5,000 cash sale in both Cash and Sales. |
| **2. Error of Commission** | Entry is posted to the **wrong person's account** within the **same class** of account (same side, same amount). | Posted a J\$12,000 credit sale to *N. Lewis* instead of *M. Lewis* (both are Debtors in the Sales Ledger!). |
| **3. Error of Principle** | Entry is posted to the **wrong CLASS of account**, violating an accounting rule (e.g. mixing up a Real Asset with a Nominal Expense!). | Bought a new J\$75,000 photocopier and debited **Purchases** (or *Office Repairs*) instead of **Office Equipment**! |
| **4. Error of Original Entry** | The **wrong figure** was written on the source document/Day Book from the very start, so the same wrong amount was posted to BOTH Dr and Cr. | Recorded a J\$615 credit purchase as **J\$651** in Purchases (Dr) and the supplier's account (Cr). |
| **5. Complete Reversal of Entries** | Correct accounts and correct amount, but the **Debit and Credit sides were swapped**! | For a J\$10,000 cash sale, the clerk debited **Sales** and credited **Cash** instead of Dr Cash, Cr Sales. |
| **6. Compensating Errors** | Two or more **unrelated errors** of the exact same dollar value happen on opposite sides and accidentally **cancel each other out**! | Overcast (over-added) the Purchases account (Dr) by J\$500 AND overcast the Sales account (Cr) by J\$500. |

*(Memory aid for the 6 errors: **O – C – P – O – R – C** — Omission, Commission, Principle, Original Entry, Reversal, Compensating!)*
