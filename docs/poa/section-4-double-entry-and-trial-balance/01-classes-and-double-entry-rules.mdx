## 1. Classification of Accounts: Personal, Real & Nominal

Every account in the entire accounting system is first split into **Personal Accounts** (people and organisations) vs **Impersonal Accounts** (everything else — further divided into **Real** and **Nominal**):

| Account Class | Plain-English Definition | Clear Examples | Balance Carried Forward to Next Year? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **1. Personal Accounts** | Accounts of **people, businesses, and institutions** connected to the firm — specifically **Debtors (Accounts Receivable)**, **Creditors (Accounts Payable)**, lenders, and the **Owner** (*Capital* & *Drawings*). | M. Gayle *(Debtor)*, K. Ellis *(Creditor)*, Kingston Wholesalers, NCB Bank Loan, Capital, Drawings. | **Yes** *(except Drawings, which closes to Capital!)* |
| **2. Real Accounts** *(Impersonal)* | Accounts of **tangible & intangible Assets** owned by the business *(everything owned EXCEPT debtors)*. | Premises (Land & Buildings), Plant & Machinery, Motor Vehicles, Furniture & Fittings, Stock (Inventory), Cash, Bank, Goodwill. | **Yes** *(Permanent Balance Sheet accounts!)* |
| **3. Nominal Accounts** *(Impersonal)* | Temporary accounts recording **Revenues (Income), Expenses, Gains, and Losses** used solely to measure profit for the year. | Sales, Purchases, Returns Inwards, Returns Outwards, Carriage Inwards/Outwards, Rent, Wages, Electricity, Insurance, Discount Allowed/Received, Bad Debts, Depreciation. | **No!** Closed to the Trading & P&L Account at year-end (reset to J\$0). |

:::tip Quick Classification Trick!
Ask two rapid questions in order:
1. **Is it a person, customer, supplier, lender, or owner?** → **Personal Account!**
2. **Is it an asset you own (building, van, cash, stock)?** → **Real Account!**
3. **Is it an income, purchase, sale, return, discount, or bill/expense?** → **Nominal Account!**
:::

## 2. The Three Ledgers (Subdivision of the Ledger)

In a real business with 400 credit customers and 80 suppliers, keeping every account in a single binder would cause chaos and stop multiple clerks from working at once. Accountants therefore divide the Ledger into **three specialised Ledgers**:

| Ledger Name | Alternative Syllabus Name | Which Accounts Live Inside? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Sales Ledger** | *Debtors Ledger / Accounts Receivable Ledger* | Individual **Personal accounts of credit customers (Debtors)** — e.g. *M. Gayle*, *A. Shaw*, *D. Grant*. |
| **2. Purchases Ledger** | *Creditors Ledger / Accounts Payable Ledger* | Individual **Personal accounts of credit suppliers (Creditors)** — e.g. *K. Ellis*, *Kingston Wholesalers*. |
| **3. General Ledger** | *Main / Nominal & Real Ledger* | **Everything else!** Every **Real account** (Premises, Equipment, Motor Vehicles, Stock), every **Nominal account** (Sales, Purchases, Returns, Rent, Wages, Depreciation), plus **Capital**, **Drawings**, and **Bank Loans**. |

*(Remember: Cash and Bank live inside the **Cash Book**, which is treated as part of the Ledger system!)*

## 3. Why Debit & Credit Rules Work (From $A = C + L$ to `DEAD CLIC`!)

Students often think "Debit" means "bad" or "Credit" means "good" — **erase that myth immediately!** In accounting:
- **Debit (Dr)** simply means **LEFT-HAND SIDE** of an account.
- **Credit (Cr)** simply means **RIGHT-HAND SIDE** of an account.

Look at the expanded Accounting Equation with everything positive arranged on its natural side:

$$
\underbrace{\text{Assets} + \text{Expenses} + \text{Drawings}}_{\textbf{LEFT SIDE (DEBIT to increase!)}} \;=\; \underbrace{\text{Liabilities} + \text{Income (Revenue)} + \text{Capital}}_{\textbf{RIGHT SIDE (CREDIT to increase!)}}
$$

THAT is why the famous **`DEAD CLIC`** rule works — it isn't random memorisation, it comes directly from the left and right sides of the Accounting Equation!

| Account Type | To **INCREASE (+)** | To **DECREASE (−)** | Normal Closing Balance (`b/d`) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Expenses** *(incl. Purchases, Returns Inwards, Discount Allowed)* | **DEBIT (Dr)** | **CREDIT (Cr)** | **Debit** |
| **Assets** *(incl. Debtors, Cash, Positive Bank, Equipment)* | **DEBIT (Dr)** | **CREDIT (Cr)** | **Debit** |
| **Drawings** *(owner's personal withdrawals)* | **DEBIT (Dr)** | **CREDIT (Cr)** | **Debit** |
| **Liabilities** *(incl. Creditors, Bank Overdraft, Loans)* | **CREDIT (Cr)** | **DEBIT (Dr)** | **Credit** |
| **Income / Revenues** *(incl. Sales, Returns Outwards, Discount Received)* | **CREDIT (Cr)** | **DEBIT (Dr)** | **Credit** |
| **Capital** *(Owner's Equity)* | **CREDIT (Cr)** | **DEBIT (Dr)** | **Credit** |

:::tip Memorise `DEAD CLIC` Forever!
- **D – E – A – D**: **D**ebit increases **E**xpenses, **A**ssets, and **D**rawings!
- **C – L – I – C**: **C**redit increases **L**iabilities, **I**ncome (Revenues), and **C**apital!
*(And to DECREASE any category, simply do the exact opposite!)*
:::

## 4. Complete 12-Transaction Double-Entry Reference Table

Study how to break down **any** exam transaction in 3 quick steps:
1. Identify the **two accounts** involved.
2. Decide what **category** each account belongs to (Asset, Liability, Capital, Income, Expense, Drawings) and whether it is **increasing (↑)** or **decreasing (↓)**.
3. Apply **`DEAD CLIC`** to pick **Dr** and **Cr**!

| # | Everyday Business Transaction | Account Debited (Dr) | Why Dr? | Account Credited (Cr) | Why Cr? |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Owner invests J\$200,000 into business bank | **Bank** | Asset ↑ | **Capital** | Capital ↑ |
| **2** | Bought delivery van by cheque J\$90,000 | **Motor Vehicles** | Asset ↑ | **Bank** | Asset ↓ |
| **3** | Bought machinery **on credit** from Ace Tech | **Machinery** | Asset ↑ | **Ace Tech** | Liability ↑ |
| **4** | Bought goods for resale for **cash** | **Purchases** | Expense ↑ | **Cash** | Asset ↓ |
| **5** | Bought goods for resale **on credit** from K. Ellis | **Purchases** | Expense ↑ | **K. Ellis** | Liability ↑ |
| **6** | Returned faulty goods to supplier K. Ellis | **K. Ellis** | Liability ↓ | **Returns Outwards** | Reduces Purchases (Cr) |
| **7** | Sold goods for **cash** | **Cash** | Asset ↑ | **Sales** | Income ↑ |
| **8** | Sold goods **on credit** to M. Gayle | **M. Gayle** | Asset (Debtor) ↑ | **Sales** | Income ↑ |
| **9** | Customer M. Gayle returned damaged goods | **Returns Inwards** | Reduces Sales (Dr) | **M. Gayle** | Asset (Debtor) ↓ |
| **10** | Paid supplier K. Ellis by cheque | **K. Ellis** | Liability ↓ | **Bank** | Asset ↓ |
| **11** | Received cheque from customer M. Gayle | **Bank** | Asset ↑ | **M. Gayle** | Asset (Debtor) ↓ |
| **12** | Paid electricity bill by cheque / Owner took cash Drawings | **Electricity** / **Drawings** | Expense ↑ / Drawings ↑ | **Bank** / **Cash** | Asset ↓ |

*(Bonus trap: When an owner takes **GOODS (stock)** for personal use, debit **Drawings** and credit **Purchases** — NOT Sales, because no profit was earned selling to yourself!)*

## 5. Past-Paper-Style Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Analysing Transactions into Account Debited, Account Credited & Ledger"
  marks={8}
  problem={
    <>
      For each of the following four transactions of <strong>Reid Furnishings</strong>, state (i) the <strong>Account to be Debited</strong>, (ii) the <strong>Account to be Credited</strong>, and (iii) the <strong>Ledger</strong> in which the personal account (if any) is kept:
      <br />
      1. Sold chairs on credit to <strong>L. Henry</strong> for J$48,000.
      <br />
      2. Returned damaged wood worth J$7,500 to credit supplier <strong>Caribbean Timber Ltd</strong>.
      <br />
      3. Paid <strong>Caribbean Timber Ltd</strong> J$25,000 by cheque.
      <br />
      4. Owner withdrew chairs costing J$12,000 for her own living room.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Never confuse Returns Inwards with Returns Outwards! Think physically from YOUR shop's doorway: goods coming BACK IN from a customer = Returns INwards (Dr); goods going BACK OUT to a supplier = Returns OUTwards (Cr)!"
>

| # | Account Debited (Dr) | Account Credited (Cr) | Ledger for Personal Account |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **L. Henry** *(Debtor ↑)* | **Sales** *(Revenue ↑)* | **L. Henry** → **Sales Ledger** *(Sales → General Ledger)* |
| **2** | **Caribbean Timber Ltd** *(Creditor ↓)* | **Returns Outwards** *(Cr)* | **Caribbean Timber Ltd** → **Purchases Ledger** |
| **3** | **Caribbean Timber Ltd** *(Creditor ↓)* | **Bank** *(Asset ↓)* | **Caribbean Timber Ltd** → **Purchases Ledger** *(Bank → Cash Book)* |
| **4** | **Drawings** *(Drawings ↑)* | **Purchases** *(Stock cost ↓)* | Both **Drawings** & **Purchases** → **General Ledger** |

</WorkedExample>
