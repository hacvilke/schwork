## 1. Classification of Accounts: Real, Personal & Nominal

Every single account in a business's books belongs to one of **three classes**. Memorise this simple table — it shows up constantly on Paper 1 and Paper 2!

| Class of Account | Kid-Friendly Definition | Everyday Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Real Accounts** | **Physical & financial Assets** — things the business actually owns (except debtors). | Premises (Land & Buildings), Machinery, Motor Vehicles, Furniture & Fittings, Stock, Cash, Bank. |
| **2. Personal Accounts** | **People, businesses, and institutions** connected to the firm — especially **Debtors (Accounts Receivable)**, **Creditors (Accounts Payable)**, and the **Owner** (Capital & Drawings). | K. Ellis (Creditor), M. Gayle (Debtor), Jamaica Wholesalers, Capital, Drawings, Bank Loan (lender). |
| **3. Nominal Accounts** | **Income, Revenues, Expenses, Gains, and Losses** — temporary accounts that exist only to measure profit for the year. | Sales, Purchases, Returns Inwards, Returns Outwards, Rent, Electricity, Wages, Insurance, Discount Allowed, Discount Received, Depreciation, Bad Debts. |

*(Note: Real and Nominal accounts together are often called **Impersonal Accounts**, while accounts of people/firms are **Personal Accounts**.)*

---

## 2. The Three Ledgers (Where Each Account Lives)

Instead of cramming every customer and supplier into one giant book, businesses split the Ledger into three neat volumes:

| Ledger | Which Accounts Live Inside? |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Sales Ledger** *(Debtors / Accounts Receivable Ledger)* | Individual **Personal accounts of credit customers (Debtors)** — e.g. *M. Gayle*, *A. Shaw*. |
| **Purchases Ledger** *(Creditors / Accounts Payable Ledger)* | Individual **Personal accounts of credit suppliers (Creditors)** — e.g. *K. Ellis*, *Kingston Wholesalers*. |
| **General Ledger** *(Main Ledger)* | **All other accounts** — every **Real account** (Premises, Vans, Equipment), every **Nominal account** (Sales, Purchases, Rent, Wages, Depreciation), plus **Capital**, **Drawings**, and **Bank Loans**. |

---

## 3. The Golden Double Entry Table (Debit vs Credit)

In **Double Entry Bookkeeping**, every transaction has **two equal sides**: one **Debit (Dr — left side)** and one **Credit (Cr — right side)**.

Look closely at the clean Increase / Decrease rules — they come straight from the Accounting Equation ($A = C + L$):

| Account Type | To **INCREASE** (+) | To **DECREASE** (−) | Normal Balance |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Assets** *(including Debtors / Accounts Receivable)* | **DEBIT (Dr)** | **CREDIT (Cr)** | **Debit** |
| **Expenses** *(including Purchases & Returns Inwards)* | **DEBIT (Dr)** | **CREDIT (Cr)** | **Debit** |
| **Drawings** *(owner takes cash/goods out)* | **DEBIT (Dr)** | **CREDIT (Cr)** | **Debit** |
| **Liabilities** *(including Creditors / Accounts Payable)* | **CREDIT (Cr)** | **DEBIT (Dr)** | **Credit** |
| **Capital** *(Owner's Equity)* | **CREDIT (Cr)** | **DEBIT (Dr)** | **Credit** |
| **Revenues / Income** *(including Sales & Returns Outwards)* | **CREDIT (Cr)** | **DEBIT (Dr)** | **Credit** |

:::tip Unforgettable Exam Memory Trick: `DEAD CLIC`!
- **D – E – A – D** (**D**ebit increases: **E**xpenses, **A**ssets, **D**rawings!)
- **C – L – I – C** (**C**redit increases: **L**iabilities, **I**ncome/Revenues, **C**apital!)
:::

### Quick Walkthrough on Debtors & Creditors
- **Debtor (Asset):** When a customer buys on credit, they owe us *more* (Asset ↑ → **Dr Debtor**). When they pay us or return goods, they owe us *less* (Asset ↓ → **Cr Debtor**).
- **Creditor (Liability):** When we buy goods on credit, we owe the supplier *more* (Liability ↑ → **Cr Creditor**). When we pay them or return goods, we owe them *less* (Liability ↓ → **Dr Creditor**).
