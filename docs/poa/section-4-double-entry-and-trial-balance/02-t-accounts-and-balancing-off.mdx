## 1. Anatomy of a Ledger T-Account (And the Opposite-Account Rule!)

A **T-Account** gets its name from its clean `T` shape dividing the page down the middle:
- **Account Name** centred neatly across the top bar (e.g. *Bank Account*, *M. Gayle Account*).
- **Left Half = Debit (`Dr`)** with four neat columns: `Date` | `Details` | `Folio` | `J\$`
- **Right Half = Credit (`Cr`)** with the same four columns: `Date` | `Details` | `Folio` | `J\$`

### The Golden "Details" Rule (Never Write an Account's Own Name Inside Itself!)
Students new to T-accounts sometimes write `"Bank"` inside the Bank Account — **never do that!** Inside every T-account, the `Details` column **ALWAYS names the OTHER (corresponding) account** that completes double entry:
- **Example:** Bought goods on credit from **K. Ellis** for **J\$54,000** on May 2:
  - Inside **Purchases Account (Debit side):** `May 2 | K. Ellis | PL 1 | 54,000`
  - Inside **K. Ellis Account (Credit side):** `May 2 | Purchases | GL 2 | 54,000`

*(Why? Because anyone reading K. Ellis's account can immediately see WHY we owe him J\$54,000 — it came from **Purchases**!)*

### Three-Column Running Balance Format (Modern Software Awareness!)
In computerised accounting (and some exam questions), ledger accounts are shown in a **Three-Column Running Balance** table (`Date | Details | Dr | Cr | Balance`) where the updated balance is recalculated after every single transaction instead of waiting for month-end. The Debit and Credit rules are **100% identical** to T-accounts!

## 2. How to Balance Off an Account in 4 Foolproof Steps (`c/d` → `b/d`)

The May/June 2025 Subject Report noted that **more than half of candidates still lose marks balancing accounts**, either putting `Balance c/d` on the wrong side or forgetting to bring `Balance b/d` down below the totals! Follow these 4 steps strictly:

1. **Add up the Debit side and the Credit side** separately on rough paper and find which side has the **LARGER total**.
2. Draw horizontal lines on the **same level** on both sides and write that **LARGER total** in both total boxes, with a **double underline (`===`)** beneath each total.
3. On the **SMALLER side** (right *above* the total line, dated the **last day of the month**), insert the exact missing difference labelled **`Balance c/d`** (*carried down* — closing off this month).
4. On the **OPPOSITE (BIGGER) side** (right *below* the double-underlined total, dated the **1st day of the next month**), write that exact same amount labelled **`Balance b/d`** (*brought down* — opening next month!).

### Two Quick Shortcuts That Save Exam Time!
- **Only ONE entry in the account?** You don't even have to panic — if the only entry is May 5 `Shop Fittings Dr 35,000`, write `May 31 Balance c/d 35,000` on Credit, total both sides at `35,000`, and bring `June 1 Balance b/d 35,000` down on Debit!
- **Both sides ALREADY equal each other?** (e.g. a customer owed J\$20,000 and paid all J\$20,000 in full!) There is **NO `Balance c/d` or `b/d`** — simply double-underline `20,000` on both sides! The account is settled (**nil balance**).

### Balancing Off vs Closing Off (Month-End vs Year-End!)
- **During the year (month-end):** All accounts can be balanced off with `Balance c/d` and `Balance b/d` to extract a monthly Trial Balance.
- **At YEAR-END (31 December):**
  - **Real & Personal accounts** *(Assets, Liabilities, Debtors, Creditors, Capital)* are **BALANCED OFF** with `Balance c/d` and `Balance b/d` — their balances carry forward onto the Balance Sheet and into next year!
  - **Nominal accounts** *(Sales, Purchases, Returns, Rent, Wages, Electricity, Discounts)* are **CLOSED OFF** by transferring their balance directly to the **`Trading Account`** or **`Profit & Loss Account`** (so they reset cleanly to **J\$0** for next year!).

## 3. Interpreting Every `Balance b/d` (Paper 2 Marks!)

An account's balance is **ALWAYS named after the side where `Balance b/d` sits** (below the totals — NEVER after `Balance c/d`!):

| Account | If `Balance b/d` Is on **DEBIT (Left)** | If `Balance b/d` Is on **CREDIT (Right)** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cash Account** | Physical cash held in the office till (*Current Asset*). | **Impossible!** You cannot pay out more cash than you have. |
| **Bank Account** | Positive cash deposited in the bank (*Current Asset*). | **Bank Overdraft** — money owed TO the bank (*Current Liability*). |
| **Customer (Debtor) Account** | Normal! Amount the customer still owes us (*Current Asset*). | Unusual credit balance (e.g. customer overpaid or returned goods after paying — we owe them a refund!). |
| **Supplier (Creditor) Account** | Unusual debit balance (e.g. we overpaid or returned goods after paying — supplier owes us!). | Normal! Amount we still owe the supplier (*Current Liability*). |
| **Capital Account** | Severe capital deficiency (business insolvent). | Normal! Owner's net equity invested in the firm. |
| **Sales / Purchases / Expense** | Normal for **Purchases, Expenses, Drawings, Returns Inwards**. | Normal for **Sales, Other Revenues, Returns Outwards**. |

## 4. Worked Example: Complete Set of Balanced T-Accounts (*D. Fraser Books*)

Follow all eight May 2026 transactions of **D. Fraser Books** from double-entry analysis right into balanced T-accounts — we will extract every single `Balance b/d` straight into the **Trial Balance** in Lesson 4.3!

| Date | Transaction | Account Debited (Dr) | Account Credited (Cr) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| May 1 | Owner D. Fraser put **J\$150,000** into the business bank | Bank *(Asset ↑)* | Capital *(Capital ↑)* |
| May 2 | Bought goods on credit from **K. Ellis** for **J\$54,000** | Purchases *(Expense ↑)* | K. Ellis *(Creditor ↑)* |
| May 5 | Bought Shop Fittings by cheque **J\$35,000** | Shop Fittings *(Asset ↑)* | Bank *(Asset ↓)* |
| May 9 | Sold goods on credit to **M. Gayle** for **J\$68,000** | M. Gayle *(Debtor ↑)* | Sales *(Revenue ↑)* |
| May 14 | Paid **K. Ellis** **J\$30,000** by cheque | K. Ellis *(Creditor ↓)* | Bank *(Asset ↓)* |
| May 18 | Received cheque **J\$42,000** from **M. Gayle** | Bank *(Asset ↑)* | M. Gayle *(Debtor ↓)* |
| May 24 | Paid shop rent by cheque **J\$18,000** | Rent *(Expense ↑)* | Bank *(Asset ↓)* |
| May 28 | Owner withdrew **J\$12,000** by cheque for private use | Drawings *(Drawings ↑)* | Bank *(Asset ↓)* |
