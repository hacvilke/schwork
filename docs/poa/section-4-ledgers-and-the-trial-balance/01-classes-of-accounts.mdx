:::info Essential Understanding
Before you can enter a single number into a ledger, you must know **what kind of account** you are holding! Every account in the entire accounting system fits cleanly into **three classic classes** — **Personal** (people & firms), **Real** (tangible/intangible property & assets), and **Nominal** (income, expenses, gains, & losses). Knowing an account’s class immediately tells you its Debit/Credit rule, which ledger holds it, and whether it belongs on the Income Statement or Balance Sheet!
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | Splitting all accounts into **Personal** vs. **Impersonal** (**Real** and **Nominal**) — and mapping them to the 5 modern account families |
| **Exam Focus** | Classifying tricky accounts accurately (e.g., *Bank*, *Capital*, *Drawings*, *Purchases*, *Machinery*, *Discount Allowed*) |
| **Learning Outcome** | Classify any business account accurately into Personal, Real, or Nominal and explain how its class controls its year-end treatment |

## 1. The Three Classes of Accounts Master Reference

All ledger accounts divide first into **Personal** and **Impersonal**, and Impersonal splits into **Real** and **Nominal**:

| Account Class | What It Represents | Classic Examples | Year-End Treatment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Personal Accounts** | Accounts of **people, businesses, lenders, and the owner** | Credit Customers / Trade Receivables (*K. Brown, Apex Mart*), Credit Suppliers / Trade Payables (*Prime Wholesale*), Bank Loan Lender, **Capital**, **Drawings**, Bank *(institution)* | **Permanent** — balanced (`c/d` → `b/d`) and reported on the **Balance Sheet** *(Drawings closes into Capital)* |
| **2. Real Accounts** *(Impersonal)* | Accounts of **assets and property** — things the business owns (tangible or intangible) | Land & Buildings (Premises), Machinery, Motor Vehicles, Fixtures & Fittings, Office Equipment, **Cash**, **Inventory**, Goodwill | **Permanent** — balanced (`c/d` → `b/d`) and reported on the **Balance Sheet** |
| **3. Nominal Accounts** *(Impersonal)* | Accounts of **revenues, expenses, gains, and losses** (exist in name for ONE period to measure profit) | **Sales, Purchases, Returns Inwards, Returns Outwards**, Rent, Wages, Electricity, Insurance, Bad Debts, Discount Allowed, Discount Received, Commission Received | **Temporary** — **closed** at year-end to the **Income Statement** (reset to $0 for Year 2!) |

:::note Two Classic Edge Cases Examiners Love Testing!
1. **Why are `Capital` and `Drawings` Personal Accounts?** Because they represent the **Owner’s** personal financial relationship with the separate business entity!
2. **Where does `Bank` fit?** In traditional strict classification, **Bank** is an artificial **Personal Account** (the bank is a legal institution holding your money or lending you an overdraft), whereas physical **Cash** is a **Real Account**. Under modern 5-family classification, both Cash and positive Bank are simply **Assets**!
:::

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Personal** = Named people, firms, debtors, creditors, **Capital**, **Drawings**, Bank.
- **Real** = Physical/intangible **Assets** (Buildings, Vans, Equipment, Cash, Inventory).
- **Nominal** = **Income & Expenses** (Sales, Purchases, Returns, Rent, Wages, Discounts) → closed to **Income Statement**!
:::
