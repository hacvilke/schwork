One of the highest-value accounting exam questions flips the script completely: instead of giving you transactions and asking for T-accounts, the examiner **shows you a completed T-account** and asks: *"Explain what happened on May 9, May 14, and May 31 — and what does the closing balance mean?"* Once you know the **3-Clue Ledger Detective Formula**, reading any T-account becomes effortless!
:::

## 1. The Universal Ledger Detective Rule

Look at any single line inside ANY T-account titled **`[Account X]`**:
- If the entry sits on the **DEBIT side** with Details = **`[Account Y]`** for **`$Z`**, the original double entry was:
  > **Debit `[Account X]` $Z** &nbsp;and&nbsp; **Credit `[Account Y]` $Z**
- If the entry sits on the **CREDIT side** with Details = **`[Account Y]`** for **`$Z`**, the original double entry was:
  > **Debit `[Account Y]` $Z** &nbsp;and&nbsp; **Credit `[Account X]` $Z**

### Quick Translation Table for Common Personal Account Entries

| Account Type | Entry Observed Inside Account | Exact Plain-English Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Customer A/c** *(Sales Ledger)* | **Dr** `Sales $900` | We **sold $900 of goods on credit** to this customer |
| **Customer A/c** | **Cr** `Returns Inwards $100` | Customer **returned $100 of goods** to us |
| **Customer A/c** | **Cr** `Bank $760` + **Cr** `Discount Allowed $40` | Customer **paid $760 by cheque** and received a **$40 cash discount** (settling $800) |
| **Customer A/c** | **Dr** `Bank (Dishonoured Cheque) $300` | Customer’s earlier **$300 cheque bounced** — so the customer owes us $300 again! |
| **Customer A/c** | **Cr** `Bad Debts $250` | Customer failed to pay; we **wrote off $250 as an uncollectible bad debt** |
| **Supplier A/c** *(Purchases Ledger)* | **Cr** `Purchases $1,200` | We **bought $1,200 of goods on credit** from this supplier |
| **Supplier A/c** | **Dr** `Returns Outwards $150` | We **returned $150 of goods** back to this supplier |
| **Supplier A/c** | **Dr** `Bank $980` + **Dr** `Discount Received $20` | We **paid $980 by cheque** to this supplier and earned **$20 cash discount** |

:::warning Two Rare "Reversed Balance" Surprises Examiners Love!
1. **Credit Balance (`Cr b/d`) in a Customer’s Account:** Normally a customer owes US (`Dr b/d`). A rare **Credit `b/d`** means **WE owe the customer money** — for instance, the customer overpaid their bill, paid an advance deposit, or returned goods AFTER already paying in full!
2. **Debit Balance (`Dr b/d`) in a Supplier’s Account:** Normally we owe the supplier (`Cr b/d`). A rare **Debit `b/d`** means **the supplier owes US money** — e.g., we overpaid our bill or returned goods after already settling the invoice!
:::

          <tr><td>Mar 31</td><td>Balance c/d</td><td>1,840</td><td></td><td></td><td></td></tr>
          <tr><td></td><td><strong>Total</strong></td><td><strong>2,920</strong></td><td></td><td><strong>Total</strong></td><td><strong>2,920</strong></td></tr>
          <tr><td></td><td></td><td></td><td>Apr 1</td><td>Balance b/d</td><td>1,840</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
      <p>
        Explain clearly in plain English the exact meaning of: (a) <strong>Mar 1</strong>,
        (b) <strong>Mar 4</strong>, (c) <strong>Mar 7</strong>, (d) the two entries on
        <strong> Mar 14</strong> together, and (e) the <strong>Apr 1 Balance b/d</strong>.
        <em>[2 marks each = 10 marks]</em>
      </p>
    </>
  }
>

- **(a) Mar 1 — Cr Balance b/d $900 (2 marks):** At the start of March 2026, Island Home Goods **owed Pacific Distributors Ltd. $900** brought forward from February’s unpaid credit purchases *(2 marks)*.
- **(b) Mar 4 — Cr Purchases $1,400 (2 marks):** Island Home Goods **bought $1,400 of goods for resale on credit** from Pacific Distributors Ltd. *(2 marks)*.
- **(c) Mar 7 — Dr Returns Outwards $180 (2 marks):** Island Home Goods **returned $180 of goods** (e.g., damaged or wrong items) back to Pacific Distributors Ltd., reducing the balance owed *(2 marks)*.
- **(d) Mar 14 — Dr Bank $855 & Dr Discount Received $45 (2 marks):** Island Home Goods **paid $855 by cheque** to Pacific Distributors Ltd. *(1 mark)* and received a **$45 prompt-payment cash discount** — settling **$900** of the debt (`$855 + $45`) in full *(1 mark)*.
- **(e) Apr 1 — Cr Balance b/d $1,840 (2 marks):** As at 1 April 2026, Island Home Goods **still owes Pacific Distributors Ltd. $1,840** *(1 mark)*, which is a **Current Liability (Trade Payable)** on the Balance Sheet *(1 mark)*.

*(Check: `$900 + $1,400 + $620 − $180 − $855 − $45 = $1,840` ✓!)*

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Golden Formula:** `Dr side of X` with Detail `Y` = **`Dr X · Cr Y`**; `Cr side of X` with Detail `Y` = **`Dr Y · Cr X`**.
- **Spot the 3 Examiner Favorites:**
  1. `Dr Customer / Cr Bank` = **Dishonoured (bounced) cheque!**
  2. `Dr Drawings / Cr Purchases` = **Owner took goods for personal use!**
  3. **Credit `b/d` in a Customer A/c** = Customer overpaid (we owe them)!
:::
