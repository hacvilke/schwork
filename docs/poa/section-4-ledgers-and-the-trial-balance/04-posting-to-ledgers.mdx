:::info Essential Understanding
Once transactions are recorded chronologically inside the **Books of Original Entry** (Section 3), they must be transferred — or **posted** — into their permanent home inside the **Ledgers**. Rather than cramming 1,000 accounts into one giant book, businesses divide the ledger into three clean volumes: the **Sales Ledger** (credit customers), the **Purchases Ledger** (credit suppliers), and the **General Ledger** (everything else)!
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | **Division of the Ledger** (**Sales Ledger**, **Purchases Ledger**, **General Ledger**) + exact posting rules from every Book of Original Entry |
| **Exam Focus** | Posting complete entries from Day Books, Cash Books, and the General Journal into the **exact right ledger** without losing double-entry balance |
| **Learning Outcome** | Classify accounts into the right ledger volume and post a full set of Day Book and Cash Book entries into Sales, Purchases, and General Ledgers |

## 1. Division of the Ledger — Which Account Lives Where?

| Ledger Name | Type | Exact Accounts Kept Inside | Why Keep It Separate? |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Sales Ledger (SL)** *(Debtors Ledger)* | Subsidiary | Personal accounts of all **credit customers (Trade Receivables)** | Lets credit-control clerks check who owes money and send monthly statements fast |
| **Purchases Ledger (PL)** *(Creditors Ledger)* | Subsidiary | Personal accounts of all **credit suppliers (Trade Payables)** | Lets accounts-payable clerks verify supplier bills and pay on time for cash discounts |
| **General Ledger (GL)** *(Nominal/Main Ledger)* | Main Ledger | All **Real accounts** (Buildings, Vans, Equipment), all **Nominal accounts** (Sales, Purchases, Returns, Rent, Wages, Discounts), plus **Capital, Drawings, & Bank Loans** | Holds the master balances used to prepare the Income Statement and Balance Sheet |
| *(Cash Book)* | *Dual Role* | Acts as BOTH a Book of Prime Entry AND the **Cash & Bank Ledger accounts** | Keeps high-volume cash/bank entries out of the General Ledger |

*(Note: Some businesses also keep a locked **Private Ledger** for highly confidential accounts such as Capital, Drawings, and Director Salaries.)*

### Complete Posting Cheat Table (From Every Book of Original Entry)

| Source Book | Daily Individual Postings | Month-End Total Posting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sales Journal** | **Dr** each Customer (SL) | **Cr** Sales A/c (GL) |
| **Purchases Journal** | **Cr** each Supplier (PL) | **Dr** Purchases A/c (GL) |
| **Returns Inwards Journal** | **Cr** each Customer (SL) | **Dr** Returns Inwards A/c (GL) |
| **Returns Outwards Journal** | **Dr** each Supplier (PL) | **Cr** Returns Outwards A/c (GL) |
| **Cash Book (Debit — Receipts)** | **Cr** the opposite account named (Customer in SL, Sales in GL, Capital in GL) — *skip Contra `C`!* | **Dr** Discount Allowed A/c (GL) *(total of Disc. All. col)* |
| **Cash Book (Credit — Payments)** | **Dr** the opposite account named (Supplier in PL, Rent/Wages/Equipment in GL) — *skip Contra `C`!* | **Cr** Discount Received A/c (GL) *(total of Disc. Rec. col)* |
| **General Journal** | **Dr** the named Debit A/c · **Cr** the named Credit A/c (exact as journalized) | *(Each journal entry posts individually)* |

### C. General Ledger *(Impersonal Accounts — 6 marks)*

**Sales Account**
- Cr May 31: Total Credit Sales (`Sales Journal`) = **$1,600**

**Purchases Account**
- Dr May 31: Total Credit Purchases (`Purchases Journal`) = **$1,400**

**Returns Inwards Account**
- Dr May 31: Total Returns Inwards (`Returns Inwards Journal`) = **$100**

**Discount Allowed Account**

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Sales Ledger** = Credit Customers ONLY; **Purchases Ledger** = Credit Suppliers ONLY; **General Ledger** = Everything else!
- **Cash Book Posting Mirror Rule:** Cash Book **Dr** posts to Ledger **Cr**; Cash Book **Cr** posts to Ledger **Dr** (always skip **Contra `C`**!).
- Remember to post **both** the cash/bank payment AND the cash discount to clear a customer or supplier’s account completely!
:::
