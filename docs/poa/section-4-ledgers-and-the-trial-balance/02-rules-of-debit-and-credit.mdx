:::info Essential Understanding
**Debit (`Dr`)** simply means the **LEFT side** of an account; **Credit (`Cr`)** simply means the **RIGHT side** — nothing more mysterious! Under **Double-Entry Bookkeeping**, every single business transaction touches at least **two accounts**: one gets a **Debit** and another gets an equal **Credit**. Once you master the **DEAD CLIC** rule and the **Three Golden Rules**, you can solve EVERY journal, ledger, and adjustment question in POA!
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | **DEAD CLIC** (Debit increases Expenses, Assets, Drawings; Credit increases Liabilities, Income, Capital) + the **3 Classic Golden Rules** |
| **Exam Focus** | Stating the exact **Account to Debit** and **Account to Credit** for any transaction without hesitating |
| **Learning Outcome** | Apply the Debit and Credit rules accurately across Personal, Real, and Nominal accounts for any business event |

## 1. Both Ways to Master Debit & Credit (They Always Give the Same Answer!)

### Method A: The Modern **DEAD CLIC** Rule *(Fastest & Most Foolproof!)*

<FormulaCard
  title="DEAD CLIC — The Instant Debit & Credit Decoder"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Expenses, Assets, and Drawings go UP on Debit; Liabilities, Income, and Capital go UP on Credit!"
>

| Group | Acronym | Accounts Included | To INCREASE (+) | To DECREASE (−) | Normal Balance |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Debit Group** | **D · E · A · D** | **E**xpenses, **A**ssets, **D**rawings *(plus Returns Inwards)* | **DEBIT (`Dr`)** | **CREDIT (`Cr`)** | **Debit** |
| **Credit Group** | **C · L · I · C** | **L**iabilities, **I**ncome, **C**apital *(plus Returns Outwards)* | **CREDIT (`Cr`)** | **DEBIT (`Dr`)** | **Credit** |

</FormulaCard>

### Method B: The Three Classic Golden Rules *(By Account Class)*

| Account Class | DEBIT (`Dr`) Rule | CREDIT (`Cr`) Rule | Quick Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Personal Accounts** *(People/Firms)* | **Debit the RECEIVER** | **Credit the GIVER** | Paid $500 cash to supplier *K. Lee* → **Dr K. Lee** (receiver), Cr Cash |
| **Real Accounts** *(Property/Assets)* | **Debit WHAT COMES IN** | **Credit WHAT GOES OUT** | Bought $4,000 equipment for cash → **Dr Equipment** (comes in), **Cr Cash** (goes out) |
| **Nominal Accounts** *(Income/Expense)* | **Debit ALL EXPENSES & LOSSES** | **Credit ALL INCOME & GAINS** | Paid $700 rent by cheque → **Dr Rent Expense**, Cr Bank; Cash sales $900 → Dr Cash, **Cr Sales** |

:::tip Why "Debit = Good / Credit = Bad" Is a Myth!
Never think of Debit as "plus" or Credit as "minus"!
- A **Debit** increases an **Asset** (Cash goes UP), **yet** a **Debit** decreases a **Liability** (Loan owed goes DOWN).
- A **Credit** increases **Sales Income** (good!), **yet** a **Credit** decreases **Cash** (cash went out).
Always check WHICH family the account belongs to (**DEAD** vs. **CLIC**) first!
:::

| # | Transaction Summary | Account to DEBIT (Class) | Account to CREDIT (Class) | Amount ($) |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- | ---: |
| 1 | Owner introduced cash | **Cash** *(Real / Asset ↑)* | **Capital** *(Personal / Equity ↑)* | 10,000 |
| 2 | Bought office furniture for cash | **Office Furniture** *(Real / Asset ↑)* | **Cash** *(Real / Asset ↓)* | 1,500 |
| 3 | Bought goods on credit (Apex) | **Purchases** *(Nominal / Expense ↑)* | **Apex Supply** *(Personal / Liability ↑)* | 2,200 |
| 4 | Returned goods to Apex Supply | **Apex Supply** *(Personal / Liability ↓)* | **Returns Outwards** *(Nominal / Credit)* | 200 |
| 5 | Cash sales of goods | **Cash** *(Real / Asset ↑)* | **Sales** *(Nominal / Income ↑)* | 950 |
| 6 | Credit sale to D. Grant | **D. Grant** *(Personal / Asset ↑)* | **Sales** *(Nominal / Income ↑)* | 1,300 |
| 7 | Paid Apex Supply by cheque | **Apex Supply** *(Personal / Liability ↓)* | **Bank** *(Asset ↓)* | 1,000 |
| 8 | Owner withdrew cash (Drawings) | **Drawings** *(Personal / DEAD ↑)* | **Cash** *(Real / Asset ↓)* | 300 |

*(Notice #4: Returns Outwards reduces our Purchases cost, so it acts like a Credit item — while Apex Supply is Debited because we owe Apex $200 less!)*
