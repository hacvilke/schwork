:::info Essential Understanding
Companies raise long-term financing from **two fundamentally different worlds**: **Share Capital (Ownership / Equity)** — split into **Ordinary Shares** and **Preference Shares** — and **Debentures (Long-Term Loans / Debt)**. Mixing up a Preference Dividend with Debenture Interest is one of the costliest mistakes on POA exams: **Debenture Interest MUST be paid in the Income Statement even if the company makes a LOSS**, whereas **Dividends are ONLY paid out of profits in the Appropriation Account**!
:::

| Quick Focus | What Matters Most |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Key Concept** | **Ordinary Shares** vs. **Preference Shares** *(Cumulative vs. Non-Cumulative)* vs. **Debentures**, plus the **Share Capital Ladder** (`Authorized → Issued → Called-Up → Paid-Up`) |
| **Exam Focus** | Knowing the exact **Payment Priority Order** (`1. Debenture Holders → 2. Preference Shareholders → 3. Ordinary Shareholders`) and ALWAYS calculating dividends on **ISSUED** (never Authorized!) Share Capital |
| **Learning Outcome** | Compare Ordinary Shares, Preference Shares, and Debentures accurately and calculate interest and dividend entitlements in both profit and loss years |

## 1. Three-Way Comparison: Ordinary Shares vs. Preference Shares vs. Debentures

| Feature | Ordinary Shares *(Equity)* | Preference Shares *(Equity)* | Debentures *(Long-Term Debt)* |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legal Status** | **True Owners** (residual risk-takers) | **Priority Owners** | **External Creditors (Lenders!)** |
| **Voting Rights at AGM** | **YES** *(usually 1 share = 1 vote)* | **Usually NO** *(unless dividends in arrears)* | **NO** |
| **Type of Return** | **Variable Dividend** *(decided by Directors)* | **Fixed `%` Dividend** *(e.g. `6%` Pref)* | **Fixed `%` Interest** *(e.g. `8%` Deb)* |
| **Must Be Paid in a Loss Year?** | **NO** (`$0` dividend) | **NO** *(carried forward ONLY if Cumulative!)* | **YES — LEGALLY MANDATORY!** |
| **Where Return Appears** | **Appropriation Account** | **Appropriation Account** | **Income Statement (Operating Expense!)** |
| **Balance Sheet Section** | **Shareholders’ Equity** | **Shareholders’ Equity** | **Non-Current Liabilities** |
| **Priority on Liquidation** | **3rd (LAST!)** | **2nd** *(before Ordinary)* | **1st (FIRST!)** *(often secured on assets!)* |

### Cumulative vs. Non-Cumulative Preference Shares
- **Cumulative Preference Shares:** If the company earns too little profit to pay the fixed `%` Preference Dividend this year, the unpaid amount **accumulates as arrears** and MUST be paid in full in future profitable years **before Ordinary Shareholders receive a single cent**!
- **Non-Cumulative Preference Shares:** If a dividend is skipped in a bad year, it is **lost forever** and does NOT carry forward.

### The Share Capital Ladder (Never Confuse Authorized vs. Issued!)
1. **Authorized (Registered / Nominal) Capital:** The **maximum legal ceiling** of share capital the company is permitted to issue under its Memorandum of Association *(shown as a **memo only** — NEVER added into Balance Sheet totals!)*
2. **Issued Share Capital:** The portion of Authorized Capital **actually sold/allotted** to investors *(entered in the ledger — **all dividends are calculated on ISSUED capital!**)*
3. **Called-Up Capital:** The amount per issued share the company has formally asked shareholders to pay so far.
4. **Paid-Up Capital:** The actual cash received by the company from shareholders *(Called-Up minus any unpaid Calls in Arrears)*.

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Payment Priority Waterfall in a Good Year vs. a Tough Year (IslandSolar Ltd)"
  marks={12}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> <strong>IslandSolar Ltd</strong> has the following capital and
        debt structure:
      </p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Authorized Share Capital:</strong> 200,000 Ordinary Shares of `$1.00` each (`$200,000`); 50,000 `6%` Cumulative Preference Shares of `$2.00` each (`$100,000`).</li>
        <li><strong>Issued &amp; Paid-Up Share Capital:</strong> 120,000 Ordinary Shares (`$120,000`); 30,000 `6%` Cumulative Preference Shares (`$60,000`).</li>
        <li><strong>Long-Term Debt:</strong> `$50,000` of <strong>`8%` Debentures (2035)</strong>.</li>
      </ul>
      <ol>
        <li><strong>Good Year (2026):</strong> Operating Profit <em>before Debenture Interest</em> is <strong>$34,000</strong>, and the Board declares a <strong>10% Ordinary Dividend</strong>. Calculate (a) Debenture Interest, (b) Net Profit after Interest, (c) Preference Dividend, (d) Ordinary Dividend, and (e) Profit Retained. <em>[7 marks]</em></li>
        <li><strong>Tough Year (2027):</strong> Suppose Operating Profit <em>before Debenture Interest</em> drops to only <strong>$3,000</strong>. Explain the exact legal treatment of Debenture Interest, Cumulative Preference Dividends, and Ordinary Dividends. <em>[5 marks]</em></li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

### 1. Good Year (2026) — Exact Payment Waterfall *(7 marks)*
*(Always calculate on **ISSUED** Share Capital — NEVER Authorized Capital!)*
1. **(a) Debenture Interest *(Paid FIRST — Income Statement Expense)*:** `8% × $50,000` = **`$4,000`** *(1.5 marks)*
2. **(b) Net Profit after Debenture Interest:** `$34,000 − $4,000` = **`$30,000`** *(1 mark)*
3. **(c) `6%` Preference Dividend *(Paid SECOND — Appropriation)*:** `6% × $60,000` *(ISSUED!)* = **`$3,600`** *(1.5 marks)*
4. **(d) `10%` Ordinary Dividend *(Paid THIRD — Appropriation)*:** `10% × $120,000` *(ISSUED!)* = **`$12,000`** *(1.5 marks)*
5. **(e) Profit Retained in the Company:** `$30,000 − $3,600 − $12,000` = **`$14,400`** *(1.5 marks)*

### 2. Tough Year (2027 — Operating Profit `$3,000` Only) *(5 marks)*
- **Debenture Interest (`$4,000`):** **MUST STILL BE PAID IN FULL!** Because Debenture holders are creditors, charging `$4,000` produces a **Net Loss of `($1,000)`** (`$3,000 − $4,000`) *(2 marks)*.
- **Cumulative Preference Dividend (`$3,600`):** **Cannot be paid** out of a loss year (`$0` paid in 2027), **BUT** because the shares are **Cumulative**, the unpaid **`$3,600`** accumulates as **dividend arrears** and must be paid FIRST in 2028 (alongside 2028’s `$3,600` = `$7,200` total) before Ordinary shareholders get anything *(2 marks)*.
- **Ordinary Dividend:** **`$0`** — Ordinary shareholders absorb the residual risk *(1 mark)*.

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- **Priority Order:** **1. Debenture Interest (`IS Expense`)** → **2. Preference Dividend (`Approp.`)** → **3. Ordinary Dividend (`Approp.`)**!
- ALWAYS multiply dividend `%` by **ISSUED** Share Capital — **NEVER Authorized Capital**!
:::
