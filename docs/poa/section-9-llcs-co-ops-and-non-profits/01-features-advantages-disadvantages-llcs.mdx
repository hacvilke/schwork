:::info Essential Understanding
When a business outgrows a partnership and needs **millions of dollars in capital** — without putting founders’ personal homes and savings at risk — entrepreneurs incorporate a **Limited Liability Company (LLC / Corporation)**. Legally, incorporation gives birth to an **artificial legal person** completely separate from its owners (**shareholders**), with **limited liability** and **perpetual succession**!
:::

## 1. The Four Legal Pillars of a Limited Liability Company

1. **Separate Legal Entity:** Upon registration under the Companies Act, the company becomes a **legal person in its own right** — it can own land, sign contracts, borrow money, sue, and be sued in its OWN corporate name, completely distinct from its shareholders!
2. **Limited Liability:** A shareholder’s maximum financial loss if the company fails is **strictly limited to the nominal (par) value of the shares they agreed to buy**! Once shares are **fully paid**, creditors CANNOT seize a shareholder’s personal house, car, or bank savings.
3. **Perpetual Succession (Continuity of Existence):** Unlike a sole trader or partnership, the company continues operating seamlessly even if a shareholder dies, retires, goes bankrupt, or sells all their shares to someone else!
4. **Separation of Ownership and Management:** **Shareholders** OWN the company and vote at the Annual General Meeting (AGM) to elect a **Board of Directors**, who MANAGE day-to-day operations and report back via audited financial statements.

### Private Limited (`Ltd`) vs. Public Limited (`PLC`)

| Feature | Private Limited Company (`Ltd`) | Public Limited Company (`PLC`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Name Ending** | Ends with **`Limited` / `Ltd`** | Ends with **`PLC`** *(or Public Limited)* |
| **Selling Shares to Public** | **Forbidden** — sold privately to family, friends, or invited investors | **Allowed** — issues a **Prospectus** to invite the general public |
| **Stock Exchange Listing** | **Cannot** be listed; share transfers need **Board approval** | **Can** list on the Stock Exchange; shares trade **freely**! |
| **Financial Privacy** | Higher privacy — accounts are **not** published broadly | **Must publish audited annual financial statements** publicly! |
| **Capital Raising** | Moderate to large | **Massive** (thousands of retail &amp; institutional investors!) |

### Key Incorporation Documents (Paper 1 Favorite!)
- **Memorandum of Association *(External Charter)*:** Governs relations with the **outside world** — states company name, registered office, business objectives, limited liability clause, and **Authorized Share Capital**.
- **Articles of Association *(Internal Rulebook)*:** Governs **internal management** — voting rights, election/powers of Directors, AGM procedures, and dividend rules.
- **Certificate of Incorporation:** The company’s official **"birth certificate"** issued by the Registrar of Companies proving legal existence.
- **Prospectus *(Public companies only)*:** Formal legal invitation inviting the public to subscribe for shares or debentures.

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Evaluating Private vs. Public Incorporation & Calculating Shareholder Liability"
  marks={10}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> Three siblings run <strong>BluePeak Foods</strong> as a booming
        partnership and want to incorporate as a Limited Liability Company to raise
        <strong> $400,000</strong> from 12 local investors while keeping full family control and
        keeping their profit margins private from competitors.
      </p>
      <ol>
        <li>Recommend whether BluePeak Foods should incorporate as a <strong>Private Limited Company (`Ltd`)</strong> or a <strong>Public Limited Company (`PLC`)</strong>, giving <strong>three clear reasons</strong>. <em>[4 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Distinguish between the <strong>Memorandum of Association</strong> and the <strong>Articles of Association</strong>. <em>[2 marks]</em></li>
        <li><strong>Limited Liability Calculation:</strong> Investor T. Grant subscribes for <strong>5,000 Ordinary Shares of `$2.00` par value</strong> (`$10,000` total) and has so far paid <strong>`$1.50` per share (`$7,500`)</strong>. If the company later goes bankrupt owing `$150,000` to creditors, what is the <strong>maximum additional cash</strong> creditors can legally demand from T. Grant? Explain why! <em>[4 marks]</em></li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

**1. Recommendation: Private Limited Company (`BluePeak Foods Ltd`) (4 marks):**
- **Recommendation:** Incorporate as a **Private Limited Company (`Ltd`)** *(1 mark)*.
- **Three Specific Reasons (3 marks — 1 mark each):**
  1. **Preserves Family Control:** Private `Ltd` restricts share transfers without Board approval — protecting the siblings from losing control or facing a hostile takeover.
  2. **Protects Financial Privacy:** Unlike a `PLC`, a Private `Ltd` does not have to publish detailed profit margins publicly where rival food distributors can copy them.
  3. **Fits Capital Scale &amp; Lower Cost:** Raising `$400,000` from 12 invited local investors does not require a costly Stock Exchange listing or expensive public Prospectus.

**2. Memorandum vs. Articles of Association (2 marks):**
- **Memorandum of Association:** External charter defining the company’s relationship with the **outside world** (company name, objectives, limited liability status, Authorized Share Capital) *(1 mark)*.
- **Articles of Association:** Internal rulebook governing **internal operations** (director powers, AGM voting procedures, share transfer restrictions, dividend rules) *(1 mark)*.

**3. Maximum Additional Liability of Investor T. Grant (4 marks):**
- Total agreed value of shares subscribed: `5,000 × $2.00` = **`$10,000`** *(1 mark)*
- Amount already paid up: `5,000 × $1.50` = **`$7,500`** *(1 mark)*
- **Maximum additional cash creditors can legally claim:** `$10,000 − $7,500` = **`$2,500` ONLY** (`$0.50` unpaid per share) *(1 mark)*.
- **Why:** Under **Limited Liability**, T. Grant’s obligation stops strictly at the unpaid balance on his shares — his personal assets cannot be touched for the remaining `$147,500` owed to creditors *(1 mark)*.

</WorkedExample>
