:::info Essential Understanding
**Non-Profit Organizations** — such as sports clubs, charities, churches, cultural societies, and youth associations — exist to **serve members or the community**, NOT to generate private profit! Because they have **NO private owners, NO share capital, and NO drawings or dividends**, accountants translate six familiar business terms into clean **non-profit vocabulary** (e.g., *Income Statement → Income and Expenditure Account*; *Net Profit → Surplus*; *Capital → Accumulated Fund*)!
:::

## 1. Core Features & The 6-Term Accounting Translator

### Four Defining Features of Non-Profit Organizations
1. **Service Mission (Not Profit Maximization):** Primary objective is social, sporting, cultural, educational, religious, or charitable welfare rather than financial gain.
2. **No Private Owners, No Drawings, No Dividends:** Because nobody privately owns equity shares, **zero profits are distributed** — any annual **Surplus** stays permanently inside the organization’s **Accumulated Fund**!
3. **Elected Volunteer Governance:** Managed by an elected **Management Committee** (President, Honorary Secretary, **Honorary Treasurer**) accountable to members at the Annual General Meeting.
4. **Member &amp; Donor Funding:** Primarily financed by **Annual Member Subscriptions**, donations, grants, fundraising events, and secondary trading activities (e.g., a club bar or snack café) rather than share capital.

### The 6-Term For-Profit → Non-Profit Accounting Translator *(Memorize Every Row!)*

| For-Profit Business Term | Non-Profit Equivalent Term | Quick Definition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cash Book Summary** | **Receipts and Payments Account** | Strictly **cash-basis** summary of all cash/bank money in &amp; out |
| **Income Statement *(P&amp;L)*** | **Income and Expenditure Account** | **Accrual-basis** statement measuring annual operating performance |
| **Net Profit** | **Surplus** *(Excess of Income over Expenditure)* | Added onto the **Accumulated Fund**! |
| **Net Loss** | **Deficit** *(Excess of Expenditure over Income)* | Subtracted from the **Accumulated Fund**! |
| **Owner’s Capital** | **Accumulated Fund** | `Opening Total Assets − Opening Total Liabilities` |
| **Drawings / Dividends** | **NONE!** | Forbidden — surpluses stay permanently inside the club/charity! |

### Special Non-Profit Receipts: Revenue vs. Capital!
- **Revenue Receipts *(Income &amp; Expenditure Account)*:** Regular annual **Member Subscriptions**, small general **Donations**, net surplus from **Fundraising Events** (raffles, gala dinners), and net profit from a **Club Bar / Café**.
- **Capital Receipts *(Balance Sheet — NOT regular annual income!)*:** **Special-Purpose Donations** (e.g., *New Clubhouse Building Fund* → separate Balance Sheet Fund), **Large Legacies / Endowments** (bequests left in a will → capitalized into Accumulated Fund), **Life Membership Fees** *(capitalized as a liability/fund and transferred gradually to I&amp;E over members’ expected years!)*, and **cash proceeds from selling old equipment**.

## 4. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Translating Terminology & Classifying Non-Profit Transactions (Kingston Youth Rowing Club)"
  marks={10}
  problem={

        <strong> Kingston Youth Rowing Club</strong>.
      </p>
      <ol>
        <li>A new committee member asks why the club’s accounts say <strong>"Surplus"</strong> and <strong>"Accumulated Fund"</strong> instead of "Net Profit" and "Capital", and asks whether members can vote themselves a cash <strong>"Dividend"</strong>. Give a clear 3-point answer! <em>[4 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Classify each of the following <strong>six items</strong> as either <strong>Revenue (Income &amp; Expenditure Account)</strong> OR <strong>Capital (Balance Sheet)</strong>, giving a brief reason for each: <em>[6 marks]</em>
          <ul>
            <li>(a) Annual member subscriptions earned for 2026 (`$12,000`)</li>
            <li>(b) Purchase of two new racing scull boats (`$9,500`)</li>
            <li>(c) Donation of `$15,000` specifically designated for a New Boathouse Building Fund</li>
            <li>(d) Net profit earned from the club’s weekend Snack Café (`$2,800`)</li>
            <li>(e) Annual depreciation charged on rowing boats (`$1,400`)</li>
            <li>(f) A `$20,000` legacy (bequest) left in a deceased founder’s will to permanently strengthen the club’s fund</li>
          </ul>
        </li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

### 1. Explaining Non-Profit Terminology & Why Dividends Are Prohibited *(4 marks)*
- **"Surplus" instead of "Net Profit":** The club exists to promote youth rowing, not commercial profit-making — any excess of income over expenditure is therefore called a **Surplus** *(1 mark)*.
- **"Accumulated Fund" instead of "Capital":** Because there are no private equity owners who invested personal capital, the club’s net worth (`Opening Assets − Opening Liabilities + Past Surpluses`) is called its **Accumulated Fund** *(1.5 marks)*.
- **No Cash Dividends / Drawings:** Members cannot vote themselves personal cash dividends because non-profit rules require all surpluses to be **reinvested 100% into the club’s mission** (boats, coaching, facilities) *(1.5 marks)*.

### 2. Classification of Six Club Transactions *(6 marks — 1 mark each)*
- **(a) Annual Subscriptions (`$12,000`):** **Revenue *(Income &amp; Expenditure — Income)*** — recurring annual operating income earned in 2026.
- **(b) New Racing Scull Boats (`$9,500`):** **Capital *(Balance Sheet — Non-Current Asset)*** — long-term asset used across multiple years.
- **(c) Boathouse Building Fund Donation (`$15,000`):** **Capital *(Balance Sheet — Specific Reserve/Fund)*** — legally restricted to a specific capital project, NOT general operating income.
- **(d) Snack Café Net Profit (`$2,800`):** **Revenue *(Income &amp; Expenditure — Income)*** — recurring annual income-generating activity supporting club expenses.
- **(e) Annual Depreciation (`$1,400`):** **Revenue *(Income &amp; Expenditure — Expenditure)*** — annual accrual-basis expense measuring boat wear-and-tear.
- **(f) Founder’s `$20,000` Legacy:** **Capital *(Balance Sheet — Capitalized into Accumulated Fund)*** — one-off substantial capital endowment rather than routine operating revenue.

</WorkedExample>
