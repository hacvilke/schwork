:::info Essential Understanding
A **Co-operative Society** is a democratic business **owned, controlled, and used by its own members** for their shared economic benefit — NOT to maximize profits for outside stock-market investors! Its #1 golden rule flips corporate voting upside down: **1 Member = 1 Vote** *(whether you own 1 share or 1,000 shares!)*, and its trading surplus is returned to members as a **Patronage Refund** based on how much they actually USED the co-op!
:::

## 1. The Six Core Principles of Co-operative Societies

1. **Open &amp; Voluntary Membership:** Anyone who shares the common bond (e.g., living in the community, working in the industry, farming the crop) and accepts member responsibilities may join or leave freely without discrimination.
2. **Democratic Control (`1 Member = 1 Vote`):** Every member gets **exactly ONE vote** at the Annual General Meeting regardless of how many shares they hold! No wealthy investor can ever buy control of the co-op.
3. **Limited Return on Share Capital:** Interest/dividends paid on members’ shares are **strictly capped** by Co-op law (e.g., max `5%–6%`) so the society focuses on serving members rather than enriching speculators.
4. **Surplus Shared by Patronage *(Patronage Refund)*:** After setting aside statutory reserves, surplus is distributed back to members **in proportion to how much business they did with the co-op** (e.g., `5%` rebate on groceries bought or milk supplied)!
5. **Continuous Member Education:** Co-ops legally allocate a slice of annual surplus (e.g., `5%`) to an **Education Fund** to train members, youth, and elected committee volunteers.
6. **Co-operation Among Co-operatives:** Local co-ops partner regionally and nationally (e.g., Credit Union leagues and agricultural federations) to strengthen the entire movement.

### Four Major Types of Co-operatives

| Co-op Type | Who Are the Members? | Primary Economic Purpose | Familiar Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial *(Credit Unions)*** | Savers &amp; borrowers sharing a common bond | Encourage regular thrift/savings and offer **low-interest loans** to members | Teachers’ Credit Union, Community Credit Unions |
| **Consumer / Retail** | Everyday shoppers &amp; households | Buy groceries/supplies in **bulk** to sell at fair prices + return patronage rebates | Community supermarkets, school canteen co-ops |
| **Producer / Agricultural** | Farmers, fishers, or artisans | Pool harvests for **shared transport, storage, processing &amp; strong market bargaining** | Coffee growers’ co-op, dairy &amp; fishing co-ops |
| **Worker / Service** | Employees themselves | Workers jointly own the business and share management &amp; surplus fairly | Taxi/transport co-ops, bakery &amp; craft worker co-ops |

### Part A: Identifying Co-operative Types *(4 marks — 1 mark each)*
1. **Financial Co-operative (Credit Union)** — mobilizes member thrift/savings to offer affordable member credit.
2. **Producer / Agricultural Co-operative** — pools produce, storage, transport, and market bargaining power for growers.
3. **Consumer / Retail Co-operative** — buys goods in bulk to provide fair prices and patronage rebates to member shoppers.
4. **Worker Co-operative** — owned and democratically managed by the workers themselves.

### Part B: Co-operative Society vs. Public Limited Company (`PLC`) *(6 marks)*
- **Three Key Differences *(4.5 marks — 1.5 marks each)*:**
  1. **Voting Rights:** A Co-op operates strictly on **1 Member = 1 Vote** regardless of shareholding; a `PLC` gives **1 vote per Ordinary Share** (largest investors hold controlling power).
  2. **Distribution of Earnings:** A Co-op caps share dividends and shares remaining surplus via **Patronage Refunds** based on member usage; a `PLC` distributes profits purely in proportion to **shares held**.
  3. **Trading of Shares:** Co-op shares are bought and redeemed directly from the society at **par value** (never listed on a Stock Exchange); `PLC` shares trade freely on the **Stock Exchange** at fluctuating market prices.
- **One Major Disadvantage of Co-ops During Rapid Expansion *(1.5 marks)*:**
  - **Limited Capital-Raising Capacity:** Because returns on member shares are capped, shares cannot appreciate on a Stock Exchange, and individual holdings are restricted, Co-ops often struggle to attract massive outside investment capital compared to a `PLC` *(also accepted: slower volunteer-committee decision-making or shortage of specialist salaried executives)*.
