## 1. What Is a Partnership & How Does It Compare?

A **Partnership** is a business owned and carried on in common by **2 to 20 people** (or more for certain licensed professions like lawyers and chartered accountants) with the shared goal of making a profit. Common Jamaican examples include law firms, accounting practices, dental/medical clinics, auto garages, and family hardware stores.

### Core Legal & Accounting Features of a Partnership
1. **Voluntary Association:** Formed freely by mutual consent — through a written **Partnership Agreement (Deed)**, verbal promise, or implied conduct.
2. **Mutual Agency:** Every active partner acts as an **agent** for the firm — meaning a business contract signed by Partner A legally binds Partner B and Partner C too!
3. **Unlimited Liability (General Partners):** Because an ordinary partnership is **not** a separate legal entity, general partners are personally liable for ALL business debts if the firm fails.
4. **Shared Ownership, Control & Profits/Losses:** Partners pool money, skills, and management effort and share the final profits (or losses) according to their agreement.
5. **Limited Continuity:** Legally, an ordinary partnership dissolves whenever a partner retires, goes bankrupt, or passes away (unless a new agreement is signed).

### Why Form a Partnership? (Advantages vs Disadvantages)

| Advantages Over a Sole Trader | Disadvantages Compared to a Sole Trader / Company |
| :--- | :--- |
| **More Capital:** Multiple owners pool cash and assets, making bank financing easier too. | **Unlimited Liability:** General partners risk their personal homes and savings for business debts — even debts caused by another partner's mistake! |
| **Diverse Skills & Specialisation:** One partner can lead sales/marketing while another manages accounting and operations. | **Risk of Disagreements:** Clashes over drawings, effort, or business direction can stall decisions or break up the firm. |
| **Shared Workload & Risks:** Partners share long hours, holiday cover, and any business losses. | **Shared Profits:** Profits must be divided among partners instead of kept by one owner. |
| **Easy & Private to Form:** Less expensive and fewer statutory reporting rules than a Limited Company. | **Limited Continuity:** Death or withdrawal of a partner legally terminates the existing partnership. |

## 2. Types of Partners

| Type of Partner | Invests Capital? | Helps Manage Daily Business? | Liability for Business Debts |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **General (Active / Ordinary) Partner** | **Yes** | **Yes** | **Unlimited** liability |
| **Sleeping (Dormant) Partner** | **Yes** | **No** *(silent investor)* | **Unlimited** liability *(unless legally registered as a Limited Partner!)* |
| **Limited Partner** | **Yes** | **No** *(cannot bind the firm in contracts)* | **Limited** to the capital invested *(at least ONE General Partner with unlimited liability must exist!)* |
| **Nominal (Quasi) Partner** *(Awareness)* | **No** | **No** *(lends only their respected name/reputation)* | May become liable to outsiders who gave credit relying on their name! |

## 3. Partnership Agreement (Deed) vs No Agreement (Partnership Act 1890!)

To prevent bitter arguments over profit and cash withdrawals, partners sign a formal written **Partnership Agreement (Partnership Deed)** spelling out:
1. **Capital contributed** by each partner (cash and non-cash assets);
2. **Profit and loss sharing ratio** (e.g. `3:2`, `50/50`, capital ratio, or percentages);
3. **Interest on Capital** *(rewards partners who invested larger sums of capital before dividing remaining profit)*;
4. **Partner Salaries** *(rewards active partners who work full-time managing the business)*;
5. **Interest on Drawings** *(a penalty charged to discourage partners from taking excessive cash out of the business!)*;
6. **Interest on Loans from Partners**, limits on drawings, and rules for admitting or retiring a partner.

### What If There Is NO Partnership Agreement? (**Partnership Act 1890 — Exam Must-Know!**)
Whenever partners fail to write an agreement (or leave out a specific clause), the law applies the **Partnership Act 1890** defaults automatically:

| Clause | **With a Partnership Agreement** | **NO Agreement (Partnership Act 1890)** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Profits & Losses** | Shared by agreed ratio (e.g. `3:2` or capital ratio) | Shared **EQUALLY (`1 : 1`)** — *even if one partner put in 90% of the capital!* |
| **Interest on Capital** | Paid at agreed % (e.g. `10% p.a.`) | **NONE (0%)** |
| **Partner Salaries** | Paid as agreed to active partner(s) | **NONE (J\$0)** |
| **Interest on Drawings** | Charged at agreed % (e.g. `5%`) | **NONE (0%)** |
| **Interest on a Loan FROM a Partner** | Paid at agreed % | **5% per annum** *(treated as an **Expense in P&L**, NOT an Appropriation!)* |

## 4. Opening Capital Journal Entries (Cash, Non-Cash Assets & Liabilities!)

Partners can contribute **cash**, **non-cash assets** (delivery vans, premises, computers, stock, debtors), and occasionally even transfer an existing business liability (like a creditor or loan) into the new partnership!
- **Debit** every Asset contributed (at its **agreed fair valuation**!).
- **Credit** any Liability assumed by the partnership.
- **Credit** each partner's **Capital Account** with their **Net Capital Contribution** (`Assets Contributed − Liabilities Assumed`)!

## 5. Fixed Capital Accounts vs Fluctuating Capital Accounts

Accountants maintain partnership capital using one of **two methods** — know the exact difference and WHY examiners prefer Fixed Capital Accounts!

| Feature | **Fixed Capital Method** *(Exam Standard!)* | **Fluctuating Capital Method** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Accounts Kept per Partner** | **TWO separate accounts:**<br/>1. **Capital Account** *(permanent investment only — stays FIXED year after year unless extra capital is formally invested/withdrawn)*<br/>2. **Current Account** *(records all annual movements: Interest on Capital, Partner Salary, Share of Profit, Drawings, Interest on Drawings!)* | **ONE Capital Account only** — opening capital, salaries, interest on capital, profit share, drawings, and interest on drawings are ALL posted into this one account! |
| **Does Capital Balance Change Every Year?** | **No** — stays fixed at original investment (e.g. Brown J\$200,000, Reid J\$100,000). | **Yes** — changes ("fluctuates") up or down every single year! |
| **Why Accountants Prefer Fixed Capital** | Keeps permanent invested capital strictly separated from annual profit shares & drawings — makes it easy to compute **Interest on Capital** AND instantly spots whether a partner is **over-drawing** (Debit Current Account)! | Simpler for tiny informal partnerships, but hides excessive drawings inside the main Capital balance. |
