## 1. What Is a Partnership? (Kid-Friendly Explanation!)

A **Partnership** is a business owned and run by **2 to 20 people** who pool their money, skills, and effort together with the shared goal of making a profit (common examples in Jamaica: law firms, accounting firms, medical practices, hardware & auto shops).

### Key Features & Reasons to Form a Partnership
- **Voluntary Association:** Partners freely choose to join together by agreement (written or verbal).
- **Mutual Agency:** Each active partner acts as an agent for the firm — contracts signed by one partner bind ALL partners!
- **Unlimited Liability (General Partners):** Just like a sole trader, general partners are personally responsible for all business debts if the firm fails.
- **Why form one?** More **Capital** than a sole trader, **diverse skills** (one partner handles sales, another handles accounting), **shared workload**, and **shared losses/risks**.

### Types of Partners
| Type of Partner | Manages Daily Operations? | Liability for Debts |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **General (Active / Ordinary) Partner** | **Yes** | **Unlimited** liability |
| **Sleeping (Dormant) Partner** | **No** *(invests capital only)* | **Unlimited** liability *(unless registered as a Limited Partner)* |
| **Limited Partner** | **No** | **Limited** to the capital invested *(at least one General Partner must exist)* |

## 2. Partnership Agreement vs No Agreement (Partnership Act 1890!)

To avoid arguments over money, partners sign a written **Partnership Agreement (Deed of Partnership)** covering capital contributed, profit-sharing ratio, interest on capital, interest on drawings, partner salaries, and loan interest.

**What if there is NO Partnership Agreement?** Then the **Partnership Act 1890** steps in automatically — **memorise these 5 exam favourites!**

| Clause | **With a Partnership Agreement** | **NO Agreement (Partnership Act 1890)** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Profits & Losses** | Shared by agreed ratio (e.g. `3:2`, capital ratio, or fixed %) | Shared **EQUALLY** (`1:1`, regardless of capital put in!) |
| **Interest on Capital** | Paid at agreed % (e.g. `10%`) | **NONE (0%)** |
| **Partner Salaries** | Paid as agreed to active partners | **NONE (J\$0)** |
| **Interest on Drawings** | Charged at agreed % to discourage heavy withdrawals | **NONE (0%)** |
| **Interest on a Loan FROM a Partner** | Paid at agreed % | **5% per annum** *(an Expense in P&L, NOT an appropriation!)* |

## 3. Opening Capital Journal Entries (Cash & Non-Cash Assets)

Partners can invest **cash** AND **non-cash assets** (such as a motor van, computer equipment, or stock). Record each asset at its agreed value on the Debit side and Credit that partner's **Capital Account**!

**Example:** On 1 Jan 2025, **Brown** contributes J\$140,000 cash + a Delivery Van valued at J\$60,000; **Reid** contributes J\$100,000 cash into the partnership bank account:

| Date | Details | Dr (J\$) | Cr (J\$) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Jan 1 | Bank *(140,000 + 100,000)* | 240,000 | |
| | Delivery Van | 60,000 | |
| | &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Capital — Brown | | 200,000 |
| | &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Capital — Reid | | 100,000 |
| | *(Being initial cash and non-cash capital contributed by Brown and Reid)* | | |

## 4. Fixed Capital Accounts vs Fluctuating Capital Accounts

| Method | How It Works | Why Accountants Prefer / Use It |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fixed Capital Method** *(Most Common in Exams!)* | Each partner has **TWO accounts**:<br/>1. **Capital Account** *(stays FIXED at original permanent investment)*<br/>2. **Current Account** *(tracks yearly salaries, interest on capital, profit shares, drawings, and interest on drawings!)* | Keeps permanent investment completely separate from annual profits & drawings — makes it easy to spot if a partner is over-drawing! |
| **Fluctuating Capital Method** | Each partner has **ONE Capital Account only** — salaries, profit shares, interest, and drawings are all posted straight into the Capital Account, so its balance changes ("fluctuates") every year. | Simpler for very small partnerships, but hides original invested capital inside daily drawings. |
