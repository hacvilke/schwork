## 1. How Partnerships Divide Net Profit (The Appropriation Account)

A partnership prepares the **exact same Trading and Profit & Loss Account** as a Sole Trader to calculate **Net Profit**. However, instead of handing 100% of Net Profit to a single owner, the partnership adds a bottom section called the **Profit & Loss Appropriation Account** to distribute Net Profit fairly according to the Partnership Agreement!

  summary="Begin with Net Profit, ADD Interest on Drawings (charged to partners), subtract Interest on Capital and Partner Salaries, then split the remaining Residual Profit in the agreed ratio!"
>

<FormulaHint

>

$$
\text{Residual Profit} = \text{Net Profit} + \text{Interest on Drawings} - \text{Interest on Capital} - \text{Partner Salaries}
$$

</FormulaHint>

### Four Ways Partners Share Residual Profit (Syllabus Objective 8.6!)
Suppose Residual Profit = **J\$120,000**, with Fixed Capitals Brown = **J\$200,000** and Reid = **J\$100,000**:
1. **Equally (`1 : 1`):** Brown gets J\$60,000; Reid gets J\$60,000. *(Mandatory if NO Agreement exists!)*
2. **Fixed Ratio (e.g. `3 : 2`):** Total parts = $3 + 2 = 5$ → Brown gets $\frac{3}{5} \times 120{,}000 =$ **J\$72,000**; Reid gets $\frac{2}{5} \times 120{,}000 =$ **J\$48,000**.
3. **Capital Ratio (in proportion to Fixed Capitals):** Ratio = $200{,}000 : 100{,}000 = \mathbf{2 : 1}$ (3 parts) → Brown gets $\frac{2}{3} \times 120{,}000 =$ **J\$80,000**; Reid gets $\frac{1}{3} \times 120{,}000 =$ **J\$40,000**.
4. **Fixed Percentage (e.g. `60% / 40%`):** Brown gets $60\% \times 120{,}000 =$ **J\$72,000**; Reid gets $40\% \times 120{,}000 =$ **J\$48,000**.

### Watch Out for Three Classic Exam Traps!
1. **Actual Drawings NEVER Enter the Appropriation Account!** Only **Interest on Drawings** goes into the Appropriation Account (`Add: Interest on Drawings`) — actual cash/goods **Drawings** go straight to the **Debit side of each partner's Current Account**!
2. **Partner Salaries vs Staff Wages vs Partner Loan Interest:**
   - **Staff Wages** → Expense in **Profit & Loss Account**.
   - **Interest on a Loan FROM a Partner** → Finance Expense in **Profit & Loss Account** *(reduces Net Profit BEFORE Appropriation! If unpaid, credit Partner's Current Account or Accrued Loan Interest)*.
   - **Partner's Salary** → Allocation in **Appropriation Account** *(never deducted in P&L!)*.
3. **What If Interest on Capital + Salaries EXCEED Net Profit?** *(Residual Loss!)*
   - Suppose Net Profit + Interest on Drawings = J\$50,000, while Interest on Capital + Salaries = J\$66,000 → that leaves a **Residual Loss of `(J$16,000)`**!
   - Simply share that `(J$16,000)` loss in the agreed ratio (e.g. `3:2` → Brown `(9,600)`, Reid `(6,400)`) and post each partner's share of loss to the **DEBIT side of their Current Account**!

## 2. How the Appropriation Account Connects to Partners' Current Accounts

Notice the **exact mirror-image double entry** between the Appropriation Account and each Partner's Current Account:

| Item | Effect in **Appropriation Account** | Side in **Partner's Current Account** | Why? |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Interest on Capital** | **Subtract** *(from profit pool)* | **CREDIT (Cr)** | Increases what the partnership owes the partner! |
| **Partner Salary** *(if not already paid in cash!)* | **Subtract** *(from profit pool)* | **CREDIT (Cr)** | Increases what the partnership owes the partner! |
| **Share of Residual Profit** | **Subtract** *(clears pool to 0)* | **CREDIT (Cr)** | Increases what the partnership owes the partner! |
| **Share of Residual Loss** *(if any)* | **Add back** *(clears loss to 0)* | **DEBIT (Dr)** | Reduces the partner's balance! |
| **Interest on Drawings** | **Add** *(to profit pool)* | **DEBIT (Dr)** | Penalty charged against the partner! |
| **Drawings** *(Cash / Goods taken)* | **NOT IN APPROPRIATION!** | **DEBIT (Dr)** | Reduces what the partnership owes the partner! |

*(Note on Paid Salary: If a question says "Reid already withdrew his salary in cash during the year", either debit Drawings & credit Salary in Current Account, or omit it from Current Account since he already took the cash — always read the wording carefully!)*

## 4. Significance of Credit vs Debit `Balance b/d` on Current Accounts (Obj 8.9!)

- **Credit Balance `b/d`** *(e.g. Brown `J\$58,000 Cr`)*: **Healthy surplus!** The partner earned more (from Interest on Capital, Salary, and Profit Share) than they withdrew in Drawings. The partnership owes the partner this retained amount — **ADDED** to Capital in the Financed By section of the Balance Sheet!
- **Debit Balance `b/d`** *(e.g. Reid's opening `J\$4,000 Dr`)*: **Overdrawn!** The partner withdrew *more* in Drawings (plus Interest on Drawings / Loss share) than their total earnings. The partner owes that money back to the partnership — shown **in brackets `(4,000)` and SUBTRACTED** in the Financed By section of the Balance Sheet!
