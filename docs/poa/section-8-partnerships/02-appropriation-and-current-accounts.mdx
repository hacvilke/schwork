## 1. The Appropriation Account: How Partnerships Split Net Profit!

A partnership prepares the **exact same Trading and Profit & Loss Account** as a sole trader to find **Net Profit**. Then, instead of handing all Net Profit to one owner, it adds one extra bottom section called the **Profit & Loss Appropriation Account** to divide that Net Profit fairly among the partners!

<FormulaCard
  title="Partnership Appropriation Flow"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Start with Net Profit, ADD Interest on Drawings (a penalty paid by partners to the firm), subtract Interest on Capital and Partner Salaries, and divide whatever Residual Profit remains!"
>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Residual Profit Shared Among Partners"
  short="Interest on Drawings increases the profit pool to share; Interest on Capital and Partner Salaries are paid out of the pool first; the leftover Residual Profit is split in the profit-sharing ratio."
>

$$
\text{Residual Profit} = \text{Net Profit} + \text{Interest on Drawings} - \text{Interest on Capital} - \text{Partner Salaries}
$$

</FormulaHint>

</FormulaCard>

:::warning Two Classic Exam Traps!
1. **Drawings itself NEVER appears in the Appropriation Account!** Only **Interest on Drawings** goes in the Appropriation Account — actual Drawings goes straight to the **Debit side of each partner's Current Account**!
2. **Partner Salaries** go in the **Appropriation Account** (NOT regular staff wages in P&L!), whereas **Interest on a Loan from a Partner** IS an **Expense in the Profit & Loss Account**!
:::

## 3. Interpreting Debit vs Credit Balances on a Current Account

- **Credit Balance b/d** *(e.g. Brown `J\$58,000 Cr`)*: **Healthy!** The partner left profits/salary in the business — the partnership owes the partner this money (**ADDED** on the Balance Sheet).
- **Debit Balance b/d** *(e.g. Reid's opening `J\$4,000 Dr`)*: **Overdrawn!** The partner withdrew *more* cash than they earned — the partner owes the partnership money (**SUBTRACTED** in brackets `(4,000)` on the Balance Sheet!).
