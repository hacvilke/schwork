## 1. Purpose & Two-Part Structure of the Income Statement

Every business owner wants to know at the end of the year: *"Did I actually make money — and WHERE did my money go?"* The **Trading and Profit & Loss Account** *(also called the **Income Statement**)* answers that question by matching all revenue earned against all costs and expenses incurred across the entire year.

Always begin with the **exact period heading**:
> **[Business Name]**
> **Trading and Profit & Loss Account for the year ended [Date]** *(never "as at"!)*

It is divided cleanly into **two connected sections**:

### Part 1 — The Trading Account *(Buying & Selling Goods Only!)*
Answers: *"How much trading profit did we make purely from buying goods at wholesale and selling them at retail?"*
- Compares **Net Sales (Turnover)** against **Cost of Sales (Cost of Goods Sold)**.
- Result: **Gross Profit** (if Net Sales > Cost of Sales) or **Gross Loss** (if Cost of Sales > Net Sales).

### Part 2 — The Profit & Loss Account *(Running the Entire Business!)*
Answers: *"After adding side incomes and paying ALL day-to-day running bills, how much final profit belongs to the owner?"*
- Starts with **Gross Profit** brought down from Part 1.
- **Adds Other Revenues** (e.g. *Discount Received*, *Rent Received*, *Commission Received*, *Reduction in Provision for Doubtful Debts*).
- **Subtracts Operating Expenses** (e.g. *Wages & Salaries*, *Shop Rent*, *Electricity & Water*, *Insurance*, *Carriage Outwards*, *Discount Allowed*, *Advertising*, *Motor Expenses*, *Loan Interest*, *Bad Debts*, *Depreciation*).
- Result: **Net Profit** *(or **Net Loss**)* — which flows directly into the **Capital** section of the Balance Sheet!

## 2. Step-by-Step Trading & P&L Formulae

Hover or tap any formula below to see the plain-English meaning:

<FormulaCard
  title="Core Trading and Profit & Loss Formulae"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Net Sales minus Cost of Sales equals Gross Profit; Gross Profit plus Other Revenues minus Operating Expenses equals Net Profit!"
>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="1. Net Sales (Turnover)"
  short="Total Sales revenue minus goods returned by customers (Returns Inwards / Sales Returns)."
>

$$
\text{Net Sales} = \text{Sales} - \text{Returns Inwards}
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="2. Cost of Goods Available & Cost of Sales"
  short="Start with Opening Stock, add Net Purchases (Purchases minus Returns Outwards) plus Carriage Inwards (shipping goods IN), subtract any goods taken by the owner for personal use, then subtract Closing Stock left unsold!"
>

$$
\text{Cost of Sales} = \text{Opening Stock} + (\text{Purchases} - \text{Returns Outwards}) + \text{Carriage Inwards} - \text{Closing Stock}
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="3. Gross Profit & Net Profit"
  short="Gross Profit measures pure trading margin; Net Profit is final profit after side incomes and all running expenses."
>

$$
\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Net Sales} - \text{Cost of Sales}
$$

$$
\text{Net Profit} = \text{Gross Profit} + \text{Other Revenues} - \text{Operating Expenses}
$$

</FormulaHint>

</FormulaCard>

### Why Do We Subtract Closing Stock from Cost of Goods Available?
This confuses many students until they picture a fruit stall:
- You started the week with **10 mangoes** (Opening Stock) and bought **90 more** (Purchases) → you had **100 mangoes available** to sell.
- On Saturday night, **25 mangoes are still sitting unsold** in your crate (Closing Stock).
- How many mangoes did you *actually sell*? $100 - 25 = \mathbf{75\text{ mangoes}}$!
Subtracting **Closing Stock** removes unsold goods so you match ONLY the cost of the goods **actually sold** against your Sales revenue (**Matching Concept**)!

## 3. Five Classic Exam Traps (Highlighted in the 2025 Subject Report!)

Examiners repeatedly see students throw away easy marks on these exact five points:

| Exam Trap | Correct Rule You Must Follow |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Carriage Inwards vs Carriage Outwards** | **Carriage Inwards** *(freight/shipping paid to bring stock IN to our shop)* → **ADD to Net Purchases in the Trading Account**.<br/>**Carriage Outwards** *(delivery paid to send sold goods OUT to customers)* → **Operating Expense in the Profit & Loss Account**! |
| **2. Treating Cost of Sales as an Expense or adding it to Sales** | **Cost of Sales** is ALWAYS **SUBTRACTED** from Net Sales inside the Trading Account to calculate Gross Profit — never added! |
| **3. Misplacing Other Revenue** | **Discount Received**, **Rent Received**, and **Commission Received** are **revenues** — ALWAYS **ADD** them directly under Gross Profit, NEVER subtract them under Expenses! |
| **4. Owner Taking Stock (Goods) for Personal Use** | If the owner took J\$5,000 of stock home for personal use, **SUBTRACT J\$5,000 from Purchases** in the Trading Account *(so Cost of Sales isn't inflated!)* AND **ADD J\$5,000 to Drawings** on the Balance Sheet! |
| **5. Putting Balance Sheet Items into the Income Statement** | **Assets** (Debtors, Bank, Equipment), **Liabilities** (Creditors, Loans), **Capital**, and **Drawings** NEVER appear in the Trading & P&L Account! *(Only **Closing Stock** appears in BOTH places: subtracted in Trading Account AND listed as a Current Asset on the Balance Sheet!)* |

## 4. Finding Missing Figures in an Incomplete Income Statement!

*(Tested directly on May/June 2025 Paper 032 Q2!)*
Examiners love giving a partially filled Income Statement with blank boxes and asking you to work **backwards** using the formulas:
- Missing **Closing Stock?** Remember: $\text{Cost of Goods Available} - \text{Closing Stock} = \text{Cost of Sales}$, so **$\text{Closing Stock} = \text{Cost of Goods Available} - \text{Cost of Sales}$**!
- Missing **Opening Stock?** Rearrange: **$\text{Opening Stock} = \text{Cost of Goods Available} - \text{Net Purchases} - \text{Carriage Inwards}$**!
- Missing **Net Sales?** **$\text{Net Sales} = \text{Cost of Sales} + \text{Gross Profit}$**!
- Missing **Operating Expense** (e.g. Rent)? Add up all known expenses, subtract from `Total Expenses` (which equals `Gross Profit + Other Revenue − Net Profit`), and the exact gap is the missing expense!
