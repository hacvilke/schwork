## 1. Two Sections, Two Questions (Kid-Friendly Guide!)

The **Trading and Profit & Loss Account** *(also called the **Income Statement**)* measures a business's performance over an entire year — so its heading ALWAYS says:
> **[Business Name] — Trading and Profit & Loss Account for the year ended [Date]**

It is split into **two clean halves** that answer two plain questions:
1. **Top Half — The Trading Account:** *"How much profit did we make purely from buying and selling our goods?"* → gives **Gross Profit** (or Gross Loss).
2. **Bottom Half — The Profit & Loss Account:** *"After adding extra side incomes (like Discount Received or Rent Received) and paying ALL our running bills (wages, rent, electricity, delivery out, depreciation, bad debts), how much final profit is actually left for the owner?"* → gives **Net Profit** (or Net Loss)!

## 2. Core Trading & P&L Formulas (Hover or Tap to Simplify!)

<FormulaCard
  title="Step-by-Step Trading & Profit and Loss Formulas"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Find Net Sales, subtract Cost of Sales to get Gross Profit, add any other incomes, then subtract operating expenses to reach Net Profit!"
>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Net Sales (Turnover)"
  short="Take total Sales and subtract goods customers sent back (Returns Inwards)."
>

$$
\text{Net Sales} = \text{Sales} - \text{Returns Inwards}
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Cost of Sales (Cost of Goods Sold)"
  short="Start with Opening Stock, add Net Purchases and Carriage Inwards (shipping goods IN), then subtract unsold Closing Stock at year-end."
>

$$
\text{Cost of Sales} = \text{Opening Stock} + (\text{Purchases} - \text{Returns Outwards}) + \text{Carriage Inwards} - \text{Closing Stock}
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="Gross Profit & Net Profit"
  short="Gross Profit is trading profit before bills; Net Profit is final profit after adding other incomes and paying every operating expense."
>

$$
\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Net Sales} - \text{Cost of Sales}
$$

$$
\text{Net Profit} = \text{Gross Profit} + \text{Other Revenues} - \text{Operating Expenses}
$$

</FormulaHint>

</FormulaCard>

## 3. Classic Exam Traps (Watch Out!)

Examiners repeatedly report these four preventable errors on Paper 2:

| Common Mistake | Correct Rule |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mixing up Carriage Inwards vs Carriage Outwards** | **Carriage Inwards** *(delivery cost to bring stock IN to our shop)* → **ADD to Purchases in the Trading Account**.<br/>**Carriage Outwards** *(delivery cost to send sold goods OUT to customers)* → **Expense in the Profit & Loss Account**! |
| **Adding Cost of Sales instead of subtracting it** | **Cost of Sales** is always **SUBTRACTED** from Net Sales to find Gross Profit! |
| **Putting Other Revenue under Expenses** | **Discount Received**, **Commission Received**, and **Rent Received** are **incomes** — **ADD** them to Gross Profit! |
| **Putting Drawings or Assets inside the Income Statement** | **Drawings**, **Debtors**, **Creditors**, and **Cash** NEVER go in the Trading & P&L Account — they belong on the **Balance Sheet**! |

On S. Morrison Hardware's Balance Sheet as at 31 December 2025, this **J\$60,000 Net Profit** is **ADDED** to Opening Capital (J\$220,000) before deducting Drawings (J\$20,000) to get Closing Capital of **J\$260,000**! *(If expenses exceeded Gross Profit + Other Revenue, the result would be a **Net Loss**, which is **SUBTRACTED** from Opening Capital.)*

<ClassifyActivity
  title="Classwork: Where Does Each Item Go?"
  instruction="Decide whether each item belongs in the Trading Account, Other Revenue (P&L), Expenses (P&L), or Balance Sheet Only."

      explanation: 'Carriage Inwards is part of the direct cost of bringing purchases into the business — added to Net Purchases in the Trading Account.',
    },
    {
      prompt: 'Carriage Outwards',
      answer: 'Expenses (Less in P&L)',
      explanation: 'Delivering sold goods out to customers is a selling expense deducted in the Profit & Loss Account.',
    },
    {
      prompt: 'Discount Received',
      answer: 'Other Revenue (Add to GP)',
      explanation: 'A cash discount received from suppliers increases profit — added to Gross Profit under Other Revenue.',
    },
    {
      prompt: 'Returns Outwards (Purchases Returns)',
      answer: 'Trading Account',
      explanation: 'Subtracted from Purchases inside Cost of Sales in the Trading Account.',
    },
    {
      prompt: 'Drawings by the Owner',
      answer: 'Balance Sheet Only',
      explanation: 'Drawings are NOT a business expense — they are deducted from Capital on the Balance Sheet.',
    },
    {
      prompt: 'Discount Allowed',
      answer: 'Expenses (Less in P&L)',
      explanation: 'A cash discount allowed to debtors is an operating expense deducted in the Profit & Loss Account.',
    },
  ]}
