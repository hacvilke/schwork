## 1. Why Do Businesses Use Accounting Ratios?

Raw dollar figures can trick you! Earning **J\$60,000** Net Profit sounds small next to a mega-chain earning **J\$600,000** — until you discover the small shop only invested J\$300,000 (a **20% return**!) while the mega-chain invested J\$12,000,000 (only a **5% return**!).

**Accounting Ratios** turn financial statement numbers into clean percentages, multiples, and ratios so owners, banks, and investors can judge two big questions:
1. **Profitability Ratios:** *"How well is the business earning profit and moving its stock?"*
2. **Liquidity Ratios:** *"Can the business comfortably pay its short-term bills on time?"*

## 2. Profitability & Stock Efficiency Ratios (Hover or Tap Any Formula!)

<FormulaCard
  title="Profitability & Stock Turnover Formulae"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Always express GP%, NP%, and ROCE as percentages (%), Average Stock in dollars (J$), and Rate of Stock Turnover in 'times' per year!"
>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="1. Gross Profit Margin (Gross Profit Percentage)"
  short="Shows how many cents of Gross Profit the business keeps out of every $1.00 of Net Sales before paying operating expenses."
>

$$
\text{Gross Profit Percentage} = \frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Net Sales}} \times 100
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="2. Net Profit Margin (Net Profit Percentage)"
  short="Shows how many cents of final Net Profit remain from every $1.00 of Net Sales after ALL operating expenses are paid."
>

$$
\text{Net Profit Percentage} = \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Net Sales}} \times 100
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="3. Average Stock (Average Inventory)"
  short="The midpoint stock held during the year — add Opening Stock and Closing Stock, then divide by 2."
>

$$
\text{Average Stock} = \frac{\text{Opening Stock} + \text{Closing Stock}}{2}
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="4. Rate of Stock Turnover (Inventory Turnover)"
  short="How many TIMES in a year the business sells and replaces its average stock. Higher usually means brisk sales and fresh inventory!"
>

$$
\text{Rate of Stock Turnover} = \frac{\text{Cost of Sales}}{\text{Average Stock}} \quad (\text{in times})
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="5. Return on Capital Employed (ROCE / ROI)"
  short="Measures the percentage return earned on every $100 of long-term capital invested in the business. Compare it to a bank deposit interest rate!"
>

$$
\text{ROCE} = \frac{\text{Net Profit (before Interest)}}{\text{Capital Employed}} \times 100
$$

</FormulaHint>

</FormulaCard>

:::tip Two Ways You May See ROCE / Capital Employed Calculated
- **Standard Formula (Official Syllabus):** $\text{Capital Employed} = \text{Closing Capital} + \text{Long-Term Liabilities}$ *(which equals Total Assets − Current Liabilities)*.
- **Average Capital Variation (Used in some classrooms):** When a question asks for Return on **Average Capital**, find $\text{Average Capital} = \frac{\text{Opening Capital} + \text{Closing Capital}}{2}$ first, then divide Net Profit by Average Capital and multiply by 100! Always show your formula clearly so examiners see your method.
:::

## 3. Liquidity Ratios (Can You Pay Your Short-Term Bills?)

<FormulaCard
  title="Liquidity Formulae (Working Capital, Current Ratio & Acid-Test)"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Liquidity ratios compare short-term resources against short-term debts. Current Ratio includes Stock; Acid-Test removes Stock because Stock takes longest to turn into cash!"
>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="1. Working Capital (Net Current Assets)"
  short="The dollar cushion left over after subtracting Current Liabilities from Current Assets."
>

$$
\text{Working Capital} = \text{Current Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities}
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="2. Current Ratio (Working Capital Ratio)"
  short="Shows how many dollars of Current Assets exist for every $1 of Current Liabilities. Healthy benchmark: roughly 2 : 1."
>

$$
\text{Current Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}} \quad (\text{written as } x : 1)
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="3. Acid-Test Ratio (Quick Ratio)"
  short="Stricter cash test! Removes Closing Stock (slowest to turn into cash) from Current Assets before dividing by Current Liabilities. Healthy benchmark: at least 1 : 1."
>

$$
\text{Acid-Test Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets} - \text{Closing Stock}}{\text{Current Liabilities}} \quad (\text{written as } x : 1)
$$

</FormulaHint>

</FormulaCard>

*(Bonus efficiency ratios on the syllabus: **Debtors Collection Period** = $\frac{\text{Debtors}}{\text{Credit Sales}} \times 365\text{ days}$, and **Creditors Payment Period** = $\frac{\text{Creditors}}{\text{Credit Purchases}} \times 365\text{ days}$.)*

## 4. Worked Example: Complete Ratio Analysis of *S. Morrison Hardware*

<WorkedExample
  title="Calculating & Interpreting All 8 Ratios (S. Morrison Hardware, 31 Dec 2025)"
  marks={14}
  problem={
    <>
      Using <strong>S. Morrison Hardware’s</strong> Trading &amp; P&amp;L figures from Lesson 5.1 plus its Balance Sheet balances at 31 Dec 2025:
      <br />
      Net Sales <strong>J$400,000</strong> | Cost of Sales <strong>J$240,000</strong> | Gross Profit <strong>J$160,000</strong> | Net Profit <strong>J$60,000</strong>
      <br />
      Opening Stock <strong>J$38,000</strong> | Closing Stock <strong>J$42,000</strong>
      <br />
      Current Assets <strong>J$90,000</strong> *(Stock 42,000; Debtors 26,000; Bank 18,000; Cash 4,000)* | Current Liabilities <strong>J$40,000</strong>
      <br />
      Opening Capital <strong>J$220,000</strong> | Closing Capital <strong>J$260,000</strong> | Long-Term Loan <strong>J$40,000</strong> *(Capital Employed = J$300,000)*
      <br />
      Calculate all five Profitability/Stock ratios and all three Liquidity measures, and comment briefly on liquidity.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Always include the right UNIT on your final answer! Percentages get '%', Stock Turnover gets 'times', Working Capital gets 'J$', and Current & Acid-Test Ratios are written in the form 'x : 1'!"
>

### A. Profitability & Stock Ratios
1. **Gross Profit Percentage:** $\frac{160{,}000}{400{,}000} \times 100 =$ **40.0%** *(earns 40 cents Gross Profit per J\$1 of sales)*
2. **Net Profit Percentage:** $\frac{60{,}000}{400{,}000} \times 100 =$ **15.0%** *(operating expenses absorbed 25% of sales — watch expense control!)*
3. **Average Stock:** $\frac{38{,}000 + 42{,}000}{2} =$ **J\$40,000**
4. **Rate of Stock Turnover:** $\frac{240{,}000}{40{,}000} =$ **6 times** *(sells and replaces its average stock 6 times per year — every 2 months!)*
5. **Return on Capital Employed (ROCE):** $\frac{60{,}000}{300{,}000} \times 100 =$ **20.0%** *(far better than a typical 4–5% bank savings rate!)*
   - *(Note: On Average Owner's Capital of $\frac{220{,}000 + 260{,}000}{2} = 240{,}000$, the return is $\frac{60{,}000}{240{,}000} \times 100 = 25.0\%$.)*

### B. Liquidity Measures & Recommendations
6. **Working Capital:** J\$90,000 − J\$40,000 = **J\$50,000**
7. **Current Ratio:** $\frac{90{,}000}{40{,}000} =$ **2.25 : 1**
8. **Acid-Test (Quick) Ratio:** $\frac{90{,}000 - 42{,}000}{40{,}000} = \frac{48{,}000}{40{,}000} =$ **1.2 : 1**

**Evaluation & Business Recommendations:**
- **Strong Liquidity:** Both the Current Ratio (`2.25 : 1`, just above `2 : 1`) and Acid-Test Ratio (`1.2 : 1`, safely above `1 : 1`) show S. Morrison Hardware can easily pay creditors and bills on time without panic-selling stock.
- **What if Current Ratio were TOO high (e.g. `4 : 1`)?** Excess idle cash sitting in a current account earns zero return — recommend investing surplus cash or expanding profitable product lines!
- **What if Acid-Test were TOO low (e.g. `0.5 : 1`)?** Too much cash is trapped in slow-moving stock — recommend reducing excess stock, chasing overdue debtors faster, or injecting fresh capital.

</WorkedExample>
