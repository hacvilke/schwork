## 1. Why Ratios Beat Raw Dollar Figures!

Suppose Shop A earned **J\$60,000** Net Profit while Mega-Store B earned **J\$600,000** Net Profit. Which business performed better? You **cannot tell** from dollar totals alone!
- If Shop A only invested **J\$300,000** of capital, it earned a brilliant **20% return**!
- If Mega-Store B invested **J\$12,000,000**, it only earned **5%** — barely better than leaving money in a fixed bank deposit!

**Accounting Ratios** turn financial statement data into standardised percentages, multiples, and ratios so managers, banks, and investors can:
1. **Track trends over time** (e.g. comparing 2024 vs 2025 vs 2026 inside the same business);
2. **Benchmark against rivals or industry averages** (e.g. comparing two Jamaican pharmacies); and
3. **Spot hidden weaknesses early** (such as runaway expenses, slow-moving stock, or looming cash shortages!).

## 2. Profitability & Stock Efficiency Ratios

Hover or tap any formula below to see what it measures in plain English:

<FormulaCard
  title="Profitability & Stock Efficiency Formulae"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Express GP%, NP%, and ROCE/ROI as percentages (%), Average Stock in dollars (J$), and Rate of Stock Turnover in 'times' per year!"
>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="1. Gross Profit Percentage (Gross Profit Margin)"
  short="Shows how many cents of Gross Profit the firm keeps out of every $1.00 of Net Sales after paying for the goods sold."
>

$$
\text{Gross Profit Percentage} = \frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Net Sales}} \times 100
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="2. Net Profit Percentage (Net Profit Margin)"
  short="Shows how many cents of final Net Profit remain from every $1.00 of Net Sales after ALL operating expenses are deducted."
>

$$
\text{Net Profit Percentage} = \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Net Sales}} \times 100
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="3. Average Stock (Average Inventory)"
  short="The midpoint stock held during the accounting year — always add Opening Stock + Closing Stock and divide by 2."
>

$$
\text{Average Stock} = \frac{\text{Opening Stock} + \text{Closing Stock}}{2}
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="4. Rate of Stock Turnover (Inventory Turnover)"
  short="How many TIMES in a year the firm sells and replaces its average stock. Always uses COST OF SALES on top (never Sales!)."
>

$$
\text{Rate of Stock Turnover} = \frac{\text{Cost of Sales}}{\text{Average Stock}} \quad (\text{times})
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="5. Return on Capital Employed (ROCE / Return on Investment)"
  short="Measures the percentage profit earned for every $100 of long-term capital invested in the business. Compare directly against bank interest rates!"
>

$$
\text{ROCE} = \frac{\text{Net Profit (before Interest)}}{\text{Capital Employed}} \times 100
$$

</FormulaHint>

</FormulaCard>

### How to Interpret Profitability & Stock Ratios (Paper 2 Insight!)

- **Gross Profit Percentage (GP%) — What makes it rise or fall?**
  - *Falls if:* the shop slashes selling prices, supplier purchase costs rise without adjusting retail prices, too many trade discounts are given, or **stock theft / spoilage** shrinks Closing Stock!
  - *Improves if:* the shop negotiates cheaper bulk suppliers, reduces waste, or focuses on higher-margin goods.
- **Comparing GP% vs NP% (The Expense Diagnostic!):**
  - If **GP% stayed steady at 40%**, yet **NP% dropped from 18% down to 9%**, you immediately know the problem is **NOT** buying/selling — it is **out-of-control Operating Expenses** (soaring electricity, excessive wages, bad debts, or advertising waste)!
- **Rate of Stock Turnover — Context Matters!**
  - A **high turnover** (e.g. 12–20 times/year in a grocery or bakery) signals brisk sales, fresh perishable stock, and cash coming in fast.
  - A **low turnover** (e.g. 2–3 times/year) is normal for luxury jewellery or furniture stores, but dangerous in a supermarket — it signals **overstocking, obsolete goods, poor marketing, or prices set too high**!
- **ROCE / ROI (Tested on May/June 2025 Paper 02 Q4!):**
  - **Capital Employed** = `Closing Capital + Long-Term Liabilities` (or `Total Assets − Current Liabilities`). *(Note: Some questions ask for Return on **Average Capital** = $\frac{\text{Opening Capital} + \text{Closing Capital}}{2}$ — always state your formula clearly!)*
  - **Decision rule:** Compare ROCE against safe alternative investments (e.g. bank deposits or industry average rate). If a business earns **20% ROCE** while the industry average is **12%** and bank fixed deposits pay **5%**, the business is an attractive investment!

## 3. Liquidity Ratios (Short-Term Survival & Solvency!)

Even a profitable business can go bankrupt if it cannot pay its electricity bill, staff wages, and credit suppliers on time! **Liquidity ratios** test short-term bill-paying ability:

<FormulaCard
  title="Liquidity Formulae (Working Capital, Current Ratio & Acid-Test)"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Current Ratio tests ALL Current Assets against Current Liabilities (benchmark ≈ 2 : 1); Acid-Test strips out Closing Stock to test immediate cash readiness (benchmark ≈ 1 : 1)."
>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="1. Working Capital (Net Current Assets)"
  short="The net dollar cushion available to run day-to-day operations after covering short-term debts."
>

$$
\text{Working Capital} = \text{Current Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities}
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="2. Current Ratio (Working Capital Ratio)"
  short="How many dollars of Current Assets exist to cover every $1 of Current Liabilities. Healthy benchmark: roughly 2 : 1."
>

$$
\text{Current Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}} \quad (\text{written as } x : 1)
$$

</FormulaHint>

<FormulaHint
  block
  label="3. Acid-Test Ratio (Quick Ratio)"
  short="Stricter immediate-cash test! Removes Closing Stock (and Prepayments if given) because stock takes longest to convert to cash. Healthy benchmark: at least 1 : 1."
>

$$
\text{Acid-Test Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets} - \text{Closing Stock}}{\text{Current Liabilities}} \quad (\text{written as } x : 1)
$$

</FormulaHint>

</FormulaCard>

### Diagnosing Liquidity Problems (Too Low vs Too High!)

| Situation | Benchmark Warning | What It Means | Practical Management Recommendations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Current Ratio < `1.5 : 1` or Acid-Test < `1 : 1`** | **Dangerously Low Liquidity** | Firm may struggle to pay creditors, wages, or bank overdraft on time — risk of insolvency! | Inject fresh owner capital or take a **long-term loan** (to pay off short-term debt), sell idle Fixed Assets, chase overdue debtors faster (offer cash discounts), cut owner Drawings, or clear slow-moving stock! |
| **Current Ratio is fine (`2.4 : 1`), BUT Acid-Test is low (`0.6 : 1`)** | **Cash Trapped in Stock!** | Most of Current Assets is tied up in unsold Stock sitting on shelves rather than Debtors or Cash! | Run clearance promotions to turn excess stock into cash, tighten stock reordering levels, and avoid bulk over-purchasing. |
| **Current Ratio > `3.5 : 1` and Acid-Test > `2.5 : 1`** | **Too High (Idle Resources!)** | Too much cash is sitting idle earning zero profit, or debtors are taking far too long to pay! | Invest excess cash into profitable expansion or interest-bearing accounts, settle creditors early to earn Discount Received, and monitor debtor credit control. |

*(Additional credit-control ratios sometimes seen: **Debtors Collection Period** = $\frac{\text{Debtors}}{\text{Credit Sales}} \times 365\text{ days}$, and **Creditors Payment Period** = $\frac{\text{Creditors}}{\text{Credit Purchases}} \times 365\text{ days}$.)*

## 4. Worked Example: Complete Ratio Analysis & Management Report

<WorkedExample
  title="Calculating & Evaluating All 8 Ratios (S. Morrison Hardware, 31 Dec 2025)"
  marks={15}
  problem={
    <>
      Using <strong>S. Morrison Hardware’s</strong> Income Statement figures from Lesson 5.1 plus its Balance Sheet balances at 31 Dec 2025:
      <br />
      Net Sales <strong>J$400,000</strong> | Cost of Sales <strong>J$240,000</strong> | Gross Profit <strong>J$160,000</strong> | Net Profit <strong>J$60,000</strong>
      <br />
      Opening Stock <strong>J$38,000</strong> | Closing Stock <strong>J$42,000</strong>
      <br />
      Current Assets <strong>J$90,000</strong> *(Stock 42,000; Debtors 26,000; Bank 18,000; Cash 4,000)* | Current Liabilities <strong>J$40,000</strong>
      <br />
      Closing Capital <strong>J$260,000</strong> | Long-Term Loan <strong>J$40,000</strong> *(Capital Employed = J$300,000)*
      <br />
      <strong>(a)</strong> Calculate all five Profitability/Stock ratios and all three Liquidity measures.
      <br />
      <strong>(b)</strong> Write a brief management evaluation commenting on profitability and liquidity, with two practical recommendations.
    </>
  }
  examTip="Never omit the UNIT on your final answer! Percentages get '%', Stock Turnover gets 'times', Working Capital gets 'J$', and Current & Acid-Test Ratios are written strictly as 'x : 1' (rounded cleanly to 2 decimal places when needed)."
>

### (a) Ratio Calculations
1. **Gross Profit Percentage:** $\frac{160{,}000}{400{,}000} \times 100 =$ **40.0%**
2. **Net Profit Percentage:** $\frac{60{,}000}{400{,}000} \times 100 =$ **15.0%**
3. **Average Stock:** $\frac{38{,}000 + 42{,}000}{2} =$ **J\$40,000**
4. **Rate of Stock Turnover:** $\frac{240{,}000}{40{,}000} =$ **6 times**
5. **Return on Capital Employed (ROCE):** $\frac{60{,}000}{300{,}000} \times 100 =$ **20.0%**
6. **Working Capital:** J\$90,000 − J\$40,000 = **J\$50,000**
7. **Current Ratio:** $\frac{90{,}000}{40{,}000} =$ **2.25 : 1**
8. **Acid-Test (Quick) Ratio:** $\frac{90{,}000 - 42{,}000}{40{,}000} = \frac{48{,}000}{40{,}000} =$ **1.2 : 1**

### (b) Management Evaluation & Recommendations *(Exam Report Style!)*
- **Profitability Assessment:** The store earns a healthy **40.0% Gross Profit Margin** and a strong **20.0% ROCE** (far exceeding typical bank savings rates), moving its average stock a brisk **6 times per year** (roughly once every 2 months). However, operating expenses absorb **25% of sales** (`40% GP − 15% NP`), squeezing final Net Profit.
- **Liquidity Assessment:** Short-term solvency is solid — Working Capital is **+J\$50,000**, the Current Ratio (**2.25 : 1**) sits just above the `2 : 1` benchmark, and the Acid-Test Ratio (**1.2 : 1**) sits safely above `1 : 1`, meaning the store can settle all creditors without panic-selling stock.
- **Two Recommendations:**
  1. **Review Operating Expenses** (especially Wages & Rent) to raise Net Profit Margin closer to 20% while keeping Gross Margin strong.
  2. **Monitor Closing Stock Growth** (stock rose from J\$38,000 to J\$42,000) so stock does not start tying up liquid cash and pulling the Acid-Test Ratio below `1 : 1`.

</WorkedExample>
