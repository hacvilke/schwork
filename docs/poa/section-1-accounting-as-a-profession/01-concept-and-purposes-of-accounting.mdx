## 1. What Is Accounting?

**Accounting** is the systematic process of **recording, classifying, summarizing, and interpreting** financial transactions and events expressed in monetary terms, in order to communicate useful financial information to decision-makers.

Notice the four sequential verbs — examiners award marks when you include **all four**:

1. **Recording:** Entering every verified financial event (from invoices, receipts, and vouchers) into the books of original entry.
2. **Classifying:** Grouping similar transactions into dedicated ledger accounts (for example, putting all electricity payments into the *Utilities Expense* account and all credit sales into *Sales*).
3. **Summarizing:** Combining ledger totals into clear periodic reports — the **Trial Balance**, **Income Statement**, **Balance Sheet**, and **Cash Flow Statement**.
4. **Interpreting:** Analyzing the finished reports using ratios, comparisons, and trends to guide future decisions (such as cutting waste, raising capital, or opening a second branch).

### Bookkeeping vs. Accounting

Students often treat **bookkeeping** and **accounting** as identical, but bookkeeping is only the *foundation stage* of accounting:

| Feature | Bookkeeping | Accounting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Main Focus** | Accurate daily recording and posting of routine financial transactions | Full process: designing systems, summarizing financial statements, and interpreting results |
| **Core Tasks** | Filing source documents, entering journals, posting ledgers, balancing cash books | Adjusting accounts, preparing Income Statements & Balance Sheets, ratio analysis, advising management |
| **Decision Role** | Supplies clean raw data | Uses summarized data to recommend business actions |
| **Scope** | Mechanical and procedural (Steps 1–2) | Analytical and strategic (Steps 1–4) |

## 3. The Six Core Purposes of Accounting

Every business — from a neighborhood bakery to an international airline — relies on accounting for six essential purposes:

| Purpose | Why It Matters in Practice | Concrete Business Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Record Keeping** | Creates a permanent, verifiable history (audit trail) of every dollar received or spent | Proving to a supplier that Invoice #412 was already paid on 14 March |
| **2. Financial Reporting** | Measures profit or loss over a period and shows financial position (what is owned vs. owed) | Preparing an annual Income Statement and Balance Sheet for the owner and bank |
| **3. Decision Making** | Replaces guesswork with evidence when setting prices, hiring staff, or expanding | Deciding whether to buy a delivery van or continue paying a courier service |
| **4. Performance Evaluation** | Tracks whether profit margins, sales growth, and efficiency are improving or slipping | Comparing this year's net profit margin (14%) against last year's (19%) |
| **5. Control & Asset Protection** | Detects errors, prevents cash theft, and tracks inventory and debts owed | Reconciling the cash register tape against actual cash banked each evening |
| **6. Legal & Tax Compliance** | Satisfies statutory filing obligations and calculates exact taxes payable | Submitting accurate income tax and sales tax returns to revenue authorities |

### Introductory Bridge: The Accounting Equation

<FormulaCard
  title="The Fundamental Accounting Equation"
  onFormulaSheet={false}
  summary="Everything a business owns (Assets) must be funded either by the owner (Capital) or by outsiders (Liabilities)."
>

$$
\text{Assets} = \text{Capital} + \text{Liabilities}
$$

- **Assets:** Resources owned or controlled by the business (cash, inventory, equipment, receivables).
- **Capital (Owner's Equity):** The owner's financial stake in the business.
- **Liabilities:** Debts owed to external parties (bank loans, unpaid supplier invoices).

</FormulaCard>

## 5. Worked Example

<WorkedExample
  title="Diagnosing Record-Keeping Failures & Explaining Accounting Purposes"
  marks={8}
  problem={
    <>
      <p>
        <strong>Scenario:</strong> Devon operates a busy mobile phone repair shop. He keeps
        all cash receipts in a drawer, mixes personal grocery payments with shop cash, never
        prepares an Income Statement, and guesses how much tax to set aside at year-end.
        Recently he applied for a $15,000 bank loan to buy diagnostic equipment, and the bank
        rejected his application.
      </p>
      <ol>
        <li>Define the term <strong>accounting</strong>. <em>[2 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Distinguish between <strong>bookkeeping</strong> and <strong>accounting</strong> in Devon's shop. <em>[2 marks]</em></li>
        <li>Explain <strong>two specific purposes of accounting</strong> that would directly solve Devon's problems and help his loan application. <em>[4 marks]</em></li>
      </ol>
    </>
  }
>

**Step 1 — Define accounting (2 marks):**
- Accounting is the systematic process of **recording, classifying, summarizing, and interpreting** financial transactions in monetary terms to provide useful information for economic decision-making. *(1 mark for systematic recording/classifying/summarizing + 1 mark for interpreting/communicating for decision-making.)*

**Step 2 — Distinguish bookkeeping from accounting (2 marks):**
- **Bookkeeping** in Devon's shop involves the routine daily recording of cash sales, repair parts bought, and rent paid from receipts into journals and ledgers. *(1 mark)*
- **Accounting** goes further by preparing formal periodic financial statements (Income Statement and Balance Sheet) and interpreting Devon's profit and liquidity to guide loan and pricing decisions. *(1 mark)*

**Step 3 — Two purposes of accounting applied to Devon's shop (4 marks — 2 marks each):**
1. **Financial Reporting & Decision Making:** Preparing an Income Statement and Balance Sheet proves Devon's true profit, cash flow, and assets to the bank, giving lenders objective evidence that he can repay the $15,000 loan. *(2 marks)*
2. **Control & Legal/Tax Compliance:** Keeping separate, organized business records prevents personal grocery expenses from distorting shop profit, protects drawer cash from theft or loss, and ensures Devon calculates and pays the exact amount of tax required by law rather than guessing. *(2 marks)*

</WorkedExample>

:::tip Quick Exam Checklist
- When asked to **define accounting**, always mention **recording, classifying, summarizing, and interpreting** financial information for **decision-making**.
- Never write "to count money" as a purpose of accounting — use precise terms: **record keeping, financial reporting, decision-making, performance evaluation, control, and legal compliance**.
:::
